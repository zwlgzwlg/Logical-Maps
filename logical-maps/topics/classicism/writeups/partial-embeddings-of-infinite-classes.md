# Distinctness-preserving necessity on partial embeddings of equivalence classes

A true proposition can have a distinctness-preserving necessity guard even when coextension classes have no transversal. In one example every relational algebra is atomic and there is Logical Necessity. In another, the scalar algebra is atomless and complete, every relational algebra has countable joins, and a particular family of unary relations has no join. A third satisfies Mathematical Possibilism with the same obstruction to a transversal.

## Coefficients and their two modal calculations

Start with the [bounded partial injections](bounded-partial-injections.html). Partition the set $A$ of sites into infinitely many infinite classes. Write $G$ for the permutations preserving the equivalence relation in both directions. Restrict the coefficient $f$ to be a partial injection **preserving and reflecting** equivalence on its live domain. Thus every restriction of a class automorphism is a permissible coefficient. The product of two permissible maps is permissible. Undefined sites go to the default point when the sites are individuals. Retain the independent free word. At relational types use exactly the rows determined by the word, the indicated tag information and the restriction of $f$ to some pin $P$, with the usual equivariance under postcomposition by $G$. A finite-pin model uses countably many countable classes. A countable-pin model uses $\aleph_1$ classes of size $\aleph_1$. Application, abstraction and intensional extensionality follow as in the bounded partial-injection model: agreement on the observed data survives left composition, the image of the live part of a source pin pins the transport, and every class automorphism still acts.

In the finite-pin setting a tag $k\in\omega\cup\{\infty\}$ bounds the rank when finite, composition takes the minimum, and a profile can read $\min(k,b)$ for some finite $b$. In the countable-pin setting with singleton individuals, use the same ranks but allow the row to read the exact tag. In the countable-pin setting with moving individuals, omit the rank and the tag entirely. The word alphabet is countably infinite. The signature can have just one propositional name.

Let $p$ be any true proposition with pin $P$. Use the true scalar $q$ requiring an empty word and every site in $P$ to be live. With a cutoff $b$ also require tag at least $b$; with exact observation require tag $\infty$. If $q$ can be recovered beyond $h$, the word at $h$ is already empty, its tag meets the requirement and every site in $P$ is live. The partial class embedding $h|_P$ extends to some $\pi\in G$: first complete the partial bijection on classes and then the bijections on their elements. The complement on each side has the full ambient cardinality. Equivariance gives $p(\pi)=p(1)$, and pinning gives $p(h)=p(\pi)$. Thus $q\land\Box(\Diamond q\to p)$ holds. This proves that every truth is ND-necessary, necessarily since the model has one object. It adapts Dorr's [guard for class-reflecting surjections](symmetric-class-reflecting-surjections.html).

For comparison the earlier, unrestricted [bounded partial injections](bounded-partial-injections.html) and [surjection product with classes](symmetric-infinite-classes-glued-constants.html) admit all permutations as units at the empty word and identity tag. In those original models the three forms of necessity agree, even though some truths fail 5-necessity. Let $U$ be this group of all permutation units, including the ones outside $G$. The row $O$ of propositions true at every member of $U$ is invariant under $G$, since right multiplication by a class automorphism permutes $U$. The operator with this same row at every arrow is therefore admitted with empty pin. Applied to $p$ at $h$ it tests $p(uh)$ for every $u\in U$. These orbits are equivalence classes, so the operator satisfies the S5 laws at every stage. A 5-necessary $p$ holds in particular at every $u\in U$. The guard requiring the empty word, a sufficiently high tag when present, and a live injective restriction to the pin is return-closed. On this guard the pin restriction extends to an arbitrary permutation; its values need not respect classes in these original models. The value of $p$ is therefore true. This proves $\Box(\Box_5p\to\Box_{\ne}p)$ universally. Choose two distinct sites in one class and let $p$ require their images to be live and in one class. The proposition is true and fails at a unit taking the pair to different classes. It fails 5-necessity by the same S5 operator. These two original-model statements are pure and pass through the pointed compactness interpretation of names.

## Selection, analysis and signatures

Write $p_x$ for the scalar testing liveness of site $x$. Under $f$ it becomes $p_{fx}$ if $x$ lives and bottom otherwise. Distinct sites give distinct scalars by the partial identity on a singleton. The current set of codes of any class is realized by a property pinned on one member: at $f$ use the class of that member's image when it is live. Generally any set of current tuples invariant under the pointwise stabilizer of $P$ is a current row. Assign its $\pi$-image when $f|_P=\pi|_P$ for some $\pi\in G$, and the empty row otherwise. The possible matching permutations agree on the value of the row.

The [two-pin proof](bounded-partial-injections.html) of failure of Transversal now applies to these properties of scalar codes. A classifier with pin $P$ would select a unique representative $Y$ of a class $C$ disjoint from $P$. Move a pin $S\supseteq P$ of $Y$ off itself outside $P$ by a class automorphism fixing $P$ and preserving $C$. This leaves $Y$ unchanged by uniqueness. The partial identity on $S$ retains $C$ as its row. If followed by a class swap fixing $P$ and moving $C$, the composite agrees with this partial identity on the moved pin of $Y$, but equivariance requires its row to move. The same class symmetries prevent a transversal of the actual equivalence relation on codes, and prevent a selection for the relation requesting a different code in the input's class. Thus Transversal, Transversal Choice and Relational Choice all fail.

At a tagged model a true scalar with a pin $P$ is still true if a site outside $P$ is deleted from the identity at tag $\infty$. Separating this case by the site's liveness splits the scalar, so no true atom exists. The untagged case has the same split. Since there is just one object, a nonzero self-atom would be instantiated as an atom at that object. This excludes propositional Self-Atomicity as well as Actuality.

The [word-label construction](bounded-partial-injections.html) gives continuum many distinct labels at each relational type, distinguished by the first letter of a future word. Any subset of their tuples and its transported exact images is available. The number and real carriers, the arithmetic and membership graphs and all the finite-arity families of subset representatives therefore have the required rigid exact images. Natural-number labels exhaust the Frege numbers under the Peano guard, by testing with the invariant current set of standard numeral values. In the moving-individual case, a common countable pin of a proposed real structure fixes its numeral orbit pointwise. The orbit itself is an admitted inductive property by the current-row construction. Every subset of these numbers is admitted by the same pin. Extensionality forces each corresponding real individual to be fixed by the pointwise stabilizer. Its only fixed individuals are the countably many pinned sites and the default. This is incompatible with the continuum many real labels required by comprehension. At singleton individual type no such structure is possible either. This proves Arithmetic Necessitism and Second-Order Analytic Necessitism in the three settings.

For names interpreted by top the one-object construction has No Signature Contingency. Alternatively add Logical Necessity by the [finite last-letter gluing argument](bounded-partial-injections.html). In our signature with one propositional name, choose one witnessed input and one distinct final letter for each of finitely many witnessed matrices, prescribing the name on the corresponding word cones. A finite union of their pins and the greatest of the finitely many cutoffs suffice. For every possible instance the transported name is already a current input at this object. Compactness preserves the complete pure pointed theory while realizing all instances. Every pure verdict used here is a sentence or a schema of such sentences.

## Rank bounds and joins

With finite pins and truncated tags, an inhabited relation at any address is also inhabited at a finite tag saturated by an allowed coefficient of that rank. Match the given values on its pin and pad the rank with a fresh source class for each fresh target class, keeping all prescribed dead sites dead. Its post-$G$ orbit together with the chosen tuple is an atom, as in the original bounded model. The original [Dedekind-finite clopen algebra](dedekind-finite-small-algebras-at-bounded-partial-injections.html) still precludes Mathematical Possibilism. Every finite live/dead pattern used to distinguish its codes is realized by a partial identity. The proof of completeness and Dedekind finiteness uses the same full class-automorphism group. The family testing the individual even tags still lacks a supremum because every profile reads only a finite cutoff.

With countably many sites allowed in a pin, the union of countably many pins is a pin. In both countable-pin settings each externally countable family has a pointwise join: the coordinatewise union is equivariant, and a common pin now suffices at every word and every exact tag. The current numeral cut described above makes the native countability graph an external countability witness. Countable Boolean Completeness therefore holds necessarily at every relational type.

When the tag is read exactly, fix a countably infinite set $P$ and take the scalar requiring all of $P$ live. It can occur only at tag $\infty$. Any nonzero subscalar can be split by the liveness of a fresh site outside its pin together with $P$. Restrict the witnessing coefficient to the old pin and then extend it on the fresh site, assigning an image compatible with its class. This proves failure of scalar Atomicity. Yet every word-specific zero-tag address is a scalar atom, giving arbitrarily many finite Boolean intervals. Since individuals form a singleton and a countable set of entities always has a common pin, the [finite benchmark argument](bounded-partial-injections.html) gives revised Mathematical Possibilism.

At tag one and the empty word, each condition that a specified site $x$ lives is an atom $a_x$. The current family of these atoms for $x$ in a fixed class $C$ is admitted. A least upper bound would contain all its members and exclude every $a_y$ with $y\notin C$, by comparison with its complement. Live singleton maps on $x\in C$ and $y\notin C$ outside the putative bound's pin agree on that pin, and the tag and word agree. This contradiction refutes full Boolean Completeness at scalar type.

## A complete atomless scalar algebra without higher-type completeness

In the untagged setting a scalar is a function of the word $w$ and the live subset $D\cap P$ of its pin. Any two partial embeddings with the same live subset there differ on its live images by a target automorphism. Conversely every such truth function defines a scalar. Every nonzero scalar splits by the liveness of a fresh site, so atomlessness holds necessarily.

For an admitted family $\mathcal F$ of scalars choose a pin $P$ whose pointwise stabilizer preserves its current extension. At $(w,D)$ define $u$ to hold if some member is true at $(w,D')$ with $D'\cap P=D\cap P$. This admitted scalar bounds the family. To see it is least, suppose a competing upper bound pinned on $Q$ fails at $(w,D)$. If $u$ held there, choose a witnessing member $s$ with pin $L$ and such a $D'$. A class automorphism fixing $P$ can move $L\setminus P$ off $Q$. The moved member still belongs to $\mathcal F$. Its required live bits on its moved pin and the bits of $D$ on $Q$ agree on their overlap. Realize their union by a partial identity at the same word. The moved member then holds and the competing upper bound fails, a contradiction. Consequently Boolean Completeness at $t$ holds necessarily.

For higher-type failure take the family indexed by one class $C$ whose member at $x$ has the singleton row $\{p_{fx}\}$ when $x$ lives, and the empty row otherwise. These individual predicates of scalars have pin $x$, and the family is a current row with a pin in $C$. For each $y\notin C$ the tester which omits $p_{fy}$ if $y$ lives and otherwise omits nothing is an upper bound by partial injectivity. Any proposed supremum with pin $P$ has the same row at the two singleton maps $x\mapsto z$ and $y\mapsto z$ where $x\in C$, $y\notin C$ and both are outside $P$. Its row must contain $p_z$ at the first and omit it at the second. Thus Boolean Completeness at all relational types fails. The lack of scalar atoms also prevents representation of the two-element Boolean algebra by a principal interval and refutes revised Mathematical Possibilism.
