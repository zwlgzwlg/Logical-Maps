# Possibly Witnessed Possibility with Mathematical Possibilism and necessary individual infinity

For every set-sized free signature the [infinite-individual Stone construction](stone-terminals-infinite-individuals.html) has a free-word version satisfying Possibly Witnessed Possibility. One version satisfies Necessity of Second-Order Analysis. In another, worlds disagree on both arithmetic and CH. The proof combines the Stone representation of small algebras with the [free-word construction for the two arithmetics](free-words-over-disagreeing-arithmetics.html).

## Word and terminal coordinates

Before choosing the Stone space, take an alphabet with at least $\max(\aleph_0,|\Sigma|)$ letters and let $W$ be its free monoid. Words are the endomorphisms of one root object. Every terminal has a distinct entrance for each word $w$. Precomposing the entrance marked $w$ with the word $u$ gives the entrance marked $wu$.

Let $J=\omega\times\prod_jK^j_e$ as in the identity case and take $I=J^W$ for the individuals at the root. A root relation has an unrestricted tuple row for each word $w$. At each $w$ independently it has a continuous section $X\to F_\tau$ and one whole value at each additional terminal. Word transport by $u$ reads the coordinates ending in $u$, with its $w$-coordinate taken from $wu$. This shift also acts on individual arrays. An individual exits by evaluating its marked coordinate and projecting to the appropriate component. These actions preserve every term. At a root word the calculation is the evaluation of the term on the shifted parameters at the same object. At fixed $w$ all ordinary terminal parameters are constant on a common clopen neighbourhood, so the whole terminal denotation is locally constant, including for lambda abstraction. The additional terminals are Henkin models. All root words and all exits are onto at each type. For a word one can copy a prescribed whole entity into its suffix coordinates and fill the others arbitrarily.

To interpret the constants, enumerate the pairs consisting of a pure matrix and a matching tuple of distinct free constants. For every pair with an existential witness at some accessible point reserve a separate last letter $d$. These decisions use only the pure reduct, already constructed. If the witness is at the root, copy the entire tuple of witnessed entities into the suffix $wd$ of the corresponding constants, at every $w$. This includes individual-array entries and all component values. Then applying $d$ to the tuple of constants gives just the witness. If a witness is at an ordinary terminal, prescribe its tuple at the $d$-marked ordinary exit using a homogeneous terminal section there. If it is at an additional terminal, prescribe its tuple at that $d$-marked component. Prescribe the corresponding individual coordinates as well. Distinct last letters give disjoint prescriptions, even when the same constant occurs in many matrices. This establishes the possible-witness antecedent of Possibly Witnessed Possibility for all instances.

## Smallness and completeness

In the ordinal test require extension invariance on the root individual set at every word and constancy across ordinary terminals separately for each word. If $Y$ is rigid with current members $U$, its word row at $w$ is exactly $w[U]$. To see the reverse inclusion to persistence, give a tester the row $w[U]$ there and make it full everywhere else. It necessarily accepts every member of $U$. At the ordinary exits marked $w$, the terminal values of the members of $U$ are individually constant. Give a tester their set as its constant section for that word and make it full elsewhere. Persistence and inextensibility again give exactly that set of members at each of those exits. The limit of the rigid family therefore passes the test. The zero and successor calculations work at each word as in the identity version. With $B=2^{|\mathcal P(I^2)\times\mathcal P(I)|}$ the ordinal bound becomes $(B\,|F_\rho|\prod_j|K^j_\rho|)^{|W|}$. Neither $I$ nor $W$ depends on the Stone space. Choose the strong limit $\mu$ above this bound. The same power-coding argument puts each revised-small complete algebra in a principal interval of the factor $L$ at the empty word.

The proposition algebra is now the product over $w\in W$ of the former factors $2\times L\times\prod_j2$. It is complete. The single-word countable family of individual properties with distinct singleton values on isolated ordinary terminals still lacks a join, by finite range at that word. The word action is onto, so Barcan holds at the root and all its translates. Root fullness and terminal arithmetic give necessary individual infinity as before. Put the chosen root well-order in each word row and give every terminal its own well-order to obtain Modal Well-Ordering.

The atom supported at the identity word is a true strong atom. An atom supported at any word factor shifts to an atom or to bottom because $vu=w$ has at most one solution $v$ at fixed $u,w$. At each ordinary or additional terminal the same is true by the singleton computation for the identity construction. This gives necessary strongness of atoms. The Cantor regular-open factor at the empty word again gives a propositional self-atom with no atom below it. Any truth is ND-necessary using the identity-only proposition as guard. After a nonempty word or an exit this guard cannot return to truth. A relation with a prescribed current row and all nonidentity and exit values empty is an inextensible coextension. The argument persists at every word, and the terminal cases are classical.

## Rigid relations at word successors

Let $F$ be weakly rigid at the root, and $E$ its current row. At every word $u$ we again have $F_u=u[E]$ using the tester with this single restricted row. Given a tester $Z$ for weak inextensibility of $uF$, extend it back to the root by copying its entire profile into the suffix ending in $u$ and assigning top everywhere outside, including all terminal sections. Every member of $E$ necessarily satisfies that extension if every member of $u[E]$ necessarily satisfies $Z$. Weak inextensibility of $F$ now gives the conclusion for $uF$. Weak persistence of $uF$ follows from $F_u=u[E]$ and persistence of the members of $E$ at every subsequent arrow. Proper terminal points have only their identity. This proves necessary Tame Rigidity.

Fix a rigid relation $R$ at a word $u$ with current row $D$. At each input type choose a section $s$ of the $u$-shift by copying the desired entity into the suffix ending in $u$ and using fixed data elsewhere. Use the tuplewise section on $D$ for the current row $E'$ of a new relation $Y$. At each word $v$ prescribe its row to be $v[E']$. On terminal coordinates inside the chosen suffix copy the terminal values of $R$ literally. On every other terminal coordinate use the exact image of $E'$ there, which is a fixed singleton tuple or empty, constant across the ordinary terminals at any one word. Inside the suffix its word rows agree with the word rows of $R$ by the word-row equality, so $uY=R$ as whole entities.

Every current tuple of $Y$ persists. Outside the suffix its terminal image is exact by prescription. Inside it the transported tuple comes from a member of $D$ and $R$ is persistent. To see weak inextensibility, suppose a tester $Z$ necessarily accepts every current member of $Y$. Exact images give inclusion at all words and at the outside exits. Since $u[E']=D$, the tester $uZ$ necessarily accepts every member of $D$. Weak inextensibility of $R$ gives inclusion at all inside exits. Thus $Y$ is weakly rigid, hence rigid by Tame Rigidity already proved.

For a prescribed terminal relation at a word, lift its tuples by varying just the selected entrance coordinate in the argument entities. When it is an ordinary terminal, use the same value over its entire homogeneous word section. Take as the relation all the exact current-image rows, including the exact word images. Each terminal section is constant at each word, and the other terminal values are empty or singletons. This gives a rigid lift. It follows that the property of being rigid has exact images at every arrow, using the preceding word lift and these terminal lifts. Hence it is rigid, as is the property of being weakly rigid by Tame Rigidity. Both hold necessarily.

## Arithmetic and powers

At every root word full current tests give exactly the standard numerals. The full-standard version has the same arithmetic and second-order analytical theory at every point where the relevant structures exist. In the mixed version, the ordinary terminal and the nonstandard one disagree on the same arithmetical consistency sentence, and the two further components disagree on CH. The tester that misses a selected nonstandard number can be restricted to its additional entrance at the empty word. Both versions have a real number structure on propositions at the root and none at an ordinary terminal.

For the failure of both powers in the mixed version, use the constant individual arrays on $W$ with values $(n,b(n),c_+,c_-)$ from the [correlated-subrelations argument](stone-terminals-infinite-individuals.html). Give the bound at each word and exit its exact image. At the isolated ordinary and nonstandard exits marked by the empty word, the identical two co-singleton tests force incompatible images of a purported weakly rigid subrelation. The tester omitting that subrelation's desired terminal value at the isolated ordinary exit is admitted, as in the identity construction.
