# Interpretation arrows and the scope of Barcan

The Barcan Formula can hold at every type at an object where only some types satisfy it necessarily. The examples use two different components under the same construction of Cian Dorr.

Take one old action model. Extend the given signature to $\Sigma^+$ with infinitely many names of every type. The new root $r$ has its identity and, for every old object $V$ and every interpretation $I$ of the expanded names there, an arrow $h_{V,I}:r\to V$. An old arrow $k:V\to V'$ satisfies $kh_{V,I}=h_{V',kI}$. Only the identity enters $r$. Its individuals are the individual names and $h_{V,I}(c)=I(c)$. A root relation has an arbitrary current row. At each fixed old object its row depends only on the interpretation of finitely many names, with one pin for the whole relation. Its transport at each entrance must be an entity of the old object. A relational name $c$ denotes $\hat c$, with empty current row and entrance row the current extension of $I(c)$. Then $h_{V,I}\hat c=I(c)$ as a whole entity, by the composition rule, and $\{c\}$ pins it.

The rule for an abstract at $r$ takes its current row from evaluation on the root tuples. Its row at an entrance comes from evaluation in the old model on transported parameters. The union of the parameter pins controls those transports, and old term closure gives the landing condition. This is the original interpretation-root construction for one component. All domains and outgoing arrows at the old objects are those of the component. [Dorr's construction and its term verification](coalesced-pinned-interpretations-root.html)

At the new root use the dual Barcan Formula $\Diamond\exists x^\sigma\,Fx\to\exists x^\sigma\,\Diamond Fx$. A witness at the identity is already a current witness. Otherwise let $y$ witness the antecedent at $h_{V,I}$ and let $S$ pin $F$. Choose a fresh auxiliary name $c$ of type $\sigma$ outside $S$. Change its interpretation to $y$, giving $I'$. The row of $F$ at $h_{V,I'}$ still contains $y$. The root entity $\hat c$ (or the individual $c$ itself) is sent to $y$ there. This gives the required current possible witness at every type, including individual type. Each old object is reachable from $r$, so the necessary instances depend also on the old objects.

## A chain with a new individual

The old component has a single nonidentity arrow $f:u\to v$. The individual sets are $\{a\}$ and $\{b,c\}$ with $fa=b$, and all relation profiles are admitted. At every relational type any entity at $v$ lifts along $f$ by prescribing its row there and taking any identity row at $u$. Thus all relational Barcan instances hold at both old points. At $u$, the individual property $H_a$ necessarily covers its only current individual. Its row at $f$ omits $c$, so the type-$e$ instance of Barcan fails there. The interpretation root satisfies all of Barcan locally and necessarily satisfies Barcan at all relational types, while all-type Barcan fails to hold necessarily. ## Periodic integer profiles

The other component has a singleton individual at $u$ and the classical full hierarchy on $\mathbb Z\sqcup\{*\}$ at $v$. There are integer translations and an entrance projecting the terminal coordinate of every old source relation. Both rows of a source relation are periodic under translation. A source proposition is a pair of bits, and every terminal proposition is the image of such a pair. Translations preserve the scalar sort bijectively. Propositional Barcan holds at both old objects.

For failure at type $et$, use the old source tester whose current row contains every individual property and whose terminal row contains precisely the individual properties with finite translation orbit. The latter row is invariant under every translation, so the tester is admitted. All current properties project to periodic subsets at $v$, hence pass the tester necessarily. The terminal subset $\{0\}$ has infinite translation orbit and fails the tester at the entrance. The new root therefore satisfies all-type Barcan locally and propositional Barcan necessarily, while Barcan at all relational types fails to hold necessarily. [The periodic component](periodic-integer-entrance.html)

Both examples have a true strong atom at every point. In either new root the scalar supported only at its identity has this property, becoming bottom at every old entrance. At the initial point of the chain take the analogous identity-only scalar, and at the initial point of the periodic model take the pair with true current bit and false terminal bit. All its loops fix this pair, and the entrance sends it to bottom. In each terminal scalar algebra top is an atom.
