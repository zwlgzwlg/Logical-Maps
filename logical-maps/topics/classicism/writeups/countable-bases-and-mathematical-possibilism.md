# Countably based scalar observations obstruct Mathematical Possibilism

Suppose a point can see individual fields of every standard finite size with admitted well-orders. Assume its current higher types realize arbitrary subsets and graphs, with representatives for those subsets available uniformly. If each nonzero principal interval of its propositional entailment algebra has an ultrafilter with a countable base, Mathematical Possibilism fails there. Finite pinning on a countable set supplies such ultrafilters, even when the algebra itself is uncountable or has no atoms.

## The Boolean-algebra obstruction

A nontrivial complete atomless Boolean algebra has no ultrafilter with a countable base. Replace a proposed base by its consecutive finite meets $b_n$. If they have a smallest member, then this member is an atom, since either a given subpart or its complement belongs to the ultrafilter. Otherwise recursively pass to a strictly decreasing cofinal subsequence, each time using the first strict decrease. Write its members $c_n$. The positive differences $c_n\land\neg c_{n+1}$ are pairwise disjoint. By completeness, the even differences have a join $q$. Every $c_n$ contains a positive even difference and a positive odd difference. Each odd difference is disjoint from $q$, since its complement is an upper bound for all even differences. Neither $q$ nor its complement contains a base member. This contradicts ultrafilterhood. The proof uses no choice.

The closed ordinal thresholds $z_n$ for standard finite $n$ give an injection of $\omega$ into the inexact ordinal benchmark $H$. For each $n$ an accessible well-order with exactly $n$ individuals in its field distinguishes the corresponding consecutive thresholds. Their distinctness as whole entities already holds at the source; the witnessing orders need not be chosen together. Using the available current representatives, code subsets of the threshold list at one relational type. The regular-open algebra of Cantor space embeds as a set into these codes, by sending an open set to the binary basic cylinders it contains. Current rows and graphs present it as a complete atomless Boolean algebra. Its carrier is revised-small. Indeed, from any comparison $H<Y$ with $Y$ a subset of this algebra, the witnessing injection sends the list $z_n$ to an explicitly countably infinite $A\subseteq Y$. We have $A<Y$, since $A$ embeds in $H$ while $Y$ does not. A representation of its power in a carrier $C<Y$ would inject $\mathcal P(A)$ into $C$ by the uniqueness clause. But $Y$ embeds into $\mathcal P(\omega)$ and hence $\mathcal P(A)$. Composition contradicts $C<Y$. Thus $Y$ is not power-closed. [The choice-free smallness argument](choiceless-ordinal-obstruction-to-mathematical-possibilism.html)

An order isomorphism of this small algebra with a scalar principal interval would transfer its external completeness and atomlessness to the interval. Its countably based ultrafilter gives the contradiction. The same conclusion follows if every nonzero interval contains an atom.

## Finite supports and a countable pinning base

In a full finite-pin interpretation of the type domains on a countable pinning base, take any nonzero scalar $p$ and an arrow $f$ in its support. Evaluation at $f$ gives a point ultrafilter on the interval below $p$. Enumerate the source sites and let $B_n$ consist of the arrows with the target of $f$ and its values on the first $n$ sites. Include any fixed discrete word or tag when relevant. These are admitted scalars. For every $q\le p$ true at $f$, some initial segment contains its pin, whence $p\land B_n\le q$. This gives a countable base. In a target-equivariant interpretation where the orbit of each such finite restriction is an admitted scalar, take those orbit cylinders instead. Full current coding is a separate hypothesis in this case.

In the original one-object finite-support family with the naturals as individuals, the action monoid is on $\mathbb N$, the designated point has individuals of every finite field size, and a constant profile provides a specified current representative for every subset and graph. Therefore the argument applies to every monoid in the family and every allowed naming. It also applies to the two-object single-flip-sections model: the two pinning bases and the individual domains are countable, finite fields admit all orders, and constant rows for each target supply the representatives. In any model meeting these hypotheses it is enough that one accessible object supply the arbitrarily large finite fields. ## Atomic scalar intervals and invariant codes

At the empty front of the iterated ultrafilter model, the individual domain is infinite. Every prescribed current row has a representative taking the empty row at all nonempty addresses. Thus the full current coding argument applies, and the established Strong Atomicity puts an atom in every nonzero scalar interval. This refutes Mathematical Possibilism in both of its naming variants. [Iterated ultrafilter fronts](iterated-ultrafilter-fronts.html)

For the two sequence covers of a swapped terminal pair and of the periodic integer entrance, the source has unrestricted current rows and infinitely many individuals. Changing the terminal component at one of the independent sequence indices gives infinitely many distinct source individuals. Finite fields and orders are available there. The recorded scalar Atomicity makes every nonzero interval contain an atom, excluding the regular-open example. [Swapped terminal pair](sequence-covers-of-a-pair-and-a-fork.html) · [Periodic integer entrance](sequence-cover-of-periodic-integer-entrance.html)

In the injective integer stages, the scalar algebra is the powerset of the arrow kinds. Its source symmetry group acts trivially on the kinds, since it fixes the markers and permutes only ordinary sites. In the integer stages with decreasing subgroups, scalars depend only on the target stage and word, and in the pair shift with an ultrafilter character they depend only on the word. All three algebras are atomic. Each contains a copy of $\mathcal P(\omega)$ given by unions of mutually exclusive word events. All current scalars in each copy, and every closed ordinal threshold, are individually fixed by the coefficient symmetries. At the source, arbitrary sets and graphs on these fixed labels have representatives. Prescribe their row on the current word and target and give the other addresses empty rows; the designated group fixes every prescribed tuple, so this has the required support. In the shifted-word model every proper suffix restriction of such a profile is empty. Individual carriers are infinite and finite fields and orders are available by finite supports. Thus the word codes suffice to present the regular-open algebra and all of the graphs on the countable set $A$ in the argument above. In particular $A$ embeds in $H$ by the graph sending its $n$th member to $z_n$. This proves failure of Mathematical Possibilism in these models, including the top-named injective variant. ## Singleton individuals with infinitely many scalar atoms

If all accessible objects have one individual, the ordinal benchmark contains at most three thresholds: well-ordered fields, nonempty well-ordered fields, and the impossible threshold. Their finite membership list is a current inductive tester. A rigid family all of whose current members belong to this list must be the corresponding finite union of haecceities, by weak persistence and weak inextensibility. Its limit is again a listed threshold, and successors stay in the list. [Singleton-individual calculation](truncation-words.html)

With unrestricted current rows, in ZFC an infinite presented Boolean algebra contains a countably infinite subset $Y$. It exceeds this finite benchmark and is power-closed: each strictly smaller part of $Y$ is finite, and every finite powerset is represented on a finite subset of $Y$ by a current graph. Thus every revised-small Boolean algebra is finite. If the scalar algebra has arbitrarily many atoms, the join of $k$ distinct atoms gives the $2^k$-element Boolean algebra as its principal interval. The finite isomorphisms are admitted. This proves Mathematical Possibilism.

In the singleton-individual variants of finite-support truncations, dyadic roundings, and truncated shifts, the scalar algebra is infinite and atomic. The full finite-pin domains have unrestricted current rows. In the ultralimit fork and in the sequence cover of both levels of the merged fork all individuals throughout are singletons, current source rows are unrestricted, and each of the infinitely many independently specified terminal addresses supports its own scalar atom. A truth appearing at just one such address satisfies the ultralimit or eventual-agreement requirement, respectively. These models therefore satisfy Mathematical Possibilism with the specified singleton settings. An independent-numeral expansion preserves every pure instance and hence this verdict, or the corresponding negative verdict, whenever it uses one of the identified source settings.
