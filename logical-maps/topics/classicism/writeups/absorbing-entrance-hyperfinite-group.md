# Four-proposition entrance into a hyperfinite group action

Potential Infinity at type $t$ can hold while Possible Infinity at type $t$ fails, even with necessary Boolean Completeness at type $t$. At the evaluation object every internal number is a standard numeral. At its only other reachable object, each of those numerals counts a property of propositions, and one additional, nonstandard finite cardinality counts all the propositions there.

The notes to [Potential Infinity at type $t$](https://github.com/zwlgzwlg/Logical-Maps/blob/4bb9287187ed7cb09567eb3a6302cb896c986ec4/logical-maps/topics/classicism/principles/potential-infinity-t.yaml#L87) proposed a four-proposition entrance into a hyperfinite world. OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, supplied the following cyclic-group construction and verifications on 26 November 2026. Two related constructions are the [full entrance with an ordinary infinite future](full-two-object-chain-free-loop.html) and the [Henkin ultraproduct of finite full S5 models](ultraproduct-finite-full-s5.html).

## The future object

Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter $U$ on the positive integers. For each $j\ge 1$ let $M_j$ be the one-object full action model on the cyclic group $G_j=\mathbb Z/j\mathbb Z$, with one individual. At a relational type $\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k t$ the full carrier consists of all subsets of

$$G_j\times D_{\sigma_1,j}\times\cdots\times D_{\sigma_k,j}.$$

The first coordinate is the arrow at which the tuple in the remaining coordinates belongs to the relation. In particular, the proposition carrier is $\mathcal P(G_j)$ and has $2^j$ elements. Give individual constants their unique value and relational constants their top value.

Present the factors as many-sorted first-order structures. Include the group sort with multiplication and identity, every type carrier with its group action, and the tagged incidence relation at each relational type. Take their ultraproduct over $U$. The group $G=\prod_jG_j/U$ acts at a single object $s$. Its relation of type $\tau$ represented by $(R_j)$ consists of the tagged tuples whose incidence holds on a $U$-large set. This realizes each ultraproduct relation as an actual set of tuples. Two distinct ultraproduct relations have different incidence at some tuple, by Łoś applied to extensionality. The action laws transfer too.

For each fixed formula, evaluation with an assignment from the type carriers transfers from the factors. One can see this by induction: application uses tagged incidence and transport, quantification at an object ranges over the indicated type sort, and the modal clause ranges over further arrows by multiplication in the group. For a lambda term the class of the factor denotations has exactly the required incidence, by that same induction for its body. Thus the admitted relations are closed under all parameterized terms. The action semantics of C applies at $s$. The use of a nonprincipal ultrafilter is an assumption of the construction in the metatheory. Once $U$ has been chosen, factor witnesses in these transfers may be selected in canonical finite enumerations of the group and type carriers.

## The entrance and its domains

Adjoin an object $r$. Its outgoing arrows are $1_r$ and $h:r\to s$, with $g\circ h=h$ for every $g\in G$. No arrow returns to $r$. Give $r$ one individual. Recursively on type, the root entities at $\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k t$ are pairs $(A,B)$, where

* $A$ is any subset of $D_{\sigma_1}(r)\times\cdots\times D_{\sigma_k}(r)$, and
* $B\in D_\tau(s)$ satisfies $g\cdot B=B$ for every $g\in G$.

The identity extension is $A$ and the extension at $h$ is the identity extension of $B$. This determines the transport $h\cdot(A,B)=B$ as a whole entity. Indeed, the extension of $g\cdot B$ at an arrow $k$ is the extension of $B$ at $kg$. Thus a fixed $B$ has the same extension at every group arrow, and is determined by its identity extension. The condition $g\cdot B=B$ is exactly what $gh=h$ requires. The two choices at each of $1_r$ and $h$ give four root propositions. The top interpretations of the relational constants at the two objects agree under transport.

For term closure at $r$, consider a parameterized relation term. Its identity row has no restriction. At $s$ evaluate the same term using the $h$-images of its free parameters. The result is admitted by term closure at $s$. It is fixed by every group element because evaluation commutes with transport and those free parameters are fixed. In particular, for $\lambda\bar x.\varphi$, the row at $h$ uses every tuple of arguments at $s$. Under further transport the usual reindexing law for the term gives the same tail entity. The fixedness condition applies to the free parameters irrespective of the values assigned to the bound arguments. This covers quantification over entities new at $s$. Pair the root identity row with this fixed tail entity. The pair belongs to the root carrier and gives precisely the term's denotation. This verifies the action-model closure criterion of Bacon and Dorr, *Classicism*, Appendix D, Proposition D.4. The interpretation of C at $s$ is preserved because all arrows out of $s$ stay there.

## Counts at the two objects

Let $\bar k=\operatorname{Suc}_t^k\mathbf0_t$ for a standard nonnegative integer $k$. By induction on $k$, the numeral $\bar k$ holds of a property exactly when its extension at the identity has $k$ members. The successor removes one member by actual identity and applies the preceding numeral to the resulting property. Transport sends $\bar k$ to the same closed numeral at its target.

At $r$ choose an admitted property $W$ of cardinalities. Give it identity extension $\{\bar k^r:k\in\mathbb N\}$ and the empty tail value. At the identity of $r$, $W$ contains $\mathbf0_t$ and is closed under $\operatorname{Suc}_t$. The impredicative definition of $\operatorname{FiniteCardinality}_t$ therefore places each root number in $W$. Conversely the standard numeral denotations are numbers by induction. Thus a root number $n$ equals $\bar k^r$ for some standard $k$, as a whole entity. At $s$ the statement $\exists X\, .\,\bar kX$ is true for each fixed $k$, by transfer from the factors with $2^j\ge k$. The arrow $h$ consequently witnesses $\Diamond\exists X\, .\,nX$. This proves Potential Infinity at $r$.

At $r$, $\bar4$ counts the universal property of propositions. In factor $j$, the numeral for $2^j$ counts that factor's universal property. Łoś applied to the single sentence

$$\exists n\, .\,\operatorname{FiniteCardinality}_t(n)\land n(\lambda p.\top)$$

makes it true at $s$. In particular the tail number counting the whole domain is the class of the factor numerals for $2^j$. It differs from each standard numeral since $2^j$ eventually exceeds every fixed $k$. The displayed sentence thus holds at both targets of the root arrows. Its negation is impossible at $r$, so Possible Infinity fails there.

Two further verdicts locate the separation. First, in factor $j$ the numeral for $2^j+1$ is a finite cardinality, and no property at the unique object has that count. Consequently $\neg\operatorname{PotInf}_t$ holds in every factor and transfers to $s$. Necessary Potential Infinity therefore fails at $r$. Second, entailment in the root proposition algebra is inclusion of subsets of $\{1_r,h\}$. The intersection of the current extensions of the propositions picked out by any property is an admitted root proposition and is their greatest lower bound, including the empty intersection. At $s$ the same type-$t$ GLB sentence transfers from the finite powersets. Thus necessary Boolean Completeness at type $t$ holds at $r$. The [positive implication using Countable Boolean Completeness](countable-boolean-completeness-and-potential-infinity-t-imply-possible-infinity-t.html) invokes completeness at a higher type to control the numbers that can enter the domain.

## Arithmetic and analysis

At each fixed type every factor of the group ultraproduct has a finite carrier. An injective successor on an inhabited set omitting zero is impossible there, both for a proposed Peano structure and for the Frege-number property. Thus at the group object $s$ the Peano condition $I^*_\sigma$ fails for each type, and no real structure exists at any type, by Łoś's theorem. All arrows from $s$ have target $s$.

The new root does have a real structure at type $tt$. To see how many labels it has, take any external $J\subseteq\mathbb N$. In the factor on $G_j$ take the property of propositions whose row at every arrow consists of the subsets $q\subseteq G_j$ with $|q|\in J$. A profile constant in the arrow is fixed under reindexing by the whole group. Distinct $J,J'$ disagree at some $k$ and give distinct rows for every $j\ge k$. Their ultraproducts therefore supply continuum many fixed $tt$-entities, each of which can be the old coordinate of a root entity. Arbitrary root rows on these labels represent the standard numbers, the real codes for all their subsets, successor, and membership. No such structure at $tt$ can remain a real structure on entering $s$. This disproves Analytic Necessitism already at one type.

Conversely, every real structure at the root has the standard natural numbers and their full power set. The external finite successor iterates form an admitted current induction predicate. All subsets of the number carrier, and all higher relations on its numbers and reals, are admitted by the same unrestricted-row construction. Thus these structures all give the same full second-order analysis. There are no other accessible real structures. This proves Necessity of Second-Order Analysis nonvacuously. Arithmetic Necessitism has a false boxed Peano premise at each type. Necessity of Arithmetic also holds, since the root is the only possible point satisfying the Peano guard at any given type, and its arithmetic there is standard.

## Atoms, orders and modal guards

The algebra of relations at the root is the product of its unrestricted current-row algebra and the algebra of group-fixed old entities. Fixedness of an old entity is a first-order condition in the action language and means that its tables are constant in the arrow. Every nonzero fixed table contains a fixed singleton-row table, by transfer from the factors. In the unrestricted old algebra, however, a nonzero fixed table can be split by restricting its occupied row to one group arrow and to the remaining arrows. The factors have at least two arrows eventually. Every atom at the old object is strong, since in the factors the singleton of an arrow and its argument tuple stays a singleton under group translation. These arguments prove necessary Atomicity at every relational type.

If a root relation has a nonzero old coordinate then it is instantiated at the old identity, where that coordinate is not an atom. Consequently every root self-atom has old value bottom and a singleton current row. These are strong. This proves that self-atoms are necessarily strong everywhere. The scalar atom with empty current bit and true old trace witnesses the failure of both Self-Atomicity at $t$ and Atoms Are Self-Atoms at $t$. A true strong atom at the root is the identity-only proposition. At $s$ the old identity singleton is a true strong atom as well.

For a modal well-order at each type, take an external well-order as the current root table. At the exit use a constant-in-arrow table, obtained by choosing a linear order on the finite carrier in each factor. This gives an admitted old internal well-order, fixed as an entity, which remains a well-order at every group arrow. The root identity-only proposition also witnesses distinctness necessity of every current truth: it becomes impossible upon taking the entrance. At $s$ ordinary necessity is itself an S5 operator, since the arrows form a group. A true singleton of a group arrow is contingent there. Hence it is not 5-necessary, refuting the necessary version of Truths Are 5-Necessary at the root.

## Rigid subrelations and their powers

Let $F$ be weakly rigid at the root, with current row $A$. Its old table has row exactly $h[A]$. Weak persistence gives one inclusion. To exclude an old tuple $b\notin h[A]$, use a tester with full root row and a constant old table deleting just $b$. Each old tuple has an admitted internal singleton table. The deletion is constant *in the arrow*, so it is an eligible exit coordinate even when $b$ itself moves. The tester holds necessarily of every current $F$-member. Weak inextensibility therefore excludes $b$. All members of $h[A]$ are individually fixed under the old group.

In each finite factor a weakly rigid relation has at every arrow the direct image of its current row. Persistence gives the forward inclusion, and testing against a profile with one tuple deleted at one arrow gives the converse. Conversely such a relation is rigid, since its images compose. This characterization is first order with the action and the rows named, hence transfers to $s$. The old table of $F$ above has these exact rows, so $F$ is rigid. Tame Rigidity holds at both objects.

At $s$, every weakly rigid subrelation of the transported $F$ has an admitted current row $V\subseteq h[A]$. Its elements are pointwise fixed, so exact images make this subrelation the constant table $V$. Lift it to the root relation with current row $A\cap h^{-1}[V]$ and old coordinate that same table. This has exact images and is rigid. Thus at the exit every eligible subrelation is the image of a current rigid subrelation. Conversely, current rigid subrelations remain rigid and below the bound after transport. The power predicate consequently has exact rows and is rigid at the root. At the old object each group transport has an inverse, which transports back every eligible subrelation and preserves its eligibility. This proves both necessary power principles.

Any current source row paired with old value bottom is inextensible at the source and remains so at the exit. At the old object the rigid direct-image extensions from the finite full factors give Inextensible Comprehension. Nevertheless neither predicate of being rigid or weakly rigid is weakly inextensible at the root. Take a nonfixed old proposition $q$, for instance the identity singleton. Its haecceity is rigid but is itself nonfixed, since a group element moving $q$ moves its haecceity. A tester with full current row and constant old table deleting that one haecceity accepts every transported root entity (all are fixed), yet rejects this old rigid entity.
