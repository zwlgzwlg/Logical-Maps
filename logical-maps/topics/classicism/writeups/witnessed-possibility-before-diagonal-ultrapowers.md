# Possibly Witnessed Possibility before diagonal ultrapowers

A model with one propositional name satisfies Possibly Witnessed Possibility, Boolean Completeness at every relational type, necessary Strong Atomicity at every relational type and necessary individual Infinity. It fails Arithmetic Necessitism and even propositional Potential Infinity. Its construction combines the [three-object diagonal ultrapower](three-object-diagonal-ultrapower.html) with the [finite-tree compactness argument for named witnesses](witnessed-possibility-implies-infinity-schema-t.html). There is also a direct free-word version in which the root propositions are infinite.

## Common terminal construction

Let \(H\) be the full classical typed hierarchy over the naturals and \(\delta:H\to{}^*H\) the elementary diagonal embedding into an ultrapower on the positive integers by a free ultrafilter. The old chain has a middle point \(s\) with individuals the naturals and one arrow to the classical terminal \(t\) with hierarchy \({}^*H\). Its relational carriers are the pairs consisting of an arbitrary current tuple row and a whole terminal entity. Individuals go to their diagonal copies. In particular middle and terminal propositions have respectively four and two possible values.

Start with a finite full binary tree of any height. Attach an independent copy of this two-point chain at each vertex. From a tree vertex \(v\) an entity at a relational type \(\tau\) specifies three rows for each descendant \(u\), including \(v\) itself. These are an arbitrary row of tuples at \(u\), an arbitrary row of tuples at the middle point attached to \(u\), and a member of \(\delta H_\tau\) at that terminal. The terminal entry is tested on all ultrapower arguments. Tree and middle individual carriers are the naturals and all transports between them are identity. A transport keeps the descendant rows visible from its target. At the middle point it retains both rows of that chain, and the terminal projection reads the whole last entry.

Abstraction at a tree vertex computes the current satisfaction sets at all its descendant tree and middle points. On each terminal the parameters are diagonal, so elementarity gives a diagonal value for the entire abstract. The middle-point abstraction uses the same calculation with arbitrary terminal parameters and has an internal terminal value. Entire profiles are separated by the stipulated tuple tests. Modality quantifies over descendants and attached components. This establishes the typed action interpretation, including applications involving diagonal parameters and new terminal arguments.

## Common verdicts

At a tree vertex entailment is componentwise inclusion. Each terminal diagonal factor, ordered on *all* terminal tuples, is isomorphic to the full powerset order of the corresponding ground tuples. Thus the product is externally complete, giving local Boolean Completeness at all relational types. All three kinds of points have complete scalar algebras.

Every nonzero row contains a singleton at a single position. In a nonempty diagonal terminal entry \(\delta B\), choose \(a\in B\) and use \(\delta\{a\}\), still a singleton on the whole ultrapower domain. At a middle point use internal terminal singletons even for nondiagonal tuples. Each resulting atom transports to bottom or to a single-position singleton. Consequently Strong Atomicity holds necessarily. The current-position-only proposition is a true strong atom. It becomes impossible at every proper successor, which proves necessary Truths are ND-necessary. All proposition transports are onto. In particular the terminal diagonal restriction admits both bits, so propositional Barcan holds necessarily.

Choose a well-order at each tree and middle row, and use the diagonal of a ground well-order at each terminal. The latter well-orders the internal subsets by transfer. This gives Modal Well-Ordering at every type. The individual Frege numbers at every tree and middle point are the standard numeral iterates since their set is an admitted inductive current row. No finite numeral counts the naturals, and individual Infinity holds at the terminal by transfer. Thus individual Infinity is necessary.

At a middle point the tester with full present row and terminal row represented at index \(i\) by the first \(i+1\) individual-type numerals contains every old numeral necessarily. It misses the new terminal number represented by the sequence of \((i+1)\)-st numerals. The Frege numbers fail weak inextensibility there despite the necessary Peano guard. Hence Arithmetic Necessitism fails at the root. Also Barcan fails there at individual-property input: at a chosen middle entrance a tester admits precisely the properties with diagonal terminal coordinate, using top elsewhere and at all terminals. Every original individual property necessarily passes, while the middle property with nondiagonal singleton terminal coordinate fails.

## Rigid Power and the finite terminal projections

Let \(F\) be rigid at a tree vertex, with current extension \(E\). At every descendant tree or middle position its extension is exactly the transported image of \(E\). Persistence gives containment of the image. For the other inclusion use a tester that omits one proposed extra tuple at the chosen position and is full everywhere else. At the terminal attached to that middle position apply the same argument to the *transported* rigid relation at the middle point. Its testers can omit any internal tuple there, including a nondiagonal tuple. It follows by composition that the entire terminal row \(\delta B\) is precisely the image from the original tree vertex. All tuples in this image are diagonal. Consequently \(B\) is externally finite. Indeed, from an infinite \(B\) choose a sequence of distinct members. Its ultrapower class belongs to \(\delta B\) and differs from every diagonal tuple.

For each external subset \(D\subseteq E\), give \(H_D\) the exact images of \(D\) at all positions. Each terminal image is a finite set of diagonal tuples and therefore a permitted whole diagonal relation. The other rows are unrestricted. An exact-image profile is rigid by composition and the two testing clauses. Now consider any rigid subrelation \(Z\le hF\) at a future point and take \(D=E\cap h^{-1}[Z_{\rm current}]\). Then \(hH_D\) and \(Z\) have the same extension and are rigid, hence identical. This supplies all members of the power by transport and all transported members remain eligible. The power thus has exact rows throughout the cone and is rigid. At a middle point the same lifting proof uses the current preimage and the chosen internal terminal value of \(Z\), as in the single-entrance construction. At the terminal use extensionality. This establishes necessary Rigid Power. The proof works for zero arguments and for mixed typed tuples.

In contrast, the top individual predicate is weakly rigid at the root. All nonterminal individuals are transported from the root. A diagonal terminal tester containing every one of their diagonal images has full ground extension and is top at that terminal. At the middle point one internal tester can omit a nondiagonal individual at the terminal and be full elsewhere. The transported top predicate therefore fails weak inextensibility. This refutes Tame Rigidity. At type \(et\) a root real structure exists by full current rows and the continuum of individual properties. A necessarily real rigid structure there would have a rigid number predicate with finite extension at every terminal. A finite number domain cannot support an injective successor omitting zero. Thus Analytic Necessitism fails.

Inextensible Comprehension holds necessarily: at every nonterminal point use the given current row with empty proper future rows, and at a terminal point use the relation itself. In the externally full ground setting each root relation with extension \(E\) also has a weakly rigid coextension. Use its exact images at all nonterminal positions. At each terminal, if its image is \(\delta[U]\), use the whole relation \(\delta U\). Fullness of \(H\) makes this admissible. Every diagonal tester containing \(\delta[U]\) contains \(\delta U\); thus the new profile is weakly inextensible as well as weakly persistent. All actual real structures at a nonterminal point give full ordinary arithmetic and analysis by current fullness: the external standard numeral orbit is inductive and every subset or graph on its codes is represented. At the terminal, uniform assertions about all real structures transfer from the classical full hierarchy. This gives Necessity of Second-Order Analysis in the externally full setting.

## The finite-tree model

In each finite factor the number of available propositions is uniformly bounded over that whole factor. Any larger standard numeral counts nothing at any accessible point. Consequently every point fails propositional Potential Infinity.

For any finite collection of Possibly Witnessed Possibility instances with the one name \(c^t\), consider the pure matrices realized with some scalar in any finite-tree factor at any point. For each such matrix choose a realization. A tree realization comes with its remaining height. Choose one taller full binary tree with disjoint subtrees of those respective heights and disjoint spare attachments for the middle or terminal realizations. This is possible by selecting disjoint ancestor subtrees at a common depth and descending within each to the required height. On a chosen tree copy assign the complete witnessing scalar profile to the restriction of \(c\). On a chosen attachment assign the witnessing middle pair or terminal bit. These finitely many prescriptions have disjoint positions. Set all other bits false. The subtrees have identical futures to their chosen prototypes, so truth of each pure matrix there is unchanged. Every matrix with possible existential antecedent in this factor is among those realized somewhere in the class. The empty-tuple instances hold directly.

Compactness for the many-sorted Henkin interpretation gives one pointed C model with all these instances and all common verdicts above. The boxed failure of propositional Potential Infinity transfers as a single sentence despite the varying finite bounds. Possibly Witnessed Possibility entails Separated Structure by the [map's equivalence](../index.html#principle/separated-structure-r).

## The free-word model

Alternatively give a single new root the free monoid on countably many letters. Its exits to the middle and terminal are distinguished by the preceding word. Profiles now have one triple for every word. Root-word transport is right suffix restriction on all three tables. The same term, order, atom and arithmetic proofs apply. Word concatenation is cancellative, so restricting a single-position atom yields at most one occupied position. Scalar suffix restriction is onto by copying a requested profile below that suffix. There are infinitely many root scalar values and the root satisfies propositional Infinity.

List the pure scalar matrices with a witness in this fixed model. For each realized at the root, prescribe its entire witnessing triple array below a separate last letter. For each chosen middle or terminal witness, use a different fresh letter and prescribe its middle pair or terminal bit on that letter's exit. Thus one name realizes all possibly witnessed matrices directly. The middle and terminal scalar values used here are all available under the diagonal restriction.

## An arithmetically disagreeing ground hierarchy

In either construction replace \(H\) by the internal classical hierarchy on \(\omega^M\) in a countable model \(M\) of \(T+\neg\mathrm{Con}(T)\), where \(T\) is Zermelo set theory with Choice plus a specified sign of CH. The consistency construction is the one in the [single nonstandard arithmetic terminal](one-nonstandard-arithmetic-terminal.html). Form the **external** ultrapower of this countable many-sorted hierarchy. The tree and middle individuals are now the externally countable set \(\omega^M\); their current rows remain unrestricted. All finite sets of ground tuples belong to \(H\), so the finite terminal projection proof still gives necessary Rigid Power. All the scalar gluing, necessary Strong Atomicity, propositional Barcan and Modal Well-Ordering calculations still apply.

Arithmetic at each tree or middle point is standard by the current inductive cut, and the ground hierarchy computes its individual arithmetic as that of \(M\). At the terminal the latter calculation transfers by elementarity. Both sides satisfy the Peano guard and disagree on \(\mathrm{Con}(T)\). At type \(et\) both have real structures, so the same sentence refutes Necessity of Analysis. The structural CH sign at the terminal is that of \(M\), and at the other points it is the ambient sign. Taking the signs alike or opposite gives both versions.

At a fixed terminal position put together the root individual predicates supported there on the diagonal singletons of the *standard* naturals. Their countable family and its injection into the root's individual-type numerals are available by full root rows. A join with that terminal coordinate \(\delta B\) must include those singletons and no other ground singleton, since any one unwanted singleton can be removed from top at that position. This would make \(B\) the external standard cut in \(\omega^M\), contrary to induction in \(M\). The same singleton-deletion testers show that a weakly rigid coextension of the root individual row consisting of the external standard cut would require this very \(B\). Thus local Countable Boolean Completeness at every type and local Weak Rigid Comprehension fail in this setting. The earlier elementary and compactness arguments apply to each of the stated native sentences.

## Why changing names in the original models is insufficient

In the original three-point chain, the pure sentence asserting the existence of eight distinct propositions holds only at the root, where there is just one root address. In the [classical one-terminal entrance](one-nonstandard-arithmetic-terminal.html) use four in place of eight. In its group variant take the pure sentence \(Q=\exists p(p\land\Diamond\Box\neg p)\). The root-only scalar witnesses \(Q\), while an invertible return in the old group makes \(Q\) impossible there. In each case call the root-isolating sentence \(Q\). For any relational name of type \(\rho\), the matrices \(Q\land X=\top_\rho\) and \(Q\land X=\bot_\rho\) both have actual witnesses. Witnessed Possibility would require the same name to equal both distinct values at the unique root address. Thus all the original frames refute even Witnessed Possibility for every signature allowed by the map. This also covers both CH choices of the one-terminal construction.
