# An actual S5 point with Rigid Comprehension and no necessary Transversal

At one point of a constant-domain S5 model, Actuality, Rigid Comprehension, Boolean Completeness at all relational types and revised Mathematical Possibilism hold. At another point of the same component there is no transversal of coextension classes. Already at the designated point, Transversal Choice, Relational Choice and Intensional Choice fail. The construction extends the map's [supported diagonal-tail countermodel](supported-diagonal-tail-permutations.html). Work in ZFC.

## Worlds and the support condition

Put $C=2^{\mathbb N}$ with its Cantor topology. Let $\Gamma$ be the countable group of permutations of finite binary prefixes, each extended identically on later digits. Let $K_n$ apply a single such permutation to the tail after the first $n$ digits, identically in every length-$n$ cylinder. The groups $K_n$ decrease with $n$. Any $g\in\Gamma$ affecting only the first $r$ digits commutes with $K_l$ for $l\ge r$. On each length-$n$ cylinder every $K_n$-orbit is dense.

Begin the space of worlds $W$ with $C$ and countably many distinct isolated $\Gamma$-fixed worlds $w_1,w_2,\ldots$ and one discrete regular $\Gamma$-orbit, as topologically disjoint components. Designate $\star=w_1$. Further components are specified below. All worlds, including $\star$, are mutually accessible in a single S5 cluster. The one individual and all typed entities transport identically. At each relational type admit exactly the profiles whose fibre over every argument tuple is clopen in $W$ and whose whole profile is invariant under some diagonal $K_n$, acting both on worlds and inputs. Such profiles form $\Gamma$-sets since conjugating a support group by any $g\in\Gamma$ still contains a sufficiently deep $K_l$. In particular every entity is fixed by some $K_n$.

At $\star$ an external tuple set is an admitted current extension exactly when it is invariant under some $K_n$. One direction uses that every element of $\Gamma$ fixes $\star$; for the other, take the same row at every world. In this constant-domain model a profile is rigid exactly when its extension is constant across worlds. A rigid profile is persistent in the entire S5 cluster, so has this property. Conversely an everywhere constant row is persistent, and any tester necessarily accepting all its members accepts every member at every world, which proves inextensibility. In fact a weakly rigid profile $F$ is rigid: weak persistence at the current world implies that each current member satisfies $\Box F$, everywhere. Weak inextensibility, applied to the tester $\lambda x.\Box F(x)$, bounds $F$ by that persistent core. Thus weak rigidity and rigidity coincide.

## Adding Boolean intervals

Use all countable Boolean algebras $D$ carrying an action of some $K_n$ by automorphisms with the following properties. Every element is fixed by some $K_m$ with $m\ge n$. Each fixed subalgebra $D^{K_m}$ is finite. For every $b\in D$ the orbit $K_m b$ has a supremum $c_{m,b}$ in $D$. Finite nontrivial Boolean algebras are allowed. Representatives of the algebras with their actions form a set, since their carriers and the acting groups are countable.

In the compact metrizable Stone space of $D$ write $[b]$ for the clopen attached to $b$. The open set
$$
 O_{m,b}=[\neg c_{m,b}]\ \cup\ \bigcup_{h\in K_m}[hb]
$$
is dense. Any nonempty clopen below $[c_{m,b}]$ disjoint from all the $[hb]$ would provide a strictly smaller upper bound. Intersect all these open sets for all $m\ge n,b\in D$ and all their translates by $K_n$. This is a countable intersection of dense open sets. The Baire theorem gives a dense $K_n$-invariant subspace $Y_D$.

For an atom $d$ of the finite algebra $D^{K_m}$ and $0<b\le d$, the supremum of $K_m b$ is nonzero, invariant and below $d$, hence equals $d$. Thus the $K_m$-orbit of each point of $Y_D\cap[d]$ meets $[b]$. Every such orbit is dense in that cell. A $K_m$-invariant open subset of $Y_D$ is a union of entire cells: otherwise its invariant closed complement in a cell contains a dense orbit and meets the open part. These finite unions are clopen and are traces of elements of $D$.

Add to $W$ the induced $\Gamma$-space $\Gamma\times_{K_n}Y_D$ for each representative. This is a topological disjoint union of copies of $Y_D$ indexed by cosets $gK_n$, with relation $(gh,y)\sim(g,hy)$ for $h\in K_n$. If a $K_m$-invariant open subset of $W$ is restricted to such a copy, choose $l\ge m,n$ above the prefix length of $g$. The subgroup $K_l$ preserves that copy, and acts inside it as $K_l$ because it commutes with $g$. The restriction is clopen by the preceding orbit argument. The restrictions on $C$ and on the isolated points and discrete regular orbit are clopen as well. Hence every $K_m$-invariant open subset of the entire sum is clopen.

This verifies term closure. The truth set of an application or Boolean combination is clopen whenever its fibres are. An existential forms the union of the clopens for the possible witnesses, an open set invariant under a common $K_m$ fixing all parameters; it is therefore clopen. The same calculation applies fibre by fibre for a lambda, with the bound arguments included among the fixed parameters, and equivariance gives the support of the whole lambda from its free parameters. Identity comparisons are rigid equality of the entire profiles. This gives a C model, with necessary distinctness and Barcan at every sort by the identity transfers.

## Actuality, comprehension and completeness at the fixed point

The singleton $\{\star\}$ is a true strong atom. The current extension of any relation $X$ fixed by $K_n$ is $K_n$-invariant there; its constant-row copy is an admitted rigid coextension and has persistent complement. This proves local Rigid Comprehension and Gallin Extensional Comprehension. The property of being constant-row is $\Gamma$-invariant, and at $\star$ it picks exactly one relation per coextension class, giving local Transversal.

A current family $S$ of relational entities is likewise invariant under some $K_n$. The pointwise union of its profiles is $K_n$-invariant. At a tuple $x$ its fibre is open, and is invariant under $K_l$ for $l\ge n$ fixing $x$. That fibre is clopen by the preceding argument. The union is therefore admitted and is the least upper bound. This proves local Boolean Completeness at every relational type.

The rigidity predicate itself is constant-row and rigid everywhere. If $F$ is a rigid bound, the rigid profiles contained in it form a constant class, preserved by a support group of $F$. The class is an admitted constant-row predicate, hence rigid. The same holds for weakly rigid bounds because they are rigid in this cluster. Thus Rigid Rigidity, Tame Rigidity and both rigid powers hold necessarily.

## Verifying revised Mathematical Possibilism

Take any revised-small internally complete Boolean algebra at $\star$, with carrier $B$ and order both preserved by $K_n$. Its ordinary finite operations are determined by its order, so $K_n$ acts by Boolean automorphisms. If $B^{K_m}$ were infinite for some $m\ge n$, choose a countably infinite $Y$ among its fixed elements. Every external subset and every graph on such tuples is locally admitted, since $K_m$ fixes the elements pointwise. The singleton individual bounds the ordinal benchmark by the three closed well-order thresholds, as in the [one-individual ordinal argument](https://github.com/zwlgzwlg/Logical-Maps/blob/4bb9287187ed7cb09567eb3a6302cb896c986ec4/logical-maps/topics/classicism/arguments/forcing-possibilism-countably-many-arrows.yaml). This makes $H<Y$ internally as well. Every strictly smaller subset of $Y$ is finite, since an external bijection of any infinite subset with $Y$ is an admitted graph, and all finite powers can be coded by finite subsets of $Y$. Thus $Y$ is power-closed, contrary to smallness. Every $B^{K_m}$ is finite. Every element is smooth, so $B$ is countable. Its orbit $K_m b$ is an admitted current subset by invariance, hence has its ordinary supremum in $B$ by the assumed internal completeness.

The universal list therefore includes this action on $B$. On its distinguished coset copy $Y_B$ let $p$ be the indicator of that entire copy, a clopen $K_n$-supported proposition. The admitted propositions below $p$ are precisely the Stone traces of elements of $B$ on $Y_B$. Each Stone trace is clopen and invariant under a sufficiently deep $K_l$. Conversely any proposition below $p$ is clopen and fixed by some $K_m$ with $m\ge n$, hence is a finite union of the fixed cells of $B^{K_m}$. Density of $Y_B$ makes the representation injective. The Stone map on $B$ has a $K_n$-invariant graph, which is admitted at $\star$ by the constant-row construction. This is the required order isomorphism; the trivial algebra uses the interval below bottom. The argument establishes the local principle at $\star$.

## Failure of necessary Transversal

Take $w=0^\omega$ in the old Cantor component. Suppose a profile $\Phi$ supported by $K_n$ picks one representative per coextension class of properties of propositions there. Let $g$ flip digit $n+1$, set $v=g(w)$, and use the admitted property $F_g(p)=g^{-1}[p]$ on the enlarged proposition domain. It has clopen fibres, and $g$ commutes with all sufficiently deep $K_l$. The chosen representative $Y$ of this property satisfies $w\in Y(p)$ exactly when $v\in p$.

The remaining contradiction is the [diagonal-tail permutation argument](supported-diagonal-tail-permutations.html). Every $h\in K_n$ fixing $w,v$ fixes $Y$ by uniqueness. Choose $m\ge n+1$ supporting $Y$ and let $k\in K_m$ flip digit $m+1$. A finite permutation of tail prefixes in $K_n$ can fix $w,v,k(w)$ and move $k(v)$: these are four distinct points in the same $n$-cylinder, so use sufficiently long distinct tail prefixes and swap the last such prefix with an unused one. Call that permutation $l$. Both $l$ and $k$ fix $Y$. Thus $k^{-1}lk$ fixes $w,Y$ and moves $v$, contradicting the displayed characterization since the Cantor clopens still distinguish the two images of $v$. This refutes necessary Transversal while local Transversal holds at $\star$.

## Choice failures and mathematical structures

At $\star$ use an equivalence relation on propositions pairing each $p\subseteq C$ with $C\setminus p$, and give each other proposition its singleton class. Its constant profile is globally invariant. Any transversal has current row invariant under $K_n$ for some $n$. At digit $n+1$ the two complementary halves of $C$ are exchanged by a member of $K_n$, so precisely one of them cannot be selected. This refutes Transversal Choice. Since local Transversal holds, the native equivalence of Transversal Choice with Relational Choice and Transversal also gives failure of Relational Choice. Ordinary Atomicity at $t$ fails: the nonempty Cantor component contains no propositional atom, as each nonempty clopen in it splits further into admitted Cantor clopens.

At $w_j$ require a proposition lying in the $\Gamma$-orbit of the cylinder $[0^j]$ in $C$; require nothing of the proposition at other worlds. The resulting property is globally invariant and each fibre is clopen since the $w_j$ are separate isolated components. It is necessarily instantiated. A necessarily singleton subchoice supported by $K_m$ would at $w_j$ select a $K_m$-fixed proposition. For $j>m$ this is impossible. The group preserves fair-coin measure on $C$, so every member of the indicated orbit has measure $2^{-j}$; every $K_m$-fixed clopen in $C$ is a union of length-$m$ cylinders and has measure an integer multiple of $2^{-m}$. Thus even unboxed Intensional Choice fails.

At any relational type, choosing an arbitrary subset of the marked points and making the relation full there and empty everywhere else gives continuum many globally fixed, distinct entities. At that type choose disjoint fixed codes for the standard naturals and their powerset and the constant relations giving ordinary successor and membership. This is a necessarily real structure, rigid and necessarily distinct. Every external relation on finite tuples of its codes has a globally fixed constant profile. The constant classes of all these profiles are themselves admitted, rigid and give the represented powers at each arity. Thus both analytic necessitisms hold, the instance at the singleton individual type being vacuous.

At the identity of the discrete regular orbit $\Gamma$, every current set $E$ of tuples is represented: prescribe row $gE$ at $g$ and empty rows off the orbit. The resulting profile is invariant, and every fibre is clopen in the discrete component. There one can admit the countable family of Cantor clopens $[0^{2n}1]$ and its injection into the standard Frege numerals at $t$. These propositions have no join: a clopen upper bound must contain $0^\omega$ and one of its whole neighborhoods, so a sufficiently deep odd cylinder $[0^{2n+1}1]$ may be removed while preserving upper-boundhood. The removed cylinder is a smooth clopen. Thus necessary Boolean Completeness at $t$ and necessary Countable Boolean Completeness both fail. The set of standard numeral denotations at $t$ is globally fixed and admits a constant-row inductive test. It exhausts the canonical numbers everywhere, while all standard finite sizes occur and none exhausts the infinite proposition carrier. The Axiom of Infinity at $t$ holds necessarily. There is only one individual at every world. The signature names denote top, so the specified relational signature also fails Independence.
