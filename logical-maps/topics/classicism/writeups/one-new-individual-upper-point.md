# One new individual at the upper point

At the unmarked point of this model, Distinctness is necessary at every type, Boolean Completeness holds necessarily at every relational type, and Strong Actuality holds necessarily. Inextensible Comprehension fails already for the universal property of individuals.

The two objects are the upper objects of [Injective entrances to old and new individuals](injective-entrances-to-old-and-new-individuals.html). The construction and the inextensibility argument originate in the [symmetric old-and-new model](symmetric-old-new-individuals.html) of Cian Dorr and Claude Fable 5.1, with the corrected proof by Claude Opus 5.5. The new Boolean Completeness verdict at the marked point uses the finiteness of the set of new individuals. The argument for the original model with infinitely many new individuals finds a new individual outside the proposed finite pin of a join.

Let $E=\mathbb N$ and $O=\mathbb N\sqcup B$ where $B$ is nonempty and finite. The recorded instance takes $B=\{*\}$. The two objects have these sets of individuals. Arrows ending at $E$ are all injections. Arrows ending at $O$ are all injections into its old part $\mathbb N$, together with the permutations of $O$ preserving $B$ setwise. The symmetry group at $E$ consists of all permutations, and at $O$ it consists of the permutations preserving $B$. Relations have the symmetric intensions with finite source pins from Dorr's ideally-full construction. Thus postcomposing with a target symmetry $g$ carries the row at $f$ to the row at $gf$ by $g$. Agreement of two arrows into the same object on a pin gives equality of their whole rows. The evaluation is at $1_E$; the symbols of a purely relational signature may all denote top.

## Boolean Completeness at both objects

Fix a source $W$ and partition the arrows from it by target, also distinguishing permutations of $O$ from arrows $O\to O$ with old range. For each kind choose a representative $b$. At $E$ use the identity and the inclusion $E\to O$. At $O$ use the identity, a bijection $O\to E$, and a bijection $O\to\mathbb N\subset O$. Enlarge each source pin at $O$ to contain $B$. The pin then distinguishes the two endomorphism kinds. For a pin $S$ and arrow $f$ of the kind represented by $b$, a target symmetry $g$ satisfies $gb|_S=f|_S$, by extending the finite partial bijection. It follows that a relation $F$ is determined by the finitely many rows $F_b$. A list of rows invariant under the target stabilizers of $bS$ defines an admitted relation with pin $S$. Its row at $f$ is $gF_b$ for any such $g$. Two profiles are ordered by native entailment exactly when their representative rows are included componentwise. For this last assertion interpolate at the union of their two pins.

Let $X$ be an admitted family of relations, with pin $N$ enlarged as above. For each $b$ put
$$
J_b=\bigcup_{F\in X_{1_W}} F_b.
$$
A target symmetry $g$ fixing $bN$ satisfies $gb=b\gamma$ for a source symmetry $\gamma$ fixing $N$. For the bijections with source $O$ this follows by conjugating on the range of $b$. Since $B\subseteq N$, the conjugate fixes every new source point. For the inclusion at $E$, restrict $g$ to the old part of $O$. The identity representative is immediate. Since $\gamma$ fixes $N$, it permutes the current members of $X$. Symmetry and transport give $gF_b= F_{gb}=F_{b\gamma}=(\gamma F)_b$. Thus each $J_b$ is invariant under the stabilizer of $bN$. The resulting profile $J$ is the least upper bound by the componentwise order criterion. This works for arbitrary finite tuples of typed arguments and at every reachable object.

## Distinctness, Strong Actuality and the failed comprehension

Each arrow $f:E\to V$ preserves distinctness of entities. For every $h:E\to U$ and finite $S\subset E$, extend $f(s)\mapsto h(s)$ for $s\in S$ to an arrow $k:V\to U$. If $U=O$, all prescribed target values are old, so an injection with old range suffices. A tuple witnessing an inequality of two relations at $h$ still witnesses their transported inequality at $k$ when $S$ contains both pins. Individual transports are injections.

The two arrow kinds out of $E$ and the three out of $O$ are the propositional atoms, by the same interpolation. At $E$ the event of ending at $E$ is a true strong atom. Its transports are the atoms of ending at $E$ at either source. At $O$ the automorphism event is a true strong atom; its transports along automorphisms remain this atom, and all its other transports are bottom.

Suppose $Y$ were an inextensible coextension of the universal property of individuals at $E$. Its row at every $s:E\to E$ is all of $E$, by finite interpolation with permutations. Use the tester whose $E$ rows are all individuals and whose $O$ rows are the old individuals. It necessarily accepts every individual at $E$. Weak inextensibility of $Y$ therefore excludes each $d\in B$ from the row at the inclusion $i:E\to O$. At $O$ fix such a $d$. Every member of $iY$ is necessarily distinct from $d$ because every individual map is injective. For any $m:O\to E$, the row of $Y$ at $mi$ is all individuals, including $m(d)$. This violates weak inextensibility of $iY$ with the tester $x\ne d$.

The all-type joins above, together with the inherited upper-point arguments, give the separation in the first paragraph. The earlier single-individual variant of the original model replaces each *entire* individual domain by a singleton; in the present construction both domains are infinite.

For the recorded one-new-individual instance, the upper objects and their typed domains are unchanged when the anterior object in the marked-entrance construction is removed. Its proofs at the upper objects therefore give necessary Atomicity and Intensional Choice, rigid standard arithmetic and the necessity of second-order analysis. To see directly that Relational Choice fails at $E$, take the relation selecting a member of each two-element individual property. Given any selector with finite pin, swap two people outside that pin. The two-person property is fixed as a whole and its possible selections are exchanged. For propositional Barcan failure, exclude the permutation event at every $E$-to-$O$ row of a tester on propositions and give it full rows at $E$. All old propositions pass at every continuation, since an old proposition has equal truth values on the two endomorphism kinds after entering $O$. The new permutation event fails the test at that entrance. For the upper arithmetic, finite cardinality bounds and five-necessity proofs, see [the marked-entrance model](injective-entrances-to-old-and-new-individuals.html).
