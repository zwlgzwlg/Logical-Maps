# Signature Possibility+ and Barcan without either Rigid Power

At one point, signature Possibility+, revised Mathematical Possibilism and the Barcan formula at every type hold. A rigid family of individual properties there has a rigid subfamily at an exit that no weakly rigid source subfamily can reach. Consequently both local power principles fail.

## Components

Take the [finitely pinned substitution root](coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root.html) $M$ for a signature with one propositional name. Its individual domain is countably infinite, and signature Possibility+ and local Barcan at every type hold there. Identify its individuals with $A=\mathbb N\times\{0,1\}$. Use two further one-world components $K_0$ and $K_1$, both with individual domain $A$.

At $K_0$ all types are full. At $K_1$ let the group independently exchange the two individuals in each pair. At each relational type take the sets of admitted argument tuples invariant under all flips outside finitely many pairs. Each element has finite support, and the group acts by sending a tuple to its translate. Interpreting a lambda uses the union of the finite supports of its parameters, since the group permutes the entire domains of bound variables. These are extensional Henkin domains. With no proper arrows, every relation in either of these components is rigid.

Give a new source $o$ the same individuals $A$ and one entrance to each of the three components, acting identically on individuals. Also attach the ordinary finite singleton-individual leaves indexed by the Stone space of a Boolean product, as in the [homogeneous Stone model](stone-terminal-maximalist.html). The product is specified below. Each root relation consists of its arbitrary current tuple row, an arbitrary whole relation at each of $M,K_0,K_1$, and a locally constant section of the finite leaf domain. Application reads the current row. Term abstraction evaluates the body on the transported parameters and the full argument domain in each coordinate. At the Stone leaves any term uses only finitely many locally constant parameters in the same finite structure, so its value is locally constant. Whole-coordinate equality is detected by the root, the individual component cones and the Stone leaves. Give the name its designated value at $M$, with top at the other components and, for example, a full source row. There are no returns to $o$.

The identity entrance to $M$ retains the individuals for each eligible signature Possibility+ instance, so its old witness remains possible at $o$. Every initial entrance is onto at every type: the three old coordinates are independent, the Stone coordinates admit constant sections, and each individual map is onto. Suppose a tester holds necessarily of every current argument. At each exit it therefore holds necessarily of every argument currently there. At $M$ apply its local Barcan principle to conclude that the tester is universal throughout its cone. In the one-world components and ordinary leaves the current conclusion already covers the whole cone. This proves local Barcan at $o$.

## The rigid subfamily which cannot be lifted

Write $H_a$ for the individual property of being identical to $a$. Take $E$ to be the union of the even-indexed individual pairs. Choose an external bijection $b:A\to A$ taking $E$ onto $\mathbb N\times\{0\}$. Write $c$ for the empty individual property in each remaining component. For $a\in A$, define a source property $x_a$ by
$$
  (x_a)_{o}=\varnothing,\qquad (x_a)_{K_0}=H_a,\qquad
  (x_a)_{K_1}=H_{b(a)},\qquad (x_a)_M=c,
$$
with constant value $c$ also on the Stone leaves. Let $F$ have current members exactly these $x_a$. Its components at $K_0,K_1$ consist of all individual haecceities, which form an invariant family at $K_1$. In each other component take the haecceity of $c$ at the higher type. These coordinates give it, at every arrow, exactly the images of its current members. In particular all later observations in the $M$-cone see only the transported $c$. The exact image condition is hereditary under composition; it gives persistence and weak inextensibility at each point. Thus $F$ is rigid.

At $K_0$ the subfamily $U=\{H_a:a\in E\}$ is rigid. For any source weakly rigid $Z\le F$, the current row of $Z$ in either terminal $K_i$ is exactly the set of projections of its source members. Persistence gives one inclusion. For the other, delete a single possible extra member $y$ from the tester at that terminal and set all its other coordinates and current row to top. In $K_1$ this deletion is admitted since the one entity $y$ has finite support. Every actual member of $Z$ necessarily passes, so weak inextensibility eliminates the extra member.

If $Z$ projected to $U$ in $K_0$, its current extension would thus be exactly $\{x_a:a\in E\}$. At $K_1$ it would have to project to $\{H_{b(a)}:a\in E\}$. This set has no finite support: flipping any pair exchanges its selected member with an omitted one. Hence no such $Z$ exists. Finally take a tester on families of individual properties deleting precisely the whole entity $U$ at $K_0$, and full elsewhere. It necessarily holds of every current rigid or weakly rigid subfamily of $F$ while failing on $U$ at the exit. Neither canonical power of $F$ is weakly inextensible.

## Mathematical Possibilism and choice

At the inexact ordinal type $\rho=(eet)(et)t$, let $Z$ consist of source entities whose current application depends only on the actual order and field on $A$, whose three old coordinates are arbitrary, and whose ordinary Stone section is constant. Their number is bounded by the number of extensional tables on $A$ times the sizes of the three prechosen $\rho$-domains and of the common finite leaf domain. The new Stone space has not entered this bound.

The current predicate of membership in $Z$ is admitted. Zero and successor preserve its extensional table and constant section conditions by their well-order and initial segment definitions. If a rigid family consists currently of members of $Z$, their constant Stone values form a finite set $S$. Take a tester with top current and old coordinates and with constant leaf predicate $S$. It necessarily holds of the current members. Weak inextensibility therefore bounds the family by $S$ everywhere in the Stone section, and persistence gives the reverse inclusion. Its limit uses the same finite list at every leaf and is extensional at the source. This puts the ordinal benchmark inside $Z$.

Choose a strong limit $\mu$ above the stated bound and let the Stone space represent the product of the nontrivial complete Boolean algebras of size less than $\mu$. Full current rows make every internally complete algebra ordinarily complete. A carrier of size at least $\mu$ contains a set $Y$ of size $\mu$ exceeding the benchmark, and any smaller subset of $Y$ has its full powerset coded on a smaller subset of $Y$ by the strong limit condition. All these graphs are admitted, contradicting revised smallness. Each smaller complete algebra has its own Stone factor, whose unit with all other coordinates bottom has exactly the required algebra as its principal interval. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism.

The identity-only proposition at $o$ is a true strong atom and is impossible after every exit, witnessing distinctness-preserving necessity of each current truth. Every external well-order and every coextension selector is represented in the unrestricted current rows. At $K_1$, however, an equivalence transversal for the individual pairs would choose one member from each, which no finite support permits. For Relational Choice, use the serial relation requiring a member of each two-point pair property and allowing arbitrary outputs for other inputs. An unused pair flip fixes its input and swaps its two possible outputs. For the same reason an admitted well-order of individuals is impossible, since a nonidentity permutation cannot preserve a well-order. Thus even though the source admits well-orders, it does not satisfy Modal Well-Ordering.
