# Rigid powers on the increasing hyperfinite stages

Use the stage and germ notation of the [increasing hyperfinite stages](increasing-hyperfinite-stages.html). For the first two arguments start in $(H_\kappa,\in,U)$ for $\kappa=(\beth_\omega)^+$. This structure has Collection and Choice, by regularity of $\kappa$, and contains the powersets used at each fixed relation type. Take each elementary extension to be the internal ultrapower of the preceding stage structure by its ultrafilter on the positive integers. Internally represented functions suffice for Łoś's theorem: Collection and Choice supply the family of witnesses for any one formula. Choose the fresh integer to be the class of the identity. Write $j$ for an elementary inclusion in this presentation. It is bijective from any internally finite source set onto the elements of its image. Indeed, internally, an ultrafilter picks one cell of every finite partition, including every partition with a nonstandard but internally finite number of cells. The same applies to the powerset of an internally finite source set and to a finite iteration of these elementary inclusions.

## An internal subset of the old elements

Let $A$ be a set in one stage structure. Suppose $B$ is an internal subset of $j(A)$ in its internal ultrapower and every element of $B$ belongs to $j''A$. Then $B=j(S)$ for some internally finite source subset $S$ of $A$.

Write $B=[y\mapsto B_y]$. Define in the source $S=\{a\in A:\{y:a\in B_y\}\in U\}$. If $S$ were internally infinite, internal Choice would provide a sequence of distinct $a_n\in S$. For each $y$ let $l(y)$ be the greatest $m\le y$ such that all $a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1}$ belong to $B_y$. For each fixed internal $n$, the set where $l(y)>n$ is large, by the finite intersection property inside the stage structure. Thus the class of $y\mapsto a_{l(y)-1}$, with any default when $l(y)=0$, belongs to $B$ and differs from every constant class. This contradicts the hypothesis. So $S$ is internally finite and $B=j(S)$.

## What weak rigidity implies

Fix a weakly rigid relation $F$ at $i$, with current tuple set $E$ and tail represented by $f$. At a loop $g$, a tester with unrestricted rows except for $g[E]$ at $g$ forces the row of $F$ to lie in $g[E]$. Weak persistence gives the opposite inclusion. The tester's strict rows can all be full.

At the first strict target, the row $f(k_{i+1})$ is exactly the image of $E$ under the strict-arrow transport $T$. To see the upper bound, suppose $z$ belongs to that row and differs from $T(e)$ for every $e\in E$. Represent the tuple $z$ by an internal family $z(y)$ of tuples at $y$. The root tester with full loop rows and tail row the complement of $\{z(y)\}$ rejects $z$. For every fixed $e\in E$, the stage-$i$ condition $e[y]\ne z(y)$ holds on a large set. Consequently the tester accepts the transport of $e$ at every later fresh stage and every loop. This contradicts weak inextensibility. Weak persistence again gives the lower bound.

The tuple of germs of a strict transport $T(e)$ is the elementary image of the tuple of germs of $e$. Ignore individual coordinates, which are singletons. The projection of $f(k_{i+1})$ onto its tuple of germs is therefore an internal subset of $j''A$ for the source product $A$ of germ sorts. By the preceding observation it equals $j(S)$ for an internally finite source set $S$. Choose $e_s\in E$ of germ tuple $s$ for every $s\in S$. There is only one strict transport for each such germ tuple, since strict transport discards every local loop table. Hence the original tail row $f(y)$ agrees on a large set with the row $\{e_s[y]:s\in S\}$, using fixed representatives for all selected germs. Representatives for this internal finite family can be chosen internally.

It follows that every strict target row is the image of the same family of representatives. This also follows at later stages because the inclusions are bijective on the elements of the internally finite indexing set $S$. Thus $F$ has exact image rows along all arrows. Exactness is preserved by composition and gives weak rigidity at every target. We have proved necessary Tame Rigidity.

## Lifting subrelations

Let $F$ be rigid and fix a strict target $j>i$. Its actual members there are the transported $e_s$ indexed by the internally finite set $S$ just constructed. They are distinct since their tuples of germs are distinct. A rigid subrelation $G$ of the transported $F$ therefore has a current extension indexed by a subset of the image of $S$. This subset comes from a stage-$i$ subset $S'$ of $S$, by the internally finite powerset observation.

There is an admitted root relation with exact image rows of $\{e_s:s\in S'\}$. Use these images on the loops and the germ of the row function $y\mapsto\{e_s[y]:s\in S'\}$. The finite indexing set guarantees exactness at every strict stage. This relation is rigid, entails $F$, and its transport has the same current extension as $G$. Coextensive rigid relations are identical. At loops every subrelation lifts by the inverse loop. Consequently the rigid power of $F$ has exact image rows along every arrow, so it too is rigid. Necessary Tame Rigidity also gives the weakly rigid version. Both conclusions hold at every stage.

The proof works with any positive starting integer $k$. It gives the uniform first-order set-theoretic translation of each closed rigidity instance at every $k$ in the starting structure. Evaluation of any fixed typed formula quantifies only over the indicated type domains, row sets and representatives; these lie in the common finite-rank levels above $\omega$ of $H_\kappa$ and $V_{\omega+\omega}$, with the same ultrafilter. The two transitive starting structures therefore agree on this translation for every positive integer. By elementarity the same verdicts hold throughout the original elementary-extension construction.

## Inextensible Comprehension and Modal Well-Ordering

For an arbitrary internal current tuple set $E$, prescribe the row $g[E]$ at each loop $g$ and an empty germ. At a loop every subsequent loop row is the exact image of the current one; at every strict target every row is empty. Thus at every accessible object a tester necessarily accepting the candidate's current members includes it at every subsequent arrow. The candidate is inextensible and has current row $E$. This proves necessary Inextensible Comprehension.

For any fixed sort $\sigma$, use internal Choice to select a well-order of $D_\sigma(y)$ for every $y$. At the current loops give a binary relation an internal well-order of the current domain as its row; take as its germ the function assigning the selected well-order at $y$. At accessible loops and strict targets the relevant row is an internal well-order by elementarity. Every admitted nonempty property has an internal current extension and hence a least element in it. This witnesses Modal Well-Ordering at every sort.

## The three modal operators

At each stage a proposition $p$ is ND-necessary precisely when all its loop values are true. For every true guard $q$ and loop $g$, the inverse loop makes $q$ possible at $g$. Thus $\Box(\Diamond q\to p)$ requires $p$ at every loop. Conversely the guard that is true at all current loops with zero germ is true at the identity and impossible after each strict entrance. It works whenever $p$ is true on every loop.

The operator that tests every loop has N and K, and satisfies B because every loop can be inverted. The characterization holds at all stages, so ND-necessity is an S5 operator in the map's sense. By its definition 5-necessity implies necessity under this particular operator. Together with the general inequalities $\Box_{\ne}\le\hat{\hat\Box}\le\Box_5$ this proves that all three coincide necessarily. In the original generic-start model the identity-only proposition is true and fails the loop test, since the first cyclic group already has more than one member. Thus there is a truth that fails 5-necessity.
