## What the weak bound forces at the entrance

Write E for a weakly rigid outer bound, U for its actual tuple set and
f for its old value at K. Its persistence holds throughout the cone:
apply weak inextensibility of E to the test x↦□E(x), using initial
weak persistence and 4. In particular f is weakly persistent, and
π(U)⊆f_current. In fact equality holds. At K the tester with actual
row π(U) and its own old descriptor top is available. Give an outer
tester this entire old value and top at all outer loop and leaf rows.
Every actual U-tuple necessarily passes it, since its first entrance
image belongs to π(U), and all proper arrows out of K see top. Thus
f_current⊆π(U). More generally, for any tester v at K necessary on
every current f-member, put v on the old coordinate of an outer tester
with top elsewhere. Every actual U-member passes it necessarily, so
outer weak inextensibility gives f≤v. Thus f is weakly rigid at K.
At K all weak bounds are fully rigid and have fully rigid powers.
Leaves are classical. Weak rigidity transports along each invertible
outer loop: transport the tester back by the inverse, use the exact
images under the loop (persistence and its inverse), and transport the
resulting entailment forward. Consequently E itself is fully rigid.

## Lifting an arbitrary old subrelation

For a rigid z≤f at K let U' consist of the tuples a∈U with π(a) in
the current extension of z. Then π(U')=z_current. For a terminal tuple
v write O_v(U')={x∈X : some a∈U' has terminal value v at x}. This is
open because every a has a continuous section into a discrete finite
set. Define Y at the outer root by actual row U', old whole value z,
and terminal v-membership set the closure of O_v(U'). These closures
are clopen. The resulting finite set of coordinates determines a
continuous terminal relation. Each closure is below the corresponding
clopen E-membership set since persistence gives O_v(U') a subset of it.

With root group G assign Y the loop row g(U') at g. Every tuple a in
U' projects to z_current, and π(g a)=π(a). Hence g(U')=gU intersect
π⁻¹(z_current). The table g↦gU is just E's table by persistence and
inverses and so has finite prefix support. Y's table has the same
support. This proves its admission also with the profinite root.

Y≤E at all coordinates. Its weak persistence follows from z's
persistence, its exact loop rows and the inclusion of each O_v(U')
in its closure. For weak inextensibility let a tester W be necessary
on all actual U'-members. At the old entrance π(U')=z_current and the
old tester is necessary on each such member, hence covers z
throughout the old cone by z's weak inextensibility. At the leaves
each clopen W-membership set contains O_v(U'), so it contains its
closure. At loops the exact row g(U') is covered by necessity on
the original tuples. This proves Y≤W. The old z is rigid, leaves
are rigid, and invertible-loop transport of weak rigidity applies.
Thus Y is a fully rigid outer subrelation of E lifting z as a whole.

## Testing the two powers

At the outer root all weakly rigid subrelations are fully rigid, as
shown for arbitrary weak bounds. At K the same holds for its local
subrelations. At proper descendants of K it holds for *subrelations of
the transported f*: f there is a finite union of the transported
haecceities of the old actual f-members representing its finite
descriptor support. At leaves it holds as well. Thus the two canonical
powers of E agree throughout as intensions. Their common predicate
is persistent.

Take an outer tester necessary on the current rigid subrelations of E.
At the old point, each current rigid z≤f has the equally rigid outer
lift above, so the old-coordinate tester is necessary on every old
power member. The old power is weakly inextensible at K, hence is
covered everywhere underneath K. Every terminal subrelation of E is
a finite set, since its tuple sort is finite. Our general finite-
subrelation theorem for an arbitrary weakly rigid bound therefore
makes it pass the outer tester, without any surjectivity claim at a
specified leaf. Equivalently, fix a leaf counterexample, take a
clopen neighborhood with fixed target relation and tester bits, and
intersect the finitely many dense open realizations of its tuples.
The corresponding finite union of root haecceities would violate
the test. The invertible loop transports preserve the entire power
membership, providing the required loop clause. This proves weak
inextensibility at the outer root. The old point sees full rigidity
of its own canonical power, and all terminal powers are rigid; the
loop clause again transports weak rigidity by inversion. The outer
power is therefore fully rigid, for both the identity-only and
profinite versions. Both conclusions are local schemata. Some old
components witness failure of the boxed versions.
