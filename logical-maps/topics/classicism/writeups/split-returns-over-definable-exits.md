## The sites and their profiles

The new sites are $r$ and $s_j$, one for each positive integer $j$. Add arrows $f_j:r\to s_j$ and two arrows $g_{j0},g_{j1}:s_j\to r$, subject only to $g_{jk}f_j=1_r$. Thus the core arrows out of $r$ are $1_r$ and the $f_i$. Out of $s_j$ they are the identity, the two $g_{jk}$, and the distinct $f_i g_{jk}$. From $r$ adjoin the original separate entrances to the old components, with all their old postcomposites freely retained. Paths from $s_j$ into the old components have two disjoint sectors according to their $g_{jk}$ prefix. No old path enters the core.

The individual carrier has one member at $r$ and $j$ at $s_j$. Each $f_j$ sends the singleton to a designated member and each $g_{jk}$ sends all individuals to the singleton. The entrance at an old point sends the root individual to its interpretation of $d$.

At $r$ supplement these rows by the old profile of one closed $T$-language term of that type. At $s_j$ supply two such descriptors, independently, for the two sectors. Quotient descriptors by equality on the complete family of old points and their continuations. The domain definition is recursive on types since the target tuples are of lower types. Transport restricts the rows and old profiles by precomposition. Thus $f_j$ duplicates the old descriptor into both sectors, and $g_{jk}$ takes the descriptor in sector $k$. The two retraction identities hold at every type.

Unequal new relational entities have a separating row either at a core arrow or at an old continuation, by the original descriptor identity criterion. A lambda takes the row obtained from its body at each core arrow on the whole target tuple domain. On an old sector it takes its ordinary old lambda with the sector descriptors of its parameters substituted. Every one of these descriptors is available. This also verifies evaluation with quantifiers over new target entities. Interpret each relational name with its own descriptor in each sector, and give the auxiliary predicates empty rows throughout the core. ## Actuality, hats and finite returns

The scalar $a$ supported only at $1_r$, with bottom descriptor, is the actual atom. For each $j$, $f_j a$ is false at the identity of $s_j$ but either $g_{jk}$ takes it back to $a$. In particular every current truth at $r$ is possible after $f_j$. The pure sentence asserting that there are exactly $j$ individuals is therefore ND-possible. The current individual Frege numbers are the standard numerals: the unrestricted row consisting of the iterates of the closed successor from zero supplies an induction test, and the finite old models distinguish the iterates. At $s_j$ the transported $(j-1)$st numeral has a successor counting all individuals. Thus even the single internally quantified sentence asserting an ND-possible return through the successor cardinality of every current number holds.

For each nonzero relational entity $X$ at $r$, choose any witness of instantiation along a core or old arrow. It gives witnesses in both return sectors of $f_j X$, since both returns recover $X$. A tester at $s_j$ includes every tuple at every core address in one return sector and none in the other, using the top and bottom old descriptors in the respective sectors. Thus $f_j X$ is nonzero and splits. This proves that there are no strong atoms at $r$, including at type $t$. The scalar supported only at a single $f_j$ is an atom at $r$ and becomes a true nonatom at $s_j$, so it has no self-atom below it. Conversely the scalar with empty core rows and old descriptor the Fregean Axiom is a self-atom. Wherever instantiated it is in an old Fregean cone, and it splits at $r$ by the one-individual and two-individual old Fregean models.

At $s_j$ the scalar $v_j$ supported just at $1_{s_j}$ with both old descriptors bottom is true. Wherever $f_j a$ holds, the evaluating arrow is one of the $g_{jk}$. No continuation of that arrow is $1_{s_j}$, so $v_j$ is impossible there. In particular $\Diamond_{\ne}(f_j a)$ fails. At all old points $a$ is bottom. Consequently at $r$ every current truth $p$ satisfies $\Box(\Diamond_{\ne}a\to p)$, which, using $a$, gives two-hat necessity of $p$. But $a$ is false at each $f_j$ where every current truth remains possible. Hence $a$ is not ND-necessary. At this point 5-necessity and two-hat necessity agree with truth.

## Rigidity and both powers

For a root weakly rigid $F$ let $E$ be its current extension. The original fresh-predicate proof shows that the tuples in $E$ represent only finitely many distinct old descriptor tuples. Indeed each auxiliary predicate necessarily misses every root tuple, since it has empty core rows and the ground nonmembership axioms hold at the old exits. The argument bounding the descriptors uses only this fact, weak persistence, weak inextensibility and the unmodified auxiliary second root in the original proof.

At each core arrow $h$ the extension of $hF$ is exactly $h[E]$. Weak persistence gives inclusion of $h[E]$. For the reverse, use a tester with all other core rows and its old descriptor top that deletes a proposed excess tuple at $h$. The same exact-image statement at each old arrow follows from the tester with all core rows full and old descriptor the finite union of the represented tuple-haecceities. The argument applies to the empty tuple as well.

For any external $E'\subseteq E$ define $W(E')$ by its exact direct images at the core arrows and the finite union of the haecceities of its represented old descriptors on the old exits. This has exactly the direct images of $E'$ at *all* arrows. Any exact direct-image profile is rigid: at an arrow $h$ and its continuation $k$ the extension at $kh$ is $k[h[E']]$, giving persistence and forcing every necessary test on members of $h[E']$ to cover it. In particular $F=W(E)$ is rigid.

At any core target $s_j$, a weakly rigid $Y\le f_jF$ is determined by its current extension $D\subseteq f_j[E]$. Indeed $D=f_j[E']$ with $E'=E\cap f_j^{-1}[D]$, and $f_jW(E')$ is rigid with that extension. Weakly rigid relations with the same current extension coincide: use each one's persistence as the other's weak-inextensibility test. At an old target any weakly rigid subrelation is the finite union of haecceities of its current tuples, and it lifts from a finite selection of original tuples. Thus every weak subrelation counted by the power at any target is rigid and lifts from $r$.

The canonical weak and rigid powers agree throughout the root cone. Their common profile consists at every arrow exactly of the image of its current root extension: forward inclusion is rigidity and persistence of order, reverse inclusion is the lift just proved. Hence this power is rigid. Weakly Rigid Power, Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power all hold at $r$. Weak Rigid Comprehension fails: a relation coextensive with the universal property of propositions would represent infinitely many distinct old descriptors, as witnessed by the finite-cardinality sentences. The same fresh-predicate witness for the failure of Barcan at $t$ as in the original model applies.

## Other current verdicts

The independent identity row gives arbitrary actual graphs, including well-orders, transversals and selector relations by external choice. The profile with only this row and empty old descriptor is inextensible. The singleton individual domain is finite. The scalar domain is infinite and its Frege numbers are the standard iterates by the same unrestricted current induction test, so it satisfies the quantified Axiom of Infinity at $t$.

The scalar algebra is $\mathcal P(\{1_r,f_1,f_2,\ldots\})\times L_T$, where $L_T$ is the old closed-sentence Boolean algebra. Let $b_j$ be the Fregean finite-size-$j$ cell. In any join of a subfamily of these pairwise disjoint nonzero scalars the bit at the all-bottom finite Fregean model of size $j$ must be its membership bit. At these particular models every old descriptor reduces to a pure sentence with the one individual marker. Their countably many traces omit a subset of the positive integers. Its family is an admitted current row with a current injection into the type-$t$ numerals. This refutes countable Boolean completeness and Boolean Completeness at $t$.

No infinite principal interval of $L_T$ is complete. Otherwise take disjoint nonzero $a_n$ in such an interval $[0,b]$. The representatives of these elements and $b$ use a countable set of names. A join of a subfamily, if it exists, is fixed modulo $T$ by every renaming preserving that set pointwise. Choose countable spare pools of every auxiliary arity and public type, adding the entire remaining pool when finite. The finitely many remaining names of each join representative can be moved into these pools by a renaming of its respective kind. The joins would therefore all be represented in one countable language, impossible for the distinct joins of continuum many subfamilies. This uses the permutation symmetry of the unrestricted auxiliary theory. As a consequence every nonzero complete principal interval of the product has an atom, either in the powerset factor or in a nonzero finite old factor.

The ordinal benchmark has at least countably many members because its standard finite thresholds are distinguished at the old finite linear orders. With unrestricted current rows and graphs the Cantor regular-open algebra of size continuum therefore has a revised-small presentation, as in [[coalesced-definable-root-signature]]: any $H<Y$ within its carrier has a countably infinite proper-smaller subcollection whose continuum many subsets cannot be coded in a set smaller than $Y$. The Cantor algebra is complete and atomless, so it has no isomorphic scalar principal interval. This refutes revised Mathematical Possibilism.
