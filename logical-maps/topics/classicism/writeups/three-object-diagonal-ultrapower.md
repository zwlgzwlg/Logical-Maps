# Three-object chain into a diagonal ultrapower

Boolean Completeness at every relational type can hold where Arithmetic Necessitism fails. The failure is at the individual-type numbers. They are weakly inextensible at the evaluation point, as follows from local Boolean Completeness. At its immediate successor an admitted tester separates the current numbers from one new number farther along the chain. This prevents the numbers from being fully rigid at the evaluation point. OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, supplied this model and its verification on 26 November 2026.

## Three levels of domains

Take the thin category $r\to s\to t$, with the composite and the identities. Work in external ZFC. Let $H$ be the full one-world extensional hierarchy over individuals $\mathbb N$. At type $\rho=\bar\sigma t$ its carrier is the powerset of the tuples of its argument types. In particular $H_t$ as a proposition sort has two elements. At the terminal object, also denoted by $t$, take the many-sorted ultrapower ${}^*H$ of this structure over a nonprincipal ultrafilter on the positive integers. Include typed incidence, application and the logical operations. Its diagonal copy $\delta H$ is an elementary substructure. Internal relations are realized as actual sets of ultrapower tuples by their incidence predicates; extensionality for each type transfers. A lambda term at the terminal denotes the class of the factor denotations, by induction on the term and Łoś's theorem. In particular, the value of any term with diagonal parameters is the corresponding diagonal entity, including at types with new, nondiagonal arguments.

At $r$ and $s$ use $\mathbb N$ as the individual carrier. The first arrow acts by the identity and the second by $\delta$. Construct relational types in order of type complexity. For $\rho=\bar\sigma t$ let $T_w=\prod_i D_{\sigma_i}(w)$. For zero arguments the product has one member. Use the carriers

$$
D_\rho(s)=\mathcal P(T_s)\times{}^*H_\rho,\qquad
D_\rho(r)=\mathcal P(T_r)\times\mathcal P(T_s)\times\delta H_\rho .
$$

The entries are the successive rows of a relation. An $s$-row is read on all $s$-arguments, and a terminal entity on all terminal arguments. For example, the final row of a diagonal entity $\delta B$ is its entire ultrapower interpretation. Transport drops the earlier entries of these pairs and triples. It is associative and the displayed domains contain the resulting values. Interpret individual constants by $0$ and its images, and relational constants by top.

These domains are closed under all typed terms with parameters. A lambda term at either nonterminal object has the unrestricted identity row consisting of the tuples satisfying its body there. The same applies to its row at $s$ when evaluated with the transported root parameters. At the terminal evaluate the term on the transported free parameters and range the bound arguments over their full terminal domains. Its value belongs to ${}^*H$ by term closure there. If the free parameters came from $r$, their terminal values are diagonal, so this whole terminal value is diagonal. Thus a root term has a triple of the required kind, and a middle term has an allowed pair. Application uses the same row evaluation on transported arguments. This verifies the action-model closure criterion for C. The ultrafilter and the many-sorted ultrapower are part of the ZFC construction. For the verdicts below one may equivalently fix this elementary ultrapower and its diagonal embedding at the outset.

## Completeness and the numbers

At each relational type order at $r$ is componentwise inclusion, since entailment tests all reachable rows and all tuples there. On the diagonal terminal factor one has $\delta B\subseteq\delta C$ iff $B\subseteq C$ in $H$, by elementarity. Hence this factor is order-isomorphic to an externally complete powerset. Together with the two earlier powersets this makes $D_\rho(r)$ complete. In particular the family selected by any current property has a greatest lower bound. At proposition type the three carriers have eight, four and two elements, respectively, each a finite Boolean algebra. Thus the root also satisfies necessary Boolean Completeness at proposition type. The ninth numeral counted at that type has no instance anywhere, so Potential Infinity at that type fails.

Both $r$ and $s$ have an admitted property with identity extension exactly the denotations of the standard individual-type numerals and empty later rows. It contains zero and is closed under successor at the identity. Thus the impredicative numbers at $r$ and $s$ are exactly those numerals. For each standard $k$, the $k$th numeral counts exactly the properties with $k$ current instances, by induction using the actual identity in the Frege successor. No such numeral counts the universal property of individuals at $r$ or $s$, where the individuals are $\mathbb N$. At the terminal, the Axiom of Infinity at $e$ transfers from $H$. In particular the Peano conditions $I_e^*$ hold at all three objects, by the recorded implication from the Axiom of Infinity to Potential Infinity.

In factor $j$ let $B_j$ be the property of cardinalities whose extension is the first $j+1$ numeral denotations, from zero through $j$. The sequence represents an admitted property $B$ at the terminal. It contains the diagonal denotation of every standard numeral, by the cofinite test. It omits the number represented by the sequence of $(j+1)$st numerals, which is a number there by transfer. At $s$ take a tester with full identity row and terminal value $B$. Every individual-type number at $s$ is a standard numeral; it passes this tester necessarily since a closed numeral transports to that same numeral. But $\mathbb N_e$ does not entail the tester, as the missing terminal number shows. This is exactly failure of weak inextensibility at $s$. Consequently $\mathbb N_e$ fails rigidity at $r$ despite $\Box I_e^*$ there. This refutes Arithmetic Necessitism.

## Further verdicts

**Strong Atomicity.** At each root relational type take a nonzero row of a given relation. At either unrestricted powerset row choose a singleton tuple contained in it. At the last row, a nonempty diagonal relation $\delta B$ comes from a nonempty $B$ in $H$. Choose a member and take its diagonal singleton, which remains a singleton on all terminal tuples by elementarity. Put the singleton in the chosen row and the empty set elsewhere. This is an atom below the original relation. After any transport, its sole row either disappears or is still a singleton in the corresponding coordinate algebra. It consequently entails every relation there or its complement. Thus it is a strong atom. At $s$ the unrestricted current row and the internal terminal row give the same argument, using internal singletons of arbitrary terminal tuples. These singletons exist at $t$ by comprehension. This proves necessary Strong Atomicity at all relational types.

**Barcan.** The three transports on propositions project the eight, four and two element Boolean algebras onto their respective codomains. In particular every possible proposition has an actual preimage at the source of any arrow. This yields BF at $t$ throughout the chain. For a failure at a relational type consider the $et$-properties at $s$. Those lifted from $r$ have diagonal terminal value. The middle property whose terminal value is the singleton of the individual represented by $(j)_j$ has nondiagonal terminal value. Indeed if that terminal singleton were $\delta B$, elementarity would force $B$ to be a singleton, whose diagonal individual would be standard. Now take a root tester of properties with full root row, middle row exactly the set of middle properties having diagonal terminal value, and top terminal row. Every root argument satisfies it necessarily. The exhibited middle argument fails it, disproving BF at type $et$.

**Well-Ordering.** In ZFC choose external well-orders of the type carriers at $r$ and $s$. Their graphs with empty later rows are admitted and each well-orders every nonempty current property. At $t$ each type's well-ordering sentence transfers from $H$, since the full hierarchy there admits the graph of an external well-order. Hence the root satisfies necessary Well-Ordering at every type.
