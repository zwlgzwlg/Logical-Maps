## What may pass through the entrance

Start with the [symmetric surjection product](symmetric-surjection-products.html) with a free word coordinate, using one actual individual. For an infinite cardinal $\kappa$, take a set $U$ of size $\kappa^+$ and a free monoid $W$ with infinitely many letters. The old model has one object. Its arrows are $(w,s)$ for $w\in W$ and $s:U\twoheadrightarrow U$. The group $G=\operatorname{Sym}(U)$ acts through the arrows with empty word. A relation $F$ has a pin $S\subset U$ of size at most $\kappa$. Its row at $(w,s)$ depends only on $w$ and $s|_S$, and postcomposition by a permutation transports that row by the permutation. Its source transport is precomposition.

A whole old entity fixed by $G$ has an empty pin. For a relational entity $F$ with pin $S$, move $S$ disjointly off itself by a permutation $\pi$. The transport $\pi F=F$ is also pinned by $\pi S$. For any two coefficient surjections $s,t$, prescribe $s$ on $S$ and $t$ on $\pi S$. This prescription extends to a surjection on $U$, since there are $\kappa^+$ remaining inputs. Comparing its row with each of the original rows shows $F_{(w,s)}=F_{(w,t)}$ for every word. Conversely, an empty pin makes all permutation transports identical to $F$. The old relations fixed by $G$ are consequently exactly the word-indexed lists of $G$-invariant tuple sets. Arbitrary external unions and intersections of such lists give relations of the same kind. Transport of any such entity along any old arrow is again fixed and depends only on the word. The single individual has these same invariance properties.

## Rigid relations and the two powers

Write $hE$ for the direct image of a set $E$ of current tuples along an arrow $h$ out of $r$. The profile $H_E$ whose row at $h$ is $hE$ is admitted. Indeed each individual entry of a tuple transported from $r$ is fixed on entrance to the old component. All further images there remain fixed and depend only on the word. The sets $hE$ therefore form an invariant old trace. Direct images compose, so $H_E$ is rigid: every future member comes from the current row, and every member persists. Thus Rigid Comprehension holds at $r$. If $F$ is weakly rigid with actual row $E$, weak persistence gives $H_E\le F$ and weak inextensibility applied to $H_E$ gives the reverse inclusion.

Whenever $Z$ is a weakly rigid or rigid subrelation of $hF$ at an old exit $h$, its actual row $D$ lies within $hE$. Put $S=\{a\in E:ha\in D\}$. The admitted $H_S$ is below $F$ and $hH_S$ is coextensive there with $Z$. In the old model weakly rigid relations are rigid, and coextensive rigid relations agree. Hence $hH_S=Z$. The same lift at the identity is the subrelation itself. The profiles defining the weak and rigid powers of $F$ therefore have exact image rows everywhere and are rigid. In conjunction with the old hereditary verdicts, both powers and Tame Rigidity hold necessarily.

In contrast, the property of being rigid and the property of being weakly rigid both fail weak inextensibility at $r$. The invariant tester just used for Barcan works at any relational sort. At the old point the haecceity of a proposition moved by $G$ is rigid, and is itself moved by $G$ since distinct entities have distinct haecceities. Every old image from $r$ is fixed.

## Revised Mathematical Possibilism

The propositional algebra at $r$ is $2\times\mathcal P(W)$. Each old word gives a different root atom. With the single individual everywhere, the inexact ordinal benchmark has a finite upper bound. Indeed the finite list consisting of the zero threshold, its successor and its following successor is closed under successor and limits of rigid subcollections. On an individual's well-ordered field these thresholds test the bounds $0,1,2$, the last of them being impossible. Every rigid subcollection of a finite list is the finite disjunction of the haecceities of its actual members; persistence and weak inextensibility give both inclusions. Its limit therefore tests the largest selected threshold, or the zero threshold for the empty selection. This is the finite-individual argument of the map's ordinal-threshold calculation.
