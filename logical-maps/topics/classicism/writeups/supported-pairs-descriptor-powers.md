# Signature Possibility+ and both rigid powers without Relational Choice

At a single point, signature Possibility+, Weakly Rigid Power, Rigid Power and Transversal hold, while Relational Choice and Transversal Choice fail. The public signature can be any set-sized free signature. The example modifies the [finite descriptor support construction](coalesced-finite-support-rigid-root.html) by restricting its roots' current rows under permutations of additional individual names. We work in ZFC.

## Individuals, old components and current rows

Add distinct individual names $L_i,A_i^0,A_i^1$ for every $i\in\mathbb N$ to the public signature $\Sigma$. Add the uncountable supply of auxiliary predicates $b$ for every nonempty argument tuple of types, as in the finite descriptor support construction. Also add countably many freely interpreted propositional names $z_j$. Call the resulting language $\Theta$. The old pointed theory $T$ is C($\Theta$) with $\Box\neg b(\bar t)$ for each such $b$ and every matching closed $\Theta$-tuple $\bar t$. It is conservative on the public language since every auxiliary predicate may be bottom and every auxiliary individual may be interpreted freely.

Let $G$ consist of independent swaps of $A_i^0,A_i^1$, fixing all labels $L_i$, public symbols, auxiliary predicates and free propositional names. Take a set of pointed components witnessing each $T$-consistent closed sentence, and include disjoint copies for all their $G$-renamings. Concretely, replace each chosen component by copies indexed by $g\in G$ in which a name $a$ has the original interpretation of $g^{-1}a$. Left multiplication permutes the copies. Old internal arrows and transports are preserved by these identifications. The new root has one exit to each distinguished point with its old postcomposites, and its only internal arrow is its identity. Its individuals are all the individual names of $\Theta$, transported by their interpretations in the components.

At each relational type take the pairs $(E,[u])$, where $u$ is a closed descriptor modulo $T$-provable equality, and $E$ is a set of current argument tuples fixed by every swap permutation fixing some finite set of pair indices. Both coordinates are chosen independently. The induced group action sends this pair to $(gE,[gu])$ and the components interpret the old coordinate as usual. The old descriptor itself has finite support because its term contains finitely many individual names. The definition therefore recurses over the argument types. Give each relational constant its own descriptor and empty current row.

For term closure, the action on the underlying structure commutes with application, transport, logical operations and the quantification clauses. At the root a permutation maps each domain bijectively onto itself. On old arrows it carries evaluation to evaluation on the correspondingly renamed copy. Thus the current row of a term with parameters is fixed by every swap fixing supports of the parameters and the finitely many option names in the term. Its old trace is the original lambda term with the descriptors of its parameters substituted. This calculates quantified terms using all the target entities at old points. The two rows together are admitted. The external swaps certify closure of the restricted rows; the only arrow from the root to itself is its identity.

For signature Possibility+, choose any current individual tuple satisfying the distinctness side conditions. Its entries are distinct individual names absent from the public matrix. Substitution of these names preserves consistency, and any old witness expands by bottom auxiliary predicates. The $T$-complete component family therefore realizes the required instance. This includes the empty-tuple Maximalism instances.

## Weak rigidity and both powers

The proof that a weakly rigid bound has finitely many old member descriptors is precisely the [fresh-predicate argument](coalesced-finite-support-rigid-root.html). Its root tests against each $b$ are admitted with empty current row. Every current tuple has a closed descriptor tuple. Necessary nonmembership and weak inextensibility consequently imply $T\vdash\forall\bar x\,(u(\bar x)\to\neg b(\bar x))$ for the old descriptor $u$ of the bound. Infinitely many distinct represented descriptor tuples would contradict this consequence using a fresh $b$, compactness and the separate unrestricted auxiliary root from that argument. This auxiliary root witnesses a consistent sentence for the component family; it can retain its unrestricted rows.

For a bound with finite descriptor support, the tester with full current row and old descriptor a finite union of tuple-haecceities is admitted. Weak inextensibility and persistence give the exact old images at every proper arrow, including all its postcomposites. There every weak subrelation of the old finite bound is a finite union of tuple-haecceities. Each lifts by choosing finitely many actual representatives and taking their finite union of tuple-haecceities at the root. These unions are supported by the union of the finitely many supports of their tuples. This is all that the power proof uses at the root. It yields Tame Rigidity and both local power schemata.

## Choice, Transversal and individual infinity

The current row selecting $A_i^0,A_i^1$ as the two permissible outputs for $L_i$, and the single output $L_0$ for every other input, is invariant. Pair it with a bottom descriptor. A purported functional subrelation has finite support. A swap at a fresh index fixes its label and interchanges the two outputs, contradicting uniqueness. Likewise the equivalence relation with blocks $\{A_i^0,A_i^1\}$ and singletons elsewhere has no supported transversal. This argument is the labelled-pair obstruction used in the [swappable-pairs model](../index.html#model/coalesced-swappable-pairs-root).

The map's narrower Transversal principle does hold. At each property type $\sigma t$, choose the property $(E,[\bot_{\sigma t}])$ for each admitted current extension $E$. The set of all these representatives is an invariant current row at type $(\sigma t)t$ and can itself be paired with bottom. It chooses exactly one property from each coextension class. The support restriction also admits the row consisting of the standard Frege numeral entities at $e$, since all numerals are fixed. This row contains zero and is successor-closed. Hence all root numbers at $e$ are standard. None counts the infinite current individual domain, so the Axiom of Infinity at $e$ holds.

The original Barcan counterexample at $t$ survives: the infinitely many inequivalent descriptors asserting the different finite individual cardinalities supply the fresh-predicate argument with pairwise distinct terms, while the unary ghost used there necessarily misses every root proposition.

## A missing supremum in the non-Fregean interval

Include, for each standard $n$, a component with two mutually accessible worlds, a common underlying set of $n+1$ individuals and the full constant-domain hierarchy. A relation has one row at each world, giving exactly four propositions. Interpret all individual names as the same chosen individual and all relational names as bottom. These are $T$-components. Every root descriptor restricts on this sequence of designated points to the trace of an ordinary pure sentence with a single individual marker. The replacement of original and auxiliary symbols is uniform across the sequence, so only countably many traces occur even for uncountable signatures. Choose $S\subseteq\mathbb N$ distinct from every such trace.

Let $a_n$ say that necessarily there are exactly four propositions and that there are exactly $n+1$ individuals. These are pairwise disjoint Boolean elements, and $a_n$ is true at its chosen component. The set of $a_n$ for $n\in S$ is an invariant current row since all these terms are pure. A proposed supremum $q$ would satisfy $a_n\le q$ for $n\in S$ and $a_n\le\neg q$ for $n\notin S$, where $\neg a_n$ is an upper bound in the second case. The designated points would therefore read exactly $S$ from the descriptor of $q$. This contradicts its choice. Since every $a_n$ entails failure of the Fregean Axiom, the missing supremum already occurs in that principal interval.

## Strong atoms and the missing finite Boolean interval

At any relational type the old descriptor Boolean algebra is atomless. If a closed descriptor $u$ is nonzero, some $T$-model instantiates it. Take a free propositional name $z$ absent from $u$. In that model assign $z$ first top and then bottom. All the ground ghost tests continue to hold: each altered tuple term is evaluated by substituting the chosen closed truth constant, which gives another tuple covered by the same scheme. Thus the old descriptors $u\land z$ and $u\land\neg z$, with conjunction taken pointwise, are both nonzero.

The root order at each relational type is the product of the supported current-row order and the old descriptor order. Every singleton argument tuple has finite support. Hence the root atoms are exactly the singleton current rows with bottom old trace. At the identity each such atom entails either a given entity or its complement; at every proper arrow the atom becomes bottom. Each atom is therefore strong.

At propositional type there is exactly one current-row atom, the true root-only proposition. Any principal interval with a nonzero old coordinate has infinitely many elements by atomlessness of the old factor. The remaining intervals have one or two elements. On four fixed individual labels take the four-element Boolean algebra and its order, both given by invariant current rows. This algebra is revised-small. For a nonempty subset $Y$ of size $k$, take an admitted $(k-1)$-element subset $A$. All subsets of $A$ are admitted, so a power code for $A$ needs at least $2^{k-1}\ge k$ elements; no carrier strictly smaller than $Y$ can supply it. The empty subset is not larger than the ordinal benchmark. The four-element algebra has no isomorphic principal propositional interval, refuting Mathematical Possibilism.
