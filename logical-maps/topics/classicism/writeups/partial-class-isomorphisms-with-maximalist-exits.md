# Partial class isomorphisms with Maximalist exits

One point satisfies signature Possibility+, full Barcan, the Axiom of Infinity at individuals, and revised Mathematical Possibilism at every carrier type. Transversal fails there already for properties of propositions. A companion point satisfies the first three principles and Atomlessness. The signature has one proposition constant. All verdicts below are at the designated point.

## The two models

Use ZFC. Let $K,w$ be the [coalesced, finitely pinned substitution model](coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root.html), with one public proposition constant $c$ and countably many auxiliary individual names. At $w$, signature Possibility+ and BF hold, and $I=D_e(w)$ is countably infinite. Fix a Cantor space $C=2^\omega$. For each $u\in C$ take a separate copy of $K,w$. The second family of exits, used only in the catalogue model, has one classical singleton-individual terminal for each point of a Stone space $X$. Write $F_\tau$ for its common finite full type domain.

At the new point $r$, use the coefficient monoid $M$ of [partial class isomorphisms](partial-class-isomorphisms-with-terminals.html). The auxiliary sites form $\kappa^+$ classes of size $\kappa^+$. A coefficient maps its live subset of each class bijectively onto a whole target class, under a permutation of classes. Its undefined inputs remain undefined under postcomposition. The units $G$ are the automorphisms of the equivalence relation. Any consistent prescription of at most $\kappa$ live and dead inputs extends to a coefficient. Every prescribed live partial class isomorphism of this size extends to a unit. All loops act identically on the actual individuals $I$. Every entrance absorbs the loops. At a $K$-entrance take the identity on $I$, followed by the original transports on further old arrows. At a classical terminal use the unique map on individuals.

For a relation $\tau=\bar\sigma t$, its data at $r$ are independent:

* an ordinary loop profile $f\mapsto R_f\subseteq D_{\bar\sigma}(r)$, left-equivariant under $G$, with one pin $P$ of size at most $\kappa$. Equality of partial maps on $P$ entails equality of the entire rows;
* a locally constant section $C\longrightarrow D_\tau(w)$, where whole old entities have the discrete topology;
* in the catalogue model, a locally constant section $X\longrightarrow F_\tau$.

Loop transport precomposes ordinary rows and fixes both sections. Evaluation of a section at an entrance determines all its further values by the target action. Interpret $c$ by its constant old section with value $c^w$, with empty ordinary and classical-terminal rows.

This recursion gives the typed action domains. An ordinary tuple transported by two coefficients agreeing on its pins has the same entire value, since their continuations agree on those pins as well. The live image of a pin pins the transported entity. Term formation at the core therefore uses the union of the parameter pins, and evaluation commutes with the target units. At a $K$-entrance, abstraction on the transported parameters takes its whole value in $K$; the familiar target calculation includes all bound arguments at that target. The parameter sections are constant on a common neighbourhood, so the resulting section of whole term values is locally constant, including for abstraction, partial application and quantification. The same calculation applies at $X$. Differing rows are distinguished at their indicated arrows by application to an appropriate tuple. This verifies the action-model term and identity clauses.

## Possibility+, Barcan and choice

For any Possibility+ instance, take its eligible distinct individual tuple at $r$ to one of the points $w$. It remains exactly that tuple and the interpretation of the public name agrees. The continuation supplied by Possibility+ at $w$ is also accessible from $r$. This argument gives both ordinary signature and pure Maximalism.

Each core coefficient $h$ is onto on entire typed domains. For a target relation with pin $T$, copy its extra sections unchanged. At the source coefficient $f$ use its target row for any $k$ satisfying $k(y)=f(h^{-1}y)$ for $y\in T$, including undefined values. Every target site has a unique live $h$-preimage. This prescription extends, and the result is equivariant and pinned by $h^{-1}[T]$. At $f=lh$ it returns the prescribed row at $l$. The individual case is the identity. The maps onto each $w$ and classical terminal are onto at every type, using constant sections. Now suppose a tester necessarily accepts all current arguments. Onto transport covers every core argument and every classical-terminal argument. At a given $w$, every argument has a source preimage and therefore satisfies the transported tester *necessarily at $w$*. BF at $w$ supplies universal acceptance at all its successors. This proves unboxed BF at $r$.

One further fact about current extensions will also be used below. If a set $E$ of tuples is invariant under the pointwise stabilizer of a pin $P$, put $gE$ at $f$ whenever $f$ agrees with a unit $g$ on $P$ and is live on all of $P$. Put the empty row at the other coefficients and take the extra sections bottom. This is an admitted property with current extension $E$. In particular all subsets of $I$ and $I^2$ are current extensions. For any collection of at most $\kappa$ source tuples, a common pin fixes each tuple. Every subset of the collection and every graph on such collections is therefore a current extension.

The same live-pin construction gives Inextensible Comprehension. At a coefficient live on $P$, the inverse partial-class correspondence can be matched by a unit on the support of any given target member and on $fP$. That unit takes the member back into $E$ and $f$ sends it back to the original member. Further transports either agree with a unit on $P$ or lose some member of $P$ permanently. All extra sections are bottom. These are the hereditary weak-inextensibility tests of the partial-class model. For an actual truth with pin $P$, the proposition that all of $P$ is live, bottom at every exit, witnesses its ND-necessity. Whenever the guard is still possible after a loop, the loop agrees on $P$ with a unit and the truth holds there.

Let $p_a$ report the liveness of site $a$, with both extra sections bottom. These distinct propositions are permuted like their sites. For each class $D$ choose $d\in D$. An admitted unary property has at $f$ the codes $p_a$ in the class of $f(d)$, with empty row if $d$ has died. Take both extra sections bottom. A putative Transversal with pin $P$ selects a unique representative $Y$ of the codes of a class $D$ disjoint from $P$. Its uniqueness makes it fixed by all units that fix $P$ and preserve $D$. Move a pin $S\supseteq P$ of $Y$ within classes to another pin $S'$, disjoint from $S$ outside $P$. A coefficient fixing $S$ and killing $S'\setminus P$ has $Y$-row exactly the codes of $D$. A unit fixing $P$ can exchange $D$ with a fresh class. Its postcomposition leaves the values on $S'$ unchanged, though equivariance exchanges the two different rows. This contradiction proves failure of Transversal. Relational Choice also fails: require a given site code to choose a *different* code of the same class and use equality on noncodes. This current relation is invariant and admitted. A transposition fixing an input in a fresh class and fixing the proposed selector's pin moves its selected classmate.

## The ordinal bound for the catalogue model

Write $E=F_1$ for the current extension of a weakly rigid $F$ with pin $N$. At every loop $h$ one has $F_h=h[E]$. Here it is important that the assertion only concerns ordinary loops. For each enlarged pin $N'\supseteq N$, the upper tester at $h$ takes the union of $t[E]$ over coefficients $t$ agreeing with $h$ on $N'$. Give it top at the extra exits. Weak inextensibility bounds $F_h$ by that union and persistence gives the reverse inclusion. For a given $h$, add to $N'$ a live $h$-input in each class meeting $N$. If $tz$ occurs in the union, prescribe $v=1$ on $N$ and $v(l)=h^{-1}(t(l))$ on the remainder of a pin of $z$, with undefined values preserved. The added inputs guarantee compatibility on classes meeting $N$, and injectivity prevents a collision with a fixed point of $N$. Extend to a coefficient. Then $vz\in E$ by persistence and $h(vz)=tz$ as a whole tuple; its extra sections are fixed by every loop.

Use the [inexact ordinal test and revised smallness](stone-terminal-maximalist.html). At ordinal type $\rho=(eet)(et)t$, let $Z$ consist of the entities with the following two properties. One function on $\mathcal P(I^2)\times\mathcal P(I)$ determines the ordinary row at *every* coefficient by the current extensions of the input order and field. The section on $X$ is constant. The section on $C$ may be any admitted section. Each such entity is fixed as a whole by every core loop. The collection $Z$ is invariant under $G$ and is admitted as a current test.

Zero has a uniform ordinary table because all current subsets of $I$ are available at the core, so well-orderhood is the usual extensional test. For successor, the strict initial segment below an individual and its restricted order are determined by the current order and field. With $I$ fixed, the same successor table works at every core coefficient. Uniform terminal input gives uniform output for both constructors.

For the limit constructor let $Y$ be rigid with all its current members in $Z$. Its row at every loop is exactly its current set of members by the image calculation and the loop-fixity of each member. The limit's well-order test and its applications of those same ordinal tables therefore give one extensional ordinary table. Let $S\subseteq F_\rho$ consist of the constant classical-terminal values of all current members. Give a tester top on the core and on $C$, and constant terminal value $S$ on $X$. Every current member necessarily satisfies it. Weak inextensibility bounds each terminal row of $Y$ by $S$, and persistence supplies every element of $S$ there. Hence the terminal limit uses exactly the same member set at every $x\in X$. This proves closure of $Z$ under rigid limits. The actual inexact ordinals $H$ lie in $Z$ by their impredicative definition.

In particular

$$
 |H|\ \leq\ |Z|\ \leq\
 2^{|\mathcal P(I^2)\times\mathcal P(I)|}
 \cdot |D_\rho(w)|^{|2^\omega|}\cdot |F_\rho|.
$$

Each bound is fixed before choosing $X$ or $\kappa$. Choose a strong limit $\mu$ above it and above $\aleph_0$, then choose $\kappa\geq\mu$. Let $L$ be the product of a set of representatives of the nontrivial complete Boolean algebras of size $<\mu$, and $X=\operatorname{Stone}(L)$.

## Revised smallness and representation

For a current carrier of external size at least $\mu$, take a subset $Y$ of size $\mu$. All comparison graphs on $H$ and $Y$ are available by the common-pin result. Thus $H$ is strictly smaller than $Y$ in the native sense. Each native strictly smaller $A\subseteq Y$ has external size $<\mu$, since a bijection otherwise exists and would be an admitted graph. Take $C_A\subseteq Y$ of size $2^{|A|}<\mu$, in external bijection with $\mathcal P(A)$. The membership graph between $A$ and $C_A$ is admitted, as are all subsets of $A$. It represents each subextension exactly once, independent of the intension presenting it. This makes $Y$ power-closed, and the larger carrier fails revised smallness.

Consequently a revised-small current CBA has size $<\mu$. Every subfamily is admitted by the common-pin result, so its order is externally complete. The root proposition algebra has the ordinary scalar factor, the locally constant scalar sections on $C$ ordered pointwise by entailment in $K,w$, and $\operatorname{Clop}(X)\cong L$, independently. The element zero on the first two factors and supported on the unit of the chosen catalogue factor in $L$ has exactly the required principal interval. Bottom represents the one-element algebra. The graph of the isomorphism between the actual carrier and this interval is admitted because it has size $<\mu$. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism at every carrier type.

## Individual infinity, countable joins and atoms

The current extension consisting of the standard Frege numerals at individual type is admitted. It is an inductive set of whole entities, as it contains zero and is closed under successor. Native induction puts all current numbers in this extension, and each standard numeral belongs to it. All finite subsets of $I$ are admitted, so the usual removal and singleton-adjunction equations give each standard numeral its ordinary finite counting extension. None counts the entire $I$. Thus the actual Axiom of Infinity at $e$ holds in both models.

Both points fail countable Boolean completeness already for propositions. Set $U_j=[0^j1]\subseteq C$. Let $p_n$ have old section top on $U_{2n}$, bottom elsewhere, and every other coordinate bottom. The family and the graph assigning the $n$-th individual Frege numeral to $p_n$ are admitted. A join must have value top on every $U_{2n}$. It must have value bottom on every $U_{2n+1}$, since the complement of the proposition supported on that cylinder is an upper bound. Its old section could not then be locally constant at the all-zero sequence. The same family witnesses failure of propositional Boolean completeness.

It also has no weakly rigid coextension. Persistence would put the old top proposition in the coextension's row throughout each even cylinder. On any one odd cylinder take the old property of being identical to bottom, with top everywhere else and at every other sector. Every actual $p_n$ necessarily satisfies this tester, so weak inextensibility excludes top from the entire odd cylinder. Local constancy again fails. This gives a unary-propositional failure of Weak Rigid Comprehension.

No true proposition at the core is an atom. Its occupied core row splits by liveness of a fresh site outside its pin. In the companion without $X$, every nonzero scalar in the old sector splits as well: pick a clopen neighbourhood on which its whole nonzero value is constant, and divide it into two nonempty clopen pieces. The same splitting applies to occupied old relations of every arity. Thus Atomlessness holds at the companion root. In the catalogue variant, a classical-terminal bit belonging to an isolated Stone point is an atom.
