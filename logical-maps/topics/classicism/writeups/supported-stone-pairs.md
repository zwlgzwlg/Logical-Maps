# Mathematical Possibilism and both rigid powers without Relational Choice

An outer Stone-terminal root preserves the signature Possibility+, rigid powers and failures of choice of the [supported-pairs point](supported-pairs-descriptor-powers.html), while realizing revised Mathematical Possibilism. The representing factor must accommodate Boolean algebras that are complete for supported families. The construction works in ZFC.

## The Boolean factor

Let $G=\prod_{i\in\mathbb N} C_2$ act by independently swapping the options in each pair of the old point $K$. Write $H_F=\{g:g|_F=0\}$ for finite $F\subseteq\mathbb N$. A set or element has finite support when some $H_F$ fixes it. The old individual domain $J$, old point and all its carriers are chosen first.

Choose a strong limit cardinal $\mu$ of uncountable cofinality exceeding the bound in the ordinal calculation below. For example iterate powersets above that bound for $\omega_1$ stages and take the limit. Form the following set of presentations: for each finite $F$ and nonzero cardinal $\lambda<\mu$, take all nontrivial Boolean algebras on $\lambda$ with an action of $H_F$ by automorphisms such that every element has finite support and every finitely supported subset has a supremum. For an $H_F$-action, finite support means being fixed by some $H_{F\cup E}$ with $E$ finite. These conditions are conditions on ordinary sets and functions in ZFC.

Write $C_F=(C_2)^F$, so $G=C_F\times H_F$. For each presentation $A$ include the algebra $K_A=A^{C_F}$ with its $G$-action: $C_F$ translates coordinate indices and $H_F$ acts on their values. The product is finite. Every element is finitely supported. A supported family has a coordinatewise supremum, since each projected family is supported for the $H_F$-action on $A$. The factor at the identity of $C_F$ is a principal interval isomorphic to $A$ by an isomorphism equivariant under $H_F$.

Take $L$ to consist of the finitely supported elements in the product of all these $K_A$, with the action in each coordinate just defined. Its finite Boolean operations are those of the product. For any finitely supported family in $L$, each projection is a supported family, and the vector of coordinate suprema is fixed by every group element fixing the family. This vector belongs to $L$ and is its supremum. For each $A$ the unit concentrated on its identity coordinate, with all other components zero, has precisely $A$ as its lower interval.

Use the **external** Stone space $X=\operatorname{Ult}(L)$. The group acts on ultrafilters by direct image. Every clopen is $\hat l$ for a finitely supported $l\in L$. For any supported family $S\subseteq L$ the closure of $\bigcup_{l\in S}\hat l$ is $\widehat{\bigvee S}$. Indeed a nonempty clopen below this upper bound and disjoint from every member would contradict leastness. This restricted closure fact supplies everything required below. Individual ultrafilters need not have finite support.

## The action model

Adjoin a root with its identity as sole loop, one entrance to $K$, and one entrance to a full one-world, single-individual hierarchy for each $x\in X$. The old entrance is the identity on individuals $J$; every terminal entrance maps individuals to its one individual. All proper arrows include their old postcomposites. At a root relational type take three independent coordinates:

- any finitely supported current tuple extension;
- any whole entity at $K$;
- any locally constant map $X\to D^{\rm leaf}_\tau$.

Each fixed terminal type is finite. A section into it therefore has finitely many clopen fibres, whose union of supports supports the whole section. The group action on old values is inherited from $K$, and on the terminal family it permutes the terminal objects. Thus all root entities have finite support. Give the public names their old coordinates, invariant current rows and homogeneous terminal values. Term closure at the current identity follows by equivariance. At the old entrance use full old evaluation. At a terminal all parameters are constant on a common clopen neighbourhood, so the whole resulting section is locally constant even for abstraction and quantification. The construction is a C action model. The entrance preserving individuals transfers signature Possibility+ by composition.

## The ordinal bound and revised smallness

Let $\rho=(eet)(et)t$ be the type of the inexact ordinal entities and $H$ the root extension of the inductively generated ordinal property. Consider the invariant current test for $\rho$-entities whose current table depends only on the current extensions of the order and field, and whose terminal section is constant. The zero ordinal has this property. The formula for current well-orderhood uses the applications of the order and field and quantifies over the same available subsets for any coextensive inputs. It therefore depends only on their current extensions even when these subsets are restricted to supported rows. Restriction to a strict initial segment likewise depends extensionally on the original order, field and cut point. Successor preserves the test. If a rigid family has current members passing the test, collect their constant terminal values in $S\subseteq D^{\rm leaf}_\rho$. The tester constantly $S$ at all leaves and top elsewhere necessarily accepts every current member. By weak inextensibility and persistence the terminal value of the family is exactly $S$ everywhere. The limit formula checks current well-orderhood and the current members' extensional tables, and its value at every terminal is the same. Induction bounds $H$ by this test, as in the [Stone ordinal calculation](stone-terminal-maximalist.html). Consequently
$$
 |H|\le 2^{|\mathcal P(J^2)\times\mathcal P(J)|}\cdot |D_\rho(K)|\cdot |D^{\rm leaf}_\rho|.
$$
All factors were fixed before choosing $\mu$.

Every member of $H$ is also fixed by all of $G$. The collection of globally fixed ordinal-type entities is an invariant current row. Zero is fixed and successor sends a fixed input to a fixed output. If $Y$ is rigid and each current member is fixed, $gY$ and $Y$ are weakly rigid and currently coextensive for every $g$. Two such relations are equal: by weak persistence the second necessarily contains every current member of the first, so weak inextensibility entails inclusion, and conversely. Thus $Y$ is fixed, and so is its limit. Ordinal induction gives the assertion. This fixedness matters because the injection in the smallness definition must be an available graph.

Now take a current carrier $B$ of external size at least $\mu$. Its elements each have one of countably many finite supports. Since $\operatorname{cf}(\mu)>\omega$, there is a finite $F$ fixing every element of some $Y\subseteq B$ of size $\mu$ individually. Every subset of $Y$ and every graph on its finite powers is an available current row. Since $H$ is fixed pointwise and has size below $\mu$, an external injection from $H$ to $Y$ has supported graph and there is no injection the other way. Thus $H<Y$ in the required internal sense. For a subcollection $A<Y$ its cardinality $\kappa$ is externally below $\mu$: any external bijection of these pointwise-fixed sets would be supported. Choose $2^\kappa<\mu$ members of $Y$ and code all the subsets of $A$ by an incidence graph on them. The graph and the code set are supported. This supplies PowerClosed$(Y)$ for the native arbitrary-subcollection definition. Hence every revised-small $B$ has cardinality below $\mu$.

For such a small current CBA, choose a finite $F$ fixing its carrier and order. Every subset of its extension with finite $H_F$-support is an admitted current predicate, so its external order has a supremum for every such subset and is a Boolean algebra. Transport the carrier and its $H_F$ action by a bijection to its cardinal. The resulting presentation occurs in the construction of $L$; the isomorphism back to the original carrier is $H_F$-equivariant by construction. The root proposition algebra has order $\mathbf2\times D_t(K)\times L$. With zero in the current and old coordinates, the unit of the chosen presentation's identity coordinate therefore realizes exactly the desired interval. The graph of the isomorphism is supported by $F$ and is an admitted current relation. The trivial Boolean algebra is realized below bottom. This verifies Mathematical Possibilism at every carrier type.

## Rigidity, powers and choice

For a weakly rigid outer bound $E$ with current tuple set $U$, its old coordinate $f$ is weakly rigid at $K$ and $f$ has exactly the projected extension $\pi(U)$. Indeed this image has finite support, so the old tester with precisely that current row and top old trace is available at $K$. In the outer model use this old tester with top elsewhere. Weak persistence of $E$ gives $\pi(U)\subseteq f_{\rm cur}$ and weak inextensibility gives the reverse. For any old tester necessary on $f_{\rm cur}$, give an outer tester that old value and top elsewhere; the original weak inextensibility forces $f$ below it. Weak persistence survives to the old entrance because a weakly rigid bound is necessarily weakly persistent (apply inextensibility to the test $a\mapsto\Box E(a)$). At $K$ weakly rigid bounds are fully rigid, with both powers fully rigid. All the leaves are classical. Hence $E$ is fully rigid.

Given a rigid old subrelation $z\le f$, take $U'=U\cap\pi^{-1}(z_{\rm cur})$. Its image is $z_{\rm cur}$ and this row is supported. For each of the finitely many leaf tuple values $v$, let $O_v$ be the union of the clopens where a member of $U'$ takes value $v$. Their family has finite support, since $U'$ does and the homogeneous terminal tuple $v$ is fixed. Its closure is therefore clopen by the supremum property of $L$. Give the lift current row $U'$, old coordinate $z$ and leaf membership for $v$ on $\overline{O_v}$. This is an admitted relation below $E$. Every current member persists. Any tester necessary on the current members covers $z$ on the old side by weak inextensibility and covers each closed leaf value since its corresponding clopen contains $O_v$. Thus the lift is weakly rigid and hence fully rigid.

For completeness this also tests all subrelations appearing only at a terminal. For $U$ itself the membership region of $v$ at the leaves is the closure of its current-image union: persistence gives one inclusion and the supported tester using those clopen closures in each of the finitely many tuple coordinates gives the reverse by inextensibility. If an outer tester necessary on all current rigid subrelations were false of a terminal subrelation, take a nonempty clopen neighbourhood on which that subrelation, the bound and the tester values are constant. Every tuple of the subrelation then has a dense open realization by a current $U$-member in that neighbourhood. Intersect these finitely many open dense sets, choose one realization for each tuple at a common leaf, and take the finite union of their root tuple-haecceities. It is rigid and below $E$ but fails the tester at that leaf. This contradiction, and the rigid lifts at $K$ just proved, give weak inextensibility for the power. The two power predicates agree throughout: at any further old point the finite-descriptor bound is a finite union of transported tuple-haecceities, so weak and full rigidity coincide for its subrelations. At $K$ its common power is fully rigid, as it is at the terminals. It follows that both local powers are fully rigid at the outer root.
