# A Boolean-valued collapse which adds no reals

First-order Analytic Necessitism is consistent with a contingent continuum hypothesis. The same model satisfies necessary Countable Boolean Completeness at every relational type, but full propositional Boolean Completeness fails to be necessary. The construction uses the bare countably closed collapse in the Boolean-valued model of Andrew Bacon. The [earlier CH-reversing example](full-boolean-valued-atom-and-atomless.html) added a Cohen real as well.

Work in a ground universe $V$ satisfying ZFC and $\neg\mathrm{CH}$, and put $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$. The consistency strength here is relative to ZFC, since a preliminary Cohen extension can provide such a ground. Take $P$ to be the countable partial functions from $\omega_1$ to $\mathfrak c$, ordered by extension, and put $A=\mathrm{RO}(P)$, $B=\mathbf 2\times A$. The union of a descending countable sequence of conditions is a condition. Deciding one term of a given countable sequence of ground elements at each step, then taking the union, shows that $P$ adds no countable sequence of elements of any ground set. In particular the forcing preserves $\omega_1$ and adds no reals. Density makes the generic function a surjection from $\omega_1$ onto the old continuum, so the extension satisfies CH. Conditions assigning a value at one fixed coordinate form a ground antichain of size $\mathfrak c$. Every condition splits below itself.

Use Bacon's full Boolean-valued interpretation over $B$, with one individual, $D_t=B$, and full ground function spaces at relational types. Identity of entities has Boolean value zero or one according to ground identity. Universal and existential quantification take the meet and join over the ground typed domain. Designated truth is projection to $\mathbf 2$; $\Box p$ is the crisp test $p=1_B$. This is the same interpretation validated in the map's [full Boolean-valued model](full-boolean-valued-atom-and-atomless.html). The forcing-coordinate reading used below concerns external ground carriers with relations applied to their tuples. Full Boolean-valued functions give all names for subsets of each fixed ground tuple set, by assigning to each tuple its Boolean membership value. Identity on the carrier itself remains crisp. In particular this calculation applies to the native real-structure and CH formulas. [Bacon, online appendix §B.2](https://andrew-bacon.github.io/papers/Could%20mathematics%20have%20been%20different.pdf)

## A necessary rigid real number structure

At every relational type $\tau$ the constant functions on all arguments with value $b\in B$ give an injection $B\hookrightarrow D_\tau$, including the identity injection at $t$. The antichain above allows us to choose disjoint ground labels for $\omega$ and $\mathcal P(\omega)$ in $D_\tau$. Give the number and real carriers and the successor and membership graphs their crisp characteristic functions. These are full ground profiles. Members and distinctness persist. If a tester necessarily covers every member of a crisp ground relation, it takes value $1_B$ on each member, so its universal coverage has value $1_B$. This is weak inextensibility over every tuple. More explicitly, at any Boolean value the antecedent testing necessary coverage of the crisp members is itself zero or one, and so is its boxed conclusion. This verifies boxed persistence and boxed inextensibility, hence rigidity, for all the indicated relations.

The resulting structure is the full ordinary number-and-set structure at the designated coordinate. At the forcing coordinate its available sets of numbers are again exactly the standard ground reals because the forcing adds none. Although an individual name for a subset may choose different old reals on different conditions, the Boolean join expressing that some old real represents it is top. The other real-structure clauses hold there as well. Thus this rigid structure is necessarily a real number structure. At individual type a one-element carrier cannot support the required Peano system in either coordinate. This verifies every instance of Analytic Necessitism.

At $t$ the same labelled real structure satisfies $\neg\mathrm{CH}$ in the ground coordinate and $\mathrm{CH}$ in the forcing coordinate. All relations of the required finite arities on its reals are available in each interpretation. Thus neither universal verdict on CH is necessary. By the map's implications from Second-Order Analytic Necessitism to the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis and then to the Non-contingency of CH, both stronger principles fail. The choice of the ground, as well as omission of the extra Cohen real, matters to this example.

## Which Boolean joins are necessary

At each relational type the native entailment order is the crisp ground pointwise Boolean order: the box in the definition tests whether the pointwise implication everywhere has value $1_B$. Every ground family has a pointwise supremum and infimum. At any counting sort the crisp property of the set of all standard Frege numeral denotations is admitted and inductive, so every entity counted as a number lies in that countable ground set. Some denotations can coincide at small types. At the forcing coordinate any family admitted as countable therefore gives a countable subset of the fixed ground carrier. No new countable sequences of its elements are added, so every such subset belongs to the ground and has its ground pointwise join and meet. The Boolean existential quantifier permits witnesses to vary by condition. At the designated coordinate arbitrary families already have their ground bounds. This proves necessary Countable Boolean Completeness, including all counting sorts and relational output types.

For full Boolean Completeness, take a ground family of pairwise disjoint $d_i>0$ in the $A$ factor of $B$ indexed by $i<\omega_1$. Code the generic surjection into ground reals as a subset $S$ of $\omega_1$ using a ground bijection $\omega_1\times\omega\cong\omega_1$. No condition can force $S$ to equal a particular ground set, since a ground decoding would already give the forbidden ground surjection. The name for $\{\neg d_i:i\in S\}$ is represented in the forcing coordinate by an admitted property of propositions. If a ground $y\in B$ were its greatest lower bound in the ground Boolean order, then
$$
 i\notin S \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad d_i\le y .
$$
Indeed for $i\notin S$, $d_i$ itself is a lower bound of all the indicated complements. For $i\in S$ one has $y\le\neg d_i$ while $d_i>0$. Thus even a condition forcing a particular $y$ to be the greatest lower bound would make $S$ a specified ground subset. Every such condition is zero. The forced existential fails, so local propositional Boolean Completeness at the designated atom is not necessary.

## Other ground-coordinate properties

The projection at the designated atom preserves arbitrary joins and meets. For any current row the crisp predicate with that row gives a rigid coextension. Every ground relation or choice graph is available, and a function with relational output is an admitted entity, so Functional Choice holds. Crisp identity and full constant domains give boxed ND and all Barcan instances. For a necessarily inhabited property, well-order its domain and trim each Boolean value by all earlier values. The resulting pairwise disjoint family has the same join and gives a necessarily singleton choice. A well-order of each ground carrier also remains a well-order in the forcing extension. The sole atom of $B$ witnesses Strong Actuality. The $A$ factor is atomless, and every nontrivial interval meeting it is infinite. Hence no interval has four elements, although the finite four-element Boolean algebra is small. Revised Mathematical Possibilism fails at the atom.
