# All truths are 5-necessary while some truths fail two-hat necessity

Restrict the arrows of the [integer-stage model](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html). Each ordinary pair must either be fixed pointwise or be sent to a single point. The source singleton of a coordinate, when present, may be sent to any point of that coordinate at the target. A composite still has this form: an ordinary pair that ever collapses remains collapsed, and a pair that never collapses is fixed pointwise. This restriction makes every truth 5-necessary, while preserving both strict modal separations.

In detail, at stage $n\in\mathbb Z$ the labelled coordinate $i\le n$ has $\omega_1$ ordinary pairs and one singleton, and coordinate $i>n$ has the same ordinary pairs alone. Use the disjoint union as the individual domain. Each arrow consists of the restricted coordinate maps just specified and a word in a free monoid on countably many letters, with composition $(h,v)(g,w)=(hg,vw)$. At a relational type a profile has a row of tuples of the target types at every arrow. A countable pin of source individuals determines all its rows between arrows with the same target and word. Transport precomposes profiles. A common pin for the parameters of an abstraction pins the table obtained by evaluating its matrix at the target on the transported parameters, including quantification over the full target domains. This gives the same term-closure and C verification as the integer-stage model.

## The stage predicate

Fix $n$. Let $P$ hold on every arrow from $n$ to $m\le n$. It says that coordinate $n+1$ still lacks a singleton. We include the modal proof to check the restricted arrows. For
$$
\hat{\hat\Box}B\leftrightarrow\ \exists q\,(q\land\Box(\Diamond_{\ne}q\to B)),
$$
the test $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ at an arrow $h$ requires for every true target proposition $r$ continuations $v,x$ such that $q$ holds at $vh$ and $r$ holds at $xv$.

At the inclusion $u:n\to n+1$, $P$ is false. Given any true $q$ at $n$ and true $r$ at $n+1$, return by $v$ which sends the new singleton to a fresh pair outside a pin of $r$, fixing ordinary sites. Continue by $x$ which collapses that pair onto the singleton and fixes the other ordinary sites. All other coordinates and words are identities. Then $vu=1_n$ and $xv$ fixes the pin of $r$ (including the singleton if it is in the pin). Hence $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ at $u$, proving $\neg\hat{\hat\Box}P$.

For $\Box_5P$, define the empty-pinned property $Y$ with full row when $m\le n$, and at $m>n$ containing exactly the propositions with a pin omitting the coordinate-$n+1$ singleton. The latter row omits the test that a target endomorphism fixes that singleton: moving the singleton while fixing all ordinary sites separates it from the identity and witnesses failure of every pin omitting it. Thus $\forall z\,Yz=P$. For any $z$ with pin $S$, use a true guard on the empty-word root endomorphisms fixing $S$ and the partners of its ordinary coordinate-$n+1$ sites. If the guard is ND-possible at $h$, a return can fix this enlarged pin, so $h$ is injective there. No coordinate-$n+1$ member of $S$ can be sent to the singleton, since its partner would have to go there too. The image of $S$ therefore pins $h\cdot z$ and gives $Yz$. This proves two-hat necessity and hence 5-necessity of each $Yz$ at $n$. The C theorem giving Barcan for $\Box_5$ gives $\Box_5\forall z\,Yz$.

Let $T$ hold on arrows to $m\ge n$, i.e. where coordinate $n$ still has a singleton. Write $a$ for that singleton at $n$. Use the guard of empty-word root endomorphisms fixing $a$. If $m<n$, $h(a)=b$ has a mate $b'$. Test the identity at $m$ by requiring an endomorphism to fix $b,b'$. A return $v$ with $vh(a)=a$ must collapse $b,b'$ onto $a$, after which no $xv$ passes the test. Thus $\hat{\hat\Box}T$. Yet for any true ND guard $r$ one may send $a$ into a pair outside its pin at stage $n-1$, then collapse that pair onto $a$ on return. The composite fixes the entire pin of $r$ while $T$ fails at the first step. Thus $\neg\Box_{\ne}T$.

Since both $P$ and $T$ are 5-necessary and $\Box_5$ satisfies K, their conjunction $D$, the proposition that the target equals the source stage $n$, is 5-necessary.

## Every truth is 5-necessary

Let $p$ be any truth at $n$. Enlarge its countable pin $S$ by the partners of every ordinary site in it, and let $q$ be the proposition consisting of the empty-word endomorphisms of $n$ fixing this enlarged set pointwise. Suppose $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ holds at $h:n\to n$; arrows with another target will automatically satisfy $D\to p$. Some return $v$ has $vh$ in $q$. The word of $h$ is empty and $h$ is injective on each of these ordinary pairs. An uncollapsed ordinary pair is fixed pointwise by the arrow rule. Thus $h$ fixes the ordinary part of $S$.

For each singleton $a$ in $S$, suppose $h(a)\ne a$. Its image must be an ordinary $b$ in that same coordinate. Use a true target test requiring an endomorphism to fix $b$ and its mate. Every $v$ with $vh$ in $q$ must send $b$ to $a$ and collapse the pair, so $xv$ cannot fix it pointwise for any continuation $x$. This contradicts $\Diamond_{\ne}q$. Therefore $h$ fixes the singleton part of $S$ as well. Its word, target and values on $S$ coincide with the identity's, so $p$ holds at $h$.

We have proved $\hat{\hat\Box}(D\to p)$, hence $\Box_5(D\to p)$. With $\Box_5D$ this gives $\Box_5p$. The proof applies at every stage to every truth there. Consequently every truth is necessarily 5-necessary in the sense $\Box\forall p\,(p\to\Box_5p)$, while 5-necessities can fail to be two-hat necessary at each stage.

## Other verifications and the two interpretations of the name

Translation of all stages and coordinate labels by one preserves the arrow restriction and the admitted profile domains. The pure reduct therefore has the same theory at all stages. One interpretation gives a single propositional name its value top everywhere. Every closed sentence of that signature is then equivalent here to its pure substitution instance with top, giving No Signature Contingency. Alternatively use the [last-letter interpretation](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html) in the very same pure model. Choose a root witness for each realizable pure one-variable formula, give each its own letter, and at a word ending in that letter let the name read the witness's profile with the final letter removed. The union of the witnesses' countably many countable pins pins the name. The loop labelled by a witness's letter therefore realizes the corresponding assignment, and pure homogeneity supplies the converse direction. In this interpretation Logical Necessity holds for the signature with one propositional name. Use the rooted unfolding for a global interpretation of the glued name.

Every current row is unrestricted. The argument for countable Boolean Completeness in the integer-stage model still works: internally countable current families are externally countable since the standard numeral denotations form an available inductive test at every counting type, and a countable union of countable pins pins the pointwise join. The same argument applies at every stage. Arbitrary target-specific well-orders with constant-by-target rows witness Modal Well-Ordering.

At each stage a nonzero proposition contains some arrow $h$. Outside a countable pin of that proposition choose an ordinary pair and one of its members $b$. Other arrows with the same word, target and pin values can send the pair to different one-point images (collapse both members to either of two distinct ordinary points). The test that $b$ has its given $h$-image splits the proposition. Thus the algebra is atomless at every stage. The individual domain is uncountable and current subsets unrestricted, giving the Axiom of Infinity at $e$ at every stage.

In the two models just described the ordinary Barcan Formula holds at all types at every stage. The lifting argument of the [integer-stage model](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html) uses only the permitted arrows. Given an ordinary arrow $(h,w)$, a pin $S$ for a tester, and a countable pin $T$ for a target argument $z$, preserve $h$ on the blocks meeting $S$ and collapse fresh source pairs in each coordinate to the respective members of $T$ to obtain $j$. Choose a countable $T'$ mapped onto $T$ by $j$. A root argument on a cylinder $(l,vw)$ with $l|_{T'}=(kj)|_{T'}$ can reproduce the target row of $z$ at $(k,v)$; use the empty row off these cylinders. Right cancellation fixes $v$ and any two such $k$ agree on $T$. Thus $(j,w)$ transports that argument to $z$ while giving the same tester row as $(h,w)$. For an individual, collapse a fresh source pair onto the desired point. This proves the ordinary Barcan formula.

Full Boolean Completeness at $t$ fails in these models and in the variants below. Use the uncountable family of the [integer-stage construction](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html): $p_i$ requires an ordinary empty-word root endomorphism sending a fixed site $d$ to a distinguished $c_i$ and a fresh site $a_i$ to a fixed $b$. The $c_i$ are distinct and the pairs of $a_i$ are distinct and disjoint from the pair of $d$. All prescriptions on these two source pairs can be performed by collapsing each pair to the requisite point. Given a pin of a proposed join, choose an $a_i$-pair missing it. Two arrows agreeing on the pin and both sending $d$ to $c_i$ disagree over whether $a_i$ goes to $b$. The first must belong to the join; the second lies in a definable cylinder disjoint from every $p_j$, whose complement is another upper bound. This contradicts the pinning condition.

## Variants with a zero coefficient

Take the same ordinary coefficient maps and add a zero coefficient from each stage to each target, absorbing multiplication with any coefficient. Decorate zeros with the same freely composed word as the ordinary arrows. Use a singleton individual domain at every stage and take the paired sets as external sites. Rows on zero coefficients are independent for every word and target, with the same countable pin requirement on ordinary rows as before. A zero transports every profile to an empty-pinned profile. For an ordinary arrow the original transport proof still applies, since zero continuations absorb the source maps. This is the [paired stages with zero arrows](paired-stages-with-zero-arrows.html) with the additional restriction on uncollapsed pairs.

Give $P$ and $T$ truth at every zero, and give $Y$ its full zero rows. All old true guards in the two games use only ordinary empty-word endomorphisms. Hence a zero never has a return to one of them. The same proofs give the two strict separations and 5-necessity of $D=P\land T$. In the proof for a general $p$, any zero arrow automatically fails $\Diamond_{\ne}q$, even if $p$ is false there. Thus $\hat{\hat\Box}(D\to p)$ still holds and all truths still are 5-necessary everywhere.

Each isolated zero address supports a propositional atom, so finite sets of such addresses realize all finite Boolean algebras as principal intervals. Over the singleton individual domain, consider the finite set of ordinals represented by the same extensional order-and-field table at every arrow and stage. The set of all entities with that property is finite and available as a current tester. Zero and successor preserve it by current fullness. A rigid family whose present members belong to the set is the finite disjunction of their haecceities, by persistence and inextensibility, so its limit tests the same finite list of tables at every arrow. This bounds the inexact ordinal benchmark by a finite number. Full current graphs then make any countably infinite set power-closed and strictly larger than the benchmark; consequently every revised-small CBA is finite. Thus Mathematical Possibilism holds. On any one zero address a tester can admit exactly the image of all present propositions, with unrestricted full rows elsewhere. Every such image is empty-pinned on ordinary continuations, whereas a target proposition can distinguish endomorphisms by the image of one ordinary site. This refutes ordinary Barcan at $t$. The ordinary-only sector has a nonzero atomless interval by varying a fresh pair, refuting Atomicity at $t$.

The target-label codes of [the zero-arrow model](paired-stages-with-zero-arrows.html) also work unchanged. At every relational type and for each subset of the integers, use a profile full precisely when the target stage belongs to that subset. All stages are reachable, so transport carries those codes bijectively to the corresponding codes at the target. For any relation on tuples of codes the target-specific row containing just the corresponding tuples is admitted and rigid by exact images. The same holds for the target-specific power families of all such relations. Use continuum many codes for the numbers and reals of the ordinary full real structure; those powers realize every restriction of a local relation to the structure at every finite arity. Thus Second-Order Analytic Necessitism holds (there is no real structure at $e$). Full current subsets and relations also give ordinary second-order analytical truth in every real structure at every stage. Countable Boolean Completeness and Modal Well-Ordering retain their proofs above. Stage translation preserves zeros. Either signature interpretation above therefore also gives its respective signature principle here.
