Fix one coordinate at stage zero and distinct ordinary doublets $D_\alpha$ indexed by $\alpha<\omega_1$. Define an old scalar $p_\alpha$ on the empty-word endomorphisms of stage zero by requiring that the map identify the two members of $D_\alpha$, while it distinguishes both members of every earlier $D_\beta$. At other addresses set it false. The countably many sites in the doublets through $\alpha$ pin this scalar, and target permutations preserve the test. These scalars are pairwise disjoint. Each is nonzero, since an endomorphism can collapse just its designated doublet and act identically on all other sites.

Partition $\omega_1$ into two cofinal sets $A$ and $B$, for instance by the parity of the finite remainder following a limit ordinal or zero. Lift each $p_\alpha$ to the root with empty current bit. The family of lifts with indices in $A$ is an admitted current family since the root has arbitrary current rows. A least upper bound would have an old scalar coordinate containing every $p_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in A$ and disjoint from every $p_\beta$ for $\beta\in B$, by comparison with the complements of their lifts. Take a countable pin for that old coordinate. Beyond the supremum of the indices of doublets meeting the pin, choose $\alpha\in A$ and $\beta\in B$. The two empty-word endomorphisms that collapse just $D_\alpha$ or just $D_\beta$ agree on the pin. The purported bound must be true at the first and false at the second. This proves the scalar failure at the root.
