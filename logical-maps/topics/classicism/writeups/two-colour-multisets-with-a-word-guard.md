# Strong Atomicity and Logical Necessity without propositional Barcan

For every set-sized free signature, there is a point satisfying Logical Necessity, necessary Strong Atomicity at every relational type and revised Mathematical Possibilism where the propositional Barcan formula fails. The failure concerns one specific proposition that becomes available after a single letter.

## Arrows, observations and terms

There is one object and one individual. Partition a set of labels into two uncountable sets $J$ and $I\setminus J$. An arrow $(s,w)$ consists of a finite multiset $s$ on $I$ and a word $w$ in a free monoid with a distinguished letter $h$. Composition adds multisets and concatenates words, with $vu$ meaning that $v$ follows $u$. For a countable set of labels $P$, the observations of an arrow are its word, its total multiplicity $|s|$, and the multiplicities $s|P$. If the word contains no $h$, include one more observation, the total multiplicity of labels in $J$. A relation at any type has a row of current tuples at each arrow. It is admitted when one countable $P$ makes the rows equal at arrows with the same observations. This modifies the [finite-multiset word model](finite-multisets-free-words.html).

Precomposing by a fixed arrow preserves agreement: multiplicities add, and the additional colour count at the composite is needed only if both words omit $h$. Thus restriction by any suffix transports an admitted profile to an admitted profile on the same pin. Postcomposition also preserves agreement by the same calculation. Consequently arrows agreeing on a parameter's observations transport its *entire* profile to the same entity, since all corresponding later rows coincide. Apply this to the finite list of parameters in a term. Evaluation of the body on each current tuple then gives equal rows at the compared arrows, including when the body quantifies over the full admitted domains. This proves closure under abstraction, application and quantifiers and the required compatibility with transport. The row at the identity is unrestricted. Distinct profiles differ on some arrow and tuple, giving intensional identity in the action model.

To interpret a signature, use one non-$h$ letter for each named instance of a realized pure matrix. For each pure matrix, type tuple and pattern of repeated names, choose a tuple of witnesses once. For each participating relational constant prescribe its entire chosen witness on the suffix of that instance. Appending the letter preserves exactly the condition that $h$ is absent; thus the witness's pin still compares the required rows. At unused letters take empty rows. Only countably many distinct matrix, type and repetition patterns occur, so the pins for any one constant have countable union even for an uncountable signature. Individual names all have the sole available value. The pure theory is the same at every arrow and each required instance obtains a named witness on its own suffix. This is Logical Necessity.

## The missing proposition

A single arrow can be isolated by its word, total multiplicity and the finitely many labels of its multiset. Agreement on these labels and on total multiplicity leaves no room for an additional label. Thus an occupied tuple at any arrow supplies an admitted subrelation supported at exactly that arrow and tuple. It is an atom. Since the monoid is right cancellative, its transport has just a single occupied arrow and tuple or has no occupied row. This proves necessary Strong Atomicity at all types. The identity-only proposition is a true strong atom. It is impossible after every proper arrow since the only left-invertible arrow is the identity. It therefore also witnesses distinctness-preserving necessity for every current truth.

Write $d=(0,h)$. Let $p$ hold precisely when the word is empty and the multiset consists of one label in $J$. The colour observation admits $p$ with empty pin. It is outside the image of transport along $d$. Indeed, for any old proposition $q$ with countable pin $P$, choose $i\in J\setminus P$ and $j\in(I\setminus J)\setminus P$. At $([i],h)$ and $([j],h)$ the word, degree and multiplicities on $P$ agree. Hence the $d$-image of $q$ gives the same value at $([i],\varepsilon)$ and $([j],\varepsilon)$, whereas $p$ distinguishes them. Use a tester whose row at $d$ omits precisely the current proposition $p$ and whose rows are full at every other arrow. It is admitted because $d$ is isolated. Every current proposition satisfies it necessarily, while at $d$ its row omits $p$. This refutes Barcan at $t$.

## Joins and Mathematical Possibilism

At any type the standard Frege numeral values form an admitted inductive current row, so there are at most countably many current numbers. Any internally countable family is therefore externally at most countable. Its pointwise union is represented on the union of the countably many pins, and is its least upper bound. This gives necessary Countable Boolean Completeness. The proposition sort is infinite, all its finite subcollections are admitted in current extension, and the current number row consists just of the standard numeral values. Hence the universal property of propositions has no finite count. The Axiom of Infinity at $t$ holds necessarily.

For failure of full Boolean Completeness, take the current family of the single-arrow atoms at $([i],h)$ for $i\in J$. Any join must contain precisely their union: the complement of each other single-arrow atom is an upper bound. That union is not admitted, since at the word $h$ degree-one labels in both colour classes outside any candidate countable pin have identical observations.

For revised Mathematical Possibilism consider the inexact ordinal type $(eet)(et)t$. Let $Z$ consist of the entities using a single extensional truth table on the current order and field of the sole individual, the same table at every arrow. This is a finite collection, and membership in it is admitted as a current predicate. Ordinal zero tests well-orderhood, and successor tests well-orderhood and an initial segment. Both operations preserve the uniform-table condition since the current individual set is a singleton throughout and its subsets are represented. A rigid family whose current members lie in $Z$ has a finite current extension. It equals the disjunction of the haecceities of those members: persistence includes this disjunction in the family and weak inextensibility gives the reverse inclusion. Each member of $Z$ is unchanged by every shift of its row table. The limit thus tests well-orderhood and the same finite list of tables throughout. The ordinal predicate is consequently contained in $Z$. [Ordinal constructors and small algebras](stone-terminal-maximalist.html)

If a Boolean algebra had an infinite carrier, take a countably infinite subset $Y$ of it. All needed current subsets and graphs are admitted. The set $Y$ strictly exceeds the finite ordinal benchmark, and every strictly smaller $A\subset Y$ is finite, so the subsets of $A$ can be coded on a finite subset of $Y$. This violates revised smallness. Finally a finite Boolean algebra with $k$ atoms is represented by the interval below the union of $k$ single-arrow atoms, using bottom for $k=0$. The isomorphism is an admitted current graph.
