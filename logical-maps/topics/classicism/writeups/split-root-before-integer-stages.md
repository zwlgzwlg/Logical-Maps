# A split root before the integer stages

Functional Choice, full necessity of distinctness and Strong Actuality can hold at a point with an accessible 5-necessary proposition that fails two-hat necessity. Another proposition there is two-hat necessary and fails distinctness-preserving necessity. The construction uses the modal tests of the [symmetric integer stages](symmetric-integer-paired-stages.html).

## The two kinds of objects

Use the symmetric integer-stage coefficient category: each stage $n$ has, at each coordinate $i$, uncountably many paired points and one additional singleton block exactly when $i\le n$. Arrows are products of block-respecting maps with free-word labels; symmetries are the empty-word products of ordinary block automorphisms. Treat the coefficient points as auxiliary. The actual individual sort is a singleton at every object. For each old stage $v$ add arrows $h_v:r\to v$ and $j_v:v\to r$ from and to one new object. For every ordinary arrow $f:u\to v$ set $fh_u=h_v$ and $j_vf=j_u$. Put $j_vh_v=1_r$. Each old hom-set $u\to v$ also contains the new arrow $k_{uv}=h_vj_u$, distinct from the ordinary arrows. These rules give an associative category: a path which passes through $r$ reduces to the unique such arrow with its endpoints, while wholly ordinary paths retain the original composition.

Construct the domains by induction on type. Let $A_w$ be the product of the argument domains at $w$. A root relation is a pair
$$
 x=(E,(V_v)_v),\qquad E\subseteq A_r,\quad V_v\subseteq A_v,
$$
where each $V_v$ is invariant under the full coefficient symmetry group at $v$. At an old stage $u$, a relation consists of an ordinary source-pinned and target-equivariant profile on the enlarged tuple domains and a return coordinate $x$ in the root domain of this relation's type. The ordinary pin bound is countable as in the source model. Ordinary arrows precompose the first component and preserve $x$. The map $j_u$ reads $x$. The image $h_u(x)$ of a displayed root relation has ordinary row $V_v$ at every ordinary arrow ending in $v$, and return coordinate $x$. The arrows $k_{uv}$ act by composition. The symmetry requirement on the $V_v$ makes each $h_u(x)$ admitted.

To abstract at the root, take the satisfaction row at $r$ and at each $h_v$ on the transported parameters. Every such parameter is fixed under the target symmetry, so the new rows satisfy the root requirement. To abstract at an upper stage, use ordinary evaluation on the entire transported parameters, and compute the return coordinate by root abstraction on their $j$-images. Agreement on the union of the ordinary pins gives equal transported whole parameters, since every ordinary map leaves the return coordinate unchanged. Induction on terms consequently gives the action law both on ordinary arrows and on $h,j$, including abstraction over new arguments. A difference of two profiles is detected by a tuple at an ordinary coordinate or by the distinct root returns. This supplies a model of C.

## Root choice and distinctness

Every arrow out of $r$ has a left inverse, so all types satisfy ND there. Given a current serial relation with relational output, select for each input $a$ an allowed whole entity $y_a$. At the identity give the operation the required current row. At $h_v$ prescribe on inputs $h_v(a)$ the current extension of $h_v(y_a)$, and use empty output rows on remaining inputs. The inputs $h_v(a)$ are distinct and fixed by the entire target symmetry group. The prescribed output rows are invariant as well. The root thus admits the operation, and its value on $a$ is precisely $y_a$ as a whole entity. This proves Functional Choice.

The scalar $a$ supported only at $1_r$ is a true atom. Its transport at $v$ has bottom ordinary component and return $a$; its only true arrow from $v$ is $j_v$, so this too is an atom. The same description survives under ordinary and absorbed arrows or returns to $r$, proving Strong Actuality. Every current truth is two-hat necessary. Use $a$ as the true witness: at $h_v$ take a current upper truth with full ordinary component and bottom return. The only continuation making $h_v a$ true is $j_v$, after which this upper truth is permanently impossible. Hence $\Diamond_{\ne} a$ at the identity cannot recur at any $h_v$ in the two-hat test. An arbitrary true proposition therefore passes that test at $r$.

## Two successive failures of necessity comparison

At the ordinary stage $0$ take $P$ true on ordinary arrows exactly when the target stage is less than $1$. Give it top root return, making all its reset rows true. Let $u:0\to1$ be the ordinary inclusion. For every true $q$ at $0$ and every true $s$ at $1$, their ordinary components still have countable pins. To see $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ at $u$, send the new singleton on a return $v$ to one point of a fresh doublet disjoint from the pin of $s$, fixing the ordinary points. Then $vu=1_0$. On the next forward map $x$ collapse that doublet onto the singleton. The composite $xv$ fixes $s$'s pin. Thus $q$ holds at $vu$ and $s$ at $xv$. Since $P$ is false at $u$, it fails two-hat necessity.

For the positive 5-test use the ordinary property $Y$ accepting all scalar inputs if the target stage is below $1$, and otherwise accepting exactly those whose ordinary component has a pin omitting the coordinate-$1$ singleton. Give $Y$ top root return. The test is invariant and admitted. At a marked target an ordinary scalar testing preservation of its singleton, with arbitrary root return, witnesses a failure. Therefore $\forall z\,Yz=P$ including the new coordinates. For any current $z$, enlarge an ordinary pin $N$ by the partners in coordinate $1$. Take as a true guard $q$ the ordinary empty-word endomorphisms of $0$ injective on $N$, with bottom return. A return or reset makes this guard permanently bottom; an ordinary arrow at which $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ holds must be injective on $N$. It cannot carry a member of a protected pair onto the target singleton, so its image carries a pin for $z$ omitting that singleton. Hence $Yz$ is two-hat necessary and therefore 5-necessary for every current $z$. The native Barcan formula for $\Box_5$ gives $\Box_5P$.

For the other comparison, on ordinary arrows let $D$ mean that the target has the coordinate-$0$ singleton; again use top root return. Guard by the true test of ordinary empty-word endomorphisms fixing that singleton, with bottom return. On an arrow to a negative stage the singleton lands in a doublet, and the true ordinary test of injectivity on that doublet also has bottom return. Every return making the first guard true collapses the doublet, after which the second test is impossible; reset arrows cannot make either guard true. This proves two-hat necessity of $D$. Given any current truth at stage zero, move the singleton into a doublet outside its ordinary pin on an arrow to stage $-1$, and then collapse that doublet onto the singleton on the return. The composite fixes the pin, witnessing possibility of that truth at an arrow falsifying $D$. Hence $D$ fails distinctness-preserving necessity.

## Root comprehension and Possibilism

For any current tuple set $E$ the pair $H_E=(E,(h_v[E])_v)$ is admitted. Its rows are the exact images of its current row along all arrows at the root and after every transport. It is therefore rigid. Conversely if $F$ is weakly rigid with current row $E$, persistence gives $h_v[E]\subseteq(h_vF)_1$. Any surplus tuple at $v$ has its entire symmetry orbit disjoint from $h_v[E]$, whose members are individually fixed. A root tester full except for deleting that orbit from its $h_v$-row contradicts weak inextensibility. Thus $F=H_E$. This gives local Rigid Comprehension and Tame Rigidity. If $Z$ at $v$ is a weakly rigid subrelation of $h_vF$, set $U=E\cap h_v^{-1}(Z_1)$. The rigid relation $h_vH_U$ has precisely $Z$'s current extension, so weak persistence and weak inextensibility in the two directions give $h_vH_U=Z$. In particular $Z$ is rigid. Every arrow from $r$ therefore takes the old rigid or weakly rigid power property exactly onto its prescribed current row. This exactness persists by composition, giving both local power principles.

The root Boolean algebra is the powerset of $\{r\}\sqcup\mathbb Z$. Higher root relation algebras also have componentwise unions: each $V_v$ is an invariant subset and arbitrary unions are invariant. The root satisfies Boolean Completeness. Yet it fails propositional Barcan. At some $v$ a scalar that distinguishes ordinary free words with the same target is fixed by all coefficient symmetries and lies outside $h_v[D_t(r)]$. Exclude this scalar only in the $h_v$-row of a root tester; all root propositions necessarily pass it.

The benchmark of inexact individual ordinals is finite. In the singleton individual sort take the closed finite collection of uniform order/field Boolean tables for the threshold predicates, as in the [finite threshold argument](../index.html#arg-forcing-possibilism-countably-many-arrows). Zero and successor depend only on the two current extension bits for relation and field. Any rigid family of these predicates has exactly the images of its current members at each arrow from $r$, as just proved. Its limit is consequently a single order/field table uniformly on all ordinary arrows; the identity table is likewise finite. This supplies the finite bound independently of the number of upper stages. With unrestricted current rows any infinite carrier contains a countably infinite set larger than the benchmark. Every strictly smaller subset of that countable set is finite, so its finitely many subsets can be coded by finitely many members of the same countable set. Hence only finite carriers are revised-small at $r$. The root powerset algebra has principal intervals representing every finite Boolean algebra and the needed isomorphisms are admitted by current fullness. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism at the root.

The claims here use ordinary proofs. The source integer-stage modal tests and the new reset-coordinate and splitting arguments received separate independent reviews.

## Self-atoms and number structures throughout the model

An upper relation with an ordinary occurrence transports to an upper relation instantiated at the ordinary identity. Such an instantiated upper relation always splits. Keep its pin fixed and choose an ordinary doublet outside it. At two ordinary arrows to the same stage with empty word, identify its points or send them to distinct points, keeping the entire prescribed pin as at the identity in both cases. The relation has the same row containing the chosen tuple. Intersecting with the scalar doublet-coincidence test and its complement gives two nonzero parts. This argument applies at the actual occurrence, even when the original profile's current row was empty. Thus an upper self-atom has no ordinary component. At $r$, a relation having some inhabited $V_v$ acquires an ordinary occurrence after $h_v$ and therefore cannot be a self-atom. All nonzero root self-atoms are consequently supported at $1_r$ on one tuple. Similarly a nonzero upper self-atom with only a return component has such a singleton at $r$: its return is possible and still meets the self-atom condition there. Both the root-only and sole-return singleton relations remain atoms under every transport on which they survive. Hence every self-atom is strong, necessarily at all relational types.

There are continuum-many distinct root scalar labels from the independent truth bits at the stages. At every relational output type lift these by constant abstraction. Every arrow from $r$ acts injectively on these labels by its left inverse. At each upper stage use their $h_v$-images; every ordinary, return and reset arrow carries their entire indexed family bijectively to that at its target. For any set of tuples of labels use its exact relation $H_E$ constructed above. The same construction at the next type lists the $H_T$ for every subset $T$ of the labelled tuples. This gives represented powers at every positive arity, with distinct labels remaining necessarily distinct. Now choose disjoint labels for the natural numbers and their subsets, and give them zero, successor and membership by these exact relations. At every stage the result is the standard full real structure with the represented powers required for Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. No real structure can occur at individual type, whose carrier is always a singleton.

At any counting type the exact relation for the standard closed Frege numerals transports to just those same standard denotations, by parameter-free term transport. It is an inductive predicate cutting down the local Frege numbers to standard numeral values. There are infinitely many scalar labels at every stage, so the universal property of propositions is never counted by a finite cardinal. This gives necessary propositional Infinity. At every Peano type the arithmetic is standard, and under a necessary Peano guard the standard closed numbers have exact images and remain distinct. This also gives Necessity of Arithmetic and Arithmetic Necessitism.
