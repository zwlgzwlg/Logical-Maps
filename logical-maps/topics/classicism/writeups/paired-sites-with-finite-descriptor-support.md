# Signature Possibility+, both rigid powers and two strict modal separations

At the same point, signature Possibility+, Mathematical Possibilism, Tame Rigidity and both rigid powers hold. A 5-necessity fails two-hat necessity, and a two-hat necessity fails ND-necessity. We give two versions. Every atom is strong in the first. The second has a propositional atom that is instantiated somewhere without remaining an atom.

## The three coordinates

Use the four core sites, the unrestricted block maps and the evaluation point $(W,V)$ of the [paired-site construction](stone-terminals-paired-sites.html). We use one public propositional name. Fix the old theory $T$ and the complete pointed family of the [finite descriptor support model](coalesced-finite-support-rigid-root.html). Include an auxiliary individual name and infinitely many free propositional names in its language $\Theta$, in addition to its fresh predicates. The latter necessarily miss every matching closed term tuple. The extra names can take arbitrary values in models of the public language, with the fresh predicates bottom. Distinct closed descriptors are identified precisely when $T$ proves their identity.

From each core site put an entrance into each designated old point and retain all its continuations. Each entrance absorbs precomposition by core arrows. Core individuals are the individual names and core transport fixes them. Each old entrance evaluates a name in its component. Also attach the independent homogeneous Stone terminals $X$ of the paired-site construction. In the second version add a further independent entrance into a full finite site $L$ with one individual and arrow monoid $\{1,z\}$, $z^2=z$. It has the unrestricted typed arrow tables and each fixed type is finite.

A core relational value has an arbitrary finitely pinned table on the core arrows, one closed $\Theta$-descriptor for all its old values, and a locally constant section on $X$ into the appropriate finite terminal type. In the second version it also has an arbitrary whole $L$-value. All choices are independent. Core transport precomposes the core rows and fixes all exit data. Give each relational name its own old descriptor and constant other coordinates, taking the fresh predicates empty on the core. A core term uses the union of its parameter pins. At every old entrance its value is the single term obtained by substituting the descriptors of the parameters, including in abstractions and partial applications. At Stone points the list of transported parameters is locally constant; at $L$ the term has its ordinary whole $L$-value. Thus these domains admit abstraction and satisfy the action-model clauses for C. The old components realize each eligible signature Possibility+ instance by interpreting its individual parameters as in the finite descriptor proof. Current rows may be chosen arbitrarily, so all current well-orders are admitted.

## A weakly rigid relation on the core

Let $F$ be weakly rigid at a core site, with current tuple set $E$. Each tuple has an old descriptor, coordinate by coordinate. Only finitely many distinct old descriptor tuples occur in $E$. Indeed each fresh predicate necessarily misses every actual tuple on the old exits. Use a tester with full core, Stone and $L$ values and old descriptor the complement of this predicate. Weak inextensibility forces the old descriptor of $F$ to entail every such complement over $T$. The [fresh-predicate compactness argument](coalesced-finite-support-rigid-root.html) then gives finiteness. In that argument the second auxiliary root is the independent descriptor model over $T$ with one predicate set to bottom throughout its old cone. It requires no restrictions on terms beyond the ground instances already included in $T$.

Take the finite union of tuple-haecceities of these represented descriptors for the old coordinate, and use top elsewhere as a tester. Weak inextensibility and weak persistence give equality with the entire old trace of $F$. Likewise the entire $L$-value, when present, is the union of the haecceities of the finite set of entrance images of $E$. This describes all further transports even when tuples merge.

For each tuple $b$ of the finite Stone-terminal type, put
$$
 O_b(E)=\{x\in X:\text{some }a\in E\text{ has leaf value } b\text{ at }x\}.
$$
This is open. Its closure is clopen since the Stone algebra is complete. The terminal membership fibre of $F$ for $b$ contains $O_b(E)$ by persistence, hence its closure. A tester having exactly these closures as its terminal fibres and top elsewhere gives the converse inclusion.

For a finite saturated pin $N$ containing a pin of $F$ its core rows are
$$
 F_h=B_N(E)_h=\{f a:a\in E,\ f\text{ has the same target as }h,\ f|_N=h|_N\}.
$$
Use this very table with top on every exit as the weak-inextensibility tester. Weak persistence and agreement on the pin give the other inclusion. Conversely these core hull identities for every such $N$, together with the stated exit data, suffice for weak rigidity: any tester on the current members covers the core by matching its pin, covers the finite descriptor and $L$ traces by persistence of their representative tuples, and contains the Stone closures because its fibres are closed.

At a core arrow $h$, $F_h$ contains $hE$ and consists entirely of core images of $E$. Thus every old descriptor, every $L$ projection and every leaf tuple at each given point of $X$ is represented by $F_h$ exactly when represented by $E$. The [paired-site factorization proof](paired-individuals-two-object.html) makes every core continuation satisfy the hull criterion at the new source. Its finite prescriptions are made separately in the two coordinates; equality on a pin still gives equality of whole transported entities. The exit criteria just established remain true there. At old exits and $L$, the whole relation is a finite union of tuple-haecceities. The leaves are classical. This proves Tame Rigidity at every core point.

## Lifting the subrelations

Let $F$ be rigid at a core source and let a tester be necessary on each of its current rigid subrelations. At a core arrow $h$ take any rigid $Z\le hF$, with current row $D$. Enlarge $N$ to contain the pins of $F$ and the tester. In each coordinate choose the special surjection $j$ of the [paired-site lifting lemma](paired-individuals-two-object.html), agreeing with $h$ on $N$. It maps a whole doublet bijectively onto every target doublet and has only finitely many nontrivial fibres, each finite. The lemma gives $jF=hF$ and $F_j=jE$ by finite pin interpolation. Let $E_0=\{a\in E:ja\in D\}$.

Choose a finite saturated $P$ containing $N$, the nontrivial fibres and the preimage of a pin of $Z$. Any endomorphism $u$ fixing $P$ descends along $j$ to a block-preserving map fixing the pin of $Z$, so $uE_0\subseteq E_0$. The stable-hull lemma gives the table $B_P(E_0)$ with current row $E_0$; it also equals $B_Q(E_0)$ for every larger pin $Q$. Give this table precisely the exit coordinates prescribed in the preceding section for $E_0$. Its old descriptor exists because the descriptor classes are a subset of the finite support of $E$. Its finite $L$-relation and clopen Stone closures are admitted. The hull criterion proves weak rigidity, hence rigidity by Tame Rigidity. This relation $Y$ entails $F$ at every coordinate. At $j$ it has row $jE_0=D$, so $jY=Z$ by uniqueness of weakly rigid coextensions. The tester therefore accepts $Z$ at $j$ and at $h$.

At an old arrow or an arrow through $L$, a rigid subrelation of the finite transported bound equals the union of the haecceities of its current tuples. For each choose an original $F$-tuple mapping to it. Their finite union of haecceities is an original rigid subrelation mapping to the given one. At a Stone point $x$ a subrelation is a finite tuple set $D$. For nonempty $D$, the clopen intersection of the closures of $O_b(E)$ for $b\in D$ contains $x$. On this intersection the finitely many open sets $O_b(E)$ are dense, hence have dense intersection. At each point of their intersection choose one original tuple attaining each $b$. The finite union of their haecceities is rigid, entails $F$ and has exactly the prescribed terminal extension there. The tester's acceptance fibre for $D$ is closed, so it contains $x$. The empty subrelation lifts via bottom.

The canonical rigid power is persistent. These tests prove its weak inextensibility, hence its rigidity at the core source. The weak power agrees with it throughout the cone: core points satisfy Tame Rigidity, and under each finite old or $L$ bound a weakly rigid subrelation is a finite union of tuple-haecceities. Stone leaves are classical. This gives Weakly Rigid Power as well. Both conclusions are the unboxed map schemata.

## Mathematical Possibilism, modality and atoms

In the [paired-site ordinal argument](stone-terminals-paired-sites.html) the old factor is now bounded by the fixed set of descriptors of the inexact ordinal type. In the second model multiply that bound by the finite size of the corresponding $L$-type. Both bounds are fixed before selecting the strong limit cardinal and its Stone catalogue. The rigid-limit tests use top at every old exit and at $L$. They still force extensional rows on the core and a common ordinal value at all Stone leaves. Thus the ordinal estimate and the representation of each revised-small complete algebra as a principal Stone interval apply. For both strict modal separations use the same core tests and restorative arrows, giving every guard false and each separating proposition true on the added exits.

The core row factor at each type is atomless by splitting on a fresh block. The old descriptor factor is atomless: for any nonzero descriptor use a free propositional name it omits and assign that name first top, then bottom, substituting the closed value throughout the ground predicate tests. Thus in the first model each atom is supported on an isolated Stone point and a single terminal tuple. It stays an atom on the core and is an atom or bottom at every exit, so is strong. The unit of the atomless regular-open Cantor factor is a propositional self-atom with no atom below it in both models. In the second model, the root scalar supported only on $L$ with old truth set $\{z\}$ is an atom in that independent factor. At the composite through $z$ it becomes top in the four-element algebra, where it is true and is split by the truth set $\{1\}$. This atom fails to be a self-atom.
