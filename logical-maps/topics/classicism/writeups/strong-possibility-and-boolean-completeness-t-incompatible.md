# Pure Strong Possibility and Boolean Completeness at type $t$ are incompatible

OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026.

One specified family of propositions has no least upper bound at any pure Strong Possibility point. The family is indexed by the canonical Frege numbers at $e$. The proof uses Boolean Completeness only at the starting point and allows nonstandard numbers at every point of the argument.

## A diagonal sentence at a Maximalist point

Write $N=\mathbb N_e$, $I=I^*_e$, $S=\operatorname{Suc}_e$, $U=\lambda x^e.\top$, and $F$ for the Fregean Axiom. For $n\in N$ put
$$
 b_n= F\land (Sn)(U).
$$
For a standard numeral $\bar j$, this is $F$ together with the assertion that there are exactly $j+1$ individuals. The [October finite-cell argument, §§3–5](https://github.com/dkodsi/Logical-Maps/blob/67b116acaf7cee286614307468f5361f6a195c6e/submissions/classicism-results-2026-10-24-v2/source/research/2026-10-22/sp-structural-proof-package/profile-root-construction-20261022.md) gives the singleton definable root used below.

At that pure Maximalist root $V$, each proposition has a current bit and a descriptor $T$ in the pure language with one individual marker. Its value at every old exit, and after every continuation there, is the value of $T$ at the resulting pointed world. The Fregean Axiom fails at $V$. In every Fregean world with $j+1$ individuals the hierarchy at each fixed type is the full finite extensional hierarchy. Indeed, all subsets of a finite tuple space are represented by finite disjunctions of tuple haecceities, and Fregean extensionality identifies coextensive relations. Permutations are transitive on choices of a single marked individual. Thus a descriptor has one truth value on the entire cell $b_{\bar j}$. Some old exit realizes that cell. At $V$ the cell is consequently nonzero and is a Boolean atom. The full current predicate row consisting of the standard numeral values is inductive. Hence the actual members of $N$ at $V$ are exactly those values. Distinct standard numerals are distinguished in the finite components, and the Peano guard $I$ holds at $V$.

Use natural numbers as finite string codes for all closed one-marker proposition terms, interpreting an invalid code as a fixed false sentence. Let $T_j$ denote the term decoded at $j$. Define $D(j)$ as one minus the truth bit of $T_j$ in the full Fregean hierarchy on $j+1$ individuals with one marker. This bit is primitive recursive. Parse the finite syntax and enumerate the full relation tables on the argument domains for its finitely many types. Boolean operations, identity, application, lambda abstraction and quantification can all be evaluated from these tables by bounded loops. For syntax of length $L$ and a base of size $j+1$, the size bounds can be generated by $O(L)$ iterations of the primitive recursive operation $u\mapsto 2^{u^{L+1}}$, starting above $j+L+2$, followed by bounds for the finite environments and evaluation tables. This gives primitive recursive bounds in both inputs, with no uniform fixed bound on the type order required. In the Fregean hierarchy every true proposition is top and every false one bottom, so the modal operations also have their extensional values there.

For a proposition $p$, write $p^1=p$ and $p^0=\neg p$. In formulas containing $p^{D(n)}$ use the two ordinary guarded clauses for the values $0$ and $1$ of the PA graph of $D$. Let $\Psi$ be the single closed pure sentence
$$
 I\ \land\ \forall p^t\,\exists n\in N\, .\, b_n\le p^{1-D(n)} .
$$
At $V$, for any $p$ choose a code $j$ of its descriptor. On the entire cell $b_{\bar j}$, $p$ has bit $1-D(j)$, establishing the displayed inequality. Thus $\Psi$ is consistent with pure Maximalism. At the same $V$ the closed pure sentence $\Pi$ asserting $I$ and the nonzeroness and pairwise disjointness of all $b_n$ for $n\in N$ holds, since the indices there are standard.

## Coding the opposite inequalities at the starting point

Suppose the starting point satisfies pure Strong Possibility. Then it satisfies pure Maximalism and $I$. The former is [[strong-possibility-r-implies-distinctness-schema-r]]. For the latter, a Peano point is possible by Maximalism, and the Peano guard is equivalent to its ordinary possibility at this type. Ordinary possibility of $\Pi$ also follows from Maximalism, since $\Pi$ is already C-consistent. If some current $n\in N$ had $b_n=\bot$, numberhood and this identity would persist at a $\Pi$-point, which is impossible. For disjointness use the [uniform finite Fregean cell lemma for pure Strong Possibility](strong-possibility-r-inconsistent.html#finite-cells). Equivalently, observe that the uniform disjointness condition is a universal Boolean combination of identities: numberhood, being persistent, can be written $Nn=\top$. A countertuple to the condition would persist along a distinctness-preserving test, contrary to $\Diamond_{\ne}\Pi$. Thus throughout the full current $N$ all cells are nonzero and distinct indices have disjoint cells.

If the family defined by the current property
$$
 X(p)\ \longleftrightarrow\
 \exists n\in N\, .\, D(n)=1\land p=b_n
$$
has a least upper bound $s$, then $b_n\le s^{D(n)}$ for every current $n\in N$. For a positive bit this is the upper-bound property. For a negative bit every selected cell is disjoint from $b_n$, so $\neg b_n$ is an upper bound and $s\le\neg b_n$. This is the scalar coding argument applied to the Fregean cells. The graph $p=b_n$ associates each selected cell to its unique index, so this particular family is internally countable. Its defining term is admitted by lambda abstraction; local Boolean Completeness at $t$ supplies the required $s$.

## The return through the diagonal sentence

Between two Peano points joined by an arrow, each primitive recursive function has the same value on transported input and output numbers. For completeness, fix the PA-provably functional graph of each such function. Zero, successor and projections commute with transport, and composition uses the existing intermediate values. For a primitive recursion on $n$, use Frege induction at the source for the modal property that every graph instance on these inputs and its output is preserved at every Peano successor. At zero this is the base function. At the induction step take the current value for $n$, transport it by the induction hypothesis, and apply the already established property for the step function and its PA recursion equation at each Peano successor. The induction predicate is admitted by lambda abstraction, including the modal operator and the quantifiers over the other inputs and output. Numberhood persists in C. This proves preservation for the fixed $D$ on nonstandard inputs as well. Both signs are preserved since $0\ne1$ at every Peano point.

At the starting point the following proposition with parameter $s$ is true:
$$
 R(s) = I\ \land\ \forall n\in N\, .\,
        \bigl(b_n\ne\bot\ \land\ b_n\le s^{D(n)}\bigr).
$$
Strong Possibility applied to $\Psi$, tested with $R(s)$, supplies arrows $g:o\to u$ and $h:u\to v$ with $\Psi$ true at $u$ and $R(hg s)$ true at $v$. Apply $\Psi$ at $u$ to $g s$ and take its witness $m\in N_u$. The Boolean inequality $b_m\le (g s)^{1-D_u(m)}$ is an identity and therefore persists to $v$. The term $b_m$ transports to $b_{hm}$, and $hm\in N_v$. Both $u$ and $v$ are Peano, so $D_v(hm)=D_u(m)$. But $R(hg s)$ at $v$ says $b_{hm}\ne\bot$ and $b_{hm}\le(hg s)^{D_v(hm)}$. A nonzero proposition cannot lie below complementary propositions. This contradiction excludes the indicated join and proves the result.

The witness $m$ can be new at $u$ and can lie above every transported starting number. The universal quantifier in $R$ at the return applies to its transported value regardless. No Actuality, Barcan principle, preserved supremum, or Boolean Completeness at an accessible point is used.

## Finite signatures

For **every finite signature** $\Sigma$, Strong Possibility for $\Sigma$ is likewise incompatible with local Boolean Completeness at $t$. In the proof replace $b_n$ by $b_n\land\delta_\Sigma$, where $\delta_\Sigma$ says that all original individual constants coincide and each original nonindividual constant equals the bottom entity of its type. Empty conjunctions are true. Use the [signature-definable root](coalesced-definable-root-signature.html) for $\operatorname{Max}(C(\Sigma))$, using one auxiliary individual marker when needed. Every standard positive finite size admits a full Fregean model with the stipulated default interpretations, so all the restricted cells are possible. At the root the cells are false and the numbers are standard by the unrestricted current numerical predicate row.

On each such cell a proposition descriptor becomes a pure one-marker sentence by substituting bottom for nonindividual constants and the common marker for individual constants. Every clause of this substitution is correct throughout the finite Fregean cell, because literal identity and term interpretation commute with transport. Consequently the single pure one-marker evaluation algorithm above gives the required diagonal on these restricted cells. The analogues of $\Pi$ and $\Psi$ are closed sentences of $\mathcal L(\Sigma)$ consistent with $\operatorname{Max}(C(\Sigma))$. Signature Strong Possibility gives their distinctness-preserving possibilities under this reference theory. The coding and return arguments apply verbatim. This includes finite signatures consisting of any number of individual names, propositions and predicates of individuals. The next argument allows infinitely many individual names.

## Arbitrarily many individual names

Let \(\Sigma\) have any set of individual constants and finitely many
nonindividual constants. Strong Possibility for \(\Sigma\) is incompatible
with unboxed Boolean Completeness at \(t\). The argument permits a
different interpretation of every individual name at the starting point.

Retain \(N,I,\mathrm{Fr}\) and \(b_n\) from the
pure proof above.
Let \(E_1\) say there is exactly one individual. Let \(\delta\) be the
finite conjunction saying that each nonindividual symbol is bottom,
and put \(c_n=b_n\land\delta\). In the standard signature-definable
root \(V\) for \(\operatorname{Max}(C(\Sigma))\), numbers are standard
and proposition values have closed descriptors in the signature with
one auxiliary individual marker if needed. This is valid also when
the root has many named individuals: a free current row isolates the
standard numeral values and finite Fregean components distinguish them.

At \(V\) one has the closed sentence
\[
 \Gamma=I\land\forall n\in N\;\Diamond(E_1\land\Diamond c_n).
\]
For each standard \(n\), take a singleton C point with an arrow to
a full finite Fregean world of size \(n+1\). Send its sole individual
to one marked element, and interpret all the original individual names
by these chosen individuals. Interpret the remaining constants by bottom.
A full action model on this two-object frame gives the required
consistent sentence and hence its possibility at \(V\).
At a signature SP point \(o\), ordinary Maximalism gives both \(I\)
and \(\Diamond\Gamma\). Any failure of the indicated possibility at
a current \(n\in N\) would persist, as would numberhood, to a
\(\Gamma\)-point. Hence \(\Gamma\) holds at \(o\) itself.

Write \(D(j)=1-\operatorname{Eval}(T_j,j+1)\) for the primitive
recursive diagonal on the pure one-marker descriptors. The following
single sentence also holds at \(V\):
\[
 I\ \land\ \forall p \ \exists n\in N\
    \Box\big(E_1\to (c_n\le p^{1-D(n)})\big).
\]
Here the sign is chosen at the Peano point before the box, using the
two guarded clauses for the numerical bit. For a given \(p\),
replace all individual names in an old descriptor by a single marker
and replace its nonindividual symbols by their bottom terms. Use a
code of the resulting pure one-marker term for \(n\).
Every arrow *through* a singleton makes all individual names equal
for the rest of that path. At a subsequent \(c_n\)-world the
nonindividual symbols are bottom and the finite Fregean hierarchy
is the one used in the evaluation. Thus the asserted order identity
holds at each old singleton. If \(V\) itself is a singleton, the
same argument applies to all its old exits and the current non-Fregean
point is outside \(c_n\).

The family \(c_n\) is nonzero at \(o\) by \(\Gamma\). It is pairwise
disjoint there by strong possibility of the disjointness sentence at
\(V\), a universal Boolean combination of identities using persistence
of numberhood. Boolean Completeness now supplies \(s\) with
\(c_n\le s^{D(n)}\) for all current \(n\), by joining the positive
cells. In strong possibility of the displayed diagonal sentence
request a return to the true proposition
\[
 \Gamma\ \land\ \forall n\in N\;(c_n\le s^{D(n)}).
\]
At the intermediate point take the index \(m\) for the transported
\(s\). Its boxed conditional remains true at the return by transitivity.
Primitive recursive arithmetic preserves the sign across these two
Peano points. By \(\Gamma\) there is then a further path through a
singleton to a point satisfying the transported \(c_m\). The boxed
conditional gives the opposite sign of \(s\) there, while the order
identity at the return gives its chosen sign. This contradiction proves the claim. In each signature case the graph
\(p=c_n\) on the selected positive cells witnesses internal countability
relative to \(e\). Consequently the single unboxed \( (e,t) \) instance
of Countable Boolean Completeness suffices for the contradiction here too.
The analogous pure instance uses \(p=b_n\). Goodsell already obtained
the incompatibility with the full Countable Boolean Completeness schema
from ordinary pure Maximalism. [Goodsell’s theorem](../index.html#result/possibility-and-countable-boolean-completeness-incompatible)

## Incomplete principal intervals

Let $N=\mathbb N_e$ and $b_n=\mathrm{Fr}\land(\operatorname{Suc}_e n)(\lambda x.\top)$. Fix any total computable strictly increasing function $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ in the external metatheory. At a pure Strong Possibility point, no internally Boolean-complete principal scalar interval $\mathord\downarrow v$ contains $b_{f(i)}$ for all current $i\in N$. Here $f$ on nonstandard numbers is read through the numerical graph of one fixed program for $f$.

In particular, if $v$ is a **closed pure proposition** true on infinitely many positive individual sizes in the ordinary full finite Fregean hierarchies, then its principal interval is internally incomplete. There is no density or growth-rate restriction on that infinite spectrum.

If the actual $N$ is externally standard, the conclusion also says that for **any** $v$ with complete interval the ordinary set $\{n:b_n\le v\}$ contains no infinite computably enumerable subset. An infinite computably enumerable set contains the range of a total strictly increasing computable function, obtained by waiting for a value larger than the previous one. Thus an infinite such spectrum must be immune in the usual computability-theoretic sense.

### The diagonal on a subsequence

Let $I=I_e^*$. In the singleton descriptor Maximalist model with standard $N$, enumerate the closed proposition descriptors with one individual marker as $T_0,T_1,\ldots$. The bit $\operatorname{Eval}(T_i,j)$ is the effective truth value of the descriptor in the full finite Fregean hierarchy on $j\ge1$ individuals. Let $D$ be the fixed program for
$$
 D(i)=1-\operatorname{Eval}(T_i,f(i)+1).
$$
It is total and returns a bit. Pure SP makes true first-order $\Pi_2$ arithmetic hold at the source. Thus the programs for $f$ and $D$ are internally total, including at its nonstandard inputs. Strict increase and bit-valuedness follow as well: a failure of either, with terminating computations, would give a finite numerical witness to a false $\Sigma_1$ statement. [Arithmetic reflection](strong-possibility-r-inconsistent.html#arithmetic-reflection)

In the standard descriptor model the following is true. $I$ holds, the two programs are total with bit-valued $D$, and for every scalar $s$ there are current numbers $i,j$ and a bit $d$ such that $f(i)=j$, $D(i)=d$ and $b_j\le s^{1-d}$. Here $s^1=s$ and $s^0=\neg s$. To see this, take $i$ to be the index of the descriptor of $s$. On $b_{f(i)}$ its value is the evaluated bit. Write all the program graphs by existential quantification of finite halting-computation certificates with primitive recursive checking. This gives one closed pure Max-consistent sentence $\Psi$.

Suppose $\mathord\downarrow v$ is internally complete and contains every current $b_{f(i)}$. Join the admitted subfamily with $D(i)=1$ inside that interval and call its join $s$. The source cells are nonzero and pairwise disjoint, and the indices $f(i)$ are distinct. Thus $b_{f(i)}\le s^{D(i)}$ for each $i$. For a negative bit, $v\land\neg b_{f(i)}$ is a relative upper bound for the whole selected family, so $s$ is disjoint from that cell.

Test strong possibility of $\Psi$ with the actual proposition asserting $I$, totality of the programs, nonzeroness of all the indexed cells and the displayed universal agreement for this particular $s$. The intermediate point finds an index and the halting computations for a bit giving the opposite inequality. Both numberhood and the order identity persist. The primitive recursive verification of the particular computation certificates is preserved between that intermediate Peano point and the return, so the two programs give the same values there; determinism ensures uniqueness. The transported nonzero cell would lie under $s$ and under its complement. Thus this one internally countable subfamily in $\mathord\downarrow v$ already lacks a relative supremum. [Original finite-cell diagonal](strong-possibility-and-boolean-completeness-t-incompatible.html)

### Closed pure propositions

For a fixed closed pure $v$, its finite Fregean spectrum is decided by an elementary-time program $A_v$ on unary input, by finite-type evaluation. At the standard descriptor point $b_n\le v^{A_v(n)}$ for every $n$. At a pure SP point the same uniform rule holds on all its current numbers: a contrary source disequality can be required again on a return through the standard-root sentence, while the arithmetic computation and the opposite inequality survive to that return. When the standard positive spectrum is infinite, take $f$ to be the increasing search for inputs accepted by $A_v$. This search is genuinely total and its algorithm ensures that every terminated output is accepted; checking the trace proves the latter in PA. All current $b_{f(i)}$ therefore lie below $v$, and the preceding obstruction applies.
