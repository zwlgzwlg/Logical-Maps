# Arithmetic Necessitism with a necessarily Peano counting type

Assume Arithmetic Necessitism at the designated point and necessary Potential Infinity at a fixed counting type $\kappa$. Then the Necessity of Arithmetic schema holds at every counting type. The two map records take $\kappa=e$ and $\kappa=t$ respectively.

In C the Peano condition $I^*_\kappa$ is equivalent to Potential Infinity, so it holds necessarily. Arithmetic Necessitism then makes $\mathbb N_\kappa$ rigid. Its weak inextensibility supplies the conclusion of Goodsell's Lemma 8 for every admitted property of numbers, including properties with parameters. This is the only use of Countable Boolean Completeness in his proof of Theorem 9. His induction therefore gives $\Box(I_\kappa\to A)\lor\Box(I_\kappa\to\neg A)$ for every arithmetic sentence $A$ at $\kappa$. Since $I_\kappa$ holds necessarily, one sign of $A$ is necessary. Now fix another type $\sigma$ and an arithmetical sentence $A$ there. Use the identically worded arithmetic sentence at $\kappa$. At each accessible point where $I_\sigma$ holds, $I^*_\sigma$ holds as well and both number systems satisfy their Peano conditions. Take the least binary relation containing the two zeros and closed under simultaneous successors. It is a bijection between the number properties by the two induction principles, injectivity of successor and the fact that zero is not a successor. Equivalently this is the natural-number part of the map's cross-type categoricity proof. The isomorphism respects the additive and multiplicative graphs by uniqueness of Peano recursion. It preserves and reflects every arithmetic formula, hence $A$ agrees there with the sentence at $\kappa$. The one necessary sign just proved gives $\Box(I_\sigma\to A)\lor\Box(I_\sigma\to\neg A)$, as required. [Cross-type isomorphism](../index.html#background/arithmetic/real-structures-isomorphic)
