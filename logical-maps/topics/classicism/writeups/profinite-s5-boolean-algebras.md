# Mathematical Possibilism in S5 without Relational or Transversal Choice

At one point of the same model, revised Mathematical Possibilism holds with C5, Boolean Completeness at every relational type, and Rigid Comprehension, while Relational Choice and Transversal Choice both fail. At another point, Mathematical Possibilism and C5 hold with Well-Ordering at every type, while even countable propositional joins and Actuality fail. Propositional Atomicity fails at both points. The construction uses a group action to determine which relations exist. Transport between worlds is the identity.

## Worlds and types

Let $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^{\mathbb N}$ and let $G_n$ consist of the sequences whose first $n$ bits vanish. Use a set $W$ of worlds with an external action of $G$. Include countably infinitely many fixed worlds $A=\{a_n:n\in\mathbb N\}$ and one copy of $G$ on which $G$ acts by translation. Additional orbits will represent the small Boolean algebras.

For each $n$ list, up to equivariant isomorphism, the nontrivial at-most-countable Boolean algebras $C$ with an action of $G_n$ by automorphisms such that each element is fixed by some $G_m$, $m\ge n$, and $C^{G_m}$ is finite for each such $m$. Take a set of representatives by forming structures on subsets of $\mathbb N$ and action maps on those sets. For each entry add a disjoint copy of $G\times_{G_n}\operatorname{Ult}(C)$. This is the union of one copy of the Stone space of $C$ over each coset of $G_n$. The identity-coset copy carries the given $G_n$-action. For an ultrafilter, the stabilizer is closed, since membership of each algebra element is preserved on an open subgroup.

Every world sees every world, with one arrow between each ordered pair. There is one individual. All worlds share the same domains and all transports are identities. At a relational type $\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k t$ the domain consists of the subsets of $W\times\prod_iD_{\sigma_i}$ invariant under some $G_n$ acting diagonally. The case $k=0$ gives the propositions. Application at $w$ reads the row there. Interpret relational names by top and individual names by the sole individual. The domains are $G$-sets by simultaneous translation of worlds and arguments. Each typed term with parameters fixed by $G_n$ is equivariant under $G_n$. The proof is by induction on the term: quantifiers range over the same entire $G$-set at each world, and a lambda term has the jointly invariant relation on its arguments and the evaluation world given by its body. Partial application shrinks the supporting subgroup to one fixing the supplied arguments as well. Identity of relations is equality of their entire profiles, since a difference is visible with some arguments at some world. This gives an S5 Henkin action model of C, including its theoremhood clause for Logical Equivalence. Identity transport gives necessary ND at every type.

At a fixed world the available current tuple sets are exactly those invariant under some $G_n$. A supported set is the current row of its constant-world profile. At the identity $o$ in the regular orbit every external current tuple set $E$ is available, even when $E$ has no open stabilizer. Give it the profile $R(g,\bar u)\leftrightarrow g^{-1}\bar u\in E$ on the regular orbit and empty rows elsewhere. This profile is invariant under the whole group.

## Small algebras at a fixed world

The ordinal benchmark at individual type is finite. With only one individual, the zero and successive inexact ordinal predicates are the three closed thresholds well-ordered, length at least one, and impossible. A rigid family whose members are among these thresholds has the same members everywhere by identity transports and persistence in both directions. Its limit is again a threshold, including zero for an empty family. Taking the property of these three closed entities as the induction test proves the bound. This is the finite-ordinal argument already used for the map's full singleton-individual models.

Fix $a\in A$. Let a revised-small complete Boolean algebra be presented there by a carrier $B$ and an order. Choose $n$ sufficiently large that $G_n$ preserves both of their current rows. Finite predicates are available on each specified finite set of inputs. The internal Boolean algebra laws in particular give an ordinary Boolean algebra with this order and with $G_n$ acting by automorphisms.

We claim that $B^{G_m}$ is finite for each $m\ge n$. If it were infinite, choose an external countably infinite subset $Y$ of its elements. Every subset of $Y$, every enumeration, and every graph between its members is invariant under $G_m$. These are all admitted at the fixed world. In particular $Y$ is internally larger than the finite ordinal benchmark. Any subcollection of $Y$ genuinely smaller than it has finite current extension, since every infinite subset has an admitted bijection to $Y$. The powerset of each finite subset can be coded bijectively inside a finite subset of $Y$ by an admitted incidence graph. This codes all subextensions, including those represented by several nonidentical predicates. Consequently $Y$ is power-closed, contrary to revised smallness. Since every member of $B$ is fixed by some $G_m$, $B$ is an external countable union of finite sets. Its action and algebra occur in the list used to construct $W$.

Choose the corresponding component and let $p$ be its identity-coset fiber. It is a $G_n$-invariant proposition. Its principal lower interval comprises precisely the open-subgroup-invariant subsets of this fiber, since entailment checks all worlds. We show that these are exactly its clopens. Each clopen comes from an element of $C$ and has an open stabilizer. Conversely take a $G_m$-invariant subset with $m\ge n$. The action of compact $G_m$ on the Stone space is continuous, because each clopen has only finitely many translates and its element has open stabilizer. Two distinct orbits are disjoint compact sets. Separate them by a clopen and take the union of its $G_m$-translates. This is still clopen, since there are only finitely many translates. It is invariant and separates the two orbits. The finite algebra $C^{G_m}$ therefore distinguishes all the orbits, so there are finitely many and each is clopen. Every $G_m$-invariant subset, being a union of those orbits, is clopen as claimed. [Stone's representation theorem](https://www.ams.org/tran/1936-040-01/S0002-9947-1936-1501865-8/S0002-9947-1936-1501865-8.pdf)

Compose the chosen $G_n$-equivariant isomorphism of $B$ onto $C$ with Stone representation on this fiber. Its graph is $G_n$-invariant and hence admitted at $a$. This represents $B$ by a principal interval. The trivial algebra is the interval below bottom. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism at every admitted type at $a$.

At $o$, arbitrary current graphs are available. Every infinite carrier has a countably infinite power-closed subcollection above the finite benchmark, by the same argument. Thus every revised-small algebra there is finite. The proposition true just at any $k$ selected fixed worlds has an interval isomorphic to the powerset of $k$ atoms. Every finite Boolean algebra is represented.

## The intermediate orbits

In fact the same Mathematical Possibilism schema holds at every world. For a world $w$ with stabilizer $H$, an external tuple set $E$ is a current row precisely if $H\cap G_n$ preserves it for some $n$. To realize it, prescribe row $gE$ at $gw$ for $g\in G_n$ and empty rows off this orbit. The prescription is independent of the choice of $g$.

For a presented small algebra choose $n$ so that $K=H\cap G_n$ preserves both carrier and order. As in the fixed-world case, its subalgebras fixed by $H\cap G_m$ must be finite for all $m\ge n$. Otherwise all subsets and graphs on a countably infinite set of fixed elements would be available at $w$. Every entity is fixed by some $G_m$, so the algebra is externally countable.

The closed subgroup $K$ is a continuous linear retract of $G$ over the field of two elements. Indeed every continuous linear character of $K$ factors through its projection on finitely many coordinates and extends by finite-dimensional linear algebra to a character of $G$. Choose a linear section $s$ of restriction on these character spaces. Define $\pi(g)\in K$ by $\chi(\pi(g))=s(\chi)(g)$ simultaneously for all characters $\chi$ of $K$. Every finite system has a solution since its proposed values satisfy the linear relations among the listed characters. Compactness gives a simultaneous solution and separation by coordinates makes it unique. These equations give linearity and $\pi|_K=1_K$. Applying them to each restricted coordinate gives continuity.

Extend the algebra action from $K$ to $G$ using $\pi$. Each element is still fixed by an open subgroup. Since $\pi(G_m)$ contains $K\cap G_m$, the $G_m$-fixed subalgebra is finite for $m\ge n$, and hence for smaller $m$ too. The list for $G_0=G$ includes this action on its carrier up to equivariant isomorphism. Its entire Stone component is a globally invariant proposition, with principal interval its clopen algebra by the orbit argument above. The representation graph is $K$-invariant for the original action, so the current-row description realizes the graph at $w$.

Coextension Transversal also holds everywhere. Fix $w$ and, for each represented current extension $E$, choose the least $n$ for which $H\cap G_n$ preserves $E$. Let $s(E)$ be the orbit extension given in the first paragraph of this section for that $n$. Its row at $w$ is exactly $E$. The group is abelian, so $hE$ has the same least $n$ as $E$ for $h\in H$ and $s(hE)=hs(E)$ as whole profiles. The range of $s$ is therefore an $H$-invariant current row and can itself be represented at $w$. It picks exactly one relation from each current coextension class. Transversal Choice, concerning arbitrary equivalence relations, fails at the fixed worlds as shown below.

## Choice, completeness and atoms

Write $p_{n,b}$ for the proposition supported only on the regular orbit at words having bit $n$ equal to $b$. Bit-flip $n$ interchanges $p_{n,0}$ and $p_{n,1}$, and the two-element set of them is invariant. The singleton propositions $d_n=\{a_n\}$ are individually fixed by all of $G$.

At $a$ take the invariant serial relation on propositions requiring input $d_n$ to select $p_{n,0}$ or $p_{n,1}$ and giving all other inputs a fixed default output. A functional subrelation choosing everywhere would have a current row invariant under some $G_m$. For $n\ge m$ the bit-flip fixes the input and interchanges its two permitted outputs, contradicting uniqueness. For Transversal Choice take the equivalence relation whose exceptional classes are these same pairs and whose other classes are singletons. The same flip prevents an invariant row from selecting exactly one member of each pair. Thus both choice principles fail. At $o$, arbitrary current rows admit all external well-orders and choice graphs. Neither at $o$ nor at $a$ can there be an intensional choice from the family whose row at world $a_n$ is the indicated pair and whose row is full elsewhere: it is necessarily inhabited, but every potential choice profile has a bounded support and must select exactly one at every $a_n$.

The root-world description gives full Boolean Completeness at $a$. Any property of relations there has a $G_m$-invariant current family at a suitable $m$. The intersection of its members as whole global profiles is $G_m$-invariant and is the required greatest lower bound. The constant-world profile of any admitted current row is rigid, giving Rigid Comprehension there. Also $\{a\}$ is a true strong atom. At $o$, every supported proposition restricts to a clopen set of the Cantor group. Every true proposition can be split on a smaller neighbourhood of $o$, so Actuality fails. The whole regular orbit gives a nonzero interval without an atom everywhere. For a missing countable join use the cylinders whose first $1$ occurs at an even position, all supported inside that orbit. Every clopen upper bound contains the zero sequence and a full neighbourhood of it, from which one can delete an odd-position cylinder. The countable family and its numerical counting graph exist at $o$ by current fullness. The standard Frege numerals counted at $t$ are distinct by current tests on finite sets of propositions.

## A represented continuum

At each relational type there are continuum many entities fixed pointwise by $G$: for each subset of $A$ take the profile true on all tuples precisely at that subset of fixed worlds. The profiles are distinct since each tuple domain is nonempty. Use some as disjoint codes for the ordinary natural numbers and all their subsets. Give the number, successor, real and membership predicates their fixed-code extensions constantly at every world. Every subset of the fixed numbers is admitted even as a globally invariant constant profile, so this is a real number structure everywhere and is rigid. For each finite arity $k$ take the family of constant-world relations with arbitrary extensions on $k$-tuples of these codes and empty elsewhere. Such relations are fixed and rigid, and the family itself is a fixed, rigid constant-world property. At any point, the restriction of any admitted relation to the codes is represented by the corresponding constant-world relation in this family. These are represented powers in precisely the map's sense. Thus Second-Order Analytic Necessitism holds, including its first-order consequence. The singleton individual type has no real number structure at any point. [Represented powers](../index.html#background/arithmetic)
