# Strong Possibility forces inextensible relations to be Frege-finite

At a pure Strong Possibility point, the current extension of each inextensible relation has a Frege finite cardinality. The same conclusion follows separately from signature Strong Possibility for every admitted signature. Neither principle is compatible with local Inextensible Comprehension. In fact its single instance providing an inextensible coextension of the universal property of propositions suffices for the contradiction.

Write $\operatorname{Fin}(F)$ for Frege finiteness of the current extension. For a relation of several arguments, count its tuple-haecceity codes. Fix the argument types and consider the single closed pure sentence
$$
 \Psi=\forall F\big(\operatorname{Inextensible}(F)\to\Box\operatorname{Fin}(F)\big).
$$

## A Maximalist model of the bound

Use the [reduced-power retraction](reduced-power-retraction.html) with one extra exit into a pointed signature-Maximalist action model $K$, for instance the [signature-definable root](coalesced-definable-root-signature.html). The stated $K$ exists for every public signature. Let $K_\tau$ denote its domain at the designated point. Use $R_e=K_e$, $S_e=R_e\sqcup\{*\}$, the inclusion as entrance on individuals, and a map $j$ retracting it. Project the root individuals identically into $K_e$. For relational $\tau=\bar\sigma t$ let $A_R,A_S$ be the tuple products of the lower-type carriers at the two kinds. Set
$$
 R_\tau=\mathcal P(A_R)\times(\mathcal P(A_S)^\omega/{=^*})\times K_\tau,
 \qquad S_\tau=\mathcal P(A_S)\times R_\tau .
$$
Here $=^*$ is eventual equality of entire rows. For $x$ at $r$ choose a representative entrance table $U_i^x$, and put $h_i x=(U_i^x,x)$, $j(V,x)=x$. Write $p$ for projection to the $K$ coordinate. In particular $jh_i$ is the identity. The individual maps satisfy that identity as well.

At an $r$-stage with outer indices $I,D$, the genuine entrances go to the Fubini product for arbitrary families of free ultrafilters on $\omega$ and use the $h_i$ pointwise. The return from an $s$-stage keeps $I,D$ and uses $j$. The extra exit at each $r$-stage enters the distinguished point in the ultrapower of the whole typed action structure $K^I/D$ and uses $p$ pointwise. Retain all old arrows there, including ultraproducts of varying arrows. A public constant uses at the initial $r$ any value having its designated $K$ coordinate. Interpret it by transport along the finite history. At the ordinary initial projection the constants have exactly the old interpretations, so the new point satisfies signature Maximalism, hence pure Maximalism.

For term closure, compute at $r$ the present row by current evaluation, the $i$th entrance row by evaluation at $s$ on $h_i$ of its parameters, and the old coordinate by evaluation in $K$ on their $p$-images. At $s$ take the present row and as return coordinate the $r$-value on the returned parameters. This is induction on term formation, computing the $r$-case first since it uses only the current truth of proper subterms. The two entrance rows of a lambda agree cofinitely as entire rows, including all newly bound arguments, and its return coordinates agree by $jh_i=1$. A difference in old coordinates is detected by some arrow and tuple in $K$. At an iterated stage select such arrows and tuples at each index where old coordinates differ, using the whole old ultrapower. The other differences are detected by the current and varying-ultrafilter entrance tests of the original retraction. This gives intensional separation and all target quantifier domains. The same construction with classical terminal coordinates appears in [the retraction over disagreeing arithmetics](boolean-retraction-disagreeing-arithmetic.html).

Here is the moving-tuple argument for full inextensibility. At base $s$, if $G=(V,H)$ is weakly inextensible then $H$'s current row is contained in $j[V]$. Use a tester full at $s$ whose return has current row $j[V]$, all entrance rows full and old coordinate top. Every member of $V$ necessarily passes: after the return it lies in the indicated $r$-row, later returns preserve the diagonal of that tuple, and all proper old exits accept it. Now suppose $F=(E,[U_i],u)$ is inextensible at the initial $r$. Every first entrance satisfies weak inextensibility of its transport. By Łoś the preceding base-$s$ condition gives $E\subseteq j[U_i]$ for cofinitely many $i$, uniformly for the whole $E$. Otherwise a free ultrafilter on the exceptional indices gives a first entrance violating that very condition.

If $E$ were infinite, choose distinct tuples $x_i\in E$ and at each good $i$ take $b_i\in U_i$ with $jb_i=x_i$. At $r$ take a tester whose current row and old coordinate are top and whose entrance rows omit only $b_i$ at the good indices. For any fixed current tuple $x$, an omission of $h_i x$ requires $x=x_i$, so at most one index is lost. This holds through every finite history: at each return the original tuple and tester recover their diagonal, and their old projections always pass. But at the first free-ultrafilter entrance the tuple represented by the $b_i$ belongs to $F$ and fails the tester. This contradicts weak inextensibility of $F$ at $r$. Hence $E$ is externally finite.

Take the finite union of the haecceities of the tuples in $E$, using bottom for the empty union. Each tuple in $E$ necessarily passes this test by necessity of identity. Weak inextensibility therefore bounds $F$ by that union as an intension. At any target the entire current extension of $F$ is contained in the finite set of the transported tuples. Members may drop out or merge. Any remaining finite tuple set is actually Frege counted by its ordinary finite numeral. This proves $\Psi$ at the initial Maximalist point without a persistence assumption on $F$.

## Strong Possibility and the contradiction

At a Strong Possibility point take a current inextensible $F$. If $\operatorname{Fin}(F)$ fails, test $\Diamond_{\ne}\Psi$ with the true current guard $\neg\operatorname{Fin}(F)$. There is an arrow to a $\Psi$-point and a continuation on which that guard is true. Inextensibility itself is boxed, so the transported $F$ remains inextensible at the intermediate point. Its $\Psi$-instance supplies $\Box\operatorname{Fin}(F)$, contradicting the return. This establishes actual internal finiteness at every arity, allowing nonstandard Frege counts. The same reasoning applies separately in either signature convention because the witness supplies the corresponding ordinary Maximalism.

The resulting finite count also bounds $F$ necessarily at the Strong Possibility point itself. By the [finite-bound lemma](../index.html#background/rigidity/internally-finite-rigid-power), the finite current extension has a rigid coextension $Y$ whose future extensions are all Frege-finite. Weak inextensibility of $F$ and persistence of $Y$ give $F\le Y$. Every later extension of $F$ is a subproperty of a Frege-finite property and therefore Frege-finite by the same internal induction. In particular the full sentence $\Psi$ holds at the original point.

[Pure Maximalism implies the quantified Axiom of Infinity at $t$](../index.html#result/pure-possibility-implies-axiom-of-infinity-t), and either Strong Possibility assumption gives pure Maximalism. Local Inextensible Comprehension would give an inextensible coextension of the universal predicate of type $tt$. The new bound makes this predicate Frege-finite. Frege counting respects actual coextension, contradicting that quantified axiom.
