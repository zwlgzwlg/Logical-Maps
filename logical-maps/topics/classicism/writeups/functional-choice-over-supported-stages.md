# Functional Choice with accessible failures of choice and rigid power

Functional Choice can hold while Relational Choice, Transversal Choice and Transversal all fail at a single accessible point. In the same model another accessible point fails both Rigid Power and Weakly Rigid Power. The construction works in ZFC.

## The arrows

Use objects $r,u,v$ and countably many copies $s_n$ of a site stage $s$. They have no ordinary arrows between copies. Apart from the arrows through $r$, the arrows at each $s$ are the bounded partial-injection, finite-tag and free-word monoid on a countable set of sites partitioned into infinitely many infinite classes. Take $G_s$ to be the group of equivalence-preserving permutations of these sites. At both $u$ and $v$ the ordinary loops are the group $G$ of permutations of a countably infinite set $A$. The ordinary arrows $u\to v$ are $ig$ for $g\in G$, with $gi=ig$. There are no other ordinary arrows between these three objects. The designated symmetry groups for the profile conditions are $G_s$ at $s$, $G$ at $u$ and the trivial group at $v$.

For each $b$ among the site stages and $u,v$ add $h_b:r\to b$ and $j_b:b\to r$, with $j_bh_b=1_r$. Every ordinary arrow $m:b\to c$ satisfies $m h_b=h_c$ and $j_c m=j_b$. Include the reset arrows $h_cj_b:b\to c$ as distinct from the ordinary arrows. Composites passing through $r$ are uniquely determined by their endpoints. Individuals form a singleton $\{*\}$ at every object. The elements of $A$ are auxiliary labels for the group arrows. Relational names may be interpreted by top and individual names by $*$.

## Typed domains

At relational type $\tau=\bar\sigma t$, let $B_b$ be the tuples of argument entities at $b$. These domains have already been defined at lower types. An $r$-entity consists of a current row $R\subseteq B_r$ and one row $V_b\subseteq B_b$ for every other object. Require $V_b$ invariant under its designated target symmetry group. All these rows are otherwise independent.

At $b\ne r$ an entity consists of a profile on the ordinary arrows out of $b$ and one whole $r$-entity as its return coordinate. The ordinary rows at $s$ use the [finite pins and tag cutoffs](bounded-partial-injections.html). An ordinary $u$ profile has one row at each $g:u\to u$ and $ig:u\to v$. For each endpoint the row depends on $g$ restricted to a finite set of inputs. At the $u$ endpoint postcomposition by $a\in G$ acts equivariantly on the tuple row. At the $v$ endpoint there is no target equivariance restriction. Ordinary $v$ profiles are arbitrary tables of rows over its group. The return coordinate at each object is unrestricted.

An ordinary arrow transports a profile by right translation on its ordinary rows and fixes its return coordinate. The arrow $j_b$ projects to that coordinate. The $b$-entity $h_b(R,(V_c)_c)$ has the original $r$-entity as return coordinate and the row $V_c$ at **every** ordinary arrow ending at $c$. This last rule includes the $u\to v$ arrows. Thus $m h_b=h_c$ holds on all types; the other composition rules follow from the projection. Profiles with different current or entrance rows are distinguished by an application at the specified arrow. A difference between return coordinates is detected after $j_b$, possibly followed by an $h_c$.

For an abstract at $r$ take its actual extension at $r$ and its extension at each $c$ on the parameters transported by $h_c$. These transported parameters are fixed by the chosen symmetries. At a designated symmetry, equivariance of evaluation follows by induction: an invertible loop transports the tuple bijectively, and right composition by it permutes the arrows out of the point in the modal clause. For an abstract at $b\ne r$, evaluate the ordinary rows at their targets on all target arguments and use its value on the returned parameters for the return coordinate. The common pin and cutoff of the parameters suffice at $s$. At $u$, two arrows to the same endpoint agreeing on the parameter pins transport the parameters equally as whole entities: any ordinary postcomposition preserves that agreement, and every reset ignores it. There is no restriction to check at $v$. The same facts handle partial application. These computations give typed term closure and the standard extensional-separation clause for C action models.

## Choice at the root and its failure at the site stage

Given a serial relation at $r$, choose one allowed output $Y_a$ for each input $a$. Write the output type as $\bar\beta t$. At $r$ use the selecting operation's row prescribed on $(a,\bar z)$ by $Y_a(\bar z)$. At $b$ prescribe its row on $(h_ba,\bar z)$ by the current application of $h_bY_a$ to $\bar z$. Set the row empty on inputs outside the image. Each $h_b$ is injective because $j_bh_b=1_r$. The transported inputs and output profiles are fixed by the designated group, so these are admissible rows even for an arbitrary original choice. Application now recovers the whole $Y_a$, including every entrance row. This proves Functional Choice for all allowed types.

For a site $a$ let $p_a$ at $s$ have the ordinary rows testing whether the partial injection is defined on $a$, and bottom return coordinate. They are distinct, and symmetries take $p_a$ to $p_{g a}$. A current class of codes has a property pinned by one of its members, with bottom on return. Any current set of tuples invariant under the stabilizer of a finite pin $P$ can be realized with bottom on return: use its image under $\pi$ at coefficients agreeing with $\pi\in G_s$ on $P$, and the empty row otherwise. The current serial relation requiring a code to choose a different code from the same class, with noncodes related to themselves, has no selector. A permutation fixing the selection pin and an input in a fresh class could move its chosen classmate. The corresponding equivalence relation has no transversal either.

Suppose a classifier selected one property from every coextension class. For a fresh site class $C$ its chosen representative $Y$ would be fixed by the subgroup preserving $C$ and fixing the classifier's pin. This remains literal equality of whole profiles because ordinary symmetries fix all return coordinates. Moving a pin of $Y$ off itself within the site classes, outside that fixed pin, gives two pins of the same representative. A partial identity on the original pin preserves the extension of $Y$; postcomposing with a swap of $C$ and a fresh class leaves the second pin's data unchanged and changes that extension. This is the [two-pin contradiction](bounded-partial-injections.html) at propositional argument type. Hence all three choice principles fail at $s$. The same partial identity preserves any currently true proposition pinned there and falsifies the live-input test of a fresh site. Thus Actuality also fails at $s$.

## An accessible failure of both powers

Fix an auxiliary label $d\in A$. For each $a\in A$ define a scalar $p_a$ at $u$ which is false on ordinary $u$ loops, is true at $ig$ exactly when $g(a)=d$, and has bottom return coordinate. It is pinned by $\{a\}$. At $v$ its image $ip_a$ has the analogous row over the ordinary $v$ group. These scalars are pairwise distinct at each point: to test $a$, choose a permutation carrying it to $d$. Right translation gives $g p_a=p_{g a}$ and $g(ip_a)=ip_{g a}$.

At $u$ define a property $F$ of propositions whose ordinary rows are the full set of these codes at the corresponding endpoint. After every return or reset take the singleton consisting of the bottom scalar. Its return coordinate is the $r$ property with row $\{\bot\}$ at each endpoint. Each row is the exact direct image of the current set along the arrow. The same is true after any transport. Exact images give persistence; any tester necessarily satisfied by the current members contains their images at each arrow. Thus $F$ is rigid.

At $v$ take $W=\{ip_a:a\in C\}$ for some infinite coinfinite $C\subset A$. Form the property with row $gW$ at each ordinary loop $g$ and with the same singleton return coordinate. Its rows, including the returns of its transports, are again exact direct images. It is a rigid subrelation of $iF$. Its ordinary rows are allowed at $v$, whereas no stabilizer of a finite set fixes this whole property: outside any finite set a transposition can exchange a member and a nonmember of $C$.

For every property $Y$ of propositions at $u$, the whole $v$-entity $iY$ is fixed by the stabilizer of a finite subset of $A$. Indeed if $P$ pins $Y$ and $a$ fixes $P$ pointwise, the ordinary rows at $l i a$ and $l i$ coincide for each $l\in G$. The return is unchanged. The same statement holds for $igY$ with the conjugate stabilizer. Use a tester with top on ordinary $u$ loops and return, and with the constant row on $iG$ consisting of all $v$ properties fixed by some such stabilizer. The tester is admitted at $u$, and along each $u$-arrow it holds of the image of every current property. It misses the constructed rigid subrelation at $i$. Consequently the property of being a rigid subrelation of $F$ fails weak inextensibility. The same witness is weakly rigid and refutes weak inextensibility for the property of weakly rigid subrelations of $F$.

## The local algebra

Entailment at $r$ is inclusion in each of its four rows: the return equations account for every further observation. At each type the algebra is the powerset of the disjoint union of the root tuples and the sets of target orbits under their designated groups. All unions and intersections exist, and every nonempty element contains an atom. At propositional type this is $\mathcal P(\{r,u,v\}\sqcup\{s_n:n\in\omega\})$. The scalar with sole nonempty row at $r$ is a true strong atom. After $h_b$ it holds only upon returning to $r$, where it tests the same one bit of any other proposition. Hence it still entails or excludes any proposition there. Every $r$-truth is possible again after every arrow by $j_b$; the root-only truth itself is false at $h_b$. It is therefore a truth at $r$ which fails ND-necessity. Finally the scalar true only in the $s$ row is a root atom. At $h_s$ it is true throughout the ordinary core and splits into the zero-tag part and its complement there, so it is not a self-atom.

## Rigid subrelations at the root

For any current tuple set $E$ take the profile $H_E$ with current row $E$ and row $h_b[E]$ at every other object. These rows are pointwise fixed by the designated symmetries, and each is the exact direct image along every further arrow. Thus $H_E$ is rigid. Any weakly rigid relation $F$ with current row $E$ must equal $H_E$: test weak inextensibility with $H_E$ for one inclusion and use persistence for the other. This proves local Rigid Comprehension and Tame Rigidity.

Given a weakly rigid subrelation $Z$ of $h_bF$, pull its current row back to a subset $D$ of $E$ by the injection $h_b$. The rigid property $h_bH_D$ has exactly that row, so it equals $Z$. Indeed two weakly rigid coextensions entail one another by persistence of each and weak inextensibility of the other. The same construction applies to rigid subrelations. Every such subrelation at any future point is therefore the transport of one at the root, and every transport from the root remains such a subrelation. This gives exact rows for both power properties throughout the cone, proving both powers at the root. The failure at the finite-pin stage proved above distinguishes the local and necessary forms. ## Mathematical Possibilism, arithmetic and analysis

At ordinal type there are only finitely many truth tables on the extensional order and field of a singleton. For any one table use that same table on the actual order and field at every endpoint. This defines a whole root entity, and its image anywhere uses the same table on every outgoing arrow. The current property of this finite set is admitted. Zero and successor preserve the uniform-table condition. A rigid family whose current members have uniform tables has at every arrow exactly their images: test by the finite disjunction of their haecceities and use inextensibility and persistence. Its limit is again a single extensional table, since well-orderhood of relations on one individual is computed identically everywhere. Thus all inexact ordinals at $r$ belong to this finite palette. The [finite ordinal bound and power-closed test](bounded-partial-injections.html) now apply. Every infinite carrier has a countable power-closed subset strictly larger than the finite ordinal benchmark since all current rows at $r$ are admitted. Each finite Boolean algebra occurs as the interval of a union of finitely many different site atoms in the root scalar algebra, with an admitted comparison graph. This is revised Mathematical Possibilism.

At each relational type there are continuum many distinct labels $a$ at $r$: specify full or empty row independently at each $s_n$. Their images $h_ba$ are pairwise distinct everywhere by the returns. Every arrow carries them to the corresponding labels at its endpoint. For each set of tuples of labels, the relation giving exactly those tuples at $r$ and their corresponding tuples at every other stage is admitted. Its row at each designated group is invariant since that group fixes all transported root labels. All rows along paths are exact images, including at every return, so the relation is rigid. The same construction works on sets of such canonical relations as the next level of labels. On distinct labels install the ordinary naturals and their powerset, with the ordinary zero, successor and membership. Every actual predicate on these numbers has its trace coded by a real, at each object, and every inductive predicate contains the ordinary zero and all its standard iterates. For each finite arity take all canonical relations corresponding to subsets of tuples of number and real labels. The rigid property of those relations represents every current relation's restriction to the labels at every stage. This verifies every Second-Order Analytic Necessitism instance through the cone; none of the singleton individual stages admits a real structure at $e$.

At each counting type the current property of the denotations of the standard Frege numerals can be represented at every object. The denotations are fixed by the symmetries, so their set can be used as a constant ordinary row at each endpoint, and at the root the current row is arbitrary. This property is inductive and rules out extra Frege numbers. At every relational counting type any finite set of the coherent labels is represented, so distinct numeral terms are distinguished by their finite counting behavior. Their sets of denotations therefore have exact direct-image rows under all arrows, with necessarily distinct members. This proves Arithmetic Necessitism; the individual Peano guard is false everywhere. In particular the propositional domain is infinite and no Frege number counts it, necessarily.

## Two returns from one site

Give one exceptional site $s_0$ two different returns $j,j':s_0\to r$. Both split its entrance, both absorb postcomposition with its ordinary loops in the sense $jm=j$ and $j'm=j'$, and each gives its own arrows $h_cj,h_cj'$ to all the other stages. For instance $(h_dj_c)(h_cj')=h_dj'$. At $s_0$ give every relation two independent whole return coordinates. Its image from $r$ has the same source entity in both positions. Existing source rows at all $h_c$ and the entire root algebra are unchanged. Term evaluation simply uses the corresponding returned parameters for each coordinate.

The root-only truth $q$ is the unique true scalar atom. It holds at the starting point, and any path on which it is currently true has target $r$ and is its identity. Thus $q$ is a self-atom. After the exceptional entrance it has empty ordinary rows and two nonzero return coordinates, both equal to $q$. The two independent profiles retaining just one of these coordinates are disjoint nonzero parts of the transported proposition. Consequently $q$ fails to be strong; since it is the unique true atom, Strong Actuality fails too. Other root atoms can have nonempty entrance rows, but they are false at the starting point.

The choice operation still prescribes the row on each $h_c$-input using the corresponding transported output. The identical source value in the two return positions gives the required whole identity at the exceptional site. Both rigid powers at the root still use exact images $H_E$, now returning along either arrow. All the labelled real structures, their powers and the finite ordinal tables are also coherent under both returns. At the site stage the ordinary pair obstruction takes both return coordinates bottom. At $u,v$ the residue test uses its same singleton reset data. Thus the previous positive and negative results, apart from the scalar strong-atom and Strong Actuality assertions altered above, give the indicated verdicts in this variant.

## Plenitude and subrelations of a rigid bound

At any point satisfying Plenitude, every represented current subset of a rigid relation $F$ is coextensive with a rigid subrelation of $F$. With a weakly rigid bound the same construction gives a weakly rigid subrelation. This restricts countermodel searches for Functional Choice implying Rigid Power.

Let $P$ be the property specifying the subset. The current graph which assigns $\top$ to a tuple in $P$ and $\bot$ to any other tuple is functional. Plenitude gives an operation $c$ realizing this graph at the current point. Thus each current tuple has $c(\bar a)=\top$ or $c(\bar a)=\bot$, and its chosen identity persists. The polyadic form of Plenitude suffices for arbitrary finite tuples.

Consider $F\land c$. Each current member persists by the weak persistence of $F$ and its positive identity for $c$. If a tester $W$ necessarily contains each current member, then $c(\bar a)\to W(\bar a)$ is necessary for every current member of $F$. For a member with negative identity for $c$ this follows from its necessary falsity. Weak inextensibility of $F$ gives $F\land c\le W$. This proves weak rigidity, and the same reasoning applies with $\neg c$.

Now suppose $F$ is rigid. Each current member necessarily satisfies $c(\bar a)=\top\lor c(\bar a)=\bot$, so weak inextensibility of $F$ gives this disjunction on every $F$-member at every successor. The preceding persistence and tester proofs can therefore be repeated at every successor. Thus $F\land c$ and $F\land\neg c$ are both rigid. The first has the requested current extension.

### An action-model consequence

Suppose at a point $r$ that every external set of current tuples is an admitted current row. Assume also that every rigid relation $F$ has at each arrow $h:r\to v$ the row $h[F_r]$, the precise direct image of its current members. If $r$ satisfies Plenitude, then it satisfies Rigid Power.

Indeed, given a rigid $Z\le hF$, take the preimage of its current row among the current members of $F$. The first part of the argument gives this preimage a rigid representative $Y\le F$. Exact images give $hY$ and $Z$ precisely the same current members, hence $hY=Z$ by uniqueness of rigid coextensions. Every eligible subrelation at every future point is therefore an image of an eligible current subrelation. Conversely rigidity and entailment persist. The power predicate thus has exact rows at every arrow from $r$. Composition gives the same property of each transported power predicate, proving rigidity throughout the cone. If all weakly rigid source bounds have exact rows at every source arrow, this argument also yields Weakly Rigid Power. Exactness makes each such bound fully rigid; any weakly rigid future $Z$ with the same row as the constructed rigid $hY$ is identical to it.

There is a useful criterion for individual arrows. Suppose distinctness of current tuples is necessary, and let $j:v\to r$ satisfy $jh=1_r$. For $b\in (hF)_v$ put $a=jb$. Each current tuple $x$ necessarily satisfies $\Diamond(x=a)\to x=a$, since even the current inequality would persist. Weak inextensibility gives this test at $b$. At $v$, $b=ha$ is possible via $j$, so $b=ha$ there. If $a$ were outside $F_r$, every member of $F_r$ would be necessarily distinct from $a$, contradicting $ha\in(hF)_v$. Weak persistence gives the reverse inclusion. Such an arrow therefore has exact rows on every weakly rigid source bound. In particular the preceding consequence applies to a current-full Functional Choice point if every outgoing arrow has a categorical left inverse. Functional Choice supplies the required Plenitude and necessary distinctness at the source.
