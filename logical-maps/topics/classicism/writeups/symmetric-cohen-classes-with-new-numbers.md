# Generic labelled classes and a successor with new Frege numbers

**Theorem.** A pointed model of C satisfies Second-Order Analytic Necessitism and necessary Axiom of Infinity at $t$, and refutes Arithmetic Necessitism. No world has a real number structure at any type. All Frege numbers at the source are standard. A proper successor has a new one.

We give the construction for the pure language. To obtain a named model interpret all individual names by the unique individual and relation names by bottom at their types.

## A Boolean algebra and its generic evaluation

Choose a countable transitive ground structure $M$ by collapsing a countable elementary substructure of $H_\kappa$, with $\kappa$ regular above $\beth_{\omega+\omega}$. Work with the collapse of the following objects and constructions in it. Only finite type recursions, a regular-open completion, ground countable sequences, a nonprincipal ultrafilter on the ordinary natural numbers, and the elementary forcing calculus for these sets are needed. All these fit in $H_\kappa$. In particular the natural numbers of $M$ are standard. Let $A$ be a countably infinite set of labels. Its group $G$ of finite permutations acts on $A$; the separate index set of natural numbers has trivial action. An object is supported by a finite $F\subset A$ if every permutation fixing $F$ pointwise fixes it.

A condition in $P$ gives a partition of a finite subset of $A$ and assigns a finite partial binary sequence on $\mathbb N$ to each block. Extension preserves the old equivalence and inequivalence assertions, and extends each old block's sequence. An $M$-generic filter yields a total equivalence relation and one binary real per class. Each class is infinite, there are infinitely many classes, and finitely many distinct classes have mutually Cohen-generic labels. For completeness, on a prescribed finite set $D$ one can first choose its partition. Below that decision the forcing restricted to $D$ is just the finite product of the Cohen forcings on its blocks. Restrictions keep the assigned bits on each block meeting $D$; any stronger restriction can be lifted to an extension of any full condition having the given restriction. In particular, if the class of $a$ misses $F$, its real $r_a$ is outside the subextension obtained from the restriction to $F$. This last assertion follows by restricting to $F\cup\{a\}$ below its actual partition and applying the fresh Cohen factor. Infinitude of every class and of the collection of classes, and realization of every finite labelled pattern, follow by extending on fresh labels. The entire generic filter is determined by its partition and labelled reals.

Fix such a generic and write $H$ for the associated ultrafilter on the ground regular-open completion. Put $B=\{b\in\operatorname{RO}(P)^M:b\text{ has finite support}\}$. The join in the full completion of any ground family supported as a set has finite support. For every ground family of Boolean values whose join is in $H$, one of the values is in $H$. Indeed the conditions below some member are dense below the join, and $H$ is ground-generic. For every nonzero $b\in B$ there is a $g\in G$ with $gb\in H$. Choose a condition below $b$; every finite labelled pattern occurs in the generic, and its finite carrier can be moved to its occurrence by a finite permutation.

**Locality.** Each $b\in B$ supported by $F$ belongs to the complete subalgebra for the forcing restricted to $F$. Use the dense conditions whose carriers contain $F$. If $p\le b$ and $q\le\neg b$ had compatible restrictions to $F$, a permutation fixing $F$ could move the private labels of $p$ off those of $q$. The two resulting diagrams can be amalgamated, identifying private blocks only when required through $F$; their common block bits are compatible by the hypothesis on the restrictions. This contradicts the invariance of $b$. The join of the restrictions of all $p\le b$, lifted to the full completion, is consequently $b$. This shows in particular that a ground sequence of values all supported by $F$, read at $H$, gives a real in the subextension $M[H|F]$.

## Permutation loops

First construct a one-object auxiliary model $E$, with arrows $G$. All its domains are ground sets. Put $E_e=\{*\}$. At a type $\tau=\alpha_1\ldots\alpha_k t$, let $E_\tau$ consist of the ground finitely supported functions from $\prod_i E_{\alpha_i}$ to $B$, with the ordinary action on functions. At $t$ this gives $B$. Application to a full tuple reads the resulting Boolean value at $H$. Each arrow acts on entities by its permutation.

These domains give a C model. In the ground, evaluate current formulas by Boolean operations, crisp identity on each carrier, and joins or meets over an entire quantified carrier. Evaluation is equivariant. Each result has the finite support of its parameters, and each abstraction is a supported function by the same reasoning. Ground-genericity of $H$ computes the actual existential and universal quantifiers, even when choices of individual witnesses depend on $H$. To distinguish two different functions, choose a tuple at which their values have nonzero Boolean difference and permute that difference into $H$, transporting the tuple as well. This proves intensionality and identifies the defined box with agreement of the scalar with top; in particular the Boolean-valued operation for box returns the crisp ground value of $b=1$. Partial application and abstraction commute with all arrows by equivariance.

## A second point and the arrows to it

Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter $U\in M$ on the natural numbers and an external ultrafilter $V$ extending it on all subsets of the actual natural numbers. Take as the terminal point the ordinary **external ultrapower** of the auxiliary one-point model by $V$, including its group, its action, all current-application predicates and its term operations. Its arrows are its permutation loops $G^\omega/V$. Ordinary Łoś for this many-sorted presentation, including the first-order separation and group-action laws and the operations for each fixed term, makes this a C action model with the same pure theory as $E$.

Write $D$ for the new source sorts. Put $D_e=\{*\}$. For a relational type $\tau$, a source entity consists of

1. a ground finitely supported current row $d:\prod_iD_{\alpha_i}\longrightarrow B$, and
2. the germ modulo $U$ of a ground finitely supported sequence $f:\omega\longrightarrow E_\tau$.

Here a sequence is supported as a function, with the index fixed. At $t$ the first coordinate is a single Boolean value. Source loops $g\in G$ act on both coordinates; evaluate the first coordinate at $H$. Let $\pi(d,[f]_U)=[f]_V$ in the terminal ultrapower and send the unique individual to itself. Ground equality sets belong to $M$, so $V$ agrees with $U$ on them. In particular different germs have distinct $\pi$-images. With $j$ the diagonal group embedding, $\pi g=j(g)\pi$. The arrows to the terminal are $h\pi$ for all its loops $h$; compose using this identity. Equivalently, one may index these arrows by $h$, taking the left regular action of the terminal group and the right action of the source group through $j$.

Source terms have the following ground operations. Compute their current Boolean value or current row by the same quantifier and abstraction rules as above, now over the source sorts; equality reads literal source equality as a crisp value. Compute the germ of any compound term on parameter germs by pointwise application of its **auxiliary** $E$-operation to representatives. A closed term therefore has the constant germ of its auxiliary value. These rules are well founded by induction on the term and finitely many free parameters. Pointwise application gives an admitted sequence and is independent of representatives modulo $U$. At the source, equality of full applications throughout the cone implies equality of entities: a difference in current rows is detected by a source permutation, and differing germs are distinguished by a terminal permutation and a terminal tuple after $\pi$. Conversely equal pairs agree throughout. This also verifies the defined box at the source; if written as a primitive in the calculation its current value is the crisp test for equality of the full scalar pair with top. The terminal part of each abstract agrees with its transported parameters by the ultrapower term computation. Quantifier truth at the source uses the ground-genericity of $H$, and equivariance verifies transport on its loops. Together these facts establish the required term closure and intensionality.

## No real number structure at either point

The same proof applies to the current sorts of both $E$ and the new source. They are nominal ground sets and arbitrary ground finitely supported $B$-valued current rows are admitted. At the new source one can give the row the constant bottom germ.

Suppose $(N,0,S,X,J)$ is a real number structure in the map's membership presentation. Choose a finite $F$ supporting all five entities. For each ordinary $i$, form the ground Boolean row $T_i(z)$ for being reached from zero by $i$ successor steps. It uses crisp equality for zero steps and the Boolean existential join for a further step. The rows form a ground sequence. The current row $\bigvee_{i<\omega}T_i(z)$ is admitted with support $F$. At the generic point its extension is exactly the ordinary finite successor orbit, by ground-genericity. This is an inductive property of the proposed Peano structure, and induction makes its numbers precisely this orbit. Each number is fixed by every permutation fixing $F$ and preserving the actual labelled partition, since such permutations preserve actual truth and the structure, and the finite iterates are unique. Each $x\in X$ is fixed by those permutations as well: all numbers are fixed, so $gx$ has the same members as $x$, and extensionality gives $gx=x$.

Each number and each real representative is therefore supported by $F$ for the full nominal group. To see this directly, take the least finite support $K$ of the entity. Finite supports for the finite-permutation group on an infinite set have least elements: the intersection of two finite supports supports the entity, since the pointwise stabilizer of their intersection is generated by the two pointwise stabilizers (use fresh labels to factor transpositions). Least supports are equivariant. If $a\in K\setminus F$, choose $b\notin K\cup F$ in the same infinite generic class. The transposition of $a,b$ fixes $F$ and the actual labelled partition, hence fixes the entity, but it moves $K$, a contradiction.

For each $x\in X$, the ground sequence of Boolean values
$$
 c_i(x)=\bigvee_z(T_i(z)\land J(z,x))
$$
is supported by $F$ in every coordinate. By locality, the membership trace $\{i:c_i(x)\in H\}$ belongs to $M[H|F]$. This uniform ground computation avoids any assumption that the externally selected sequence of number entities belongs to the ground. Now choose $a$ whose class misses $F$, and let $b_{a,i}$ be the Boolean value that its class label has bit $i$. The current property with row
$$
 z\longmapsto\bigvee_{i<\omega}(T_i(z)\land b_{a,i})
$$
is supported by $F\cup\{a\}$. Its actual trace on the numbers is $r_a$: distinct standard iterates are distinct by the Peano conditions. Fullness would give an $x\in X$ with exactly this trace. The fresh Cohen factor gives $r_a\notin M[H|F]$, a contradiction. This excludes a real number structure at every type. For the terminal point apply Łoś to the pure sentence excluding such a structure at each fixed type in $E$. There is thus no possible real number structure at the source, so Analytic Necessitism and its second-order strengthening both hold there.

Every countermodel to Analytic Necessitism implying Arithmetic Necessitism must exclude possible real number structures at *every* type. A single possible real structure, even at a carrier different from the counting type in a failed instance, would suffice by the [cross-type Peano matching lemma](analytic-necessitism-and-transversal-imply-arithmetic-necessitism.html#matching-numbers).

## New Frege numbers

The standard Frege numerals at $t$ form a ground sequence in each of $E$ and the source; its members are fixed by $G$. Admit their set as a current Boolean row, with values zero and one. Induction gives exactly the standard numerals as the numbers. The scalar carrier is infinite, since the class-bit Boolean values include arbitrarily large finite sets of distinct elements. Each ordinary $k$-element collection of scalars is the current extension of an admitted property (use the finite union of singleton haecceities). The ordinary finite successor/removal calculation shows that the $k$th numeral counts exactly the properties with $k$ current instances. Consequently the numerals are distinct, successor is injective on them, and no numeral counts the entire carrier. This gives $I^*_t$ and the Axiom of Infinity at $t$ in both models; Łoś gives both at the terminal point. In particular their necessitations hold at the source.

Give a source tester $W^{\nu_t t}$ the full current row. Its germ at $i$ is the auxiliary property $\lambda z.\,z\ne\mathbf i_t$. The sequence is ground and equivariant. Every current number is a standard numeral $\mathbf m_t$. Every source loop retains the full current row. At the successor the transported tester holds of $\mathbf m_t$, because $\{i:i\ne m\}\in V$; these testers and numerals are fixed under all terminal loops. Hence $W$ holds necessarily of every current number. But $n^*=[i\mapsto\mathbf i_t]_V$ is a number at the successor by Łoś, and the transported $W$ fails of it there. This violates weak inextensibility of $\mathbb N_t$ at the source despite $\Box I_t^*$, and therefore refutes Arithmetic Necessitism.

## Further properties and infinite individuals

The scalar algebra is atomless at both points. In the auxiliary algebra, if $b\ne0$ is supported by $F$, choose $a\notin F$. Both signs of the class bit $b_{a,0}$ meet $b$, by locality and by extending any condition on $F$ so that $a$ occupies a fresh class. In the germ factor the representative sequence has a uniform finite support $F$. Intersect each coordinate with the same fresh class bit. Wherever the old coordinate was nonzero, both cuts are nonzero; hence a nonzero germ splits. The source product of these two atomless algebras is atomless, and atomlessness transfers from the auxiliary algebra to the terminal by Łoś.

The same argument proves atomlessness at **every relational type**, necessarily. For a nonzero supported $B$-valued row $R$, choose a tuple $\bar x$ at which its value is nonzero. Fix a label $a$ outside a support $F$ of $R$. The tuple can be chosen with support missing $a$: move any occurrence of $a$ in its support to a fresh label while fixing $F$, preserving nonzeroness by equivariance. The value then has support in $F\cup\operatorname{supp}(\bar x)$, so both signs of $b_{a,0}$ meet it by locality. Thus pointwise intersection with this bit splits the row. For a uniformly $F$-supported sequence of rows the same $a$ splits each nonzero coordinate, even though the witnessing tuple can vary between coordinates. This proves the germ claim; the product and the ultrapower arguments give the result at both points.

Propositional Boolean Completeness fails at both points. Fix labels $a,p$. Let $d_n$ be the regular open saying the first one in the real on $p$ is at position $n$. These ground Boolean values are nonzero and pairwise disjoint. In the source use $(d_n,0)$; in the auxiliary model use $d_n$. Admit the current predicate assigning to the $n$th such element the Boolean value of bit $n$ on $a$, and zero to all other elements. This ground row has support $\{a,p\}$; at the source pair it with bottom germ. If the actual family had a least upper bound $s$, its Boolean order would satisfy $d_n\le s$ precisely for $n$ in the real on $a$, since the complement of an omitted cell is an upper bound. At the source read $d_n$ with its zero germ. The sequence of cells, each candidate $s$, and the Boolean orders of both factors are in the ground, so this would recover the fresh Cohen real as a ground real. The auxiliary failure transfers to the terminal by Łoś.

This witnesses failure of **Countable Boolean Completeness at output type $t$** as well. Use the counting type $\sigma=t$ and its numeral type $\nu_t$. The ground-supported current relation pairing each cell $d_n$ with $\mathbf n_t$ is one-to-one on the entire sequence. Its restriction to the chosen subfamily witnesses $\operatorname{Ctbl}_{t,t}$, since all its members are cells and distinct cells have distinct numeral codes. The same typed failure holds at the terminal by Łoś.

**Plenitude** fails with input type $\nu_t$ and output type $t$. Make $P(z)$ have current Boolean value $b_{a,n}$ at $\mathbf n_t$ and zero elsewhere. Take the total functional current graph
$$
 (P(z)\land y=\top)\ \lor\ (\neg P(z)\land y=\bot).
$$
If an operation $X^{\nu_t t}$ represented it, the entire entity $X(\mathbf n_t)$ would equal top exactly on the bits of $r_a$, and bottom on the others. Ground application to the ground sequence of standard numerals and the ground literal equality with top would recover $r_a$ in $M$. This fails in the auxiliary and source models and, by Łoś, at the terminal.

Even **weakly persistent comprehension** fails for this current property $P$ at $\nu_t t$. If $Q$ were a weakly persistent coextension, let $v_n$ be its ground current Boolean row at $\mathbf n_t$. Whenever $P(\mathbf n_t)$ is true, weak persistence makes $Q(\mathbf n_t)$ true on every permutation loop, so $v_n=1$: otherwise some permutation would send its nonzero complement into $H$. The canonical numeral is fixed by these permutations. If $P(\mathbf n_t)$ is false, current coextension gives $v_n\notin H$ and hence $v_n\ne1$. Thus the ground comparison $v_n=1$ again recovers $r_a$. In particular Weak Rigid Comprehension fails in both base presentations and throughout the two-point model by Łoś.

Ordinary Relational Choice fails even with both inputs and outputs propositions. Write $u_a=b_{a,0}$ in the auxiliary algebra and $u_a=(b_{a,0},0)$ at the source. These are distinct for distinct labels, since a condition can put them in different classes and assign opposite bits. Give $(u_a,u_b)$ for $b\ne a$ the Boolean value of their being in the same generic class, and give each input outside the label codes the unique output bottom. This equivariant row is admitted, with bottom germ at the source, and serial because every generic class is infinite. If an admitted functional subrelation selected one output for every input and its support were $F$, choose $a$ from a class missing $F$. Its selected $u_b$ can be exchanged with another $u_c$ in this class by a transposition fixing $a,F$ and the generic. That transposition must preserve the selection, contradicting uniqueness. This argument also applies at the auxiliary point and transfers to the terminal.

Necessity of Arithmetic also holds. At every type the ground set of standard numeral denotations gives an inductive current row in the source and the auxiliary model. Wherever the Peano condition holds they are pairwise distinct and exhaust the numbers, giving the usual first-order arithmetic; the recursion relations for sum and product have their usual values by induction on the standard numbers. Each fixed true guarded arithmetical sentence transfers from the auxiliary point to the terminal. For the possibility guard, use the equivalence in C of the local and possible Peano conditions.

In the model above the individual sort is a singleton throughout. The numeral for two never counts any individual property, and the numeral for one counts all individuals everywhere. Alternatively, use $\{*\}\sqcup A$ for both the source and auxiliary individual sorts, with the given label permutations fixing $*$. The proper map on individuals is the diagonal into the terminal ultrapower. It intertwines the group actions. The constructions of rows, germs, terms, local traces and their supports apply without change. Now every individual carrier at the two base points is infinite, and the same numeric calculation as at $t$, with Łoś at the terminal, proves necessary Axiom of Infinity at $e$. The same germ tester also refutes weak inextensibility of the individual-type numbers in this version.
