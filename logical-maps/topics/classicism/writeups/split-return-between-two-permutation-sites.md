# Both rigid powers can fail after a return-compatible entrance

A Functional Choice point can have both rigid powers and necessary Rigid Comprehension, while at one accessible point both powers fail. In particular, even necessary Tame Rigidity together with either local power does not imply necessary Weakly Rigid Power.

Use the [Functional Choice construction with supported stages](functional-choice-over-supported-stages.html). Retain its root $r$, its finite-support permutation stage $u$, and its unrestricted permutation stage $v$. Replace the bounded partial-injection stages by countably many separate sites $d_n$ whose only ordinary arrow is their identity and whose symmetry groups are trivial. The following description specifies the replacement and its consequences.

## Profiles and their direct images

Let $G$ be the full permutation group of a countably infinite set $A$ of auxiliary labels. The ordinary loops at $u,v$ are copies of $G$; the ordinary arrows $u\to v$ are $ig$ with $gi=ig$. The designated group at $u$ is $G$, and at $v$ it is trivial. There are no further ordinary arrows. At every nonroot object $b$ give the root an entrance $h_b:r\to b$ and a return $j_b:b\to r$ satisfying $j_bh_b=1_r$. An ordinary $m:b\to c$ satisfies $mh_b=h_c$ and $j_cm=j_b$. There is also the reset $h_cj_b$, distinct from every ordinary arrow. The individual sort is a singleton everywhere; give the relational names their top values.

At every relational type a root entity has an arbitrary current tuple row and one row at each nonroot object, invariant under that object's designated group. At each nonroot point the entity is an ordinary profile together with a whole root entity as an independent return coordinate. At $u$ the ordinary row at a fixed endpoint depends on a permutation only through its restriction to one uniform finite pin. The rows with target $u$ respect left $G$-equivariance. All ordinary rows at $v$ are unrestricted. At $d_n$ one arbitrary ordinary row suffices. Ordinary transport is precomposition on the ordinary profiles and leaves the return coordinate fixed. The map $j_b$ projects to the return coordinate, and $h_bY$ has return $Y$ and its specified root entrance row at every ordinary arrow to the given endpoint. Composition follows directly. The term-closure calculation in the cited model applies: at $u$ agreement on the pins transports all finitely many parameters equally through every continuation, and the designated permutations transport the satisfaction row equivariantly. The unrestricted and single-identity stages accept all required rows.

At $r$, for every tuple set $E$ let $H_E$ have current row $E$ and entrance rows $h_b[E]$. The latter are pointwise fixed by the designated groups. These are exactly the images of $E$ at every root arrow. Such a profile is rigid because exact image rows persist through composition, and every tester necessarily containing the current members contains their entire image at each arrow.

We now obtain the same conclusion for every admitted current extension at **each nonroot object**. At $u$ a current row $E$ is invariant under the stabilizer of a finite pin $P$: pinning equates the rows at the identity and at a permutation fixing $P$, and equivariance identifies the second row with its image of $E$. Set the ordinary rows to $g[E]$ at $u$ and $(ig)[E]$ at $v$. Two permutations agreeing on $P$ give the same image, so the new profile has pin $P$. Left equivariance at $u$ holds by composition. For the return coordinate use the whole root relation $H_{j_u[E]}$. On every reset it therefore has exactly the image $h_c j_u[E]$. These formulas include continuations of ordinary arrows since $j_c m=j_u$. At $v$ and at each $d_n$ set every ordinary row to the corresponding image and use $H_{j_b[E]}$ for the return; all these profiles are admitted. Thus every current extension has a rigid direct-image coextension, necessarily. Furthermore a weakly rigid relation with row $E$ must equal $H_E$. It lies below $H_E$ by testing weak inextensibility with this rigid coextension, and contains $H_E$ by the same test in the reverse direction. This proves necessary Tame Rigidity as well.

## The root powers and the missing power at the next site

At the root every input transport $h_b$ is injective. For a serial relation choose one output per source input. Prescribe the selector on that input in the current row, and on its $h_b$-image in the entrance row use the $h_b$-image of the chosen whole output. Set the selector empty elsewhere. All the specified entrance inputs and output entities are fixed by the designated symmetries. Their output rows transform equivariantly even on new target arguments, so this defines a selecting operation. This gives Functional Choice.

For a rigid root bound $H_E$, consider an eligible rigid or weakly rigid subrelation at $b$ with current extension $T\subseteq h_b[E]$. Put $D=E\cap h_b^{-1}(T)$. The root rigid subrelation $H_D$ has transport with exactly that extension, and two weakly rigid coextensions are identical. All eligible subrelations at an entrance therefore lift from the root, and all root eligible subrelations persist. Both power predicates have exact image rows on every root arrow, hence are rigid at $r$.

Fix an auxiliary label $d\in A$. At $u$ take the scalar $p_a$ which has bottom return, is false on $u$ loops, and has bit $g(a)=d$ at $ig$. These are distinct and singly pinned. The $ip_a$ are likewise distinct, and the loops permute the codes by their action on $a$. Let $F$ be the rigid exact-image relation of the set of all $p_a$ at $u$. At $v$ take the exact-image relation of $\{ip_a:a\in C\}$ for an infinite coinfinite subset $C\subset A$. This rigid subrelation of $iF$ is available there, but no stabilizer of a finite set fixes it: a suitable transposition exchanges a member of $C$ with a nonmember. The set is nonempty, so its return image is the same singleton bottom scalar as for the full bound.

Every whole property transported from $u$ along $ig$ has a finite support as a $v$-entity, by the finite pin on all its ordinary exit rows and the unchanged return coordinate. At $u$ use the tester with full ordinary loop rows and full return coordinate, assigning at every ordinary exit the set of $v$ properties fixed by some finite-set stabilizer. It necessarily accepts every current property. It misses the displayed rigid subrelation at $i$. Hence the power of $F$ is not weakly inextensible at $u$, for either the rigid or the weakly rigid membership test.

## Mathematical Possibilism and analytical structures

Countably many $d_n$ preserve the [finite ordinal calculation and the continuum of rigid labels](functional-choice-over-supported-stages.html). On one individual there are finitely many current tables for an order and its field. The uniform profiles for these tables are closed under ordinal zero and successor and under the limits of rigid subfamilies: the finite disjunction of their haecceities gives exact images at every arrow and the resulting extensional limit table is again uniform. The root ordinal benchmark is therefore finite. Every infinite root carrier has a countably infinite subset, power-closed since each smaller subset is finite, with its power comparisons all admitted by the unrestricted current rows. Every revised-small Boolean algebra at the root is consequently finite. Finitely many distinct scalar atoms at the $d_n$ generate an interval representing each such algebra, giving revised Mathematical Possibilism.

At every relational type the independent $d_n$ rows give continuum many root labels. The returns keep them distinct, and all ordinary arrows between targets preserve their corresponding labels. Every set of tuples of these labels has the rigid relation containing precisely the corresponding tuples at each object. The same construction applies a type higher to any family of such relations. Place the ordinary naturals, their powerset, successor and membership on disjoint labels. Every target property induces an ordinary subset of the labelled numbers, and the labelled family includes its real representative. For each arity take the rigid family of all displayed relations on tuples of numbers and reals. Each target predicate's restriction is represented. The singleton individual type cannot carry a real structure. This gives Second-Order Analytic Necessitism.
