# Mathematical Possibilism with necessarily infinitely many individuals

There is a model of revised Mathematical Possibilism in which individuals are necessarily infinite, propositional Boolean Completeness and Barcan at every type are necessary, and accessible worlds disagree about arithmetic, analysis and CH. There is also a model with the same three necessary principles in which arithmetic and second-order analysis are necessary. Both models lack a join for a countable family of individual properties.

The [free-word variants](stone-terminals-witnessed-possibility.html) satisfy Possibly Witnessed Possibility as well.

## The terminals and root

Work in ZFC. Use the [Stone-terminal construction](stone-terminal-maximalist.html) with countably infinitely many root individuals $I$ and only the identity root loop. The ordinary terminals are copies of one full classical one-world type hierarchy on $I$. Write $F_\tau$ for its domain at type $\tau$. A second version includes three additional one-world Henkin components, described below. Write $K^j_\tau$ for their domains. There are no arrows between terminals. Use $I=\omega\times\prod_j K^j_e$ as the root individual domain. All ordinary terminals receive the first coordinate, and each additional terminal receives its respective coordinate. The product with no additional components identifies with $\omega$. Interpret any free relational names by top and individual names by fixed individuals and their images.

Fix a strong limit cardinal $\mu$, chosen below. Let $L$ be the Boolean product of representatives of the nontrivial complete Boolean algebras of cardinality below $\mu$ and $X$ its Stone space. There is an ordinary terminal at every $x\in X$. An entity of relational type $\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_n t$ at the root has the following independent coordinates.

1. An arbitrary subset of the product of its root argument domains, its current row.
2. A locally constant map $X\to F_\tau$, with $F_\tau$ discrete.
3. One whole $K^j_\tau$ value for each additional entrance.

Application takes the corresponding slices on transported arguments. To check a lambda term at an ordinary terminal, fix all its parameters on a common clopen neighbourhood. The whole denotation there is constant, including its values on all bound arguments, because the terminal structure and the parameter values are identical. The computation applies equally to quantified bodies. At each additional terminal use its own Henkin closure. All current rows are admitted. This gives term closure and reindexing at every type. The original Stone construction proves soundness for C with its theoremhood clause for Logical Equivalence.

## The ordinal bound and Mathematical Possibilism

Let $\rho=(e\to e\to t)\to(e\to t)\to t$. Use the current test $Z$ of ordinal-type entities whose current value depends only on the order and field extensions and whose ordinary terminal value is constant on $X$. Give this tester arbitrary other rows. Zero and the successor of a member of $Z$ belong to $Z$. Indeed, current fullness makes well-orderhood and restriction to an initial segment extensional, and every ordinary terminal performs the same computation on a constant input.

For a rigid family with current members in $Z$, let $S\subseteq F_\rho$ be the set of their constant ordinary terminal values. The predicate constantly $S$ at all ordinary terminals and full elsewhere necessarily holds of every current member. It exists because the ordinary terminal hierarchy is full, even for an infinite $S$. Inextensibility bounds the family by $S$ at each ordinary terminal, and persistence gives the reverse inclusion. Its limit consequently has a constant ordinary terminal value. Its current application depends only on the order and field extensions, by the defining limit test on its current members. This proves closure of $Z$ under all the constructors of the inexact ordinals. Their impredicative definition therefore puts the entire current ordinal benchmark $H$ in $Z$.

The number of possible extension-invariant current rows is at most $B=2^{|\mathcal P(I^2)\times\mathcal P(I)|}$. Hence $|H|\leq B\,|F_\rho|\prod_j|K^j_\rho|$, a bound fixed before $X$ is chosen. Take $\mu$ above this bound and $2^{\aleph_0}$, for example by iterating the power-set cardinal operation and taking its supremum after countably many steps. In any current carrier of size at least $\mu$, a subcollection of size $\mu$ is larger than $H$ and power-closed. Every smaller subcollection $A$ has $2^{|A|}<\mu$ subsets. Code those subsets bijectively in the chosen subcollection and use membership for the graph. All these current rows are admitted. Thus every revised-small algebra has size below $\mu$.

At the root the propositional order is the product of the current two-element algebra, one copy of $2$ for each isolated component, and $\operatorname{Clop}(X)\cong L$. A nontrivial revised-small complete algebra appears as the principal lower interval supported at the top of its own factor of $L$. The trivial algebra is the interval below bottom. Current fullness admits the isomorphism graph. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism at every admitted carrier type and gives Boolean Completeness at $t$. Every terminal has proposition algebra $2$, so propositional Boolean Completeness is necessary.

## Infinity and a missing countable join

At the root, take the property of Frege cardinalities whose current row is exactly the actual standard numerals. It is inductive in the map's unboxed sense. Conversely every inductive property contains those numerals. The $n$th numeral counts the $n$-element current extensions by the finite successor and removal calculation. The root individuals therefore satisfy the quantified Axiom of Infinity. The ordinary terminals have the usual infinite domain. Each additional terminal satisfies the same assertion by its internal finite-cardinality calculation. This proves necessary individual infinity. Each entrance is onto in every type, by constant sections, independent component values and the projections on individuals. Every terminal has only its identity arrow, so Barcan holds necessarily at every type. [Frege cardinalities and the Peano condition](../index.html#background/arithmetic)

The same current numeral cut at type $t$ proves the unboxed propositional Axiom of Infinity. The root has infinitely many propositions from the $\mathcal P(\omega)$ factor, so no standard finite numeral counts its universal property. At an ordinary terminal the proposition domain has two elements. The third successor of the Frege zero is a number by induction, and no property there has three instances. That terminal sees only itself, so the number cannot even possibly count a property. This refutes necessary Potential Infinity at $t$ for all four choices of root frame and component list.

The factor $\mathcal P(\omega)$ of $L$ has infinitely many atoms. They give distinct isolated points $x_n$ of $X$. Take the property $U_n$ of individuals with empty current and additional-component rows, value $\{n\}$ at $x_n$ and empty value at every other ordinary terminal. The family and the injection $U_n\mapsto\overline n_e$ exist as current rows. In a least upper bound the value at $x_n$ would have to be exactly $\{n\}$, since restricting an upper bound to that value at this one clopen singleton leaves an upper bound. Every continuous map from compact $X$ to discrete $F_{et}$ has finite range. The displayed family therefore has no join.

## Choice and atoms

Choose an external well-order of each relevant root domain, a constant ordinary terminal well-order of the corresponding domain, and an internal well-order at each additional component. Their independent rows give a necessary well-order at each type. The proposition true just at the root is a strong atom, as is the true proposition at each terminal. For a relational atom supported entirely at ordinary terminals, two different points of support could be separated by a clopen, splitting the atom. Its nonempty support is clopen by local constancy, so it must be a single isolated point. Its value there must be a singleton tuple. The other atomic possibilities are one current tuple or a singleton in one independent component. All atom transports are therefore atoms or bottom, giving necessary strongness of atoms. The factor given by the regular open algebra of Cantor space has no atoms. Its supporting proposition is nevertheless a self-atom since every ordinary terminal where it is true is classical. Atomicity at $t$ and the claim that all propositional self-atoms are atoms both fail.

Any current row has a coextension empty at every proper exit. It is weakly inextensible at the root, since every required future inclusion at an exit is vacuous. It remains weakly inextensible at every terminal. This gives necessary Inextensible Comprehension. A weakly rigid relation at the root is rigid since all proper targets have only the identity arrow. This gives necessary Tame Rigidity.

## Rigidity of the rigidity predicate

Fix a terminal relation $T$ of any arity. For each argument type lift its entities to the root by holding all other independent coordinates fixed. For an individual use the coordinate product. For a relational argument use arbitrary current data and the given terminal value, choosing a constant ordinary section whenever the ordinary terminal is the chosen one. Take as the new relation's current extension the resulting lifted tuples of $T$. At every terminal give it the exact image of these tuples. At the chosen component the image is $T$. At others it is empty or a fixed singleton tuple, with all ordinary terminal images identical. These rows exist even in the Henkin components. For propositions take the empty tuple in the same construction.

This relation is rigid. It is persistent by construction. A tester necessarily accepting all old tuples accepts every member at every terminal because every such member is their image. Inextensibility at a terminal is automatic. Thus every terminal relation has a rigid representative at the root. Applying the same exact-image argument to the property of being rigid gives Rigid Rigidity. Tame Rigidity identifies the weakly rigid and rigid relations at every point, giving Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity as well. Both properties hold necessarily. Together with the atomless factor this gives a counterexample to the implication from necessary Actuality and necessary Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity to Atomicity, already at propositional type.

## A power obstruction using only ordinary terminals

Choose distinct isolated points $x_*,x_0,x_1,\ldots$ from the $\mathcal P(\omega)$ factor, and distinct labels $b_j=\{j\}$ in the ordinary terminal type $\sigma=et$. For each $n$ let $\pi_n$ exchange $b_{2n}$ and $b_{2n+1}$ and fix the other labels. Define a root entity $d_j$ of type $\sigma$ with value $\pi_n(b_j)$ at $x_n$ and $b_j$ at every other ordinary point. For a fixed $j$ its section differs from $b_j$ only on a single clopen point, so it is continuous. Use empty current rows and bottom in the additional components. In the free-word versions repeat this entire choice at every word. Then every root word fixes $d_j$.

Let $D=\{d_j:j\in\omega\}$ and $B=\{b_j:j\in\omega\}$. Form a predicate $R$ with word rows $D$, constant ordinary value $B$, and singleton $\{\bot_\sigma\}$ in each additional component. Each row is exactly the image of the current members of $R$. This gives both persistence and inextensibility throughout the cone, so $R$ is rigid.

At $x_*$ the set $A=\{b_{2n}:n\in\omega\}$ is a rigid subproperty of $B$. Suppose a current weakly rigid $Y\le R$ transported to $A$ there. At each isolated ordinary terminal $x$, the extension of $Y$ is exactly the projection of its current extension. Persistence gives one inclusion. For the reverse, any terminal element outside the projection can be omitted by a tester full everywhere else, including every root-word row and every other word's terminal section. The exceptional point is clopen and the test's ordinary range is finite. Weak inextensibility gives the required exclusion.

At $x_*$ the distinct values $b_j$ now force the current members of $Y$ to be precisely $d_{2n}$. Its values at the $x_n$ must then be $\pi_n[A]$, all different. A continuous section on compact $X$ into the discrete terminal domain has finite range, a contradiction. Finally take a tester on subproperties that accepts everything except the entity $A$ at this one $x_*$ entrance. Every current weakly rigid subproperty of $R$ necessarily satisfies the tester, yet the future rigid and weakly rigid powers both contain $A$. Their weak inextensibility fails. The proof applies to all four variants, including those with only standard terminals.

At every root object the proposition supported just at its identity word witnesses distinctness-preserving necessity of every truth. At a terminal each truth is necessary, so the same implication holds necessarily throughout. The closed pure sentence $\exists p\,(p\land\Diamond\neg p)$ holds at a root, witnessed by the identity proposition. At an ordinary terminal it is impossible. This refutes its instance of B for pure sentences. [Free-word construction](stone-terminals-witnessed-possibility.html)

## Agreement with standard terminals

In the version with only ordinary terminals, current fullness at every point gives exactly the standard Frege numerals, with the ordinary arithmetic, whenever the Peano condition holds. The root domains are infinite at every type (the propositional algebra is infinite and the empty-argument distinctions also give infinitely many distinct relations at each positive arity). At a classical terminal a finite counting domain fails the Peano condition. Thus all instances of Necessity of Arithmetic hold. When the Peano condition is necessary, the actual numbers transport to exactly the standard numerals everywhere by definability and remain pairwise distinct. Exact transport of the membership sets gives persistence and inextensibility at each point, so Arithmetic Necessitism holds.

A real number structure at any point has the standard natural numbers by full local induction. By local fullness and extensionality its reals represent exactly their ground-model powerset, and quantification over relations on these carriers ranges over all such relations. Every real number structure at every point therefore has the same full second-order analytical theory. This proves Necessity of Second-Order Analysis, including Non-contingency of CH in this version.

In both versions the $\mathcal P(\omega)$ factor gives at least continuum many distinct root propositions. Choose a real number structure on propositions using current fullness. An ordinary terminal has only two propositions. Even the first three numerals of a real number structure must be distinct. Thus no such structure at $t$ can be a real number structure necessarily. This refutes both Analytic Necessitism principles at this type.

## Components that disagree

Write ZC for Zermelo set theory with Choice, using the well-ordering form of Choice and omitting Replacement. In ZFC, $V_{\omega+\omega}$ is a model of ZC. In particular its finite products, power sets, subsets in the ambient universe, and well-orders of its sets all have rank below $\omega+\omega$. Gödel's second incompleteness theorem and completeness give a countable model $M_0$ of $\mathrm{ZC}+\neg\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZC})$. Its natural numbers are nonstandard.

Choose also countable models $M_+$ and $M_-$ of ZC in which the structural formulation of CH has opposite truth values. This asserts that every subset of $\mathcal P(\omega)$ injects into $\omega$ or is in bijection with $\mathcal P(\omega)$. For the positive theory use $V_{\omega+\omega}$ as computed in $L$. For the negative theory, adding $\aleph_2$ Cohen reals is ccc and forces the indicated rank segment to satisfy ZC and the negative structural statement. A finite proof of contradiction from that theory in the ground universe would remain a proof in the forcing extension. Thus the negative theory too is consistent in the ground universe, and completeness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem supply the models. The structural formulation requires only finite-rank powersets and function graphs above $\omega$. [The map's construction from components disagreeing on CH](coalesced-two-extensional-ch-models-root.html)

For each of $M_0,M_+,M_-$ use as an additional component its internal full hierarchy over its own $\omega$. The external individuals form a countably infinite set. At each standard relational type take the external members of its internal power set of the argument product. Induction on typed formulas gives internal and external Henkin satisfaction in agreement, and Separation gives closure under every lambda term with parameters. The Frege numbers at individual type identify with the internal natural numbers, with internal addition and multiplication. At type $et$ the singleton sets code the numbers, all internal subsets of $\omega$ serve as reals, and the membership and successor graphs are internal. These are real number structures in the sense of the map. [Coalescing components that disagree about arithmetic](coalesced-two-extensional-arithmetics-root.html)

An ordinary terminal and $M_0$ now disagree on the fixed arithmetical sentence $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZC})$. Both satisfy the Peano condition at $e$, so this refutes Necessity of Arithmetic. Reading the same sentence in their real number structures at $et$ refutes Necessity of Analysis. The structures at $et$ in $M_+$ and $M_-$ disagree on CH. Hence both necessity disjuncts in Non-contingency of CH fail at that one type.

Finally, omit one internal nonstandard Frege number in the $M_0$-row of a root tester and make it full elsewhere. Its exceptional row is an internal complement of a singleton. Every root Frege number counted at $e$ necessarily satisfies it, since each is a standard numeral and transports to the corresponding standard numeral. The omitted object is a number in the $M_0$ component. The number property therefore fails weak inextensibility despite the necessary Peano condition. This refutes Arithmetic Necessitism.

### A rigid bound whose power is extensible

Choose an external bijection $b:\omega\to\omega^{M_0}$ and fixed individuals $c_+,c_-$ in the two CH components. Take a root property $F$ of individuals with current members $(n,b(n),c_+,c_-)$, and exactly their images in each terminal. These terminal values are whole domains or singletons. Thus $F$ is rigid by exact transport.

Choose $A\subseteq\omega$ such that $b[A]$ is not the extension of any internal subset of $\omega^{M_0}$. Such an $A$ exists because $M_0$ is countable. At an isolated ordinary Stone terminal $x$, $A$ is a rigid subrelation of the whole image of $F$. If a root weakly rigid $U\le F$ transported to $A$ there, its current tuples would project to exactly $A$ at $x$. Persistence gives one inclusion. For the other, any value outside the projected current set can be omitted by a tester full everywhere else and omitting only that value at $x$. This tester exists since $x$ is clopen. The same argument works at the isolated $M_0$ component using the internal complement of a singleton. Thus the current tuples of $U$ would be exactly $(n,b(n),c_+,c_-)$ for $n\in A$, and its $M_0$ value would have extension $b[A]$. No such $U$ exists.

At the higher type now take a tester full everywhere except at $x$, where it omits just the entity $A$. Every current weakly rigid subrelation of $F$ necessarily satisfies it. The future power at $x$ includes $A$, so its weak inextensibility fails. This refutes both power principles despite necessary Rigid Rigidity and necessary individual infinity. The separation of Rigid Rigidity from Rigid Power without these additional premises was already known for Dorr's coalesced arithmetic model.
