# Mathematical Possibilism requires infinitely many propositional atoms

At a point satisfying revised Mathematical Possibilism, the current property of being a propositional atom has no Frege-finite cardinality. In particular, for every external finite $k$ the propositional entailment algebra has at least $k$ atoms. These atoms can all be found below a single proposition whose principal interval has exactly $2^k$ members. The finite cases extend the two-element-algebra argument for Atomlessness. ## Finite powers at a point

Let $U$ be a current Frege-finite property of propositions. There is a Frege-finite property $L$ of predicates of propositions such that their current extensions lie in $U$ and give exactly one representative for every admitted current subset of $U$. Prove this by Frege induction on the cardinality of $U$, quantifying over all properties it counts. For the empty $U$ take $L$ to contain only the constantly false predicate. At a successor count, remove a member $a$ and construct $L'$ for the remainder. Take the members of $L$ to be the members $x$ of $L'$ and the predicates $x\lor H_a$ for $x\in L'$, where $H_a(p)$ means $p=a$. The latter rows add $a$, so the two copies are disjoint and each copy has distinct current extensions. Restricting any proposed subset to the remainder gives its unique old representative. Its membership decision on $a$ gives the required representative in $L$. Existential quantification over $L'$ defines the new property; the graph adding $H_a$ is an admitted bijection of the two copies. A bijective image of a Frege-finite carrier is Frege-finite, by transporting the single-element removals in the successor clause; disjoint finite unions are finite by the same induction. Thus $L$ is finite at its own sort. All these constructions use current parameters.

Order $L$ by inclusion of current extensions. The usual unions, intersections and complements relative to $U$ exist by the preceding representation property and are unique. For an arbitrary admitted subfamily $Y$ of $L$, its union on $U$ is given by $p\mapsto U(p)\land\exists x\,(Y(x)\land L(x)\land x(p))$. Its representative is the supremum. Hence this is a complete Boolean algebra with finite carrier, and therefore small for revised Mathematical Possibilism. Its atoms are precisely the representatives of the singletons of $U$. The relation pairing each $a\in U$ with its unique singleton representative is admitted.

Suppose the scalar atoms themselves form a Frege-finite property. Take $U$ to consist of those atoms together with $\bot$. Mathematical Possibilism supplies an isomorphism from the algebra above onto a principal interval of the scalar entailment order. An atom of that interval is an ambient atom, since every scalar below it lies in the interval. Composing the two represented graphs injects $U$ into the property of scalar atoms, contradicting finite pigeonhole. This argument applies also to a hypothetical nonstandard internal cardinality.

For the external finite version one can work entirely with closed labels. Starting with $\bot,\top$, form all finite unions of the haecceities of a list of distinct labels to get a longer list at the next type. Each union has exactly the indicated members at the current point. After finitely many iterations choose $k$ labels together at one type, and form their $2^k$ unions. Their order by inclusion of current extensions represents the required small algebra. Its principal representation supplies $k$ scalar atoms. This version and the internal argument use no choice.

## Scalar atoms in the named models

The following counts concern the designated object. All the scalar sectors used below are definable by the target, the indicated finite tests, or both. They are independent of the interpretation of signature constants and of choosing the pinning base or a singleton for the individuals.

* In **symmetric-collapse-pair** and **symmetric-qualitative-links** the permutation sector is a single atom by left symmetry. Every other arrow is a collapser. The published splitting proofs take a nonempty pinned part of that sector, keep its finite prescription, and vary a fresh merge. In the qualitative model the new point and its companions are included in the extension of the prescription. The two resulting arrows can be separated by a finite equality test. There is exactly one scalar atom in each model. [Fixed-pair collapsers](../index.html#model/symmetric-collapse-pair) · [Qualitative links](../index.html#model/symmetric-qualitative-links)
* In **symmetric-range-gap**, the sector of permutations fixing the distinguished point is one left orbit. Every other arrow omits that point. Given any finite pin and any allowed values on it, choose two fresh inputs and send them to the same or to different nondistinguished outputs. Equality of their images separates two arrows in the original pinned cylinder. This leaves exactly the permutation atom. [Range gap](../index.html#model/symmetric-range-gap)
* In **symmetric-collapse-pair-and-point** at the natural-number object, the permutation sector and the unique exit to the point each give one atom. In the remaining sector the old collapser argument also splits any cylinder that contains a constant map. Keep its values on the pin together with the distinguished pair, send a fresh input either to the pair's image or elsewhere, and cover the target by the remaining inputs. Both extensions are surjective collapsers in the same cylinder. There are exactly two scalar atoms. [Collapsers and a point](../index.html#model/symmetric-collapse-pair-and-point)
* In **symmetric-two-object-all-maps** and **finite-support-two-object-all-maps** at the natural-number object, the sole map to the singleton gives one atom. At an endomap with a finite prescribed restriction, two fresh inputs can have equal or unequal images. That equality test is also invariant under every target permutation in the symmetric model. Thus the singleton exit is the only scalar atom. [Symmetric all-maps model](../index.html#model/symmetric-two-object-all-maps) · [Finite-support all-maps model](../index.html#model/finite-support-two-object-all-maps)
* In **symmetric-two-object-unpinned** and **symmetric-finite-image-target** the permutation loops at the initial object form one atom. Every function to the other natural-number object is an entrance. The same fresh-input equality test splits each nonempty supported part of the entrance sector. Restrictions on subsequent loops at the target do not enter this count. [Unpinned target](../index.html#model/symmetric-two-object-unpinned) · [Finite-image target](symmetric-finite-image-target.html)
* At either object of **symmetric-qualitative-contrast**, every arrow to $W_0$ is a permutation, and all of them lie in one orbit under its full target symmetry group. The target symmetry at $W_1$ is trivial. If an occupied arrow to $W_1$ has its values prescribed on a finite pin, choose a fresh input $x$ and two target values outside the image of the pin. Two permutations extending the prescription can take $x$ to those respective values. The finite test that $x$ takes the first value splits the cylinder. This gives one atom at either choice of designated object. [First object](../index.html#model/symmetric-qualitative-contrast) · [Second object](../index.html#model/symmetric-qualitative-contrast-at-w1)
* In **isolated-root-before-symmetric-paired-stages** the current truth bit gives one atom. The old scalar factor is atomless, so there is no second atom. [Isolated entrance](isolated-entrances-and-modal-necessities.html)

In an independent-numeral expansion the sentence asserting a specified finite bound on the number of scalar atoms remains true because the complete pure theory at the point is preserved. The finite obstruction therefore transfers to the indicated source settings. [Independent expansions](independent-numeral-predicates.html)
