# Injective integer stages

This model satisfies Logical Necessity, necessary Strong Actuality and necessary Boolean Completeness. At every stage it has a weakly rigid individual property that loses weak inextensibility after one entrance. Its actual extension has no inextensible coextension. The construction combines the stage translations of the [paired integer model](symmetric-integer-paired-stages.html) with the injections and single new individual of the [upper-point model](one-new-individual-upper-point.html).

## The action

Choose $\kappa\ge 2^{\aleph_0}$ and use a pool of size $\kappa^+$ at each coordinate $i\in\mathbb Z$. At stage $n$ the $i$th individual set is its ordinary pool together with one marker $*_i$ if $i\le n$, and just the ordinary pool otherwise. Individuals form the disjoint union of these sets. At an unmarked target all local arrows are injections. At a marked target arrows are injections into the ordinary pool, or, when the source is also marked, permutations preserving the marker. The arrows $n\to m$ consist of all products of permitted local arrows together with a free-word label from a countably infinite alphabet. Compose by coordinates and concatenate words. The target symmetry group consists of the coordinatewise permutations of the ordinary pools, fixing every marker and bearing the empty word.

A relational entity is a left-equivariant profile pinned by at most $\kappa$ source individuals: at the same target and word, agreement of two underlying maps on the pin gives equality of their whole rows. Transport is precomposition. Transport takes a pin to its image, and agreement on a pin persists under every postcomposition. Term evaluation at a target on transported parameters therefore gives the pinning and equivariance of each abstraction, uniformly over its bound arguments. The admitted type domains give an action model of C. Evaluate at stage zero.

## Strong Actuality and joins

At a fixed source define an arrow kind by its target, its word and, at each coordinate with two marked ends, whether the source marker is preserved. All the source markers together form an admitted pin. In a given kind choose one representative $b$, coordinatewise bijective onto each permitted range. For any arrow $f$ in that kind and set $S$ of at most $\kappa$ source individuals, a target symmetry $g$ has $gb|_S=f|_S$. At each coordinate extend the induced partial bijection of ordinary points to a permutation, possible since the ordinary pool has size $\kappa^+$. On the markers use the specified kind. Each kind consequently is a propositional atom, and every proposition is a union of kinds.

At $n$ let $P_n$ consist of the empty-word endomorphisms keeping all markers. This is a true atom. After any arrow with nonempty word or one losing an original marker it is impossible: an ordinary point at a future marked target cannot become its marker. Otherwise the target $m$ satisfies $m\ge n$. A return to $n$ making $P_n$ true must have empty word and preserve precisely the marker conditions at all $i\le n$. At each $n<i\le m$ its target is unmarked and there is only the injection kind. This describes exactly one return kind, a propositional atom. So $P_n$ is a true strong atom at every stage.

For a family $X$ of relations at $n$, enlarge its pin $N$ by all source markers. At each kind representative $b$ take the union $J_b$ of the $b$-rows of its current members. If a target symmetry $g$ fixes $bN$, there is a source symmetry $u$ fixing $N$ with $gb=bu$. Restrict $g$ to the range of $b$ in each coordinate and conjugate by the bijection onto that range. Each range is group-invariant and each marked source point belongs to $N$, so the conjugate preserves all source markers. It permutes the current members of $X$. Hence $J_b$ is invariant under every target symmetry fixing $bN$. Extend it to an arrow $f$ of that kind by $J_f=gJ_b$ whenever $gb$ agrees with $f$ on $N$. This is well defined, equivariant and $N$-pinned; the finite word and target are among the allowed row arguments, and the marker part of $N$ distinguishes all kinds. To compare the resulting profile with any one family member or any upper bound, interpolate at the union of their two pins. Inclusion of representative rows is exactly the native order. This proves necessary Boolean Completeness at every relational type, including arbitrarily high typed arguments.

## A weakly rigid property at each stage

Fix $i=n+1$. Take $F$ whose row at every arrow from $n$ consists of the ordinary individuals in coordinate $i$ of the target. At $n$ these are all the individuals in that coordinate and they necessarily stay ordinary. To prove weak inextensibility, let a tester necessarily accept all these individuals and let $y$ be ordinary in coordinate $i$ at an arrow $h$. Modify $h$ only at that coordinate, keeping it fixed on the tester's pin. If $y$ is absent from the image of that pin, extend the partial injection so that a fresh source individual maps to $y$. There are always enough unused ordinary individuals. The coordinate's source is unmarked, so this remains an allowed arrow of the same kind. The tester has the same row there as at $h$, hence accepts $y$ at $h$. This proves weak rigidity at $n$.

Let $b:n\to n+1$ be the inclusion, preserving the old marks and bearing the empty word. Its new coordinate marker $*_i$ is absent from the row of $bF$. Therefore all actual members there are necessarily different from $*_i$, since every individual map is injective. Return by $c:n+1\to n$, an injection on coordinate $i$ and the identity everywhere else, with empty word. The row of $F$ at $cb$ contains $c(*_i)$. Weak inextensibility has failed at $b$.

More generally, let $Y$ be any inextensible coextension of the coordinate at $n$. The tester of being ordinary in coordinate $i$ necessarily accepts all its current members. Weak inextensibility at $n$ forces $Y_b$ below that tester. But $cb$ is an endomorphism preserving all original markers; on a pin of $Y$ it agrees with a base symmetry. Since the current row is exactly the whole coordinate, $Y_{cb}$ is again that whole coordinate. This includes $c(*_i)$, contradicting weak inextensibility at $b$ as above. The same two arguments at each stage refute Tame Rigidity and Inextensible Comprehension there.

## Logical Necessity and other verdicts

Translation of the integers, relabelling stage and coordinate together, preserves the pure structure. For the recorded one-proposition signature, assign one distinct final free letter to each pure matrix with an actual witness for its proposition variable at stage zero. Prescribe the name on all arrows ending in this letter by the whole witness profile with the final letter stripped; on remaining words use bottom. The countably many pins have size at most $\kappa$ in union. Transport by that letter at the identity coefficient realizes the entire chosen witness. Translation ensures every pure matrix instantiated at any accessible stage is instantiated at zero. Conversely any possible named instance is an instance there after forgetting its name and using translation. This gives Logical Necessity with a nonvacuous signature.

Propositional Barcan fails. At $m=n+1$ the scalar event that an empty-word endomorphism preserves the $i$th marker is invariant under the site's symmetries. No proposition transported from $n$ to $m$ can distinguish keeping this marker from sending it into the ordinary pool: the composites of either empty-word arrow with the given entrance have the same kind at $n$. Use a tester excluding this event at all arrows from $n$ ending at $m$, full elsewhere. Every original proposition necessarily passes; the excluded one fails at $m$.

Ordinary Relational Choice fails at $e$: for any proposed membership selector, interchange two ordinary individuals in the same coordinate outside its pin. The intension of their pair of haecceities is fixed while the two candidates are exchanged. The pure standard Frege numeral terms at each type provide invariant rows at each target, hence an admitted current numerical cut. Every stage has infinitely many individuals and propositions (the distinct word flags suffice). Thus both Axioms of Infinity hold necessarily, and the guarded arithmetic is always standard. Under the necessary Peano hypothesis its number predicate has exact images and necessarily distinct members, giving Arithmetic Necessitism.

The integer-stage and old-and-new models whose methods are used here have their original attributions in the two source write-ups. These injective-stage results are ordinary mathematical proofs with two independent reviews.

## Atomicity, Transversal and Intensional Choice

For Atomicity take a tuple $y$ in a nonempty row of a relation $F$. Keep the arrow's kind and its restriction to a pin of $F$. Extend that restriction to an arrow $b$ whose range contains a support of $y$ apart from the target markers, which all target symmetries fix. Enlarge the source pin to $L$ by the chosen preimages and all source markers. At just this kind, take the tuples $gy$ where $g$ is a target symmetry and the arrow agrees with $gb$ on $L$. This is one tuple at each occupied arrow, as $bL$ and the target markers support $y$. It lies below $F$. Any further pin used by a proposed nonempty subrelation still fits the bound. Within this kind a target symmetry aligns any two occupied arrows on that larger pin together with $L$, by extending the induced partial bijections. The same symmetry carries their specified tuples to one another. The proposed subrelation must contain every such tuple, proving atomhood.

For Transversal, a support of an actual extension $E$ is a pin whose pointwise stabilizer preserves $E$. Include original markers in all supports. If $P,Q$ support $E$, so does $P\cap Q$. For a source symmetry $g$ fixing their intersection, first move $Q\setminus P$ away from $P\cup gP$ by a symmetry fixing $P$. The moved $Q$ still supports $E$. Extend $g|P$ to a symmetry fixing the moved $Q$; it acts on $E$ as $g$ does and fixes $E$. The spare ordinary points exist in every coordinate. For each $E$ take its representative with empty rows except at empty-word endomorphisms $f$ that agree with some source symmetry $g$ on a support of $E$; there give it row $gE$. Two such witnesses agree on the intersection of their supports, and any chosen support pins the profile by the same intersection argument. Its identity row is $E$. Under a source symmetry $u$ this representative transports to the representative of $uE$ by conjugating supports. Their family therefore has an invariant current row and is admitted, for example by using that row at all endomorphisms with empty word and empty rows elsewhere. It selects exactly one entity per coextension class.

For Intensional Choice fix a necessarily inhabited profile $F$ and a pin $N$ containing the original markers. At each stage, word and injection kind choose a representative $b$ as above, a member $y$ of its row and a target support $T$ for $y$. For each ordinary point in $T\setminus bN$ assign a distinct fresh ordinary source probe in the corresponding coordinate outside $N$. For an arrow $f$ of the same kind prescribe a target symmetry sending $bN$ to $fN$ by the pointwise correspondence. Send the remaining ordinary support points to the images under $f$ of their assigned probes. All prescribed ordinary values are distinct by injectivity, and every marker is fixed. The prescription extends to a target symmetry $g$. Its value $gy$ is uniquely determined by the prescription and belongs to $F_f$ by equivariance and pinning. There are at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ kinds including words and targets. The union of their probes with $N$ has size at most $\kappa$. The singleton rows $\{gy\}$ form an admitted selector at every reachable arrow. All three arguments apply at each stage.
