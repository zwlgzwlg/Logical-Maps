# Necessary rigid powers with disagreement about arithmetic

Both Rigid Power and Weakly Rigid Power hold necessarily in a model with revised Mathematical Possibilism and necessarily infinitely many individuals. Its classical terminal worlds disagree about arithmetic and the continuum hypothesis. In particular neither necessary power principle implies Necessity of Arithmetic.

## Four extra coordinates

Start with the [universal Boolean retraction](universal-boolean-retraction.html). Its two base individual sets are $E^r_e=\mathbb N$ and $E^s_e=\mathbb N\sqcup\{\star\}$. The entrance includes $\mathbb N$ and the return $j$ sends $\star$ to zero. The entrance indices form $P=A\sqcup B$, with $B$ countably infinite. The ideal $J$ on $P$ restricts to an ideal $K$ on $A$ and to the finite-set ideal on $B$. The quotient $\mathcal P(A)/K$ is the product $L$ of representatives of all nontrivial complete Boolean algebras of cardinality below a strong limit $\mu$. Choose $\mu$ after the bound in the last section. The construction of $K$ and the componentwise Boolean operations are those of the universal Boolean retraction.

Fix four classical one-world Henkin models $C_d$, with carriers $C^d_\sigma$. The first is the full hierarchy over $\mathbb N$. For the others use the internal full hierarchies on the naturals of countable models of Zermelo set theory with Choice, denoted ZC. Choose one model of $\mathrm{ZC}+\neg\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZC})$, one of $\mathrm{ZC}+\mathrm{CH}$, and one of $\mathrm{ZC}+\neg\mathrm{CH}$. In ZFC, $V_{\omega+\omega}$ models ZC. Incompleteness and completeness give the first countable model. The constructible universe and a Cohen extension violating CH give consistency of ZC with the indicated two signs by their corresponding rank segments, hence countable models by completeness. The internal full hierarchy at each standard finite type gives a classical Henkin model with the required internal arithmetic, real structures and internal well-orders. [Detailed component construction](stone-terminals-infinite-individuals.html)

Write $A_d$ for the product of the appropriate argument carriers at $d=r,s$ in a relational type $\tau=\bar\sigma t$. Define, recursively in the types,
\[
 E^r_\tau=\mathcal P(A_r)\times
  \bigl(\mathcal P(A_s)^P/{=}_J\bigr)\times\prod_{d=1}^4 C^d_\tau,
 \qquad E^s_\tau=\mathcal P(A_s)\times E^r_\tau.
\]
The quotient identifies sequences whose **entire** rows agree outside a set in $J$. Return by $j(S,x)=x$. Choose representative entrance rows $U_i^x$ for each $x\in E^r_\tau$ and put $h_i x=(U_i^x,x)$, so $jh_i=1$ literally. Write $p_d$ for the projection from $E^r_\tau$ onto $C^d_\tau$. At individual type $p_d$ sends the root individuals to any one fixed individual in $C_d$.

Use the finite-history construction of the [reduced-power retraction](reduced-power-retraction.html). At an $r$-stage based on an index ultrafilter $D$ on $I$, each old entrance chooses a $J$-disjoint ultrafilter on $P$ separately at each outer index. It passes to the corresponding Fubini product and applies the $h_i$ pointwise. At $s$ the sole nonidentity immediate step retains $D$ and returns by $j$. In addition, at **every** $r$-stage there are four exit arrows, retaining $D$ and applying $p_d$ pointwise, into the classical one-world models $C_d^I/D$. Such a terminal has no further proper arrow. Compose all arrows along their finite histories. At a later exit after any number of returns, the original terminal coordinates have been carried diagonally through the intervening products.

For the action-model check, distinct relational entities at $r$ differ in the current row, on a $J$-positive set of entrance rows, or in one of the four terminal coordinates. A $J$-positive set belongs to some $J$-disjoint ultrafilter. A differing terminal coordinate is distinguished at its projection by a tuple there, using extensionality of that terminal. At an ultrapower stage one of these finitely many kinds of disagreement occurs on a $D$-large set; choose the required local ultrafilters or tuples on that set. Distinct $s$-entities differ currently or after their return.

In computing each $r$-term on parameters, keep the original current row and the entrance row evaluated on $h_i$ of the parameters. For its $d$-coordinate evaluate the same term on $p_d$ of the parameters in the whole model $C_d$. Abstraction there ranges over all terminal arguments. The $s$-term has its own current row and the corresponding $r$-term on the returned parameters. The $J$-large equality of entire entrance rows in the original term calculation is unaffected, and $jh_i=1$ recovers the extra coordinates along with the original ones. Thus all terms commute with every entrance, return and exit. Current truth and term evaluation at the iterated stages satisfy Łoś by the same induction, using usual many-sorted Łoś at the four terminal kinds. This verifies the C action-model clauses, including the comparison of abstractions of H-provably equivalent formulas at all continuations.

## Why all the powers remain rigid

At base $r$, every weakly rigid relation has finitely many current tuples. Suppose distinct current members are $x_n$ for $n\in\omega$. For each $n$ weak persistence puts $h_i x_n$ in the $i$th entrance row for cofinitely many $i\in B$, since all free ultrafilters on $B$ are allowed. Choose $k(i)\le i$ tending to infinity in the cofinite filter such that $h_i x_{k(i)}$ belongs to that row on a cofinite set of $B$. For example use the largest $k\le i$ for which the first $k+1$ tests hold, where defined. Omit that one tuple at those indices in an otherwise full tester. Put top on $A$ and **every terminal coordinate**. Each fixed current tuple passes at all but finitely many indices by injectivity of $h_i$. The return restores the original tuple, and future entrances with iterated indices satisfy the same almost-everywhere observation. Every arithmetic exit also accepts it. Thus all current tuples necessarily pass, yet the variable tuple fails at the first free-ultrafilter entrance on $B$. This contradicts weak inextensibility.

A weakly rigid finite bound is the finite union of the haecceities of its actual members. Persistence gives one containment, and testing inextensibility against that union gives the other, including on all four exits. The union is rigid. Both power predicates below it are rigid by the [finite-bound theorem](../index.html#background/rigidity).

At base $s$, let $G=(S,H)$ be weakly rigid. The current row of $H$ is $j[S]$. Persistence gives inclusion of these projected members. For the reverse, use a tester full at $s$ whose return has current row $j[S]$, full entrance rows and full terminal coordinates. To see that $H$ is weakly inextensible, lift any $r$-tester $T$ necessarily accepting $j[S]$ to $(\top,T)$ at $s$. Persistence of $H$ follows from persistence of the chosen preimage of each member in $S$. Therefore $H$ is the finite haecceity union of $j[S]$. Conversely any $(S,H)$ with rigid $H$ and that current row is rigid, since every proper continuation from $s$ factors through $j$. This proves Tame Rigidity locally at both base kinds and at the classical kinds, hence at each iterated stage by Łoś.

For a proper continuation $fj$ from $s$, any weakly rigid subrelation of $fH$ is a union of haecceities of a subset of the finitely many transported tuples. Choose a subset $T\subseteq j[S]$ having exactly those images, allowing different tuples to coincide after transport. It lifts to the rigid subrelation
\[
 \bigl(S\cap j^{-1}[T],\;\bigvee_{t\in T}H_t\bigr)
\]
of $G$, where $H_t$ denotes the tuple-haecceity. At the identity the subrelation lifts to itself. Thus every future eligible subrelation, including every such relation at an arithmetic terminal, necessarily satisfies any tester that necessarily accepts every current eligible subrelation. This gives weak inextensibility of both power predicates. They are persistent because rigidity and entailment persist and weak rigidity coincides with full rigidity throughout. Tame Rigidity gives full rigidity. Both powers hold at base $r,s$ and in the one-world classical models, and all their fixed type instances pass by Łoś to every iterated stage.

## Arithmetic, CH and small Boolean algebras

At both retraction base kinds the current subsets of each carrier are unrestricted, so the standard finite Frege numerals form an available inductive current row. Their individual domains are infinite, giving the quantified individual Axiom of Infinity. Each terminal has its own internal infinite individual set. By Łoś the assertion holds throughout the model. At the direct exits into the standard and arithmetically nonstandard terminals the Peano guard holds at type $e$ and the arithmetical sentence $\operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZC})$ has opposite truth values. Thus Necessity of Arithmetic fails. The same arithmetic is interpreted in their real number structures at $et$, giving failure of Necessity of Analysis. At that same type the other two terminals disagree on structural CH. The root has continuum many propositions and full current rows, hence a current real number structure at type $t$. Since the terminal proposition domains have two elements, none can host such a structure, refuting Analytic Necessitism.

For revised Mathematical Possibilism use the two-table argument of the [universal Boolean retraction](universal-boolean-retraction.html). At ordinal type $\rho=(eet)(et)t$ let $Z$ consist of the entities defined, on the current $r$-extensions of the order and field, by one Boolean table and, on all entrance rows outside a $J$-small set, by a single Boolean table on the current $s$-extensions. Leave all four terminal values unrestricted. Zero and successor preserve this condition by the extensional current computation of well-orderhood and initial segments. A rigid limit family of such ordinals has finite current row and equals its members' haecceity union. Outside a single $J$-small set its transported members therefore use their common $s$-tables, and the extensional limit calculation again gives a single $s$-table. The current row is extensional as well. The full current $Z$-test bounds all current inexact ordinal codes. Its external cardinality is at most
\[
 2^{2^{\aleph_0}}\prod_{d=1}^4|C^d_\rho|.
\]
This bound is fixed before choosing $\mu$. Choose $\mu$ strong limit strictly above it. A current carrier of size at least $\mu$ has a $\mu$-sized power-closed subset above the ordinal bound: for $\lambda<\mu$ a $\lambda$-element subset has $2^\lambda<\mu$ subextensions, which can be coded by other members with their ground membership graph. The required current rows are unrestricted. Each revised-small CBA therefore has size below $\mu$. It occurs in its own factor of $L$, with bottom on every other coordinate including the four terminals. Use bottom itself for the trivial algebra. The isomorphism graph is an admitted current row.

The orders on the two retraction carriers can be chosen as actual well-orders in their current rows, with one common $s$-order on the entrance rows and the four respective internal or full well-orders on their exit coordinates. These give Modal Well-Ordering by Łoś. Each retraction mode has a true current-only strong atom, and each terminal a true classical atom. The atomless $\mathcal P(B)/\mathrm{Fin}$ factor keeps its disjoint countable family without a join; the family admits a current injection into the Frege numerals. It also witnesses the failure of propositional Atomicity. At $r$ the universal current individual row cannot have a rigid coextension because that row is infinite. Finally the root has a continuum of propositions and only countably many closed pure terms. Choosing the sole proposition name outside their denotations gives Independence.

## Further local separations

At each $r$-stage the true current-only proposition $a$ holds along the even returns, where the original parameters are diagonal and preserve every current truth. At an odd $s$-stage take its current-only truth $v$. It becomes bottom at the return, so no $a$-point reachable from this stage can make $v$ possible. Thus $\Diamond_{\ne}a$ fails there. At each terminal $a$ is bottom as well. The guard $a$ proves that every $r$-truth is two-hat necessary. The current-only guard at $s$ is bottom after its return, and at a terminal every truth is necessary. These statements pass by Łoś, giving necessary two-hat necessity of truths. In contrast, at a first $s$-entrance the transported $a$ is false, but every true $r$-guard is possible by the diagonal return. Hence $a$ is not ND-necessary. The pure sentence asserting the existence of a contingent truth holds at $r$ and is impossible at a classical terminal, refuting B for pure sentences. The third Frege numeral at a classical terminal with two propositions counts nothing even possibly, refuting necessary Potential Infinity at $t$.

For the propositional Barcan counterexample, retain the old tester with full current row and entrance row $h_i[E^r_t]$, putting top on all the terminal coordinates. Each transported old proposition passes through every round. At a free-ultrafilter entrance choose, at every index, an $s$-proposition with a fixed return coordinate but a current bit opposite to the corresponding $h_i$-image. This tuple is outside the image because $jh_i=1$. The old proof that an inextensible root extension must be finite also survives: the $s$-return tester proving $R\subseteq j[S]$ is top at all four terminals, and the diagonal tester omitting one varying tuple on the countable $B$ part is full there as well. Consequently the universal infinite individual row has no inextensible coextension. Gallin Extensional Comprehension fails for a different reason. Choose a current predicate containing one individual and omitting a second. A persistent coextension with persistent complement would have to keep them apart at the standard terminal, where the individual projection is constant. [Diagonal comprehension argument](reduced-power-retraction.html)

Every weakly rigid root property of individuals projects to a finite union of singletons. In the standard arithmetic terminal its universal property is infinite and rigid. The tester full elsewhere and admitting just the finite properties of individuals at this terminal necessarily holds of every current weakly rigid property, yet misses that universal property. This refutes weak inextensibility of both the rigidity predicate and the weak-rigidity predicate.

A nonzero proposition supported solely on the mod-finite part admits no self-atom below it. Any nonzero part has a $J$-disjoint ultrafilter concentrating on its support. At that entrance its $s$-image has both a true current bit and a nonzero return coordinate. Splitting off the current bit shows that it is instantiated there without being an atom. The same reasoning applies to a root atom supported solely on an atom of $L$, for instance the unit of its two-element factor. Hence even a propositional atom can fail to be a self-atom. In the other direction, take the proposition supported solely on two of the four terminal units. All its $r$- and $s$-current truth bits are false. Wherever it is instantiated it is the top of a two-valued terminal, hence an atom there. The two distinct terminal contributions split this self-atom at the root, so it fails to be a strong atom.

Choose the one propositional name $c$ outside all pure denotations with the additional requirement that its four terminal values be top. There remain continuum many choices in the countable entrance factor. Its value can never equal bottom, since the terminal coordinates survive every retraction history and each direct terminal value is true. Witnessed Possibility fails on the pure matrix $p=\bot$. Let $F$ be the Fregean Axiom. At a retraction stage it is false, and at a terminal it is true. Thus $c\land F=F$ as entities. The pure functions $p\mapsto p\land F$ and $p\mapsto F$ agree at $c$ but differ at bottom, since $F$ is possible. Separated Structure fails for this independent name.
