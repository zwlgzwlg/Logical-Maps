# Analysis in the modalized Cohen and lottery clouds

At the designated root of either construction, Analytic Necessitism and Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fail in every ground universe. The Necessity of Analysis depends on which parameter-free sentences of second-order arithmetic retain their ground truth under the component forcings.

## The absence of a necessarily real number structure at type t

The root proposition algebra contains the complete atomless Cohen factor. Choose countably many disjoint nonzero elements of this factor. Their subfamily joins give at least continuum many distinct root propositions. Every set of current tuples of root identity classes occurs as a relation at the root (Bacon and Opus, Lemma 6.7). Choose codes of type $t$ for the elements of $\omega$ and $\mathcal P(\omega)$ and realize the usual successor and membership as root extensions. The resulting structure satisfies the map's leastness, extensionality and fullness clauses. Its existence gives the antecedent of Analytic Necessitism already at the root.

In each cloud the internal statement that every proposition equals top or bottom has full truth value (Remark 4.6(iv)). Three pairwise distinct propositions are impossible there, while the first three iterates of zero in any Peano structure are pairwise distinct. Thus no type-$t$ real number structure can be a real number structure necessarily. This same instance refutes Second-Order Analytic Necessitism for every positive arity. ## Exactly when first-order analytical truth is necessary

Write $\mathcal A^V=(\omega,\mathcal P(\omega)^V,+,\cdot,\in)$ in the ground universe. For each parameter-free first-order analytical sentence $A$, let $A^V$ denote its truth there. The schema at the root holds exactly when for every such $A$ and every component algebra $B$, the Boolean value in $V^B$ of $A$ is $1_B$ if $A^V$ holds and $0_B$ otherwise. In the lottery this ranges over all nontrivial complete Boolean algebras of cardinality less than $\mu$.

At the root every extension on current identity classes is represented. Any real number structure there has the usual natural numbers by leastness and exactly their ground powerset by fullness and extensionality. In a component the full modalized hierarchy over the individuals $\omega$ has the typed interpretation of the Boolean-valued extension. Each set of naturals there is represented at type $et$ by its Boolean-valued characteristic function. At that type the canonical real number structure can use singleton sets as codes for the numbers and all sets as its reals. Within the component the usual categoricity proof identifies any other real number structure with this one, on the condition where that other structure exists. At the root there are at least continuum many distinct $et$ entities, since arbitrary subsets of the current individual domain $\omega$ are available as extensions. Root fullness supplies the ordinary successor and membership relations on chosen codes. It follows that a component condition forcing the opposite truth from the root refutes both necessity disjuncts at this one type. Agreement on all component conditions conversely gives the correct necessity disjunct at every type, including types which cannot carry a structure at some points. Inside $L$, the analytical sentence asserting that every real is constructible holds at the root. The predicate that a real is constructible has a $\Sigma^1_2$ definition, so its universal closure is a parameter-free first-order analytical sentence in the map's sense. Both named constructions include the Cohen algebra, which in the lottery has cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}<\mu$. Cohen forcing over $L$ adds a real outside $L$ and preserves the ordinals, so that the constructible universe of the extension is still $L$. Thus the Cohen factor forces the negation. The generated variants built in $L$ consequently both fail Necessity of Analysis. [Earlier constructible-universe calculation for full Boolean-valued models](full-boolean-valued-atom-and-atomless-universe-constructible.html)

In a ground universe with a proper class of Woodin cardinals, Woodin's generic absoluteness theorem gives the same parameter-free theory of $L(\mathbb R)$ in any two set-forcing extensions. Take one extension to be trivial. The two-sorted analytical structure and the interpretation of each of its sentences are definable in $L(\mathbb R)$, which contains every real of that universe. Hence every component forces agreement with its ground analytical theory. In this setting both constructions satisfy Necessity of Analysis. This positive statement is conditional on the ground-universe hypothesis. [Woodin's 1985 theorem as stated in John Steel, *Gödel's program*, Theorem 3.2](https://math.berkeley.edu/~steel/papers/godelsprogrampub.pdf) [Steel's account of generic absoluteness for $L(\mathbb R)$](https://www.fomarchive.ugent.be/2000-January/003643.html)
