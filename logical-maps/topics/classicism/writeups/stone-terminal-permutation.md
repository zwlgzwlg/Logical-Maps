# Necessary Rigid Power with homogeneous Stone terminals

OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026.

This is an ordinary ZFC argument first at the identity-only new root, with one old exit to the finite-support permutation group on the old object, and continuous finite-valued sections on the terminal Stone space X. It shows □Tame Rigidity, □Rigid Power and □Weakly Rigid Power at all relational types. Its special hypotheses include invertibility of all old arrows, independent full old lifts, and completeness of the Boolean algebra used for the terminal Stone space.

Write r for the root, I_h(U) for the image of an external set U of current tuples under an arrow h, and E(h) for an admitted relation's actual extension along that arrow. A terminal tuple v lives in a fixed finite tuple set T, and the transported value of a fixed root tuple is a continuous T-valued function. The case of zero arguments has T={the empty tuple}. The subset of X where v∈E(u) is clopen for each v.

**Tame Rigidity.** In C weak rigidity implies persistence throughout the future (weak inextensibility tested against the pointwise box of the relation, using 4). A persistent relation in the permutation group has exact image along each arrow: apply the inverse arrow to obtain the reverse inclusion. Hence every persistent relation in that group is fully rigid. The same holds at classical terminals. Any weakly rigid root relation has weak inextensibility at the root by hypothesis, and it has weak inextensibility at every successor by these observations. It is fully rigid. The old and terminal points themselves satisfy Tame, hence □Tame holds.

**Closure seen by a rigid relation.** Fix a rigid root relation E and let U be its root extension. At the first old exit, E's extension is exactly the projected image of U. One inclusion is persistence. For the opposite inclusion, suppose an old tuple y in E there is not the image of any current U-member. Take the admitted tester P with full current row, full terminal sections, and old profile the complement of the tuple-haecceity of y. Because old transports are bijections, P holds of all images of U throughout the old cone. Every actual U-member therefore satisfies P necessarily. E's weak inextensibility forces E≤P, contradicting E(y) at the old entrance. Rigid persistence and inverse transport give the corresponding exact images at old composites.

For each terminal tuple v define an open set O_v(U) by the points u at which some actual U-member transports to v. It is a union of clopen sets because every given tuple has a continuous section. Then the clopen truth set E_v={u:v∈E(u)} is exactly the closure of O_v(U). Persistence gives O_v(U)⊆E_v. If a point u∈E_v is outside its closure, choose a clopen neighbourhood N of u disjoint from O_v(U). The tester that is top at the root and old cone and only deletes v on N among leaves holds necessarily of every actual U-member. Inextensibility would force E_v∩N empty, a contradiction. This is a topological closure statement, not an assumption that every E-member at every leaf has an actual preimage.

**Rigid subrelations on the old branch.** Given any old rigid Y≤E_old, choose U′⊆U with old image exactly Y's actual old row (e.g. use all U-members with old image in that row). Define a new root relation F by current row U′, old whole entity Y, and leaf truth region at each v the closure of O_v(U′). This is admitted: X is Stone of a complete Boolean algebra, a set-product of complete Boolean factors. Hence the closure of any open set is clopen. Directly, if the open set is a union of clopens [b_i], its closure is [∨ b_i]: every nonempty clopen inside the latter meets some [b_i] by distributivity of a fixed meet over arbitrary joins. Since T is finite these regions define a continuous section.

F is weakly persistent at the root by its construction. If P is necessary on every root U′-tuple, it holds of Y's exact old images throughout the old group and of every O_v(U′) on the leaves. P's section is clopen at each v and hence covers the closure, so F≤P. This is weak inextensibility. At the successors F is rigid (Y by hypothesis, and the leaves are terminal), giving full root rigidity. All root U′-members satisfy E necessarily by E's persistence. Thus applying F's weak inextensibility to E gives F≤E. At an old composite first pull Y back along the group inverse. We thereby reach every future rigid old subrelation of E by a rigid root subrelation.

**All terminal tests for the power property.** The root has enough rigid subrelations to pass every continuous test, even where their values might not cover a particular terminal exactly. Put J(F) ≡ Rigid(F)∧F≤E; this predicate is persistent in C. Let P be an admitted tester with each actual J-member necessarily satisfying P. Current inclusion is immediate. Old inclusion at every composite follows from the previous lifts. Suppose for contradiction P excludes some terminal V⊆E(u). Choose a clopen neighbourhood N of u where E's terminal relation is constant and P's terminal predicate excludes this same V throughout. For each v∈V, O_v(U) is dense and open in N by the closure computation above. Their finite intersection is nonempty, so choose w∈N in that intersection. For each v∈V choose one tuple a_v∈U transporting to v at w. The finite union F of their tuple-haecceities is rigid (its extension is the finite tuple image at every arrow). Since all these root tuples belong to E and E is persistent, F≤E. Thus F is an actual J-member whose terminal transport at w is exactly V. Our premise about P forces P(V) at w, contradicting w∈N. If V is empty use the globally empty F. This proves weak inextensibility of J at the root.

At every old point J is persistent and the arrow group makes it rigid; classical terminal predicates are rigid. Thus J is fully rigid at the root. The same group/terminal reasoning gives Rigid Power at every successor, so □Rigid Power holds. Because all points satisfy Tame, the weak and full rigidity predicates coincide throughout this entire cone (and hence as predicate intensions); this also gives □Weakly Rigid Power. Equivalently apply the native C-implication from □Tame and Rigid Power at each point.

For a direct arity-zero check, rigidity at the new root forces a proposition to be globally top or bottom. If true, its persistence implies top. If false, its weak inextensibility tested against bottom gives bottom. The old group and classical terminals likewise have just these two rigid propositions. For the power of bottom use the haecceity of bottom, and for the power of top use the rigid-predicate lift theorem. The finite tuple and clopen argument above also runs literally when T has one empty tuple.

The root considered here has an identity loop only, one old exit to the one-object finite-support permutation model K (and its composites with all group arrows), and singleton-individual terminal leaves indexed by the Stone space X. At each type the current relation row is arbitrary, the old value is arbitrary, and the leaf section into the finite terminal type is continuous. Root individuals map identically to the old individuals and constantly to the terminal singleton. I find both proposed unboxed/boxed verdicts valid at every relational type, including t.

## □Atoms are strong atoms

Fix τ=σ₁⋯σₙt. Write T for the product of terminal argument domains, including the one-element set of empty tuples for n=0, and T₀ for the current tuple set. The root Boolean algebra of τ-relations is the direct product of P(T₀), the old τ-algebra, and Clop(X)^T. The last identification records, for each terminal tuple, the clopen set at which the tuple is in the section's value; the inverse is continuous since T is finite. In particular root Boolean order is coordinatewise on the old algebra rather than just inclusion of its identity-arrow row.

A product atom has one nonzero component, which is an atom in that component's Boolean algebra. A current-row atom thus becomes zero on all exits. An old-algebra atom transports to zero at all leaves, and transports to atoms throughout the old component since each group arrow is a Boolean isomorphism with inverse. A leaf-coordinate atom at tuple u comes from an atom b∈Clop(X). Its terminal evaluation is either the singleton relation {u} or zero, and its old value is zero. At the root itself a Boolean atom implies every Y or ¬Y in the Boolean order. At any future point the same conclusion applies to a transported atom, and zero trivially entails both. Thus the native strong-atom condition (W≠bottom and □∀Y(W≤Y∨W≤¬Y), where the disjunction is between the two order statements) holds at the root for each root atom. At every old point it holds for every atom by the same isomorphisms; at the terminals it holds by the ordinary finite Boolean algebra. This proves the leading box as well. For τ=t the current and leaf tuple spaces each have one element; the same calculation gives false or the unique positive terminal atom.

## □Rigid Rigidity

If the extension of a root relation F at each exit and its further composites equals the direct image of its root extension under tuple transport, then F is weakly persistent and weakly inextensible at the root. Indeed, if each root member satisfies an admitted Q necessarily, each later F-member has a root preimage and satisfies Q. To infer full Rigid(F) also check these two facts at every later point. They hold for the relations used below. Within an arrow group any persistent relation has exact image at the next arrow: the forward inclusion is persistence, and applying persistence along the inverse to the transported relation gives the converse. This gives weak inextensibility there. At a classical terminal all relations are rigid.

For a rigid old τ-relation Y, lift it to a root relation F. Give F old profile Y and current row all tuples whose old image is in Y at the old identity. Give its constant leaf section the full relation when Y's old current row is inhabited and the empty relation otherwise. Every old tuple has root lifts. Given any compatible pair consisting of an old tuple and a terminal tuple, the root has a lift of both with constant leaf values: choose the old relation value and the constant leaf value independently for every relational argument; for any individual argument the terminal value is forced and compatible. Thus the current row's image is exactly Y at the old exit and exactly the prescribed constant relation at every leaf. At old composites exactness follows by the preceding inverse-arrow argument for Y. F is therefore rigid. For a requested rigid value at a composite exit g, first use the old inverse to obtain its preimage at the first exit; rigidity of that preimage follows by the same invertible transport.

For any specified terminal τ-relation V⊆T use the constant section V at all leaves. At the root take exactly the tuples whose arguments have constant terminal sections forming a tuple in V; all terminal individual components are automatically constant. Give F the old profile top when V is nonempty, bottom otherwise. The independent lifting property again makes the current row's image exactly V at every leaf and exactly its chosen old relation, and group transports preserve the old top and bottom. Hence F is rigid. This lift simultaneously covers every terminal, though the theorem needs only a lift at the particular exit.

For arity zero, a rigid old proposition on the group is top or bottom: persistence and inverse transport make all its truth bits identical. The classical terminal possibilities are exactly top and bottom. The corresponding globally constant root propositions are rigid and lift all of these values. There is no dependence on a nonempty tuple of positive arity in this case.

The higher property J(X)=Rigid(X) is persistent in C (rigidity is necessary weak rigidity and 4 applies). Its extension has the exact-image property at the root by the two lifting constructions, and so J is weakly inextensible at the root. On the old group J is persistent and therefore has exact images and is weakly inextensible throughout. The terminal clause is immediate. This proves Rigid(J) at the root and at every accessible point, precisely the native □Rigid Rigidity schema. It uses no assumption that all predicates are rigid at the new root.

## Cantor root loops

In the Cantor-loop variant the loop row of a persistent relation with current members U is exactly gU, by applying persistence to g and its inverse. Weak inextensibility also transfers around a root loop: for any tester on gU pull it back along the inverse, use weak inextensibility at the identity, then transport the resulting inclusion forward. Thus the Tame argument extends. For the old lift of a rigid subrelation with old row Y take U′ = U ∩ π⁻¹(Y), where π projects to the old tuple. The table gU′ is the intersection of gU with the same loop-invariant preimage; it depends on a finite prefix because the table of the original rigid bound does. The old and clopen closure arguments above now give the same lift. At a root loop each member of the canonical power pulls back along the inverse preserving the boxed rigidity and inclusion clauses. The finite terminal tuple lifts and the clopen tester argument are unchanged. The unrestricted rigid lifts for Rigid Rigidity use only old and terminal projections and hence have invariant current rows. Finally any nonzero loop table splits by a fresh bit on a cylinder with nonempty row, so there are no loop-factor atoms. The old and terminal atoms still transport to atoms or bottom. This proves the same boxed power, rigidity and strong-atom conclusions in the Cantor-loop variant.
