# A finite-image target for Dorr’s two-object construction

At the lower object Tame Rigidity and Rigid Comprehension fail. At both objects Boolean Completeness, Strong Actuality, Weak Rigid Comprehension and Inextensible Comprehension hold, truths are ND-necessary, and every self-atom of every relational type is strong.

Cian Dorr introduced the two-object construction and proved the original comprehension separation in *Boolean Completeness does not imply Rigid Comprehension*, Definition 39 and Propositions 40–41. GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Cian Dorr, gave the singleton-individuals version of the separation on 11 October 2026. OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, restricted the target monoid and proved the necessary self-atom bridge on 27 November 2026. Two other GPT-6 researchers independently reviewed the change and its consequences. [Original model](../index.html#model/symmetric-two-object-unpinned)

## Objects and arrows

Use two copies \(R,S\) of \(\mathbb N\). The loops at \(R\) are its permutations. Every function \(R\to S\) is an entrance, with no arrow back. At \(S\) take the permutations and all finite-image functions. Composing a finite-image map on either side with an endomap leaves its image finite, so these arrows form a category. At each object use its full permutation group for the target symmetry of rows. The source pin is finite at \(R\) and unrestricted at \(S\). A relational entity is an arrow-indexed tuple row obeying these symmetry and pin requirements, and transport is precomposition. These are Dorr’s symmetric ideally-full domains. The individual type can consist of the sites with their given maps or of a singleton with identity transports.

## The permutation scalar

At either object write \(G\) for the proposition supported precisely on its permutation arrows. It is admissible and true. By target equivariance every scalar true at the identity is true on every permutation. Thus \(G\) is an atom. Every nonpermutation out of \(S\) has finite image and can never reach a permutation by postcomposition. At \(R\) no entrance can return. Consequently the transport of \(G\) along a permutation is the target’s \(G\) and its transport elsewhere is bottom. It is a true strong atom. For any current truth \(p\), the guard \(G\) also gives \(\Box(\Diamond G\to p)\). This proves Truths are ND-necessary at both objects.

Let \(X\) be any relational entity, including a scalar. If \(X\) has a tuple on a finite-image arrow \(f:S\to S\), choose a finite-image endomap \(m:S\to S\) fixing \(f[S]\) pointwise. Then \(mf=f\). The transport \(fX\) has the given tuple in both its identity row and its \(m\)-row. Intersecting it with the constant-on-tuples lift of \(G\) yields a nonzero proper part. Thus \(fX\) is instantiated and fails to be an atom.

For \(X\) at \(R\), suppose it has a tuple on an entrance \(h\), and let \(N\) be a finite pin for \(X\). Choose a finite-image \(m\) fixing \(h[N]\), using any constant if \(N\) is empty. The rows \(X_h\) and \(X_{mh}\) are equal because the two entrances have the same target and agree on \(N\). The same splitting therefore shows that \(hX\) is instantiated and fails to be an atom. It follows that any self-atom at either object has no tuples except on permutation arrows. Some such arrow instantiates it. There it is an atom, hence it is already an atom by invertibility. Every nonunit kills it and every unit preserves atomhood. This proves the necessary all-type bridge from self-atoms to strong atoms.

## Comprehension

At \(R\) Dorr’s Boolean hull conditions hold with empty distinguished set. The loops are separated as permutations. On entrances arbitrary functions splice on compatible finite prescriptions, and a permutation fixing a finite pin can move the remaining points of a finite set off any prescribed finite set. These checks concern just the hom-sets out of \(R\). At \(S\), each relation is an arbitrary target-equivariant profile, with no pin restriction. The union of any current family of its profiles is again target-equivariant and is its least upper bound. Thus both objects satisfy all-type Boolean Completeness. Together with Actuality this gives Weak Rigid Comprehension at both. [Weak Rigid Comprehension from Boolean Completeness and Actuality](../index.html#result/completeness-and-actuality-imply-weak-rigid-comprehension)

For Inextensible Comprehension at \(R\), take an admitted current row \(E\) with pin \(N\) for its original profile. Form a profile with row \(gE\) at each permutation \(g\) and empty entrance rows. Permutations fixing \(N\) fix \(E\), so the profile has pin \(N\). Any test necessarily accepting the current members contains \(gE\) at \(g\), and all entrance rows are already empty. The same proof applies after a permutation, and after an entrance the profile is bottom. This proves inextensibility. At \(S\) every row \(E\) has a rigid coextension with rows \(kE\) for all arrows \(k\): it is target-equivariant and its rows are exact images after every continuation. In particular Inextensible Comprehension holds necessarily at \(R\).

## The untamed relation

With the sites as individuals, let \(F\) be the universal individual property. For a finitely pinned tester \(Z\) necessarily true of every current \(a\in R\), a permutation \(g\) has \(g[R]=R\), so \(Z_g\) is full. At an entrance \(h\), for any \(y\in S\) choose \(a\) outside the pin and change \(h(a)\) to \(y\) without changing \(h\) on the pin. The new entrance has the same \(Z\)-row, which therefore includes \(y\). This gives weak inextensibility of \(F\), and its persistence is immediate. At \(S\) the tester with row \(k[S]\) at each \(k\) is target-equivariant and necessarily accepts every current individual. Its row at a constant map omits other individuals. The transported \(F\) therefore fails weak inextensibility. Since coextensive weakly rigid relations are equal, \(F\) has no rigid coextension. [Definitions and coextension uniqueness](../index.html#background/rigidity)

For singleton individuals use the site labels only to define propositions, following Astra’s original argument. At either object let \(q_{ab}\) be true at exactly the arrows identifying sites \(a,b\). It has pin \(\{a,b\}\), and \(h q_{ab}=q_{ha,hb}\). At \(R\) fix a site \(a\) and put \(X_h=\{q_{y,h(a)}: y \text{ a site at the target of }h\}\). This profile is equivariant and pinned by \(\{a\}\). It is weakly persistent. If a test necessarily accepts all \(q_{x,a}\) at \(R\), then at a permutation all its required images are present. At an entrance \(h\), change the value of a fresh \(x\) outside \(\{a\}\) and the test pin to any desired \(y\), keeping \(h(a)\) fixed. Thus the test includes \(X_h\), giving weak inextensibility. After an entrance \(h\), take a constant \(k\) with value \(c\). The image \(k[X_h]\) is \(\{\top\}\), while \(X_{kh}\) contains \(q_{y,c}\ne\top\) for \(y\ne c\), as witnessed by the identity arrow. The exact-image tester of \(X_h\) at \(S\) detects this difference. Hence Tame Rigidity and Rigid Comprehension also fail in this variant.

## Rigid Power

At the upper object any tuple set \(D\) admits its exact-image profile with rows \(k[D]\). This profile is rigid. Every weakly rigid relation there equals the exact-image profile of its actual row by coextension uniqueness. In particular every bounded rigid subrelation at \(k\) lifts by inverse image within the actual bound.

Let \(F\) be fully rigid at \(R\), with current tuples \(E\) and nonempty finite pin \(N\). At an entrance \(h\), weak rigidity gives
\[
 F_h=\{f a:a\in E,\ f|_N=h|_N\}.
\]
Persistence and pinning give inclusion from right to left. For the reverse, use the displayed right-hand side as the entrance rows of a tester, with permutation rows \(gE\). This is an equivariant \(N\)-pinned profile. Each member of \(E\) necessarily passes it, so it bounds \(F\).

Fix a bijective entrance \(b\). Choose a finite-image retraction \(m\) of \(S\) onto \(b[N]\). Since \(bF\) is rigid at \(S\), its exact-image property gives \(m[F_b]=F_{mb}=F_b\); the second equality uses the pin \(N\). An idempotent fixes each element of its image. In particular \(m(ba)=ba\) for every \(a\in E\).

If entrances \(h,h'\) agree on \(N\), then \(ha=h'a\) as whole tuples for every \(a\in E\). Indeed, let \(M\supseteq N\) be a finite pin for \(a\). The finite prescriptions \(b(x)\mapsto h(x)\) and \(b(x)\mapsto h'(x)\), for \(x\in M\), extend to finite-image endomaps \(k,k'\) of \(S\). Thus \(ha=kba\) and \(h'a=k'ba\). Because \(k,k'\) agree on \(b[N]\), \(km=k'm\); the fixed-point identity for \(ba\) now gives the claim. In the displayed hull formula this gives \(F_h=h[E]\).

Given rigid \(Z\le hF\), let \(D\) be its upper current row and \(A=\{a\in E:ha\in D\}\). Any lower permutation \(g\) fixing \(N\) preserves \(E\) and satisfies \(hga=ha\) there, so it preserves \(A\). Form \(Y\) with rows \(g[A]\) at permutations and \(f[A]\) at entrances. Its permutation rows have pin \(N\) by invariance of \(A\), its entrance rows by the common pin just proved. This exact-image profile is rigid, lies below \(F\), and \(hY=Z\) because their current rows agree and both are rigid. At a lower permutation an inverse transports each bounded subrelation back. It follows that the power of a rigid lower bound has exact transported rows, hence is rigid. All upper bounds have rigid powers by the preceding upper argument. This proves necessary Rigid Power, while the untamed relation excludes Weakly Rigid Power. The retraction-on-a-finite-pin step adapts the power argument for [Dorr’s original model](../index.html#model/symmetric-two-object-unpinned).
