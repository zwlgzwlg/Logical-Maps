# Boolean Completeness and Functional Choice without Tame Rigidity

At one point, every relational Boolean algebra is complete for admitted families, every relation has a weakly rigid coextension, Functional Choice holds, and every rigid relation has finite extension. Nevertheless a weakly rigid relation at that same point fails to be rigid. In one version every local algebra is atomic. Another version satisfies revised Mathematical Possibilism and sees a change in the continuum hypothesis.

## The two sorts of stage

Use the [mixed-ideal retraction](countable-entrances-with-finite-ideal-returns.html), replacing its first generic ultrapower by ordinary entrances indexed by a nonempty set $I$. Take a countable elementary substructure $M$ of a sufficiently large regular $H_\theta$, with the choices for the finite types under discussion. For the forcing version take the starting structure in the constructible universe, with GCH. Its collapse has standard natural numbers. Individuals are either the naturals at each stage or a singleton at each stage. Use $I=1$ with natural-number individuals or $I=\omega$ with singleton individuals. All sets and quotients in a domain formula are computed inside the indicated structure.

For a relational type $\tau=\bar\sigma t$, write $A_d$ for the product of its argument domains at stage $d$. The domains at root and site are
$$
 R_\tau=\mathcal P(A_R)\times\mathcal P(A_S)^I,\qquad
 S_\tau=\mathcal P(A_S)\times(R_\tau^\omega/{=_{\rm fin}}).
$$
Construct the root sort from lower argument types, then the site sort at the same type. At the ordinary entrance $h_i:R(M)\to S_i(M)$, send $x=(Q,(U_j))$ to $(U_i,[\operatorname{const}x])$. The individual map is the identity. From each $S_i(M)$ take all generic ultrapower arrows for $\mathcal P(\omega)/\mathrm{Fin}$, as in the linked model. For the embedding $j:M\to N$ the site entity $(V,[v_n])$ goes to $[v_n]$ as an element of $R_\tau(N)$, and individuals go by $j$. In particular each path of length two **starting at a root** sends the entire root tuple by $j$. There is no analogous assertion for a pair of steps starting at a site. Use the tree of finite histories. Every internally infinite condition belongs to a generic because each successive structure is externally countable.

There is also an optional forcing entrance as in the [Cohen return construction](cohen-returns-and-countable-joins.html). Use the ground Boolean completion of the forcing adding $\omega_2$ Cohen reals. Add to each $R_\tau$ a coordinate which is a name for a subset of the lower forcing tuple domain, modulo forced equality. At a generic extension $W=M[G]$ the forcing-site carrier is $\mathcal P^W(A_F)\times R_\tau(M)$. Its entrance sends $x$ to its evaluated name row with return tag $x$. Its only outgoing generator returns to the same ground root by projecting to the tag. Names for subsets have bounded representatives, obtained from Boolean membership values on a set of names for the elements of the domain. This is the same type recursion as for the ordinary sites.

Here is the verification of the domain clauses. At a root, form the current row of an abstract and the current row at each of its entrances by evaluating its matrix on the transported parameters. At a forcing entrance the forcing theorem gives the name for this row. At an ordinary site, choose the current row and the low germ of whole root abstracts on representatives of the returned parameters. The latter uses the root evaluator for this abstract, already defined from its proper subterms. Changing finitely many entries in any one of the finitely many representative sequences has no effect. An abstract transported along $h_i$ has the right current row by construction and the constant germ of its original root value, since every parameter has that constant return. Transport along the low arrow agrees by Łoś’s theorem. The forcing-site abstract has the row evaluated in $W$ and the ground value on returned parameters, so it also commutes with both arrows. The same row restrictions verify application. A difference in root rows is seen either at once or at an entrance, including a generic meeting a nonzero Boolean difference. A difference of low germs is seen after a generic through the infinite set of differing indices and, if needed, a further observation at the new root. This gives intensional extensionality. The formula evaluations at a fixed root type are internal first-order calculations and transfer along each completed root excursion. These verifications apply also at externally nonstandard later structures.

## Local completeness, selection and weak rigidity

Root entailment is inclusion at every displayed coordinate, with forced inclusion at the optional forcing coordinate. These conditions are necessary by observing the corresponding rows. They suffice because they transfer through completed excursions and cover the only remaining step, an entrance. An admitted family has an internal current row. Form the union of all its members' rows coordinate by coordinate. In the forcing coordinate use the name for the union of the interpretations of all the ground family’s names. A ground name forced to bound each member is forced to bound that union. This gives the least upper bound at every relation type. Without forcing all factors are powersets, so each nonzero relation contains an occupied singleton at one coordinate and is locally atomic.

For Functional Choice, choose a whole permissible output $D_x$ for each current input $x$ in the ground. Program the operation's current table to have the row of $D_x$ there. At each entrance prescribe at $h_i x$ the row of $h_iD_x$. Every $h_i$ is injective because the constant low return remembers the whole input. At a forcing site the checked ground tag does the same job, and the ground family of prescriptions gives a name for the row. Applied to $x$, the resulting operation agrees with the chosen output at every coordinate. For Modal Well-Ordering prescribe a well-order at the root and each site and a name forced to well-order the entire forcing sort. Complete excursions preserve the root well-order and its site prescriptions. The resulting whole relation is necessarily a well-order.

For each internal current tuple set $E$, let $K_E$ have row $E$ and entrance rows $h_i[E]$, including the name for the image of the checked ground $E$ at a forcing site. A current member persists along every path, by the description of completed excursions and the final entrance. If a tester necessarily accepts every member of $E$, it contains $E$ currently, each $h_i[E]$ at its entrance, and the image of $E$ in every forcing extension. These are internal coordinate inclusions, so $K_E$ entails the tester. Thus $K_E$ is weakly rigid. In particular Weak Rigid Comprehension holds locally. At a site a relation with the desired current row and bottom return is inextensible, since its proper transports are bottom. All stages therefore satisfy Weakly Inextensible Comprehension.

Furthermore every weakly inextensible root relation with row $E$ lies below $K_E$, since $K_E$ necessarily accepts each member of $E$. Consider any such relation $P$ with internally infinite row $E$. Choose an internal sequence of distinct tuples $a_n\in E$. At an ordinary site following $h_i$, every current member of $h_iP$ lies in $h_i[E]$, hence has the constant low return of some tuple in $E$. Use a tester with full current site row and low sequence of whole ground predicates of differing from $a_n$. At a low arrow its omitted tuple is $[a_n]$, distinct from the image of every fixed old tuple. These distinctions persist because all arrows out of roots are injective on old tuples and all completed excursions on them are elementary. So the tester necessarily accepts every current member of $h_iP$. Yet the transported $P$ contains $[a_n]\in jE$ at the new root. Thus $h_iP$ is not weakly inextensible. Every inextensible root relation has internally finite extension. The [finite-bound theorem](../index.html#background/rigidity/internally-finite-rigid-power) now gives local Rigid Power. Any infinite $E$ gives a weakly rigid $K_E$ that fails rigidity, and has no inextensible coextension. For singleton individuals use the set of all root propositions, which is infinite when $I=\omega$.

## Atoms and the distinction between the root and site

A root self-atom can have no occupied entrance row. At an entrance observing such a row it would be instantiated and split into its nonempty current coordinate and its nonzero return. This also applies to any nonzero forcing name via a generic observing it. Thus each self-atom at the root is a current-only singleton. Such a singleton is an atom and is an atom whenever instantiated. But at an ordinary site its nonzero constant return germ splits on even and odd low indices. In particular the actual root scalar atom is not strong. A scalar supported only at a single ordinary entrance is an atom at the root and fails selfhood there. Hence scalar Self-Atomicity fails locally.

At an ordinary site every nonzero low germ splits by partitioning its internally infinite support. Thus even propositional Atomicity fails there. For the failure of Countable Boolean Completeness, partition the low index set into disjoint infinite blocks $B_n$. Take as the $n$th scalar the empty current bit with return top on $B_n$ and bottom elsewhere. This family and its numerical indexing graph are current rows, using the infinite scalar sort for the Frege numbers. An upper bound must return top cofinitely on each $B_n$. Deleting one such entry from every block makes a strictly smaller upper bound. This also proves failure of necessary Boolean Completeness at $t$. Local propositional Barcan fails at the root: in the row at a designated $h_i$ omit the scalar with a nonconstant low germ, keep all other rows full, and at the root keep the full current row. Each old scalar is accepted necessarily because its low tag at any ordinary entrance is constant and each transported tester repeats this calculation at later roots.

Let $a$ be the root scalar true only in its current coordinate. It holds at all later roots and is false at sites. At each site the current-only truth becomes impossible after departure. It witnesses the failure there of distinctness-preserving possibility of $a$. Thus $a$ guards two-hat necessity of every root truth, since a root truth recurs at each later root on its transported parameters. At sites the current-only truth guards even ND-necessity of every truth. At the root, $a$ itself fails ND-necessity, since every true guard remains possible beyond an ordinary entrance. These are the two-hat calculations of the mixed-ideal model.

## Numbers, possibility and the continuum

In each mode, the full internal current rows identify the Frege numbers at an infinite sort with the set model’s own naturals by the usual numeral and induction test. Full current powersets also make any membership-presentation real structure, when one exists, read its set model’s full real hierarchy. Necessity of Arithmetic follows because elementary returns preserve each fixed arithmetic sentence and forcing leaves arithmetic unchanged. At an infinite counting sort with the Peano guard throughout ($e$ with natural individuals or $t$ with infinitely many ordinary entrances), the infinite number property cannot be rigid by the bound above. This refutes Arithmetic Necessitism. An internally infinite powerset of a countably infinite set embeds in a suitable root sort, giving room for a real structure with disjoint number and real labels. Its infinite number carrier cannot be rigid. Thus Analytic Necessitism fails as well.

With one ordinary entrance and no forcing, the root has exactly four propositions and each ordinary site has infinitely many. The root misses the finite algebra on three atoms, which is revised-small. With singleton individuals and countably many ordinary entrances the inexact ordinal benchmark is bounded by the finite set of uniform extensional truth tables on the order and field of the singleton. This set is admitted. The zero and successor operations give such tables. Any rigid subfamily of the finite set equals the finite union of the haecceities of its current members, so its limit again uses one of the tables. A ground-infinite algebra carrier has a countably infinite current subset; this subset exceeds the finite benchmark and its smaller subcarriers are finite, with all their subcollections represented. Thus a revised-small carrier must be internally finite. Any finite Boolean algebra is represented below the join of as many distinct singleton-address atoms as it has atoms. Each interval and its required graph are available at the root, also with the forcing coordinate present. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism.

Without forcing, elementary returns preserve the second-order analysis of the initial ground. With the given Cohen forcing, CH holds in the initial constructible ground and fails at the forcing site. Both points have real structures at $t$, using the countable entrance coordinates at the root and their checked ground return tags at the site. The ground number and real labels and the new model’s own larger family of real labels must be distinguished: inside the extension the scalar sort has at least the new continuum many values because the complete Cohen algebra in the ground has at least $\aleph_2$ elements and these survive in the checked scalar return sort. The same forcing changes the first-order analytical sentence that every real is constructible. The ground and extension calculations, including the projective definition of constructibility of a real, are given in the [Cohen return proof](cohen-returns-and-countable-joins.html). The forcing coordinate is atomless, so local propositional Atomicity fails in this version.

In the countably indexed settings take a single propositional name denoting the atom supported just at one ordinary entrance. Permuting the ordinary indices is a symmetry of the pure construction: permute the tuple domains inductively, transport rows, and permute the corresponding branches and low germs. In the forcing case act on names recursively. The name is moved, while every closed pure term is fixed, giving Independence. It remains nonbottom after every arrow, by the entrance tag and elementary return. This refutes Possibly Witnessed Possibility using the pure matrix $X=\bot$.
