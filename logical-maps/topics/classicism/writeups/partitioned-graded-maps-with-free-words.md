# Graded maps collapsing one part of the individuals

Logical Necessity and necessary Barcan at every type can hold where Tame Rigidity fails. The same model has necessary Strong Actuality, Self-Atomicity, Countable Boolean Completeness and Inextensible Comprehension. Its universal individual property is rigid and its rigid power gains an instance. Both rigidity predicates also fail weak inextensibility.

## The arrows

Given a set-sized signature $\Sigma$, choose $\kappa\ge\max(2^{\aleph_0},|\Sigma|)$. Partition the individuals $A=L\sqcup C$ into two sets of size $\kappa^+$ and fix a two-element subset $B\subset L$. An arrow at the one object is a function $f:A\to A$, a natural grade $n$, and a finite word $w$ on $\kappa$ letters. At grade zero require $f=\mathrm{id}$. At positive grades require $f[L]\subseteq B$ and allow any restriction to $C$. Composition composes functions, adds grades and prepends the later word. This is closed because every positive-grade function sends $B$ into $B$.

A relation assigns a tuple row to each arrow. For a single pin $P\subseteq A$ of size at most $\kappa$, the row at fixed grade and word must depend only on $f|_P$. Individuals travel by $f$ and relations travel by precomposition. At fixed grade and word, agreement on $P$ gives the same whole transported relation, since postcomposition preserves agreement on $P$. The union of the pins of a term's finitely many parameters therefore pins its abstraction, including with quantifiers and boxes. Extension and order are checked at the identity and by inclusion at every row, respectively. Every subset of the current tuples is an admissible current row. [The unrestricted graded action and its term verification](graded-maps-free-words-glued-constants.html)

At any positive grade, every map $f$ agrees on the pin of a tested property with a surjection $s$. Keep $f$ on the pin and cover all of $A$ using fresh members of $C$. Every typed target entity lifts through $(s,n,w)$. For an individual choose a preimage. If $T$ pins a target relation, choose one $s$-preimage of each point of $T$ and use those preimages as a pin for its lift. On a map which extends a prescribed composite $g s$ on that pin, at grade $m+n$ and word $vw$, assign the target row for $(g,m,v)$. Agreement on the lift pin forces agreement of the postmaps on $T$. Grade subtraction and suffix cancellation make the prescriptions consistent, and unprescribed rows may be empty. This also works at grade zero using the identity map. Every possible witness to an old predicate therefore has an actual preimage witnessing its possibility. This proves Barcan at all types, necessarily by the one-object semantics.

For Logical Necessity, start the individual names at distinct members of $C$. Each realizable signature sentence gets a fresh letter and a realizing assignment. A positive-grade surjection $s$ can implement that assignment on the finitely many named individuals used by the sentence. Transport lifts for all its relational names along the same $s$, then place the entire lifted profiles on the right cone of the chosen letter. Distinct last letters have disjoint cones and the union of the at most $\kappa$ required pins has size at most $\kappa$. Thus each realizable assignment on the names occurring in a sentence occurs at its chosen arrow. Conversely, each possible assignment is an assignment at the one object. This is the [signature gluing proof](graded-maps-free-words-glued-constants.html) with the initial individual names in $C$.

## A weakly rigid property and its transport

Let $F$ have individual row $L$ at every grade-zero address and $B$ at every positive grade. It is weakly persistent. Suppose a tester $T$ necessarily accepts every current member of $L$. Its grade-zero rows contain $L$, since the map is the identity. At a positive address, change the value of the coefficient map on one fresh member of $L$ outside a pin of $T$ to any prescribed $b\in B$. The changed arrow must put $b$ in the tester, whose row stays the same. Thus $F\le T$, establishing weak rigidity.

After a positive arrow $F$ is constantly $B$. The tester with rows $g[B]$ is pinned by the pair and necessarily accepts both current members of $B$. A positive continuation may collapse them to one point. Its row then misses the other member of $B$, refuting weak inextensibility of the transported $F$.

The universal individual property is rigid. Any tester necessarily accepting all $A$ must have full positive rows, as one can change the image of a fresh member of $C$ to any desired individual. The same proof works at every point. More generally, a weakly rigid property with current row $D$ has positive rows of one of two kinds. If $|D\cap C|\le\kappa$, a tester with full grade-zero rows and positive rows $f[D\cap C]\cup B$ is pinned by $D\cap C$ and necessarily accepts every $D$-member. Its positive rows are small. If $|D\cap C|>\kappa$, changing $f$ at a fresh member of $D\cap C$ outside the property's pin shows by persistence that each positive row is full.

Choose a large proper subset $D_0\subset C$. The property with grade-zero rows $D_0$ and full positive rows is rigid. Persistence is immediate, and every tester necessarily accepting $D_0$ is full at positive grades by the same fresh-input argument. Every positive arrow transports the property to the rigid universal property. An empty-pin tester of properties can now accept everything at grade zero and accept just those properties with a small or full current extension at positive grades. It necessarily accepts every actual weakly rigid property, and so every actual rigid property. At a positive address it misses the rigid property whose current extension is $D_0$. This refutes weak inextensibility of the rigid and weakly rigid powers of the universal property. Because every property entails the universal property, the same test directly refutes both the rigid-rigidity and weakly-rigid-weak-rigidity principles.

## Atoms, countable joins and analysis

Every nonzero relation contains a nonzero self-atom. Restrict it to one occupied tuple, grade and word. After any instantiation the only continuation with the same grade and word is the identity, so the result is then the singleton current atom. A positive-grade occurrence always splits by changing the coefficient value at a fresh $C$-site. Thus all genuine atoms are one-tuple singletons on a grade-zero word. Their transports are again such atoms or bottom. The identity-only truth is a true strong atom and witnesses ND-necessity of each truth. Any current tuple set has an inextensible coextension supported solely at the identity, since all its proper transports are bottom. These statements hold at every point. [Typed atom calculation](graded-maps-free-words-glued-constants.html)

Every countable family has its rowwise union, supported by the union of its pins. The unrestricted current row of standard numeral denotations shows each internally countable family is externally countable. At type $t$ the grade-one, empty-word interval is nonzero and has no atom. The family from the [failure of propositional Boolean Completeness](graded-maps-free-words-glued-constants.html) uses its inputs $d,a_i$ in $C$. The two maps agreeing on an alleged join's pin can therefore be chosen exactly as in that proof, so the family still lacks a join.

A constant-row external well-order of each type remains a well-order at every address. Every carrier is infinite, and the standard numeral cut at $e$ and $t$ gives their necessary Axioms of Infinity. At $e$, there are enough labels for an actual full real structure, since $|A|\ge2^{\aleph_0}$ and all current tuple sets are admitted. There is no necessarily real rigid structure at $e$. A positive arrow can send every individual to a single fixed member of $B$, identifying, in particular, any two numerical labels. This contradicts the necessary distinctness required by Analytic Necessitism. Every actual real structure at any of the points interprets ordinary full analysis because the current sorts have all subsets and relations.

Finally, the infinite current individual carrier and all current graphs give a revised-small complete presentation of $\mathrm{RO}(2^\omega)$. In the scalar algebra every external countable meet is literal intersection of rows, since the union of countably many pins is a pin. In any nonempty scalar interval, evaluation at a point of its top gives a countably complete ultrafilter. A countably complete ultrafilter on $\mathrm{RO}(2^\omega)$ would select a nested binary prefix at each finite length. These clopens have regular-open meet zero, contradicting countable completeness. Thus the required small algebra is absent from every scalar interval. [Smallness of the Cantor regular-open algebra](choiceless-ordinal-obstruction-to-mathematical-possibilism.html)

## The same coefficients with one individual

Keep the sets $A=L\sqcup C$ and $B$ in the definition of the arrows. Use just one individual in the typed domains, fixed by every arrow. The qualification on positive coefficients and the pin comparisons stay exactly as above. The Barcan lifts apply to every relational type and the only individual lifts along every arrow. The last-letter gluing works for every set-sized signature because the sole individual realizes every possible individual-name assignment.

Fix $b_0\in B$. For each coefficient site $x\in A$ let $p_x$ be the proposition whose row at $(g,m,v)$ tests $g(x)=b_0$. It has pin $\{x\}$ and its transport along $(f,n,w)$ is $p_{f(x)}$. Distinct sites give distinct propositions. At a positive grade two designated sites can be sent separately to $b_0$ and the other member of $B$. For $S\subseteq A$ write $Q_S=\{p_x:x\in S\}$. These sets are available as arbitrary tuple rows.

The property of propositions with grade-zero row $Q_L$ and positive rows $Q_B$ is weakly rigid by the preceding fresh-input proof on $L$. After a positive arrow it is constantly $Q_B$ and fails weak inextensibility, witnessed by the tester with rows $Q_{g[B]}$. The property with constant rows $Q_A$ is rigid by the fresh-input proof on $C$. If $Z\le Q_A$ is weakly rigid and has current row $Q_D$, all its positive rows are small when $D\cap C$ is small, since the tester can bound them by $Q_{f[D\cap C]\cup B}$. When $D\cap C$ is large they all equal $Q_A$. For a large proper $D_0\subset C$, the property with grade-zero rows $Q_{D_0}$ and positive rows $Q_A$ is rigid. The higher tester accepting only small rows or $Q_A$ at positive grades therefore misses a rigid subrelation of $Q_A$ there, while accepting every transported old weakly rigid subrelation of $Q_A$. Both power principles fail. This proof uses a bound on the coded propositions.

The scalar algebra still has a separate atom at each grade-zero word, and therefore it has arbitrarily many atoms. One individual throughout bounds the inexact ordinal benchmark by three finite thresholds. Full current rows then make every infinite carrier contain a countably infinite power-closed subset larger than the benchmark. Thus the small complete algebras are finite, and finite sets of grade-zero word atoms represent them in scalar intervals. This proves revised Mathematical Possibilism. [Finite-threshold argument](mathematical-possibilism-in-full-action-models.html)

At every relational type construct a rigid real structure with all its represented powers. Index a countably infinite set of letters by $\omega$. For each $S\subseteq\omega$, use the proposition whose row on a nonempty word tests whether its leftmost letter is indexed by a member of $S$, and whose empty-word row is false. Lifting this scalar to a relation constant in any nonempty tuple of arguments also gives labels at that relational type. After any common transport distinct labels stay distinct: a further one-letter prefix tests their bits independently of the word already traversed. The transported labels as whole entities depend only on the traversed word.

Use disjoint labels for the natural numbers and all subsets of the naturals. At each address put the exact images of the corresponding sets of labels, of successor and of membership in their respective rows. These profiles have empty coefficient pins. The exact images compose, giving hereditary rigidity, and distinctness of labels at every word gives necessary distinctness. The resulting local structures are full standard real structures throughout. For each fixed finite arity and each subset of the tuples of labels, form its exact-image relation in the same way. The family of all such relations also has an admitted exact-image profile, since every transported member and the family of their images depend only on the word. Every admitted relation at a target restricts to one of these subsets there. This gives all the represented powers required for Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. At the individual type a real structure is impossible because the individual domain is singleton. The propositional Axiom of Infinity holds necessarily, as the scalar carrier is infinite and the current standard numeral cut is admitted.

All countable-completeness, rowwise well-order, inextensible coextension and graded atomicity arguments above use only the coefficient pins and unrestricted rows, so they apply to the singleton-individual setting as well.
