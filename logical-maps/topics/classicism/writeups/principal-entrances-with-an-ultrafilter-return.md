## Rigid bounds and Gallin comprehension

All root entrances are injective on existing entities. Programming the selected outputs on their images gives Functional Choice. Coordinatewise unions, including the forced union of the checked family in the optional Cohen coordinate, give local Boolean Completeness at every relational type. The stagewise prescription of current well-orders gives Modal Well-Ordering. For a current tuple set $E$ use the root relation $K_E$ with entrance rows the exact images of $E$. The proof of local Weak Rigid Comprehension in the principal-entrance model applies: coordinate inclusions for a necessary tester hold after every elementary or literal return and at the final entrance. Any weakly inextensible relation with row $E$ lies below $K_E$. If $E$ is internally infinite, take a sequence of distinct $a_n\in E$. At the ordinary site, the tester with full current row and low tag requiring inequality to $[a_n]_U$ necessarily covers every tuple in its current row, since each has the constant return of an old tuple. But the returned root row contains $[a_n]_U\in jE$. The site image fails weak inextensibility. Thus all inextensible root bounds are internally finite, the finite-bound theorem gives local Rigid Power, and Tame Rigidity and Inextensible Comprehension fail. Each site separately has an inextensible representative for every current row by taking the bottom return, so Weakly Inextensible Comprehension holds throughout.

For Gallin Extensional Comprehension, retain $E$ as the current root row. At an ordinary entrance prescribe the row on a site tuple $b$ by

$$b\in V_i\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \ell b\in jE.$$

This is an internal set in $M$. On representatives it is the condition that the indices at which their entries are in $E$ belong to $U$, using constant representatives of individual arguments. At a forcing site pull $E$ back along the literal return map. Along a high step on an old tuple $a$, the displayed test has truth $j a\in jE$, equivalent to $a\in E$. On an arbitrary site tuple it agrees with membership at the next root by its definition. These two statements and their negations hold at all subsequent ground embeddings. Both signs of the resulting relation are therefore persistent in the hereditary sense required by Gallin.

An ordinary entrance omits the scalar with true current bit and bottom return. The root tester omitting just that value at the designated entrance, and otherwise full, witnesses failure of propositional Barcan. Its future omissions continue to miss all transported old scalars by the constant-return tag. Arithmetic Necessitism fails at the throughout-Peano sort $e$ in the one-entrance setting and at $t$ in the sequence settings. Its numerical row is infinite and cannot be rigid. In the first setting the type $et$ already has enough labels for a current real structure, and $t$ does in the sequence settings. An infinite number part prevents any necessarily real rigid presentation there. These are local failures of Analytic Necessitism. Ordinary Necessity of Arithmetic holds because completed excursions preserve arithmetic. Without forcing the full current rows in each set structure and elementarity also give Necessity of Second-Order Analysis. In the Cohen case the ground and forcing site disagree on CH and on the analytical assertion that every real is constructible, using the real-structure labels verified in the [original construction](principal-entrances-with-generic-returns.html).

## Rigid Power at the sites

Let $G$ be rigid at an ordinary or forcing site, with current row $E$ and returned relation $B$ at its unique next root. Write $\ell$ for this return in either case. Then $B_{\mathrm{current}}=\ell[E]$. Persistence gives one inclusion. For $b\notin\ell[E]$, take the site tester with full current row and root tag the predicate of differing from $b$. Each old member of $E$ necessarily satisfies it, since all later arrows starting at the returned root are injective on its old tuples. Weak inextensibility excludes $b$ from the returned row.

Each rigid $X\le B$ at that root admits a rigid site lift below $G$. Give the lift $Y$ the return tag $X$ and the current row $E\cap\ell^{-1}[X_{\mathrm{current}}]$. This row is internal by the ultrafilter test on representatives, or by the literal return at a forcing site. Its direct image is the entire current row of $X$. Persistence of $Y$ follows from persistence of $X$. A site tester necessarily accepting every current member of $Y$ therefore necessarily accepts every current member of $X$ past the return, by taking a preimage of each such member. Weak inextensibility of $X$ gives coverage throughout the later cone. Thus $Y$ is weakly rigid at the site, its proper images are rigid, and $Y\le G$ in both coordinates.

Consider a tester necessarily accepting every rigid site subrelation of $G$. The lifts just constructed show that after return it necessarily accepts every rigid subrelation of $B$. The root finite-bound theorem already established Rigid Power at the returned root, so the tester there covers the whole power throughout its cone. This proves weak inextensibility of the power at the site. Persistence is automatic, and its proper image is the rigid root power of $B$. It follows that **Rigid Power holds at every stage** even though Tame Rigidity fails at the initial root.

At that root the predicates of rigidity and weak rigidity themselves both fail weak inextensibility. Any weakly rigid bound with current row $E$ equals $K_E$ by uniqueness of weakly rigid coextensions. Its row at a chosen ordinary entrance on a scalar argument therefore contains only entrance images of ground scalars. The scalar $y$ with current bit true and constant bottom return is outside this image. Its haecceity $H_y$ at the site is rigid and cannot be the entrance image of any weakly rigid root relation. A root tester full everywhere except for omitting this one haecceity at the chosen ordinary row consequently necessarily accepts every existing weakly rigid, and thus every rigid, root relation. The computation repeats at completed excursions by elementarity. At that entrance the tester omits $H_y$ itself. This refutes Rigid Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity locally.

## Atoms at each point

Every root self-atom is a current-only singleton. Indeed, any nonempty entrance row would be instantiated there with both its current part and its nonzero return part, so the transported relation would split at that point. A nonzero forcing coordinate has a generic observing a nonempty row and gives the same split. Once confined to the current coordinate, a self-atom must be a singleton in that coordinate. Such a singleton is strong. On high entrances its image is return-only and remains an atom because the return factor is the entire algebra at the elementary successor. At the forcing entrance the return factor is the literal old algebra. Each completed excursion preserves the corresponding current-only singleton.

At a site an inhabited self-atom must be an atom there and hence a current-only singleton, which dies on departure. A self-atom with empty current row is a return-only entity whose unique return is self-atomic. This return is a strong root atom by the preceding argument. The site product order then makes the return-only entity itself an atom, and all its future images are atomic or bottom. Hence **every self-atom is strong at every type and every point**. The current-only true scalar is a strong atom at each stage, giving necessary Strong Actuality. A scalar confined to one ordinary entrance is an atom at the root but splits where it is instantiated, refuting even propositional Self-Atomicity and the converse atom-to-self-atom bridge. Without forcing all root algebras are products of powersets, and the ordinary site is the product of a powerset and an atomic root algebra in its ultrapower. This proves necessary Atomicity at all types. The Cohen coordinate instead gives an atomless principal interval at the root. The calculation with the current-only guards in the [principal-entrance model](principal-entrances-with-generic-returns.html) still gives necessary truth-implies-two-hat-necessity and failure of two-hat-implies-ND at the root.

## Which countable joins survive

With one ordinary entrance and no forcing the scalar algebras have exactly four elements at roots and eight at sites. An ultrapower of a finite algebra is that same algebra. Thus Boolean Completeness at $t$ holds necessarily, Potential Infinity at $t$ fails, and the eight-element Boolean algebra has no principal representation at the four-element root.

Nevertheless Countable Boolean Completeness at all relational types fails at the ordinary site in every setting. In the first setting let $p_n$ be the ground unary relation on individuals supported only on the current root tuple $n$. In the sequence settings take instead the scalar atom supported on ordinary entrance $n$. These are a ground sequence of disjoint atoms. At the ordinary site take the countably indexed family of current-empty relations with the respective constant $p_n$ as return. The full current rows supply the numerical indexing graph, using counting sort $e$ in the first case and $t$ in the others.

An upper bound has low tag $z=[z_i]_U$ above each $j(p_n)$, so $\{i:p_n\le z_i\}\in U$ for each $n$ of the ground. Inside that ground choose $k(i)\le i$ maximal with $p_0,\ldots,p_{k(i)}\le z_i$, defaulting to zero when the list is empty. Then $k(i)$ tends to infinity modulo $U$ and $p_{k(i)}\le z_i$ on a member of $U$. The nonzero low atom $[p_{k(i)}]_U$ lies below $z$ and is disjoint from every $j(p_n)$. Removing it gives a strictly smaller site upper bound. This proves the countable-join failure already for a scalar-valued family of scalars in the sequence settings.

Necessary Weak Rigid Comprehension also fails. Choose distinct root inputs $x_n$, using individuals in the first setting and scalars in the other two. At the ordinary site the set of $h_i x_n$ is a current row. Any weakly rigid coextension has at its low successor exactly the set of diagonal images $j(x_n)$ of the ground list. Persistence gives one inclusion. For each surplus tuple $b$ the site tester with full current row and low predicate $x\ne b$ proves the reverse inclusion by weak inextensibility: all later arrows on old root inputs are injective, so the tester necessarily covers each $h_i x_n$. The diagonal range cannot be a current row of the ultrapower. Applying the preceding unbounded-index construction to a representative sequence of putative rows yields a member $[x_{k(i)}]_U$ distinct from all ground diagonals.
