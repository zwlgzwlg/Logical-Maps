# Functional Choice and rigid real structures through a change in CH

At the designated point Functional Choice and Analytic Necessitism hold. Both rigid power principles fail, and an accessible point has the opposite truth value for the continuum hypothesis. There are two kinds of excursion from the root. One introduces a new subset of the ground continuum in a generic ultrapower; the other changes CH by forcing without adding reals.

## The new forcing coordinate

Begin with the [cardinal-ideal retraction at the continuum](countable-ideal-retraction.html). Use a countable ground structure $M$ with standard natural numbers satisfying $\neg\mathrm{CH}$ and with the finite-type set constructions used there. Such a ground can be obtained in ZFC: collapse a countable elementary substructure of a sufficiently large regular $H_\theta$ to a transitive structure, and if necessary first take a generic extension adding sufficiently many Cohen reals. The finite rank constructions and their forcing names can be accommodated by choosing $\theta$ above their bounds. The high generic embeddings below are elementary and may have externally ill-founded targets; each assertion used at a fixed type is interpreted internally.

In every root structure let $\lambda=2^{\aleph_0}$ and $I=\lambda^+$. The high-index germ compares total functions on $I$ off a set of size at most $\lambda$. Retain the generic ultrapower entrances and their deterministic returns. In addition, let $\mathbb B$ be the regular-open completion of the forcing of countable partial functions from $\omega_1$ to $\lambda$, ordered by extension. The forcing is countably closed, adds no countable sequences of ground objects and preserves $\omega_1$. Its generic union maps $\omega_1$ onto the old continuum. Thus CH holds after this excursion. These are the forcing calculations in the [continuum collapse without new reals](boolean-valued-collapse-without-new-reals.html).

For each root structure $M$ and each $M$-generic $G$ for $\mathbb B$ add a site over $W=M[G]$ whose unique outgoing generator returns to $R(M)$. Take finite histories and all their postcomposites. All three individual domains are their respective natural numbers, and the new entrance and return identify the ground and forcing natural numbers. At a relation type define

$$
\begin{aligned}
 R_\tau&=\mathcal P(A_R)\times(\mathcal P(A_H)^I/{=_{\le\lambda}})\times C_\tau,\\
 H_\tau&=\mathcal P(A_H)\times R_\tau,\\
 F_\tau^G&=\mathcal P^W(A_F^G)\times R_\tau^M.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $A$'s are the tuple domains at the respective modes, using only lower types. The first two lines are sets in $M$; the last line is formed in $W$ with the checked ground set of possible returns. Here $C_\tau$ is the set in $M$ of $\mathbb B$-names for subsets of the lower forcing tuple domain, modulo forced equality of whole rows. Use a set of bounded representatives: starting with a name for the tuple domain, each subset can be named by assigning Boolean membership values to a fixed set of names for its elements. Thus the recursion first constructs $R_\tau$ from lower types and then constructs the high site and the name for the forcing site at that type. All data for each fixed type have bounded rank.

Write $h_yx=(f_x(y),x)$ for the virtual high entrance as in the original retraction, with an internal choice of representatives. At a genuine high entrance use $h_k(jx)$ in the elementary generic ultrapower, $k=[\mathrm{id}_I]$. For the forcing entrance use $h_Gx=(\dot V_x^G,x)$, where $\dot V_x$ is the third coordinate. Return in both kinds of site by projection to the whole root entity. In particular a forcing excursion followed by a return fixes every root entity; a complete high excursion is an elementary embedding on root entities.

For a root lambda abstract, use its ground current row and the high germ of evaluating on the virtual images, as in the original construction. Its third coordinate is the name for the root parameters' transported abstract at a forcing site. This name exists by the forcing theorem for the current set-theoretic evaluation: the returned parameters lie in checked ground carriers, and any ground term evaluator used after projection is a fixed ground table on those carriers. No ground-universe truth predicate in the extension is needed. More explicitly, at each term first form its $R$-value using the proper subterms' current truth at the three modes. Its two site values then have their respective current rows and the already defined $R$-value on returned parameters. The same recursion handles partial application. Equality of names on a nonzero Boolean condition can be tested in a generic through that condition. This, high-germ separation by generics, and current-row separation verify intensional identity and transport. Use zero and top for the individual and relational names.

## Selection, bounds, and the missing powers

For a serial current relation choose outputs $d_x$ in the ground. At the high indices assign the row of $h_y d_x$ on the distinct $h_y x$ inputs. For the third coordinate form the name for the analogous row on $h_Gx$, with the row of $h_Gd_x$ there. These prescriptions use one ground family of names and the return tags keep the inputs distinct. They give a whole selecting operation. Pulling each current row back by the site's return gives its Gallin coextension. Internal well-orders of the carriers can be chosen for each ground mode, and a name for a well-order at the forcing mode; the corresponding root profile gives a necessary well-order at each type.

For $|E|\le\lambda$ in a root, the canonical relation $K_E$ has current row $E$, high rows $h_y[E]$ and the name for $h_G[E]$. It has exact images through every history. At a high step the elementary embedding takes $E$ onto its pointwise image, since a generic for the ideal decides any ground partition into at most $\lambda$ parts. A forcing step and return carry each member forward and back as specified, with the return restoring $E$. Thus $K_E$ is rigid. Conversely a weakly rigid root relation has current row of size at most $\lambda$. For a larger row, use the original moving-omission proof at $\lambda^+$ distinct high indices, with the added forcing coordinate of its tester full. The proof still contradicts weak inextensibility. When $|E|\le\lambda$ uniqueness of weakly rigid coextensions identifies the relation with $K_E$. This proves Tame Rigidity at every root.

At either site write its relation as $(T,A)$ with $A$ the ground return. For a weakly rigid relation, $A$ has current row exactly the image of $T$ under return. Persistence gives one inclusion. For the other, if a particular ground tuple $x$ lies outside that image, use the full-site-row tester returning the ground predicate of differing from $x$. Distinct returned ground tuples remain distinct through every subsequent arrow, since the entrances retain their return tags and the high embeddings are elementary. Next, any root tester necessarily accepting the image of $T$ lifts to the site tester with full current row and that return. Hence $A$ is weakly inextensible; its weak persistence follows on each member from a preimage in $T$. So $A$ is rigid by the root characterization, making the site relation rigid too. This proves necessary Tame Rigidity without assuming the forcing extension's projected row is itself a ground set.

The two power failures still use the high entrance. Take $\lambda$ distinct scalar labels $p_\xi$ on disjoint positive high supports and $E=\{p_\xi:\xi<\lambda\}$. The bound $K_E$ is rigid. A weakly rigid source subrelation has form $K_{E_A}$ for $E_A=\{p_\xi:\xi\in A\}$ with $A$ a ground subset of $\lambda$. Fix an internal injection $D:I\to\mathcal P(\lambda)$. At a high index $y$ let a tester reject properties whose current row is $h_y[E_{D(y)}]$. Its current row and forcing coordinate are full. For each old $A$ the exceptional equality $D(y)=A$ occurs at most once, and all complete excursions on old root parameters are identities or elementary images. The tester necessarily accepts each eligible root subrelation. At a generic high site the excluded set of indices is $(jD)(k)$, distinct from every ground image $jA$. The excluded row there has the rigid representative with that current row and the corresponding $K$ for its return; its images through the two kinds of future excursion are exact by the same construction. This refutes weak inextensibility for the properties of being a rigid and of being a weakly rigid subrelation of $K_E$.

## Arithmetic, reals and CH

At every relational carrier type there are enough labels for the disjoint union of the natural numbers and the reals. At scalar type use disjoint positive high sectors; at higher relational types use the corresponding all-or-nothing row on nonempty argument domains. The carriers of labels, the successor graph and the membership relation all have ground size at most $\lambda$. Use their rigid $K_E$ representatives. Along a high step the number labels and real labels all lift from the preceding model, by the partition property at $\lambda$. At a forcing site the collapse adds no new real, so every available subset of the number labels is still coded by one of the old real labels. The tags and elementary maps preserve distinctness and the indicated graphs. The same assertions hold along any finite history. At $e$ the full internal current subsets of a countable domain rule out a Real structure at every point. This proves Analytic Necessitism at the root. The Frege numerals give the same countable rigid structure at every counting type, since all stages are $\omega$-standard with infinite current carriers and full internal current rows. This gives Arithmetic Necessitism and the necessary infinity axioms at $e,t$.

At each of the three modes, classical current arithmetic and all first-order analytical quantifiers on a real structure compute its model's naturals and reals. The occurrences of the number and real quantifiers are preserved throughout the histories. This proves Necessity of Analysis. The real structure at $t$ exists at both the root and at a forcing site. Each site's actual second-order relation quantifiers range over the full internal current rows. Its structural CH is therefore the ordinary CH of its set model. The ground satisfies $\neg\mathrm{CH}$ and the forcing site satisfies $\mathrm{CH}$. Non-contingency of CH and Necessity of Second-Order Analysis both fail.

## Suprema and atoms

Internally countable families at the root have coordinatewise joins. The high ideal absorbs countable unions, and a countable sequence of forcing names admits a name for the union. At a forcing site a countable family of return entities from the ground is countable in the ground, because the forcing adds no new countable sequences of ground objects. Take its return join and the union of the forcing current rows. At a high site take the return join and current union in the same set structure. This proves necessary Countable Boolean Completeness. The original high-germ scalar interval retains its missing join for a ground partition of $I$ into $\lambda^+$ positive blocks. In that quotient an upper bound for the block classes can omit one point per block while staying an upper bound; the omitted points are themselves a positive set. Hence Boolean Completeness at type $t$ fails at the root.

Both noncurrent root coordinates are atomless. A nonzero high support splits into two positive subsets. A nonzero forcing row splits by strengthening its positive Boolean support into two incompatible nonzero parts. The root atoms are current-only singletons. At a site a current-only singleton is strong, and any further atom lies wholly in a root atom's return coordinate. Through every history these atoms are singletons or bottom in one coordinate, giving necessary strength of atoms and a true strong atom at each point. A root self-atom cannot have an occupied site coordinate since it would there be instantiated and split by its nonzero return. At a site a current-empty self-atom has a nonzero self-atom for its return, because that return is its only proper generating step. This gives necessary strength of self-atoms as well.
