# Mathematics in the pair-flip word models

The [pair-flip word models](free-words-supported-pair-flips.html) satisfy Second-Order Analytic Necessitism and fail revised Mathematical Possibilism. These conclusions hold for both countable and uncountable infinite word alphabets. All calculations use the stated classical external metatheory.

## A small atomless complete Boolean algebra

The flip group acts on the individual pairs and fixes the words. In particular it acts trivially on the whole propositional carrier $D_t=\mathcal P(W)$, where $W$ is the free word monoid. Every external set or relation on propositional inputs is a current row with empty group support. Fix a scalar code for each member of the complete atomless Boolean algebra $B=\mathrm{RO}(2^\omega)$ of regular open subsets of Cantor space, and use its external order as the presented order. This coding is possible since $|B|=2^{\aleph_0}$ and $W$ is infinite. Each regular open is determined by a set of basic cylinders, giving the upper bound. For a lower bound, take the regularizations of unions of subfamilies of a countable family of pairwise disjoint basic cylinders, each cylinder retaining its membership bit. Arbitrary joins are given by regularization of open unions. Every nonempty regular open contains two disjoint basic cylinders, so $B$ is atomless.

The inexact ordinal benchmark contains infinitely many group-fixed values: take zero and the successive finite order thresholds obtained by applying its successor. The $i$th threshold holds of a well-order on a finite field exactly when the field has at least $i$ elements, by induction using an initial segment below its last member. Every finite ordered set of individuals is admitted with its finite support. These thresholds are thus distinct, and each is fixed by the entire group as a closed term.

No countable subset $Y$ of the scalar carrier of $B$ strictly exceeds the benchmark. An injection from $Y$ into those fixed finite-order thresholds has empty support and is a current graph. Consequently any $Y\subseteq B$ strictly above the benchmark has uncountable external cardinality, at most $2^{\aleph_0}$. Choose an externally countably infinite subset $A$ of $Y$. All subsets of $A$ are admitted, so representing its powers requires at least $2^{\aleph_0}$ distinct representatives. Every graph between subsets of $Y$ is admitted. Hence a subset $C$ strictly smaller than $Y$ in the model has strictly smaller external cardinality and cannot supply those representatives. This rules out the power-closed $Y$ forbidden by revised smallness, and proves that $B$ is small. But every principal interval in $\mathcal P(W)$ is a power set, including the one-element interval at bottom. None is isomorphic to the nontrivial atomless $B$.

## Rigid real structures at the relational types

Choose countably many letters from the infinite alphabet. For each $S\subseteq\mathbb N$ take a proposition whose truth at a nonempty word records whether the word's first letter belongs to the corresponding set, using false at the empty word. For every fixed suffix $h$ the transported labels remain pairwise distinct, since prepending a single test letter reads that letter's original membership. Lift the labels to any relational sort $\tau$ by taking the full argument row when the proposition holds and the empty row otherwise. Every such entity is fixed by the flip group. Nonemptiness of all argument domains ensures the same distinction among the labels after all words.

Use disjoint such labels for the usual numbers and reals, with their successor and membership graphs. For any set $A$ of tuples of labels, prescribe the relation whose row at $h$ is the image $h[A]$. This is an admitted group-fixed profile. All its rows are exact images, so the profile is rigid. At each word the labels are distinct, and every subset of the number labels occurs as the membership set of the appropriate real label. Every admitted local relation on the number and real labels has an external tuple restriction $A$. Taking all the profiles for such $A$ and putting their images at each word gives an admitted group-fixed power relation, itself rigid by exactness. At a later word, each restriction of a newly available local relation is still the image of some such $A$ because transport is a bijection between the original labels and their designated images. This constructs the necessary real structure and its represented powers at all finite arities.

There is no real structure at the individual type. Suppose one is presented, and take a common countable support for its finitely many parameters. Its pointwise stabilizer fixes the zero and every standard iterate of its successor. Their set is a supported current property, so Peano induction exhausts the number row by those standard iterates. Every subset of this infinite row has the same stabilizer and is admitted. For any element in the real row, its image under any stabilizer flip has exactly the same number members. Extensionality therefore fixes that real individually. But the stabilizer fixes only the individuals in pairs meeting the countable support. Fullness for subsets of the number row requires continuum many distinct reals, a contradiction. The same reasoning applies to the stated finite-support interpretation.
