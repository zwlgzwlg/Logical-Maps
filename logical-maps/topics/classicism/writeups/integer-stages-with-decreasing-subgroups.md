# Integer stages with decreasing subgroups

Necessary Rigid Comprehension and Logical Necessity can hold while Rigid Power fails at every stage. The same model satisfies necessary Tame Rigidity and Boolean Completeness. The missing future subrelation distinguishes between infinitely many finite cycles of a permutation.

## The action and domains

Let $G=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z q_i$ and $H_n=\bigoplus_{i\ge n}\mathbb Z q_i$. The objects are the integers. There are arrows $n\to m$ exactly when $n\le m$. Each such arrow bears an arbitrary group coefficient and a word over a countably infinite alphabet. Compose by group addition and word concatenation. At every stage the individual domain is the same disjoint union $\bigcup_i A_i$. The set $A_i$ consists of one cycle of each positive finite length, rotated by $q_i$; all other generators fix these points. An arrow acts on individuals by its group coefficient. Every individual has a finite group orbit.

At relation type, form profiles of arbitrary tuple rows on arrows out of $n$ satisfying two conditions. First, postcomposition by a target group unit $g$ transports a row by $g$ on its tuple inputs. Second, the orbit of the *whole profile* under the source units in $H_n$ is finite. The actions on tuple arguments are already defined by induction on type. On the ordinary profile, source units and all other arrows act by precomposition. Since $H_m\subseteq H_n$ and coefficients commute, transport to $m$ has finite $H_m$ orbit. Target equivariance survives restriction as well. Group units act bijectively on all types.

For an abstraction, evaluate its body at each target on the transported parameters and all the target arguments. Equivariance under group units follows by induction on terms: they act bijectively on quantified variables, and also on the outgoing arrows by right composition. The source translates of this entire abstract depend on the source translates of its parameter tuple. This tuple has finite $H_n$ orbit, and the abstract therefore meets the second condition. Partial application and abstraction respect transport by the same evaluation formula. Different profiles differ at a definite row and tuple, giving the separation needed for the C action semantics.

## Exact relations and Boolean joins

For a current relation $X$ at $n$, its current tuple set $E$ has finite $H_n$ orbit: for each group unit $g$, the current row of $gX$ is $gE$. The exact-image profile
$$
 K_E(f)=f[E]
$$
is target-equivariant and its source translate by $g$ is the exact-image profile of $gE$. Thus it has finite $H_n$ orbit and is admitted. Its rows are exact after every transport. This gives persistence and weak inextensibility everywhere: any future member is the image of a current one along the tested arrow. Hence $K_E$ is a rigid coextension. If $X$ itself is weakly rigid, persistence gives the inclusion from $K_E$ to $X$, and its weak inextensibility gives the reverse inclusion, since $K_E$ necessarily accepts each current member. Therefore $X=K_E$ and is rigid. This proves necessary Rigid Comprehension and necessary Tame Rigidity.

For a current family $E$ of relation entities take the pointwise union $J_f=\bigcup_{Y\in E}Y_f$. Target equivariance is immediate. Its source translate by $g$ is the union formed from the family $gE$, so the finite orbit condition follows from that of the current family. Because the native order compares all rows on all future tuples, this union is the least upper bound at its type. Boolean Completeness holds necessarily.

## A missing rigid subrelation

At $n$ the universal individual property is exact, since every arrow is a bijection on the identical individual carrier. It is therefore rigid. At $n+1$ let $W\subseteq A_n$ contain one point of each $q_n$-cycle. Its whole $q_n$-orbit as a set is infinite: an integer fixing this choice must be divisible by every positive length. Every member of $H_{n+1}$ fixes $A_n$ pointwise. Thus $W$ has its admitted rigid exact representative $K_W$ at $n+1$. Its $q_n$-orbit is still infinite by evaluating the current rows. It lies below the transported universal individual property.

For every arrow $h:n\to n+1$ the image of any source relation $Y$ has finite $q_n$-orbit: some positive multiple of $q_n$ fixes $Y$ as a whole entity at $n$, and commutation carries this identity to $hY$. At $n$ form a tester whose row at arrows to $n+1$ requires finite whole $q_n$-orbit, and which is full elsewhere. It is target-equivariant since all group units commute. It has trivial source orbit since its rows depend only on the endpoint. Hence it is admitted and necessarily accepts every current relation. Yet it rejects $K_W$ at $n+1$. This refutes weak inextensibility for both the rigid and weakly rigid power properties of the bound, as well as ordinary Barcan at the type of individual properties. The argument works at every $n$.

## Logical Necessity and the scalar algebra

Shifting the stage, generator index and individual coordinate together by one is an isomorphism of the pure systems. In particular the same pure matrices are instantiated at every stage. Scalar profiles are unrestricted subsets of the pairs consisting of an accessible target stage and a word: target equivariance makes them independent of the coefficient, and then their source group orbit is trivial. For one proposition name select a witness at stage zero for each instantiated pure matrix, allocating distinct final letters. On each sector ending in its assigned letter specify the scalar by the witness on the word with that letter stripped. Off those sectors use bottom. Transport through the assigned letter at the identity coefficient recovers the full witness. This gives Logical Necessity for a nonvacuous signature.

The scalar algebra is atomic at all stages, with atoms the singleton stage/word addresses. After any transport the permitted stage order and cancellation of words leave one possible address or none. The atoms are therefore strong. The true atom at the current stage and empty word is possible only after an empty-word arrow remaining at that stage, where all true scalars still hold. It witnesses ND-necessity for each truth. Scalar transport is onto: any desired post-entrance profile can be prescribed arbitrarily on that entrance's word sector at the later stages and extended elsewhere by bottom. The individual maps are bijections. Thus BF at $e$ and $t$, strong scalar atomicity and truth collapse all hold necessarily.

## Atoms at higher types

At one target-stage and word address $(m,w)$ the whole table is determined by its row at the zero coefficient, by target equivariance. Restricting a relation to this single address preserves the finite source orbit. Any atom therefore has exactly one nonempty address, with a row $D$ fixed by a finite-index subgroup $K$ of $H_n$.

For $x\in D$, the tuple has finite $H_m$ orbit. As $m-n$ is finite, $H_n/H_m$ is finitely generated abelian. Thus $H_n/\operatorname{Stab}_{H_n}(x)$ is finitely generated abelian too: the image of $H_m$ is finite and the quotient by this finite group is finitely generated. If $Kx$ is not a singleton, there exists a finite-index subgroup of $K$ whose orbit of $x$ is a proper nonempty part of $Kx$. Indeed every nonzero element of a finitely generated abelian group survives in some finite quotient, by its decomposition into cyclic factors. This suborbit has finite orbit as a set under $H_n$ and gives an admitted proper part of the one-address relation. If instead $Kx$ is a singleton, the singleton row itself is admitted, so atomhood forces $D=\{x\}$. Conversely, any one-address singleton whose tuple has finite $H_n$ orbit is an atom. Its sole address either disappears after transport or yields a new single address with one translated tuple, whose orbit under the new subgroup is still finite. Consequently all atoms are strong at every type and stage.

The orbit under $H_n$ of the witness $K_W$ at $n+1$ from the power counterexample defines an admitted row at one address. It is nonempty and contains no tuple with finite $H_n$ orbit. Hence this nonzero higher relation has no atom below it, although the scalar algebra is strongly atomic.

## Analysis and arithmetic

Choose continuum-many binary sequences on the integers with pairwise cofinally different values. For instance, on positive stages repeat the $j$th input bit on an unbounded set of addresses disjoint from those used for other bits. Let a sequence's scalar label depend only on the final absolute stage. From every starting stage these labels are still distinct, and transports take each to the label of the same sequence. Their orbits under all the coefficient groups are trivial. Constant abstraction supplies such labels in every relational type.

For any external subset of tuples of these labels, its exact profile is available by the same construction as $K_E$. The family of all such profiles on a fixed arity of labelled tuples is again a trivial-orbit set, with an exact profile of its own. Disjoint sets of labels for the naturals and their subsets thus support the ordinary full real structure, persistently, with represented powers of every positive arity. At the individual type there cannot be a real structure. A common finite-index subgroup of $H_n$ fixes its finitely many proposed parameters. It fixes each standard successor iterate of zero. Arbitrary subsets of the standard iterates therefore have exact, admitted current predicates, including the set of those iterates itself. Induction identifies the number row with the standard iterates, and fullness would require continuum-many distinct coded reals on a countable individual carrier. This proves Second-Order Analytic Necessitism.

At any counting type the standard closed Frege numeral values have a trivial group orbit as a set, so they too have an exact current representative. This inductive cut restricts the local Frege numbers to standard values. The individual and scalar carriers are infinite at every stage. Their Infinity Axioms hold necessarily. Guarded arithmetic is standard, with all the same closed numeral values transported to each subsequent stage under the necessary Peano guard. Arithmetic Necessitism and Necessity of Arithmetic follow.
