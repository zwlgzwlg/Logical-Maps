# Functional Choice without either rigid power

The universal property of individuals is rigid at every point. At the designated point Functional Choice holds, but a rigid subproperty of that universal property becomes available at an entrance and violates a test necessarily passed by every current weakly rigid subproperty. The construction changes the ideal of finite sets in the [reduced-power retraction](reduced-power-retraction.html) to the ideal of countable subsets of $\omega_1$ and takes generic entrances. It works in ZFC.

## The types and entrances

Start with a countable elementary substructure $M$ of $H_{(\beth_\omega)^+}$. The latter has Choice, Collection and all powersets needed in the following fixed finite-type constructions. At each subsequent root use the same internal definitions in its elementary extension. Write $I=\omega_1^M$ and $\mathcal I=[I]^{\le\aleph_0}$ internally. In each such $M$ form two typed domains $R,S$ with $R_e=S_e=\omega^M$. For a relation type $\tau=\bar\sigma t$ let $B_R=\prod_i R_{\sigma_i}$ and $B_S=\prod_i S_{\sigma_i}$, using a singleton for the empty tuple. Set
$$
 R_\tau=\mathcal P(B_R)\times(\mathcal P(B_S)^I/{=_{\mathcal I}}),
 \qquad S_\tau=\mathcal P(B_S)\times R_\tau .
$$
The equivalence compares whole rows off an internally countable set. The first row gives current application. Write $\pi(C,X)=X$ at $S$. For each $X=(A,[f])$ choose a representative $f_X$ internally and put $h_yX=(f_X(y),X)$ for $y\in I$. At $e$ both virtual maps are the identity. In particular $\pi h_y=1$ for these virtual maps.

The designated object is $r(M)$. Take a child $s(N,k)$ for every generic ultrafilter on the quotient Boolean algebra $\mathcal P(I)^M/\mathcal I^M$, generic over this countable structure. Let $j:M\to N$ be the associated ultrapower by the internally represented functions and $k=[y\mapsto y]$. The entrance acts by $a\mapsto ja$ on individuals and by
$$
 X\longmapsto ((j f_X)(k),jX)
$$
on relations. Its child $s(N,k)$ has one child $r(N)$, with map $\pi$. Iterate through finite histories, always using fresh successor objects and composing the indicated transports. Every stage remains externally countable. Łoś's theorem applies using the internal choice witnesses; well-foundedness of subsequent ultrapowers is unnecessary. Every nonzero quotient condition occurs in a generic branch. For the signature verdict use a single propositional name with current bit false and with entrance support $C$ such that both $C$ and its complement are internally uncountable. The pure arguments below work under every interpretation.

Every $j$ is onto its target $\omega$ when restricted to its source $\omega$. Indeed, for each represented $v:I\to\omega^M$, conditions contained in one fiber of $v$ are dense: an internally uncountable positive condition cannot be an internally countable union of internally countable intersections. The generic meets this dense set. In contrast choose an internal injection $D:I\to\mathcal P(\omega)$. The real $(jD)(k)$ differs from $jA$ for every old $A\subseteq\omega$ since each fiber of $D$ is null. Both facts apply at every iteration.

## Term closure

For each fixed finite type, choose the representative-selection functions in the initial model and take their elementary images on descendants. A term's local truth calculation uses current application, entity equality, Boolean operations and the quantifiers over the indicated internal domain. For an abstract at $R$, use its actual extension for the first row. Its second coordinate is the germ of the rows at $S$ given by evaluating the matrix on all current bound arguments there with parameters transported by $h_y$. For an abstract at $S$, take its current extension and as its return coordinate use the just-defined $R$-abstract on the projected parameters. The same rule with no bound arguments computes propositions, and partial application evaluates the supplied arguments in the appropriate row. This simultaneous recursion first defines the $R$ case of each term using the local evaluations of proper subterms. Its $S$ case can then use that $R$ case. Each fixed calculation is an internal set-theoretic definition.

At a genuine entrance, changing representatives of finitely many parameters affects only a null set of indices. The entire row of the abstract at $s(N,k)$ therefore equals the $k$-row of the elementary image of its root representative, including when quantification ranges over new arguments in $N$. The return coordinates coincide by elementary transfer of the root term on $j$-images. Partial application has the same whole-row agreement off the finite union of exceptional sets. Terms commute with $\pi$ by construction. A difference between root entities is seen in the current row or on a positive set of differing entrance rows. In the latter case choose a generic containing the difference set and use a distinguishing argument tuple in its ultrapower. At $s$ any difference outside the current row is visible after projection. This verifies term transport and extensional separation for the C action semantics.

## The selector and the missing subset

At $r(M)$ choose internally an allowed whole output $d_x$ for each input $x$ of a currently serial relation. For an output type $\bar\beta t$ prescribe the selector's current row on $(x,\bar z)$ by the current application of $d_x$. At virtual index $y$ prescribe its row on $(h_yx,\bar z)$ by the current application of $h_y d_x$, and use the empty row on other inputs. The assignments agree because $h_y$ is injective. For each fixed $x$, application recovers the chosen whole $d_x$: the current rows agree and its entrance data agree modulo the ideal. This proves Functional Choice at all its types.

The universal individual property $U$ is rigid throughout the cone since every individual transport is bijective. If an individual property $Y$ is weakly rigid at a root, write $A\subseteq\omega$ for its current row. Its members persist, and for every individual $a$ outside $A$ the inequality tester $x\ne a$ necessarily holds of each present member, so $Y\le(\lambda x\, .\,x\ne a)$. As no new individuals occur, the row at every continuation is exactly the image of $A$. This also makes $Y$ fully rigid.

Define a root tester $T$ of individual properties with full current row. Its $y$th entrance row contains exactly those $S$ properties whose current row differs from $D(y)$. At every root descendant the transported tester has full current row. At each subsequent generic entrance the excluded row is the new real given by the transported $D$ and that entrance's index. It differs from the image of every subset in the immediately preceding model, including the transported original $A$. Thus every current weakly rigid subproperty of $U$ necessarily satisfies $T$.

At the first entrance put $A'=(jD)(k)$. Internally at $r(N)$ the property $K=(A',[y\mapsto A'])$ has exact image rows throughout its cone, by bijectivity on individuals. At $s(N,k)$ the property $(A',K)$ likewise has exact image rows. It is a rigid subproperty of the transported $U$, and fails the transported $T$ there. The properties of rigid and of weakly rigid subrelations of $U$ both fail weak inextensibility at the root. This refutes Rigid Power and Weakly Rigid Power.

## The size of a weakly rigid extension

Work inside the current root model when assigning cardinalities to present rows. Suppose a weakly rigid $F=(E,[V])$ has uncountable $E$. Every $x\in E$ satisfies $h_yx\in V(y)$ on a conull set, since a positive set of exceptions would occur in a generic branch and contradict persistence. This criterion is internal for all $x$ in $E$. Choose distinct $x_\gamma\in E$ for $\gamma<\omega_1$. Recursively choose distinct $y_\gamma$ with $h_{y_\gamma}x_\gamma\in V(y_\gamma)$. Use a tester with full current row, excluding just that last tuple at $y_\gamma$, and full on all other rows. Because every $h_y$ is injective, each fixed tuple is excluded at most once. This statement transfers to the root descendants, and their entrance generics avoid its exceptional set for each previously fixed tuple. Every present member thus necessarily passes, while a generic through the uncountable set of selected indices supplies a later member not passing. This contradicts weak inextensibility.

For an internally countable current $E$, form $H_E=(E,[y\mapsto h_y[E]])$. Every generic embedding sends each internal countable set onto the pointwise image of its members, by applying surjectivity on $\omega$ to an internal enumeration. It follows that $H_E$ has exact direct-image rows along each entrance, each return and all compositions, making it fully rigid. A weakly rigid coextension equals $H_E$ by persistence and weak inextensibility. At $s$, if $Z=(T,H)$ is weakly rigid, the current row of $H$ is $\pi[T]$. Persistence gives one inclusion. For the other use the tester with full current $s$-row, root return row $\pi[T]$ and all its entrance rows full. It necessarily holds of each $z\in T$, including along all subsequent returns by elementary embedding. Any root tester necessarily holding of each member of $\pi[T]$ similarly lifts with full current $s$-row. Thus $H$ is weakly rigid on return, using also persistence of $Z$ through that return. It is rigid by the root result. Hence $Z$ is itself fully rigid as its proper continuations all go through $H$. This gives necessary Tame Rigidity. An uncountable current row has no weakly rigid coextension, and the root proposition sort furnishes such a row.

For full Inextensible Comprehension the same bound holds without persistence. Let $F=(E,[V])$ be inextensible. Weak inextensibility at an entrance forces its current return row into the projection of its current $s$-row by the full-return tester above. Therefore $E\subseteq\pi[V(y)]$ on a conull set of indices, simultaneously for this entire row; any positive set of exceptions has a generic through it. If $E$ were uncountable, choose $y_\gamma$ distinct in this set and tuples in $V(y_\gamma)$ with pairwise different projections in $E$. The tester omitting just the selected tuple at each selected index, full elsewhere, can exclude $h_yx$ at most once for each fixed $x$. It contradicts weak inextensibility just as above. In contrast a current row with empty entrance rows at $r$, or with bottom return at $s$, is always a weakly inextensible coextension: each tester already accepts the required tuples at the current row and at all its subsequent elementary root images.

## Algebras, comprehension and choice

At a root, for every current extension $E$ take its coextension with row $\pi^{-1}[E]$ at each virtual entrance (with the projection acting coordinatewise). Along every entrance the original root input and its projected image agree on membership, including for new tuples at subsequent roots. At $s$, membership of every tuple agrees with membership of its projection after the return. Thus this coextension and its complement are both persistent. This gives Gallin Extensional Comprehension at the root. An internal well-order on each current carrier exists, and the root relation using the chosen $r$-order and the constant $s$-order on entrance rows is a necessary well-order. Its defining well-order assertions transfer between elementary stages.

The algebra at $r$ is a product of the current powerset and the sequence-row algebra modulo the countable ideal. Any positive germ splits by partitioning its uncountable set of occupied indices into two uncountable sets. The only atoms at $r$ are therefore current singletons with empty germ. At $s$ the atoms are the current singletons with bottom return and the atoms of the return factor with empty current row. A current root singleton becomes an atom on the return factor at every entrance and returns to its elementary image. A current-only $s$ singleton goes to bottom upon return. This proves necessary strongness of all atoms and necessary Strong Actuality via the true current singleton at each kind. A nonzero scalar germ alone has no atom below it.

Every family that is countable in the native sense admits an internal enumeration: at each kind all current internal relations exist and the internal natural numbers enumerate the Frege numerals. In the map's countability relation choose the least assigned number for each member. The componentwise union of countably many rows gives their join in the germ algebra, since a countable union of null sets is null. Products with the current-row powersets preserve these joins, also at $s$. Thus Countable Boolean Completeness holds necessarily at all types. Full propositional Boolean Completeness fails: partition $I$ into $\omega_1$ uncountable blocks internally. Any upper bound of their scalar germs contains all but countably many points of each block. Remove one member of the upper bound from each block. This strictly lowers it modulo the ideal while leaving it an upper bound, and the tested family is admitted by the full current row.

At propositional input let the Barcan tester be full currently and at index $y$ admit only the virtual images of $h_y$. Every present input necessarily passes, by the pointwise definition, which persists at elementary root descendants. At a genuine entrance, a scalar with the same return as an admitted input and the opposite current bit lies outside the image, since the return uniquely determines that input. It fails the tester there.

## Arithmetic and analysis

Each current internal subset of a type is represented. In particular the set of values of the standard Frege numerals is a current inductive predicate at either kind. All stages are $\omega$-standard and all types are internally infinite, so every natural finite size occurs, the numerals are distinct, and their values are precisely the number property at that type. The denotation of each numeral transports to that of the same numeral; the number property has exact image rows along every continuation. This proves Arithmetic Necessitism and necessary Axioms of Infinity at $e$ and $t$.

The root scalar sort carries an internal real-number structure: partition $I$ into countably many uncountable blocks to give distinct labels for all internal subsets of $\omega$, and use full current rows for the arithmetic and membership graphs. In any real-number structure there the internal current real carrier has size at least the internal continuum, since all internal subsets of its numbers are tested. Such a carrier cannot have a weakly rigid extension by the size calculation. This refutes Analytic Necessitism. At either kind, every real-number structure nonetheless computes the full ordinary analysis of its underlying set model, including all internal relations of each finite arity. This follows from the current powersets at all types and the ordinary categoricity of the full real structure inside that model. All the successive set models are elementarily equivalent. Consequently every fixed parameter-free sentence of full second-order analysis has the same truth value at all possible real structures throughout the cone, as required for Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.

## Self-atoms, the hats and the signature

At a root a relation $X$ with a nonzero germ is instantiated on a generic entrance through its occupied rows. The transported relation has both that inhabited row and the nonzero return $jX$. It splits there, so $X$ is not a self-atom. Any remaining self-atom is a singleton current row with empty germ. At an entrance, a self-atom with inhabited current row is already an atom. One with empty current row has a nonzero self-atom return, since every instantiation of its return is an instantiation after a return path. This proves necessary strongness of self-atoms at every type. A nonzero scalar germ alone has no self-atom below it.

The true current-only scalar at an entrance becomes bottom after the return. It witnesses ND-necessity for every truth there. At a root let $a$ be the scalar with true current bit and empty germ. At every later root it is true, and any original root truth remains true there by elementarity. At every entrance $a$ is false and is not ND-possible, by the current-only entrance guard. In the definition of two-hat necessity take $a$ as the hat guard. This proves two-hat necessity of each root truth, hence necessarily at all stages by the entrance calculation. On the other hand every true root guard is again possible after an entrance via its return, while $a$ is false at the entrance. Thus $a$ is not ND-necessary at the root.

The sole named scalar cannot equal any pure closed term, since the current truth of a closed term at the virtual entrance has no parameters and hence is independent of $y$. Its root germ is constant. This gives Independence in the one-name signature. The four-element Boolean algebra is realized on four scalar labels using current rows and is revised-small by the finite-power bound. Its two atoms would map to two distinct interval atoms; each atom of a principal interval is a global atom. The root proposition algebra has exactly one, so Mathematical Possibilism fails.

## Larger ideals and analytic necessity

For an internally infinite cardinal $\lambda$ equal to the continuum or its power-set cardinal, use $I=\lambda^+$ and quotient by the ideal of sets of size at most $\lambda$. All these parameters and their required finite-type powersets lie in the same $H_{(\beth_\omega)^+}$. A generic embedding $j$ maps every set of size at most $\lambda$ onto the pointwise image of its members: for an internal function from $I$ into such a set, every positive condition has a positive refinement on a single fiber. Thus $j(\lambda)=j[\lambda]$, while each fixed injection $D:I\to\mathcal P(\lambda)$ gives a subset $(jD)(k)$ different from the elementary image of every old subset. All cardinalities and powersets here are internal to the set model in use.

The preceding proof of the weak-rigidity and inextensibility bound now rules out current rows larger than $\lambda$. Run the diagonal recursion for $\lambda^+$ steps, picking a fresh index in each required conull set. Conversely $H_E$ is a rigid exact-image representative for each $|E|\le\lambda$ by surjectivity on $E$. The arguments for the algebra, atoms, selection, the hats and arithmetic continue to apply: a positive index set splits into two positive sets, the ideal absorbs countable unions, and a partition into $\lambda^+$ positive blocks gives the missing full join.

To test rigid power, take a family $E=\{p_\xi:\xi<\lambda\}$ of distinct root scalars, using disjoint positive supports in the entrance germ. Its canonical representative $F=H_E$ is rigid. Every root weakly rigid subproperty of $F$ has current extension $E_A=\{p_\xi:\xi\in A\}$ for an internal $A\subseteq\lambda$, and equals $H_{E_A}$. At virtual index $y$ let the tester omit just properties whose current extension is $h_y[E_{D(y)}]$. Its root row is full. As $h_y$ is injective, the virtual exclusion can apply to a fixed canonical $H_{E_A}$ only on a null set, and the same calculation holds at all further roots by elementary embedding. At the first generic entrance, let $T$ be the excluded row, now given by applying the virtual map at $k$ to the subset of elementary labels indexed by $(jD)(k)$. Its size is at most $j\lambda$. It occurs as the current row of the entrance property $(T,H_{\pi[T]})$. This property is rigid: at the return its row is exactly $\pi[T]$, and $H_{\pi[T]}$ has exact images at every later arrow. It entails the transported bound, since these rows are contained in its corresponding exact rows. Thus the same two power principles fail at the larger fixed carrier.

At every relational type there is a coherent family of at least continuum many distinct root labels. One may use the scalars above for $t$, and the constant-in-the-arguments relation with value a chosen scalar at the other types; evaluation on any arguments recovers the scalar. Every transport of a root label is injective through its retained elementary return. Give separate labels to the natural numbers and to all their subsets. Use canonical exact-image relations $H_E$ for these carriers, the successor graph and membership, whose sizes are at most the continuum. Their members remain distinct throughout the cone. At every continuation, including an entrance with additional current relations, the current sets of indices determined on the number labels are internal subsets of $\omega$. The generic embedding carries the old set of reals onto the new one because its size is at most $\lambda$. Thus the real labels still represent every current subset of the number labels. Induction gives the usual natural numbers there, so the structure is necessarily a real structure. At $e$ the individual domain remains countable with full current internal rows and cannot carry a real structure at any stage. This proves Analytic Necessitism for both larger ideals.

If $\lambda$ is the continuum, every actual real structure at $t$ has at least continuum many real members. A represented unary power would have at least $2^{\lambda}$ distinct current members since every internal subset of those reals is tested. Its representing family must be rigid, contradicting the bound of $\lambda$. Thus Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fails there.

If $\lambda$ is the cardinality of the power set of the continuum, fix any finite arity and take all $H_B$ for internal relations $B$ on the tuples from the number and real labels. Each is rigid and bounded by the carriers. The family of all these $H_B$ itself has size at most $\lambda$, so its canonical exact-image property is rigid. At any later stage every relation restricts to an internal subset of the appropriate label tuples. The embeddings map the entire old power set of these tuples onto the new one since its size is at most $\lambda$. Hence the current restriction agrees with some transported $H_B$ at each continuation, as the represented-power clause requires. All standard finite arities are covered. Second-Order Analytic Necessitism consequently holds together with the failures of both general rigid powers.
