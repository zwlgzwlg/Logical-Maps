# Tame Rigidity at the paired stages

Necessary Tame Rigidity holds in the [unrestricted paired stages](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html), their [fixed-or-collapsed restriction](fixed-or-collapsed-pairs.html), and the [variant with zero coefficients](paired-stages-with-zero-arrows.html). In the unrestricted case the finite-support factorization for the original two-object model extends to countable pins. The restriction calls for a different factorization, since a pair sent to two different intermediate sites must stay fixed. [The original paired-individuals model](paired-individuals-two-object.html)

Fix a weakly rigid relation $F$ at stage $n$, with current row $E$. For any countable pin $S$ containing a pin of $F$, define $B_S(E)_a$ to consist of $d z$ for $z\in E$ and ordinary arrows $d$ of the same word and target as the ordinary arrow $a$, with $d|S=a|S$. In a model with zero coefficients, put $B_S(E)_a=a[E]$ at each zero of its specified word and target. This is an admitted tester with pin $S$. It necessarily accepts every member of $E$. Weak inextensibility yields $F\le B_S(E)$. Weak persistence implies $dz\in F_d$, and $F_d=F_a$ at the compared ordinary arrows, so the reverse inclusion holds. At zeros it follows directly by weak persistence. In particular,
$$
F_a=B_S(E)_a
$$
for every such enlarged $S$. Weak inextensibility applied to $x\mapsto\Box F(x)$ likewise gives persisting membership at every successor, since $\Box F(x)$ already holds necessarily for each $x\in E$.

Let $h:n\to m$ and $k:m\to l$ be ordinary arrows. Suppose a tester $W$ at $m$, pinned by $P$, necessarily accepts every tuple in $F_h$. To prove $F_{kh}\subseteq W_k$, it suffices to factor a member $y$ as $k'h'z$, with $z\in E$, $h'$ agreeing with $h$ on the original pin of $F$, and $k'$ agreeing with $k$ on $P$. Keep the words of $h,k$ respectively on $h',k'$.

At any two stages the ordinary pair labels in each coordinate are identical. Start the source pin $S$ with the original pin and all source singletons. Close it under ordinary pair partners. For each intermediate ordinary pair meeting $P\cup h[S]$, add both seats of its source copy to $S$. Repeat these enlargements countably many times and take the union. It is still countable, and any intermediate ordinary pair meeting $P\cup h[S]$ now has its source copy in $S$. Let $Q$ be the block closure of $P\cup h[S]$ at $m$. By the row equality, $y=dz$ for $z\in E$ and ordinary $d$ agreeing with $kh$ on this final $S$. Let $L$ be a countable, block-closed pin of $z$.

Prescribe $h'=h$ on $S$ and $k'=k$ on $Q$. These prescriptions already yield $k'h'=d$ on $S$. In the fixed-or-collapsed model, take each ordinary pair of $L$ outside $S$ that $d$ leaves pointwise fixed. Its intermediate copy misses $Q$ by the closure rule. Set $h'$ and $k'$ to the identity on its two copies. For each remaining ordinary pair of $L$ outside $S$, the two images under $d$ coincide. Choose a separate fresh intermediate ordinary pair in its coordinate, avoiding $Q$ and the pairs just required to stay fixed. Collapse the source pair to one seat of this intermediate pair under $h'$ and collapse the intermediate pair to the prescribed value under $k'$. These countably many prescriptions never exhaust the uncountable supply in any coordinate. Complete both maps blockwise on the remaining sites. All source singletons already lie in $S$. We have $k'h'=d$ on $L$.

In the unrestricted model one may instead send each new $L$-pair bijectively to a fresh intermediate pair, on which $k'$ copies both of its values under $d$. Its two output sites belong to one block by admissibility of $d$. This also gives $k'h'=d$ on $L$.

In either case $h'z\in F_{h'}=F_h$ by persistence and pinning. Necessity of the tester at that tuple gives $k'h'z\in W_{k'}=W_k$. The matching word and agreement on $L$ give $k'h'z=dz=y$ as whole tuples. This proves weak inextensibility after $h$. If either coefficient in the composite is zero, $F_{kh}=(kh)[E]$ by the isolated zero row. Each member thus already has the form $k(hz)$ with $hz\in F_h$, giving the same conclusion directly. Together with the persistence assertion, every transport of $F$ is weakly rigid. Since the initial stage was arbitrary this proves necessary Tame Rigidity.
