# Strong atoms at every type without higher-type Atomicity

All propositional atoms are strong and propositions are necessarily strongly atomic. At each point every atom of every relational type is strong. Nevertheless a specific property of individuals at the root has no self-atom below it. The difference is visible in the action of the integers on the terminal individuals.

## Periodic rows

There are two objects $r,s$, with an entrance $h:r\to s$, no returns, and an additive copy of $\mathbb Z$ acting at each. Let $hn=nh$ and include all translates of the entrance. At $s$ take the classical full hierarchy on $A=\mathbb Z\sqcup\{*\}$, with integers acting by translation on $\mathbb Z$ and fixing $*$. They act on sets and relations by direct image. These invertible actions preserve application and classical truth, including the truth of formulas on transported parameters.

At $r$ there is one individual, sent to $*$. A relation of type $\tau=\bar\sigma t$ is a pair
$$
 (R,Y),\qquad R\subseteq D_{\bar\sigma}(r),\quad Y\subseteq D_{\bar\sigma}(s).
$$
Each coordinate is fixed by all multiples of some positive integer. Finite intersections of such subgroups again have finite index, so one period works for both. Integers translate both rows and $h$ projects to $Y$. These definitions use only the already constructed actions on the lower argument types. Every source entity and tuple has finite orbit. A scalar is a pair of bits. Use the fixed individual and top relations to interpret names.

For a lambda abstract at $r$, evaluate its body on all current tuples for its first coordinate; for the second evaluate its classical abstraction on the transported parameters at $s$. Every common period of the parameters preserves both resulting sets. Indeed translating by an integer preserves application and bijects each domain and the arrows with a given endpoint, so by induction it preserves satisfaction on transported assignments. Term evaluation therefore commutes with all the arrows. Any difference between two relations is detected at an actual tuple or a terminal tuple. These give the action-model clauses, with entailment exactly inclusion in both coordinates. At $s$ entailment is ordinary inclusion.

## Why the atoms stay strong

A nonzero root atom is confined to one coordinate. Every current tuple has finite orbit, so its singleton with empty terminal row is admitted. Thus a purely current atom consists of one tuple. Suppose a purely terminal atom has nonempty row $Y$ fixed by $n\mathbb Z$. For $y\in Y$, its orbit under $n\mathbb Z$ is an admitted subrow. A nonsingleton orbit itself has an admitted proper nonempty subrow. Indeed the stabilizer of $y$ in the infinite cyclic group $n\mathbb Z$ is either zero or $dn\mathbb Z$ for an integer $d\ge1$. In the first case use the orbit under $2n\mathbb Z$, and in the case $d>1$ use the singleton orbit under $dn\mathbb Z$. The case $d=1$ was already a singleton. Hence every terminal atom is one finite-orbit tuple in that coordinate. After every arrow each of the two kinds of atom is still a singleton in one coordinate or has become bottom. The terminal hierarchy is full, so singletons are atoms there too. This proves necessary Atoms are strong atoms at every type. At $t$ both root bits are atoms in a four-element algebra, and $s$ has two propositions. This gives necessary Strong Atomicity at $t$.

Take the property $(\varnothing,\mathbb Z)$ of individuals. A nonzero admitted property below it has terminal row a nonempty periodic subset of $\mathbb Z$. This row is infinite, and at $s$ any one of its members supplies a proper nonempty singleton subproperty. The row is therefore inhabited at a point where it is not an atom. The source property contains no self-atom, in particular no atom or strong atom. Conversely any self-atom with a nonempty current row must already be an atom by the definition, and any self-atom with empty current row has a terminal singleton. This also gives necessary Self-atoms are strong atoms.

The scalar $(1,0)$ is a true strong atom, impossible at the terminal. Every root truth is invariant under the root loops, so this guard witnesses its ND-necessity. At the terminal all truths are necessary.

## Completeness, comprehension and powers

For the current extension $E$ of an admitted family of relations, take the union of the first rows of its members and the union of their terminal rows. If $n\mathbb Z$ fixes $E$ setwise, it fixes both unions because both projections commute with translations. The resulting pair is the least upper bound by coordinatewise order. Complements give meets. The terminal is full, so Boolean Completeness holds necessarily at all relational types.

For any current tuple set $E$ occurring as a row define $H_E=(E,h[E])$. Both rows are periodic and at every arrow its row is the exact direct image of its actual extension. The same holds after every transport. Exact images give weak persistence, and every tester necessarily satisfied by each current member contains their images at every arrow. Thus $H_E$ is rigid. Conversely if $F$ with current row $E$ is weakly rigid, test with $H_E$. It necessarily contains each current member, so weak inextensibility gives $F\le H_E$ and persistence gives the reverse inclusion. Hence Rigid Comprehension and Tame Rigidity hold necessarily.

Let $E$ consist of root individual properties with empty current row and terminal row a single residue class modulo $n$, for any $n\ge2$. The set is invariant under translation. At $s$ take $W\subseteq h[E]$ to contain precisely $0\pmod n$ for each $n\ge2$. The sets of different moduli are different. No nonzero translation fixes $W$, since its period would have to be divisible by every positive integer. This is a terminal rigid subproperty of $hH_E$. The tester full on the current row and having as terminal row the set of all finite-orbit properties of this type is admitted: its terminal row is invariant. Every current property necessarily passes, but $W$ fails. Thus the property of rigid subproperties of $H_E$ is not weakly inextensible. The same example refutes Weakly Rigid Power and both rigidity predicates. In particular not every terminal relation is in the entrance image. Propositions, on the other hand, project surjectively, yielding necessary propositional Barcan.

## Choice and arithmetic

Coextension at the root has the unique canonical representatives $(R,\varnothing)$. Their set is invariant. At the terminal coextension is identity, so Transversal holds necessarily. At the root form the equivalence classes of codes for individual residue classes, one orbit of codes for each modulus $m\ge2$, with singleton classes for the remaining inputs. This relation is invariant. A transversal fixed by $n\mathbb Z$ cannot choose one of the $m$ cyclically permuted codes for $m$ not dividing $n$. Relational Choice fails as well. The current property consisting of one such complete orbit, with empty terminal row, is fixed by every translation. A selector for membership on nonempty properties, with period $n$, must choose an $n\mathbb Z$-fixed code from this orbit, an impossibility. Intensional Choice fails for the property with full current individual row and terminal row $\mathbb Z$. It is necessarily inhabited but admits no necessarily singleton subproperty: no singleton integer is periodic.

Every Peano system at the root is standard externally. A common period of its structure fixes its zero and every successive numeral. Their external set is an admissible inductive current row, with empty terminal coordinate, and therefore exhausts its numbers. If it is the number system of a real structure, every subset of these numbers is likewise admitted. Extensionality fixes each real under the same period, and every relation on the numbers and reals is therefore also admitted. Every real structure at the root interprets full standard second-order analysis, as does every real structure in the classical terminal. This gives Necessity of Second-Order Analysis. At any counting type the denotations of all standard Frege numeral terms form an admitted inductive current row at both objects. They give all current numbers. When the Peano guard holds they are pairwise distinct, giving standard arithmetic. If the guard holds necessarily, their sets have exact direct-image rows and their distinctions persist. Thus Necessity of Arithmetic and Arithmetic Necessitism hold.

A real structure at the root requires at least continuum many elements of its carrier by the same subset argument. The root individual properties form a countable set: their terminal rows are periodic subsets of the integers, optionally with $*$, and the current row has one bit. In contrast the terminal has continuum many individual properties and supports a real structure of that type. This refutes Analytic Necessitism. Lastly the finite Boolean algebra with three atoms can be coded in a higher root type, for example using eight distinct translation-fixed labels among the sixteen current predicates on the four root scalars. The terminal infinite individual domain supports finite well-orders of every standard size, so the finite successive inexact ordinal terms are pairwise distinct there and hence at the root. Their benchmark cannot embed into any subset of this finite algebra. The finite algebra is therefore revised-small. No interval in the four-element root proposition algebra realizes eight elements. Thus revised Mathematical Possibilism fails.

## A shared individual domain of finite cycles

Use $T=\{*\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{n\ge2}\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ for **both** individual domains. The integers rotate each cycle and fix $*$; the entrance is identity on individuals. Every individual now has finite orbit, as do all source entities by the same type recursion. The atom splitting, unions, rigid comprehension, Tame Rigidity and coextension transversal arguments apply without change. Barcan at $e$ holds necessarily by bijectivity on individuals. At each point the external standard numeral cut is admitted and represented finite subsets of arbitrary finite sizes exist. No current Frege number counts the whole infinite individual domain, giving necessary individual Infinity.

At the terminal let $W\subseteq T$ select the zero element from each cycle. Its stabilizer in $\mathbb Z$ is trivial. Thus its orbit in the terminal individual-property domain is an infinite regular orbit. A source property of properties with empty current row and precisely this orbit at the terminal is nonzero. Every nonzero periodic subrow contains infinitely many members there and fails self-atomhood. This gives the higher-type failure despite the bijection on individuals. The universal individual property is now rigid with exact rows, while its rigid terminal subproperty $W$ cannot be the image of any source property. The finite-orbit tester again proves failure of both powers and both rigidity predicates. The cycles themselves form an equivalence relation whose transversals cannot have a period. For Relational Choice, give the $n$th orbit as input to the membership selector using its invariant property with empty terminal coordinate; a selector with period $k$ has no $k\mathbb Z$-fixed output on that property when $n$ does not divide $k$.

There is a change on analysis. At each relational type whose arguments contain $e$ at any depth there are continuum many translation-fixed terminal labels. At a direct individual argument, use arbitrary unions of the cycles, ignoring any other arguments. If the occurrence is deeper, use arbitrary sets of the previously obtained fixed argument labels as the next predicate row. All other argument domains are nonempty, so the labels thus obtained remain distinct. Lift each to a fixed root entity with empty current row and that terminal coordinate. These labels remain distinct across the entrance. For every set of their tuples, its root extension and its corresponding terminal extension form an exact rigid relation. Likewise the canonical relations on these labels are fixed as entities, and every subcollection of them forms an exact rigid property. On the continuum of labels install the usual numbers, reals and membership, together with every relation of the required finite arities. This is a necessarily real structure with represented powers both at the root and at the terminal. At $e$ neither countable domain can support a real structure by the common-period subset argument. A type built without $e$ has finite domains at both points by type recursion and cannot support a Peano system. Hence Second-Order Analytic Necessitism holds. The original proof also gives Arithmetic Necessitism.

The infinite terminals distinguish the standard finite inexact ordinals in this version as well. The root scalar algebra is still four-element, so the finite algebra with three atoms again refutes revised Mathematical Possibilism.
