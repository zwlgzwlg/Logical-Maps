# Homogeneous forcing with free words

Logical Necessity and necessary Rigid Comprehension can hold while Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fails. With countable conditions the model has Analytic Necessitism. With finite conditions even Analytic Necessitism fails. Both models have the same pure theory at every point of their respective cones.

### Ground models and arrows

Use one of two definable forcing notions $Q$. The first consists of countable partial binary functions on $\omega_1$ ordered by extension. The second consists of finite partial binary functions on $\omega$. The first adds a subset of $\omega_1$ and no countable sequences of ground elements. The second adds a real. In either case $Q$ remains the same poset after any finite iteration. In the first case use countable closure, which also preserves $\omega_1$. A product of any positive finite number of copies is isomorphic to $Q$ by reindexing coordinates. These posets are weakly homogeneous, since bit flips can make two conditions compatible.

Start with a countable transitive collapse $M$ of an elementary substructure of a sufficiently large regular $H_\theta$. Choose $\theta$ larger than all the finite simple-type ranks and the sizes of their required forcing-name sets, for example above $\beth_{\omega+\omega}$. The constructions below use forcing for finite products, bounded names for subsets of given sets, and ordinary choice and replacement on the displayed sets. Equivalently one may perform them in the corresponding $H_\theta$ of the ambient forcing extensions. Force once before taking the designated site, which has ground $K=M[G_0]$. At any site with current universe $L$, the letters $a,b$ keep $L$, and $f$ can enter $L[H]$ for every $L$-generic filter on $Q$. Sites are finite histories starting at $K$. Retain all composites and identities.

### Type domains and terms

The individual type is a singleton. Construct the relational domains by induction on simple types. In each current universe $L$ a relation of type $\bar\tau t$ is an $L$-sequence indexed by the finite words $w$ on $a,b,f$. If $w$ contains $j$ forcing letters, its coordinate is a $Q^j$-name, modulo forced equality, for a subset of the product of the $\bar\tau$-domains in the extension. For $j=0$ it is an ordinary ground row. At each type use a set of bounded names for these subsets. For example, the Boolean-valued powerset of the product of the previously constructed domain names supplies representatives for every row. No compatibility restriction is placed between the coordinates. The empty tuple convention gives the scalar type. Application at the current point reads the empty-word row.

After an arrow with word $v$, the remaining coordinate at $w$ is the coordinate originally at $vw$, partially evaluated at the first forcing filters. Successive partial evaluations compose. Quotienting names by forced equality makes the result independent of all representatives. Different profiles disagree at some word on a nonzero forcing condition. Since the universe is externally countable there is a generic containing that condition, with an actual tuple distinguishing the rows. Thus profiles distinguish precisely the required relational entities.

For a fixed native term, its value is uniformly definable in the current universe. In an abstraction, at $w$ use the name for the extension of its matrix on all current argument tuples in the $Q^j$-extension, with the old parameters transported to that word. This is a bounded row by induction on the subterm, so the forcing theorem gives its name. Application and current quantification are evaluated on the full current domains, equality is whole-profile equality, and $\Box p$ is $p=\top$. Induction on terms, using the product forcing theorem for partial evaluation, proves transport under every arrow, including on new arguments in extensions. In particular each fixed closed pure sentence has a parameter-free set-theoretic translation, uniform at all sites. The original homogeneous forcing $Q$ decides its translation at the top condition. Since every finite positive product is isomorphic to $Q$, this sign in $M[G_0]$ is also its sign at every later site. Hence there is necessarily No Pure Contingency.

### Rigid relations, joins and choice

For every current ground set $E$ of tuples let $H_E$ assign at each word the direct image of $E$ under its tuple transport. The map on the checked ground tuple domain is definable in the extension by partial evaluation of its names, so replacement and the forcing theorem supply the required row name uniformly. These direct images compose. Every member persists, and a tester necessarily holding of every current member therefore covers the entire transported relation. The same argument works at each continuation, so $H_E$ is rigid. Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical, by applying each relation's weak inextensibility to the other's persistent membership. Thus every weakly rigid relation is $H_E$ for its current extension. This proves necessary Rigid Comprehension and necessary Tame Rigidity.

Every current family of relations has an ambient Boolean join. At $w$ take the union of the rows there of its transported ground members. The name is formed in the same way as the direct image. Any upper bound covers their union at every word and every generic. This proves necessary Boolean Completeness at all types. Each fixed type also has a necessarily well-ordering relation. At every word appeal to choice in the forcing extension and the maximum principle to choose a name for a well-order of the entire current domain there. Assemble the names into a single profile. At every site there is a true strong atom with true empty-word bit and all nonempty coordinates false.

Every arrow is onto at each type. For $a$ or $b$ put a desired profile on its cylinder. For a forcing entrance choose a $Q$-name for the desired target profile, forced to have that type everywhere by using a default off a condition in the actual generic. At all words $fw$ use the product-name version of this name's $w$-coordinate. This gives a ground profile specializing to that entire target entity. Consequently Barcan holds at all types throughout the cone.

### Naming the witnesses

One scalar name suffices for a nonvacuous signature. More generally use any of the map's fixed set-sized signatures. For each requested pure matrix whose existential closure holds, take a tuple of current witnesses. For finitely many such matrices choose pairwise incomparable ordinary words, for example $ba^ib$ for distinct positive $i$. On the corresponding cylinder of coordinates, give each named relational constant in that matrix the whole profile of its witness, stripping the chosen prefix. Words are incomparable and the names in any one tuple are distinct. Individual names automatically name the sole individual. A possible instance of a pure matrix always gives the pure existential at the source by No Pure Contingency. Thus every finite set of the dual Logical Necessity instances can be realized, together with the complete original pure pointed theory. Compactness for the many-sorted pointed action semantics gives a single expansion satisfying Logical Necessity along with all the stated pure verdicts. Constants are interpreted along histories by transport. This compactness step asserts Logical Necessity at the designated point.

### The continuum and its powers

For each ground real $A\subseteq\omega$ let a scalar read the bit $A(n)$ at every word ending in $ba^n$, and read false otherwise. Distinct reals give distinct profiles after every path, since one can always append $ba^n$. At any other relational type use the same bits as full or empty tuple rows. All tuple domains are inhabited. These labels provide disjoint, necessarily separated copies of the numbers and reals at every relational type, and at each site their full current number, successor, real and membership rows form an actual real structure. At the individual type the singleton precludes a Peano structure. The canonical Frege-number instance of Arithmetic Necessitism follows in both variants from necessary Rigid Comprehension by the [native implication](../index.html#result/necessary-rigid-comprehension-implies-arithmetic-necessitism). With countable conditions there are no new subsets of the numbers at a successor, and the transported real labels and their exact-image membership rows give a necessary real structure. This proves Analytic Necessitism in that variant.

Consider any proposed rigid, necessarily real structure at type $t$. Its current number part is standard internally in $L$, by induction using all current subsets, and extensionality identifies its real part $X$ with $\mathcal P(\omega)^L$. Its structure relations must all be the exact-image relations described above. The necessary-distinctness clause in RigidStr makes the map $h$ at a forcing entrance injective on its numbers and reals. With finite conditions the new Cohen real defines a new subset of the transported numbers. Each transported old real has exactly the transported old members by injectivity, and the real carrier at the successor is precisely $h[X]$. No member there represents the new real. This contradiction excludes Analytic Necessitism.

With countable conditions suppose also that there is a represented unary power. Its rigid property is the exact image of its ground members. By the definition of a represented power, each ground member is rigid and its row is contained in the original carrier $D=N\cup X$. At the successor its row is exactly the image of this ground row, and $h$ is injective on all of $D$. Fix a ground injection $i:\omega_1\to X$. If $G$ is the new generic subset, $B=i[G]$ is a new subset of $X$. The extension admits $h[B]$ as a current subset of $h[X]$. Were a member of the transported power to agree with it on the carrier, injectivity of $h$ on that carrier would give $B$ as the real-carrier portion of an old row. This contradicts its newness. Thus even the unary instance of Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fails.

Both variants also fail the weak and strong rigid power principles. Take $H_E$ on a continuum of separated real labels as above. At the source the rigid and weakly rigid subrelations are exactly $H_A$ for ground $A\subseteq E$. At the next forcing site the newly added subset gives a new rigid subrelation. The exact-image tester of the old family necessarily covers each old member and misses the new one. Thus the defining family of subrelations fails weak inextensibility in either case. For finite conditions use a countably infinite subfamily of labels.
