# Atomic return sites with failures of choice and rigid power

Functional Choice and Mathematical Possibilism can hold where every accessible relation algebra is atomic, yet all three choice principles and both rigid powers fail at a single accessible site. The one-return model also satisfies necessary Self-atoms are strong atoms. Replacing one return by two makes the unique true atom at the source fail to be strong. The construction combines [Functional Choice with returns](functional-choice-over-supported-stages.html), [bounded partial injections](bounded-partial-injections.html) and [finite multisets with free words](finite-multisets-free-words.html).

## The model

Use a root $r$ and countably many sites $s_j$, with singleton individuals everywhere. At each site partition a countable auxiliary set $A$ into infinitely many infinite classes, and let $G$ be its entire equivalence-preserving permutation group. An ordinary site loop has data $(f,k,v,w)$. Here $f$ is a partial injection on $A$, $k\in\omega\cup\{\infty\}$ bounds its domain size when finite, $v$ is a finite multiset on $I=\omega_1$ and $w$ is a word on a countably infinite alphabet. Compose the partial maps, take the minimum tag, add multisets and concatenate words with the later word on the left. The group $G$ acts by its full-domain maps at infinite tag, zero multiset and empty word.

Give each $s_j$ an entrance $h_j:r\to s_j$ and a return $j_j:s_j\to r$, with $j_jh_j=1_r$, $a h_j=h_j$, $j_j a=j_j$ for ordinary loops there. Include all reset arrows $h_i j_j$ between sites, distinct from ordinary arrows. In the two-return version provide $s_0$ with two distinct choices of $j_0$, each obeying the same equations. There are no further arrows. Use a single propositional name interpreted as top.

At relational type a root entity is a current tuple subset $R$ and a $G$-invariant tuple subset $V_i$ at each site. These rows are independent. A site entity consists of an ordinary row profile and an independent whole root entity for each of its returns. The ordinary profile is left-equivariant under $G$. It admits a finite $P\subset A$, a finite cutoff $b$ and a countable $Q\subset I$ such that its whole row depends only on $f|_P$, $\min(k,b)$, $|v|$, $v|_Q$ and $w$. The entrance sends the root entity to the constant ordinary table $V_i$ with that entire entity in each return position. Ordinary arrows shift the ordinary table and preserve the returns. Returns are coordinate projections.

Agreement on these controls persists under common postcomposition, so it implies agreement of whole transported parameters. The images of the live members of $P$ pin an ordinary transport; the same $b,Q$ suffice. In abstraction use the union of the finitely many parameter pins and maximum cutoff. The tuple of transported parameters then determines a whole row for all new arguments. At an entrance the transported parameters are fixed by $G$, so the computed row is invariant, even on arguments moved by $G$. At a return use the abstract evaluated on the respective root parameters. This gives the term and composition laws. Every difference of whole entities is visible at an address and tuple.

## Atomicity at every point

At $r$ the algebra is a product of the powerset of the current tuples and the powersets of orbit sets at the sites. Every nonzero element contains an atom. At a site, any occupied return contains the matching return-only copy of a root atom. For an occupied ordinary row at $(f,k,v,w)$ and tuple $x$, take a finite tag $k'$ and an injection $f'$ with domain of size exactly $k'$ such that $f'|_P=f|_P$ and $\min(k',b)=\min(k,b)$. When $k<b$ keep $k$; otherwise choose $k'\ge\max(b,|P\cap\operatorname{dom}f|)$ and use fresh sites to fill the available places. Keep $v,w$ unchanged. The original row still contains $x$.

Support a new relation just on the simultaneous $G$-orbit of $((f',k',v,w),x)$, with bottom at the returns. Its partial pin is the entire finite domain of $f'$, its cutoff is $k'+1$, and the finite multiset together with its word is isolated by $|v|,v|_{\operatorname{supp}v},w$. At the specified tag the matching domain exhausts the whole coefficient, so these data suffice for the orbit test. Any inhabited subrelation must contain the whole orbit by equivariance. This gives an atom at any arity.

In contrast, **no relation instantiated at a site's identity is then an atom**. Its whole row keeps the given tuple at every sufficiently large finite tag on a partial identity which agrees on its finite pin, at the same zero multiset and empty word. Two such distinct tags give disjoint, separately admissible nonempty parts. There is no true scalar atom at the site.

With a single return per site, every self-atom at $r$ must have all site rows empty since any nonempty one would instantiate the self-atom at a site. Its current row is then a singleton and it has no other coordinate. Its images are that root atom or its single return-only copy. A site self-atom can have no ordinary occurrence for the same reason. Upon return its nonzero whole coordinate must be a root self-atom, so it too is a return-only copy of a current singleton. These all remain atoms or become bottom. This proves the necessary all-type self-atom strength implication. The root-only scalar is a true strong atom. In the two-return case this same unique true root atom is a self-atom but after $h_0$ splits into the two independent copies on the returns. Strong Actuality consequently fails at the root in that version.

## A single site's missing choices and powers

For every $a\in A$ the scalar $p_a$ tests whether $f(a)$ is live and has bottom returns. It codes $a$ equivariantly. The usual two-support proof of failure of Transversal applies: use the property with current extension the $p_z$ from a single fresh class, force the selected representative to have two pins disjoint off the selector's pin, and take a partial identity on the first that erases the added points of the second. In this test set tag to infinity, multiset to zero and word to empty. Postcomposing by a class swap gives the contradiction. Independent return coordinates do not affect either comparison. The same classmate and class-selector relations refute Relational Choice and Transversal Choice. [Details of the two-support and class arguments](bounded-partial-injections.html)

To refute the powers, choose different single letters $d,k$ and for $i\in I$ choose pairwise distinct nonzero scalars $q_i$ supported on degree-zero words, with bottom on returns. Their ordinary contents are independent of the partial coefficient and tag. There are continuum many subsets of words. Define $x_i$ by grafting $q_i$ on the degree-zero word suffix $d$: at $wd$ use the $w$-bit of $q_i$. The sole positive-degree occurrence of $x_i$ is at multiset $\delta_i$ and word $k$, at every partial coefficient and tag. All other rows and all returns are bottom. Each $x_i$ needs only the multiset pin $\{i\}$. Translation by $d$ gives $q_i$.

For $D=\{x_i:i\in I\}$ take the exact-image relation $F$ whose ordinary row at $a$ is $a[D]$ and whose return coordinate on every return is the root haecceity of the bottom scalar. Its ordinary rows have empty pins: degree zero has only one multiset; at degree one $\delta_j$ only $x_j$ can survive and its residual marker, if present, depends on the word alone; at higher degrees all values are bottom. The return haecceity gives exactly the image at every reset, since all members of $D$ return to bottom. Consequently $F$ is admitted and rigid.

Choose $J\subset I$ such that both $J$ and its complement are uncountable. Put $U=\{q_i:i\in J\}$. Its exact-image relation $Z$ is admitted with empty pins and the same haecceity on each return, since every positive degree kills every member of $U$. It is rigid and below $dF$. Suppose $Y\le F$ is weakly rigid with current row $E\subseteq D$. At every ordinary address its row is exactly $a[E]$, where $a$ specifies just the multiset and word. Weak persistence gives one inclusion. For the other, any surplus $z$ lies in $a[D]$ and is invariant under the partial-coordinate group. A tester can delete precisely $z$ at this multiset and word, at **every** partial coefficient and tag, and be full throughout the reset sector. This is an admissible tester, and necessarily accepts every member of $E$, contradicting weak inextensibility if $z$ is present.

Thus if any ordinary transport of $Y$ at the empty multiset and word $d$ were $Z$, then $E=\{x_i:i\in J\}$. At the empty word and multiset $\delta_j$, the exact row would now contain the nonzero residual $k$-marker exactly for $j\in J$. Two fresh labels outside its countable multiset pin contradict the profile condition. Finally the higher tester which deletes $Z$ at empty multiset and word $d$, for every partial coefficient and tag, is admissible since $Z$ is $G$-invariant. It has full return coordinates. It necessarily accepts each current weakly rigid subrelation of $F$ and misses $Z$ as a member of the future power. Both canonical powers therefore fail weak inextensibility at a site.

The site's exact even finite tags at empty word and zero multiset form a countable family of scalar propositions. Their standard-numeral indexing is invariant, so its graph is represented as a current row. If a supremum existed, it would contain all the even tags and avoid every odd finite tag since the complement of each odd tag bounds the family. At the empty partial map all sufficiently large finite tags have the same observation for any given scalar. Thus this countable family has no join. The [Dedekind-finite clopen algebra](dedekind-finite-small-algebras-at-bounded-partial-injections.html) additionally witnesses failure of revised Mathematical Possibilism at this site.

## The source

The [split-return construction](functional-choice-over-supported-stages.html) gives Functional Choice at $r$: given selected outputs $y_a$, at site $i$ prescribe the desired current output rows on the distinct embedded inputs $h_i a$, from $h_i y_a$, leaving other inputs empty. These inputs and outputs are fixed by $G$, making the prescriptions admissible. The choices give the selected *whole* outputs because entrance rows and the current root row specify a root entity. This argument works with two returns too.

For every current tuple set $E$ the core's relation with site rows $h_i[E]$ is rigid by exact images. Any weakly rigid coextension equals it, by persistence and weak inextensibility. Every future weak subrelation of a root bound lifts by choosing the inverse image of its current row inside $E$ and using this rigid representative. The resulting transport agrees with the future relation by uniqueness of weakly rigid coextensions. These facts prove local Rigid Comprehension, Tame and both powers. The free root rows give Boolean Completeness and Well-Ordering. Its root-only scalar is lost on every entrance even though every actual truth remains possible by returning, so truths need not be ND-necessary.

The singleton individual yields the finite bound on the inexact ordinals. All external current graphs exist and finite unions of the scalar site atoms provide all finite Boolean intervals, proving revised Mathematical Possibilism at the root. The countably many site rows separately encode continuum-many root labels of every relational type; the returns keep their transported copies distinct. Sets of tuples of corresponding labels and their higher power families have exact-image rows, since they are invariant in every ordinary sector. They supply the necessary full real structures and their families of all restrictions for Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. At each point the denotations of the standard numerals give an admitted current test, as they are fixed by its symmetries; finite subsets of the labels distinguish numerals at each relational type. Thus Arithmetic Necessitism and necessary Infinity at $t$ hold too. [Ordinal, power-family and Frege-number verification](functional-choice-over-supported-stages.html)
