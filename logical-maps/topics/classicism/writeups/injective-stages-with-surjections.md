# Injective stages with a surjection coordinate

At every point this model satisfies Boolean Completeness and Atomlessness. With one proposition name it satisfies Logical Necessity. A weakly rigid individual property loses weak inextensibility after one entrance, and its actual row has no inextensible coextension.

Use the [injective integer stages](injective-integer-stages.html), including their actual individuals, permitted coordinate injections and free words. Take an auxiliary set $U$ of cardinality $\kappa^+$. Each arrow also bears an arbitrary surjection $s:U\to U$, composed as functions. Extend target symmetry by $\operatorname{Sym}(U)$. Add up to $\kappa$ auxiliary points to each support, and let the auxiliary maps act trivially on actual individuals. Rows are equivariant under the whole target group and equal for arrows agreeing on their supports at the same target and word. Precomposition transports relations and pins as before. Term abstraction is admitted because the finitely many parameter pins give equality of the entire transported parameters, and evaluation is equivariant at each target.

## Boolean joins

Fix a stage and a family $A$ of relations with current members $E$. Write its pin as $(N,P)$, where $N$ contains all source markers and $P\subseteq U$. For each stage, word and injection kind choose a representative $b$ coordinatewise bijective onto its permitted range, as in the injection model. Write $(b,s)$ for the arrow with auxiliary surjection $s$. Set
$$
 J_{b,s}=\bigcup\{F_{b,t}: F\in E,\ t|_P=s|_P\}.
$$
These rows are $P$-pinned in the auxiliary coordinate and equivariant there. If a target injection-coordinate symmetry fixes $bN$, it conjugates along $b$ to a source symmetry fixing $N$. Its action permutes $E$ and sends $J_{b,s}$ to itself. Thus for an injection coefficient $f$ in this kind set $J_{f,s}=gJ_{b,s}$ for any target injection-coordinate symmetry $g$ with $gb|_N=f|_N$. This is well defined and equivariant with combined pin $(N,P)$.

The representative rows contain $F_{b,s}$ for all $F\in E$. They are also below every upper bound $V$ at every $(b,s)$. To see this, take $F\in E$ and $t|_P=s|_P$. For the auxiliary pins $L$ of $F$ and $T$ of $V$ take a permutation $\pi$ fixing $P$ and moving $L\setminus P$ off $T$. It acts only on $U$ and keeps $\pi F$ in $E$. Extend the compatible prescriptions $r|_T=s|_T$ and $r\pi|_L=t|_L$ to a surjection of $U$, using the untouched $\kappa^+$ points. With the injection coefficient $b$ unchanged,
$$
 F_{b,t}=F_{b,r\pi}=(\pi F)_{b,r}\subseteq V_{b,r}=V_{b,s}.
$$
For general $(f,s)$ use a target injection-coordinate symmetry matching $b$ to $f$ on the union of $N$ with the injection pin of whichever member or upper bound is being compared. The inclusions then transport from $(b,s)$ to $(f,s)$. This gives the least upper bound at every relational type and every stage. The argument at a fixed representative is the one used for [symmetric surjection products](symmetric-surjection-products.html).

## Splitting and weak inextensibility

Given a nonempty row of any relation, take two distinct auxiliary source points outside its pin. The scalar condition that their two images coincide splits the relation into two parts. At the chosen row, extend the same restriction on the auxiliary pin to surjections identifying the two fresh inputs or separating them. The original row, including the chosen tuple, is the same on both extensions. Thus every nonzero relation splits at every stage. In particular propositional Atomlessness holds necessarily. There are neither atoms nor self-atoms of any relational type at any stage.

The old-and-new coordinate tests work unchanged. At $n$ the ordinary individuals of coordinate $i=n+1$ form a weakly rigid property. While testing weak inextensibility keep the auxiliary surjection fixed and place a fresh ordinary individual onto a prescribed target point by extending the injection on the tester's pin. At the entrance to $n+1$ and the return to $n$ choose the identity auxiliary coordinate. All original markers survive the composite. It agrees on any proposed coextension's pin with a source symmetry acting identically on $U$. The missing marker test at the entrance and its contradicting instance on the return therefore apply exactly as in the injection model. Every stage fails Tame Rigidity and Inextensible Comprehension.

Integer translation preserves the augmented pure system. Disjoint final-word prescriptions for one proposition name have a common pin on each coordinate, and give Logical Necessity as in the injection model. The argument for the failure of propositional Barcan compares the identity and a marker-losing endomorphism at the stage after the entrance, both with the identical auxiliary coordinate. A transported original scalar has the same value on their composites by matching just the injection coordinates while keeping that auxiliary coordinate fixed. The fresh ordinary transposition still refutes Relational Choice. Standard Frege numeral cuts remain admitted and the guarded arithmetic stays standard.
## Rigid Power with auxiliary surjections

Write $\lambda=\kappa^+$ and $U$ for the auxiliary set of size $\lambda$. For the injection coordinates use the representatives bijective onto their permitted ranges in [the injective integer stages](injective-integer-stages.html). In the auxiliary coordinate use surjections $s:U\to U$ for which every fibre has size $\lambda$. Every prescribed function on at most $\kappa$ elements extends to such a surjection. Two such surjections agreeing on a set $P$ of size at most $\kappa$ differ by precomposition with a source permutation fixing $P$, by matching each pair of fibres off $P$.

Let $E$ be a set of current tuples supported under symmetries by an individual pin $N$ and an auxiliary pin $P$. Enlarge $N$ by all source markers. Give a relation $W(E)$ the row $b[E]$ at every representative $b$ as above. At another arrow take the row of any representative of its injection kind agreeing on $N$ and $P$. For two such representatives, the injection components differ by a target symmetry fixing the individual images of $N$. Conjugation along their permitted ranges gives the corresponding source symmetry fixing $N$. The auxiliary components differ by a source permutation fixing $P$. Thus the prescribed direct images agree. The rows are equivariant and pinned by $(N,P)$. Weak persistence follows by choosing the representative also agreeing on the pin of any particular member of $E$; the resulting transported member belongs to the row. If a tester necessarily covers all members of $E$, it contains these rows at every representative. Matching its pin in addition proves inclusion at every arrow. At the identity one can choose a representative with identity injection coordinates and auxiliary map agreeing with the identity on $P$. Its action sends $E$ into $E$ whenever $E$ is the current extension of a weakly persistent relation pinned by $(N,P)$: interpolate on that pin and use persistence. Additional facts about the current extension needed below are verified explicitly.

For such a weakly persistent relation $F$ with current extension $E$, the identity row of $W(E)$ is exactly $E$. For the reverse inclusion, given $x\in E$, choose an auxiliary representative agreeing with the identity also on a pin for $x$. It then transports $x$ to itself. These representatives all give the same row at the identity. Thus whenever $F$ is weakly rigid the profile $W(E)$ has the same current extension and is itself weakly rigid by the preceding tester argument. Uniqueness of weakly rigid coextensions gives $F=W(E)$.

Consider a fully rigid $F=W(E)$ and a subset $E_0\subseteq E$ supported by enlarged pins $(N,P)$. We shall use the following sufficient additional condition. For every auxiliary surjection fixing $P$, with identity injection and word coordinates, its action carries $E_0$ into $E_0$. Under this condition the same identity-row argument gives $W(E_0)_1=E_0$ and proves $W(E_0)$ weakly rigid. Its row at every representative is contained in that of $F$.

In fact $W(E_0)$ is fully rigid. For successive representatives $b,c$, their composite still has an auxiliary surjection with all fibres of size $\lambda$. In the representative $d$ of the composite injection kind use exactly this composite auxiliary coordinate and make $d$ agree with $cb$ on $N$. On the target individual coordinates take the virtual injection $t$ with $td=cb$ from the injective integer-stage argument, and let $t$ be the identity on the auxiliary coordinate. On tuples it acts by interpolating the injection part with a target symmetry on the tuple’s pin. Full rigidity gives
$$
 t(d[E])=(cb)[E]=F_{cb}=d[E].
$$
Here the two representative direct-image formulas apply successively at $b$ and then at $c$. The row $S=d[E_0]$ is invariant under the individual target symmetries fixing $dN$ and acting trivially on auxiliary labels, by conjugating on the permitted ranges. The map $t$ preserves and reflects membership in $S$ by interpolation on $dN$ and a tuple pin. Thus $tS=S$. It follows that the $c$-row of $bW(E_0)$ is $c[b[E_0]]$. At $m$, the target of $b$, its current extension $b[E_0]$ is preserved into itself by auxiliary surjections fixing a target pin: for such an endomorphism $k$ fixing $bP$, the composite $kb$ remains an auxiliary representative agreeing with $b$ on the full pin, so $k(b[E_0])=(kb)[E_0]=b[E_0]$. Consequently the representative and tester characterization makes $bW(E_0)$ weakly rigid there. Every arrow agrees with some representative on the pin of the whole profile, hence the result holds after every arrow.

To construct the required $E_0$, take at a representative $b$ any rigid subrelation $Y$ of $bF$ with current extension $T\subseteq b[E]$. Let $s$ denote the auxiliary coordinate of $b$ and $Q$ an auxiliary pin for $Y$. Choose a section $j:U\to U$ of $s$ such that $j(s[P])\subseteq P$. Take the source auxiliary pin $L=P\cup j[Q]$. Enlarge the source individual pin $N$ by the inverse image of an individual pin of $Y$ under the injection coordinates of $b$.

For each $y\in T$ choose $x\in E$ with $bx=y$. Make an auxiliary surjection $u$ fixing $P$ and sending each $a$ of a pin of $x$ outside $P$ to $j(s(a))$. Its partial prescription leaves $\lambda$ unused inputs to assure surjectivity. Use the identity on the other coordinates. Since $F$ is persistent and $u$ fixes its pin, $x'=ux$ belongs to $E$. Also $bx'=bx=y$, and the auxiliary pin of $x'$ lies in $P\cup j[U]$.

Let $E_0$ contain all images of these $x'$ under source endomorphisms whose individual coordinates are symmetries fixing $N$, whose auxiliary coordinates are arbitrary surjections fixing $L$, and whose word is empty. These transformations form a monoid and preserve $E$ by persistence of $F$ and pinning. Thus $E_0\subseteq E$ is symmetry-supported by $(N,L)$ and is preserved by auxiliary surjections fixing $L$, exactly the extra condition above.

For any of these endomorphisms $v$, $bvx'$ still belongs to $T$. Conjugate its individual symmetry along $b$ and fix omitted markers to give a target symmetry fixing the individual pin of $Y$. On the auxiliary pin of $x'$ factor $sv$ as $ks$, with $k$ a target surjection fixing $Q$. Indeed distinct points of its normalized pin with the same $s$-image must both lie in $P$, where $v$ is the identity. At an $s$-value in $Q$ its normalized preimages lie in $P$ or equal $j(q)\in L$, where $v$ again is the identity. This makes the prescription for $k$ well defined, and its domain of size at most $\kappa$ leaves room for surjectivity. Combining the target maps gives an endomorphism fixing a pin of $Y$ and sending $y$ to $bvx'$. Persistence of $Y$ therefore puts $bvx'$ in its current extension. This proves $b[E_0]=T$. The rigid subrelation $W(E_0)$ now transports to $Y$ by uniqueness at its current row.

Thus, for any fully rigid bound $F$, each representative row of its canonical power consists exactly of direct images of the current rigid subrelations. That power is persistent. The representative and tester characterization gives weak rigidity, and applies again to every transported rigid bound at every stage. This proves necessary Rigid Power.
