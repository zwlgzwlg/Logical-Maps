# A stitched CH-changing collapse with Analytic Necessitism

Barton and Goodsell's two-world stitching construction with the bare countably closed collapse has a rigid real number structure at every type where a real number structure is possible. Nevertheless the two worlds disagree on the continuum hypothesis. The map's 25 October record already identified that the bare collapse preserves the reals. The all-type argument below uses a family of ground codes whose transport remains injective. [Earlier CH-reversing instance](../index.html#model/stitched-ch-reversing) · [Analytic Necessitism](../index.html#principle/analytic-necessitism)

## The forcing and its two worlds

Work over a ground universe $V$ of ZFC with $\neg\mathrm{CH}$ and put $\kappa=(2^{\aleph_0})^V$. Conditions are countable partial functions from $\omega_1$ to $\kappa$, ordered by extension. A descending countable sequence has its union as a lower bound. Hence the forcing adds no countable sequences of ground objects and preserves $\omega_1$. The generic union maps $\omega_1$ onto $\kappa$, so CH holds in $V[G]$. The forcing is weakly homogeneous. Given two conditions, ground permutations of the range at their common coordinates can make the first agree with the second there. Consequently any subset of a ground set definable in $V[G]$ from ground parameters belongs to $V$. For each ground candidate for membership, weak homogeneity decides that statement at the top condition.

Use the stitching construction of Barton and Goodsell, §8, with the trivial root group. There is one proper arrow $h$ from the ground root to the full one-world extensional hierarchy on $\mathbb N$ in $V[G]$. It is the identity on individuals. At a relational type a root relation is an arbitrary current extension $E$ in $V$ with a ground-parameter-definable terminal relation $D$. The encoding in $V$ is the ground naming construction of their Definition 1. The two extensions determine the curried function coherently on all arguments. The image set $h[E]$ is ground-parameter-definable via the ground set of names for the members of $E$, as in their Lemma 16. Thus the exact-image pair $(E,h[E])$ is admitted. Persistence and weak inextensibility are equivalent to the two inclusions giving $D=h[E]$. For the second inclusion take the exact-image pair as tester. A terminal relation is already rigid. These facts give necessary Rigid Comprehension and Tame Rigidity. This is a relative consistency construction in the usual forcing sense. ## The types admitting real number structures

The individuals form a countable set at both objects. A real number structure there would have the standard natural numbers as its number part by full local induction and distinct real codes for all their subsets by local fullness, exceeding the carrier. A type formed solely from $t$ is finite at both objects. This follows by induction from the four root propositions and two terminal propositions, since functions on finite ground domains and their compatible terminal coordinates have only finitely many possibilities. The antecedent of Analytic Necessitism fails at each of these types.

At every other relational type $\tau$, the individual type occurs somewhere in its argument tree. Construct a ground-coded family of root labels with pairwise distinct terminal images. The individuals already give a uniformly coded sequence of such pairs, $n\mapsto(n,n)$. At an argument position whose type contains $e$, use a sequence of such input labels and fix a ground-coded label in each other position. This gives distinct tuples $a_n$ with distinct terminal tuples $h(a_n)$. For every $u\subseteq\mathbb N$ in $V$, take the root relation with current extension $\{a_n:n\in u\}$ and terminal extension $\{h(a_n):n\in u\}$. Its ground code is uniform in $u$, and distinct $u$ give distinct images. Taking the singleton-indexed subfamily $u=\{k\}$ supplies the sequence required for the next type. Induction therefore gives continuum many labels at each such $\tau$, injective under $h$.

Choose disjoint subfamilies to label the numerals and the subsets of $\mathbb N$; for example reserve separate first-bit tags in the real indices. On these labels place the standard number set, successor graph, real set and membership relation. For each of the four root relations use the exact terminal image. They are rigid, and the members of the two carriers remain pairwise distinct because the transport of the joint label family is injective. All subsets of the number labels in $V$ are represented at the root. At the terminal its number set still has the same enumeration by $\omega$ and $V[G]$ has no new subsets of $\omega$. Thus every terminal subset of the numbers is represented by exactly one transported real, and all the other clauses of the real number structure hold there as well. The four actual witnesses give a rigid structure that is necessarily a real number structure. This proves every instance of first-order Analytic Necessitism.

At type $et$ this structure exists at both worlds. Current relations there range over the ground sets of tuples, and terminal relations over all sets of the extension. Structural CH consequently has the two respective truth values of CH in $V$ and $V[G]$. This refutes Non-contingency of CH and, by its recorded consequence, Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. ## Where the power fails

The root properties of individuals transport onto every terminal property of individuals because both universes have the same reals. For any rigid individual property with current extension $E$, each subset of its terminal extension $h[E]=E$ lies in $V$ and lifts by its exact-image pair. The power thus has exact image and is rigid. Weak and full rigidity coincide for this two-world model. Both power principles hold at this specific argument type.

Now take $\sigma=et$ and the universal relation with one argument of type $\sigma$. It is rigid because transport at $\sigma$ is onto. A terminal entity of type $\sigma t$ is a set of reals. The collapse adds a new subset of the ground reals. Indeed the generic surjection can be coded as a subset of $\omega_1$, using a fixed ground coding of its values by reals and pairing $\omega_1\times\omega$ with $\omega_1$; an injection of $\omega_1$ into the ground reals gives the claim. Every terminal value of a root entity at $\sigma t$ is a ground-definable subset of the ground reals, hence belongs to $V$ by weak homogeneity.

Take a root tester full on the current row and admitting just the members of $\mathcal P^V(\mathbb R^V)$ on the terminal row. The terminal predicate is definable from that single ground set. Every current root $\sigma t$-entity necessarily satisfies the tester. Yet at the terminal every subset of the reals is a rigid subproperty of the indicated bound, including new subsets outside the tester. Thus Rigid Power, Weakly Rigid Power, Rigid Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity fail locally. The same test refutes relational Barcan. Its terminal complement is inhabited and has no ground-definable member. Use that complement with full current row to refute Intensional Choice: a necessarily singleton selection would give a ground-definable singleton in the terminal complement. A root witness to Modal Well-Ordering at $\sigma t$ would also define its least member there. Each world separately has ordinary well-orders by choice in its own universe.

Pair the class of new subsets with an empty root row. This is a nonzero predicate. Any nonempty ground-definable subclass contains at least two different subsets, since a definable singleton has a definable member. Choose a ground real at which their membership differs, and split the subclass by that ground parameter. This shows directly that Atomicity fails at this higher type. For Boolean Completeness, the current join of any family $E$ of relational entities has current row the union of the rows of its members and terminal value the union of the relations in $h[E]$. These are a ground row and a ground-definable terminal relation. They give the least upper bound under the coordinatewise order. The terminal itself is full, so Boolean Completeness at all relational types holds necessarily.

At $t$ the root has the four possible pairs of truth bits, every truth bit at the terminal has a preimage, and the singleton root truth guards the distinctness-preserving necessity of each current truth. Both points have a true strong atom. For a self-atom at any relational type, a nonempty current row entails that the relation itself is an atom, so it consists of one current tuple and empty terminal value. If only the terminal is inhabited, its value there must be a singleton by self-atomicity. Its transports are then atoms or bottom. Thus at both objects every self-atom is strong. A higher-type root atom formed from the orbits of generics splits on transport, so the converse implications from atoms fail.
