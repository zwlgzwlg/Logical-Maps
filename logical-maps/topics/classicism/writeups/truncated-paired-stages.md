# Atomic paired stages with 5-necessity strictly above two-hat necessity

Atomicity at all relational types can hold at every world while a 5-necessary proposition fails to be two-hat necessary at the evaluation world. Every truth at every stage is 5-necessary. The model also satisfies Mathematical Possibilism and both necessary rigid powers. It uses the [paired-stage modal tests](fixed-or-collapsed-pairs.html) and the [truncation method for atomicity](truncation-words.html).

## Maps, cutoffs and pins

At integer stage $n$, coordinate $i$ has ordinary pairs $B_\xi=\{(\xi,0),(\xi,1)\}$ indexed by $\xi<\omega_1$. It also has one distinguished singleton $a$ when $i\le n$. Coordinates are disjoint. These are auxiliary sites; the object-language individual domain is a singleton at every stage.

For each $\alpha<\omega_1$ give each coordinate a partition at resolution $\alpha$. Every site of a pair $B_\xi$ for $\xi<\alpha$ forms its own cell. The remaining ordinary sites and the distinguished singleton, if present, together form one tail cell. An ordinary coefficient $g:n\to m$ preserves coordinate labels, sends each ordinary pair either identically to itself or constantly to one point, and sends each distinguished singleton to any site in the same coordinate subject to the following condition: at each resolution $\alpha$, inputs in a common cell have outputs in a common cell. Call such maps nonexpansive. Both this condition and the fixed-or-collapsed requirement are preserved by composition.

An arrow $(g,\gamma,w)$ has a cutoff $\gamma\le\omega_1$ and a word in a countable free alphabet. Cutoff $\omega_1$ places no extra restriction. A bounded cutoff $\gamma<\omega_1$ requires $g$ to be constant on every source cell at resolution $\gamma$, separately in each coordinate. Composition takes the composite coefficient, the minimum of the cutoffs, and the concatenation of words with the later word on the left. Indeed if the first map is constant on the cells at the smaller cutoff then so is the composite. If the second map has the smaller cutoff, nonexpansiveness of the first sends each of those cells into one cell of that resolution; the second is constant on it. The identity has cutoff $\omega_1$ and empty word.

A relational entity at $n$ has one tuple row at each arrow out of $n$. Some countable set $S$ of source sites and some $\beta<\omega_1$ support the profile in the following sense: the row is the same for arrows with the same target and word, the same $\min(\gamma,\beta)$, and the same values of $g$ on $S$. There is no restriction on admissible rows beyond this. Transport by precomposition is supported on $g[S]$ and the same $\beta$. Arrows agreeing in the displayed data transport each whole supported parameter equally: composing either one with the same continuation preserves agreement on $S$, on the cutoff truncated at $\beta$, and on target and word. A common pin and bound for finitely many parameters therefore support their lambda abstract, by evaluating the matrix on all the tuples at the target. This proves the usual term-closure and quantification clauses of the action semantics of C. Any given current row is available, for example by assigning it on every arrow with the current target and the empty row elsewhere.

For each stage $n$ and $\alpha$ define an idempotent endomorphism $t_\alpha$ with cutoff $\alpha$ and empty word. Its coefficient fixes the sites below $\alpha$ in each coordinate. In the tail send everything to the distinguished singleton if present, and otherwise to the first member of $B_\alpha$. This is constant on every $\alpha$-cell. For resolutions at most $\alpha$, sites in the common tail either remain there or move within it. For larger resolutions any common tail lies inside the old $\alpha$-tail and is collapsed. This proves nonexpansiveness. Its image is countable across all coordinates. Given any countable pin $S$, taking $\alpha$ greater than the levels of all its ordinary sites makes $t_\alpha$ fix $S$, including its distinguished singletons.

## Necessary Atomicity and countable completeness

Every bounded-cutoff arrow $h=(g,\gamma,w)$ is isolated. A tester can specify the word, target, and the exact cutoff using the bound $\gamma+1$. It can specify the value of $g$ on one representative of every $\gamma$-cell in every coordinate: there are only countably many. All maps of cutoff $\gamma$ are constant on these cells. Thus any single tuple on just this arrow, with empty other rows, is an admitted atom.

If a nonempty relation is instantiated on an unbounded-cutoff arrow $h$, choose $t_\alpha$ fixing its pin with $\alpha$ also at least its support bound. The bounded-cutoff arrow $ht_\alpha$ has the same target, word, pin values and truncated cutoff, so the same tuple belongs to its row. Hence every nonzero relation contains an atom. This works at each relational type and stage. Current fullness gives the standard numeral denotations as the current numbers of every counting type; an internally countable family is externally countable. The union of countably many supported profiles is supported by the union of their countable site pins and the supremum of their countably many bounds, still below $\omega_1$. This gives Countable Boolean Completeness necessarily.

## The two modal tests

Use the hat criterion from [the untruncated model](fixed-or-collapsed-pairs.html). At $h$, $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ means that for every true target $r$ there are successive arrows $v,x$ with $q$ true at $vh$ and $r$ true at $xv$.

At stage $n$ let $P$ say the target $m$ satisfies $m\le n$, i.e. coordinate $n+1$ still lacks a distinguished singleton. The ordinary inclusion $u:n\to n+1$ has empty word and unbounded cutoff. Given any true $q$ at $n$ and true $r$ at $n+1$, choose $\xi$ beyond every ordinary site in their two pins. At coordinate $n+1$ let $v:n+1\to n$ fix all pairs below $\xi$ and send its entire tail, including the new singleton, to one member of $B_\xi$. Let $x:n\to n+1$ fix all pairs below $\xi$ and send the entire tail to the singleton. These tail replacements are nonexpansive by the calculation for $t_\alpha$. Give both arrows unbounded cutoff and empty word; use identities on every other coordinate. Then $vu$ fixes the pin of $q$ and $xv$ fixes the pin of $r$, including the new singleton. Both have the identity's word, cutoff and target. Thus $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ holds at $u$ although $P$ is false there. No true guard witnesses two-hat necessity of $P$.

For 5-necessity of $P$, use the target-specific property $Y$ with full rows when $m\le n$, and otherwise containing exactly those propositions admitting a pin omitting the distinguished singleton of coordinate $n+1$. A bound on the cutoff is permitted in such a pin. At $m>n$ the test of whether an empty-word endomorphism fixes that singleton has no pin omitting it. Given any proposed pin missing the singleton and any proposed cutoff bound, choose a tail beyond its ordinary sites and collapse that tail, including the singleton, to a member of its first pair while retaining unbounded cutoff. This fails the test and agrees with the identity on the entire proposed pin. Hence $\forall z\,Yz=P$ as entities. Given a current $z$, enlarge its site pin by the partners in coordinate $n+1$. Use the true guard of empty-word endomorphisms fixing the enlarged pin. Suppose $\Diamond_{\ne}q$; some return meets this guard. Therefore the outgoing coefficient is injective on the enlarged pin and cannot send one of its pinned ordinary pairs to a singleton. The image of the original site pin still supports the transported $z$ with its original cutoff bound. Thus $Yz$ is two-hat necessary for each current $z$. It follows that each $Yz$ is 5-necessary and the C Barcan theorem for $\Box_5$ gives $\Box_5P$.

Let $T$ say that $m\ge n$, so the coordinate-$n$ singleton $a$ is present. Use the guard consisting of empty-word endomorphisms fixing $a$. At a false target, $h(a)=b$ has a mate $b'$. The true target test fixing $b,b'$ defeats every return to the guard because such a return must collapse the pair onto $a$. Hence $\hat{\hat\Box}T$. For any true $q$ at $n$, the exit $n\to n-1$ sending the tail from a fresh pair level to one member of that pair, and the return sending that tail to $a$, have composite fixing the pin of $q$. Use unbounded cutoffs and empty words. Thus $\Diamond q$ at the exit and $T$ is false, refuting $\Box_{\ne}T$.

The conjunction $D=P\land T$, saying that the target stage is $n$, is now 5-necessary. For any true $p$ with support $(S,\beta)$ use the guard of empty-word endomorphisms fixing $S$ and all its ordinary partners, with cutoff at least $\beta$. If $\Diamond_{\ne}q$ at an endomorphism $h$ then a return meets the guard, so $h$ has empty word and cutoff at least $\beta$. Each pinned pair must remain injective, and therefore be fixed pointwise. Moving a pinned singleton to an ordinary point would again defeat ND-possibility by testing that point and its mate. Thus $h$ agrees with the identity on every site of $S$ and on the truncated cutoff, giving $p$ at $h$. This proves two-hat necessity of $D\to p$, hence 5-necessity of $D\to p$ and of $p$. All these arguments hold at every stage.

## Rigid powers

If $F$ is weakly rigid with current row $E$, then $F_h=h[E]$ for every bounded-cutoff arrow $h$. Persistence gives the forward image inclusion. For the reverse, use a tester full except at the isolated $h$, where its row is precisely $h[E]$. Each current member necessarily passes this tester, so weak inextensibility gives the required inclusion.

Choose $t=t_\alpha$ fixing a site pin of $F$, with $\alpha$ at least its bound. Then $F_t=E=t[E]$. Since $t^2=t$, it fixes every member of $E$. At any arrow $h$, the support condition gives $F_h=F_{ht}$ and the latter arrow has bounded cutoff. Thus $F_h=ht[E]=h[E]$ at every arrow. Exact image rows make $F$ hereditarily rigid: the current row after $h$ is $h[E]$ and the next row after $k$ is exactly $k[h[E]]$. This gives necessary Tame Rigidity.

For every $E'\subseteq E$ the exact-image table $h\mapsto h[E']$ is admitted. Indeed $h[E']=ht[E']$, and $ht$ depends only on the target and word, the coefficient on the countable image of $t$, and the cutoff truncated at $\alpha$. It is rigid by the same exact-image calculation. At $h$, a weakly rigid or rigid $G\le hF$ is determined by its current row $D'\subseteq h[E]$. Take $E'=E\cap h^{-1}[D']$. The exact-image profile of $E'$ entails $F$, and after $h$ is coextensive with $G$, hence identical to it since both are weakly rigid. Thus every possible member of either power of $F$ is already the image of a current power member, and all the current power members transport to such members by rigidity. Both power properties have exact-image rows and are rigid. This proves both necessary rigid powers.

## Mathematical Possibilism, analysis and the signature

The individual domain is a singleton and all current rows are available. At the ordinal type consider the finite set of entities represented by a single extensional table on the current individual order and field at all arrows. Zero, successor and the inexact ordinal limit of a rigid family with actual members in this set are again of this form. For the limit use that such a rigid family, having only finitely many current members, is exactly the finite union of their haecceities by persistence and weak inextensibility. These finite tables form an available induction predicate. Thus the inexact ordinals are finite in number. Any infinite carrier has a countably infinite power-closed subcollection exceeding this benchmark, since it can code all powersets of its finite subsets by full current graphs. Each revised-small CBA is finite and is realized below the union of the requisite finite number of isolated propositional atoms. This proves Mathematical Possibilism.

For each subset of the integer stage labels and each relational type take the target-specific profile full exactly on arrows whose target belongs to that subset. All stages are reachable; transport preserves the labels and carries the codes bijectively. As in [the paired-stage analytic construction](symmetric-integer-paired-stages.html), arbitrary sets of tuples of these codes have target-specific relations with exact-image rows, including the entire families of such relations at each finite arity. Take continuum many codes for the standard numbers and reals and their represented powers. The resulting structure and powers are rigid and represent every current relation's restriction everywhere. At the individual type there is no real structure. This proves Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. Independently, full current subsets and graphs give the usual standard second-order analytical theory wherever a real structure is present. Target-specific well-order rows give Modal Well-Ordering.

To refute ordinary Barcan at $t$, choose a cutoff-zero arrow $h$ of empty word and use a tester full except at that isolated arrow, where it contains exactly the image of the source proposition domain. Every current proposition necessarily passes. At the target the proposition distinguishing cutoff zero from positive cutoffs is outside the image: compare two continuations with the same constant-per-coordinate map, target and word, but cutoffs zero and one. Their composites with $h$ are the same while the target proposition separates them.

Stage and coordinate translation by one preserves the partitions, cutoffs and pins. With a single propositional name given the value top this proves No Signature Contingency. Alternatively give the name the [last-letter interpretation](integer-stages-paired-individuals-glued-constants.html). For each realizable pure one-variable matrix choose one witness. A distinct letter for each witness lets the name reproduce its entire profile after the corresponding loop with identity coefficient and unbounded cutoff. A countable union of witness site pins and the supremum of their countably many cutoff bounds support the name. Pure homogeneity at all stages gives the converse witnessed direction. This proves Logical Necessity in the second interpretation; use the rooted unfolding for coherent named values.

Full Boolean Completeness at $t$ fails. Take the current property containing the atoms supported singly at $t_\alpha$ just for nonzero limit ordinals $\alpha<\omega_1$. These addresses are all distinct by their cutoff. A join must include each selected address, and must exclude each unselected $t_\gamma$ because the complement of its singleton atom is an upper bound. Yet every root profile is eventually constant along $t_\alpha$: all sufficiently high $t_\alpha$ fix its site pin and exceed its cutoff bound, so agree with the identity on its support. Both the limit ordinals and their complementary successors are cofinal. Thus the join cannot exist.

To see directly that Atoms are self-atoms fails, take an isolated cutoff-zero endomorphism $h$ whose coefficient sends the whole of each coordinate to a single site, using its distinguished singleton whenever present. Its singleton address is an atom. After $h$ the atom holds at the identity; it also holds at a distinct $t_\alpha$ with unboundedly many choices of $\alpha$ fixing every chosen image site, since $t_\alpha h=h$. Those two continuation addresses are distinguishable by their cutoffs, so this transported true proposition is not an atom.

At every stage all current subsets are available and the standard numeral denotations form an inductive current test at every counting type. Conversely every inductive test contains all standard numeral denotations. At each relational counting type the stage-label codes give infinitely many distinct objects, so all finite sizes occur and the standard numerals are distinct. Transport commutes with those closed terms and hence maps the current number extension bijectively to the target number extension. These are exact images after every arrow; the natural number property is rigid and the numbers necessarily distinct. At the singleton individual type the Peano guard is false. This gives Arithmetic Necessitism and the usual sentence-level Necessity of Arithmetic. In particular the Axiom of Infinity at type $t$ holds everywhere.
