# One nonstandard terminal separates arithmetic from both rigid powers and Barcan

There is a two-world model with necessary Barcan at every type, necessary Rigid Power, necessary Weakly Rigid Power and disagreement about arithmetic. In the first version the disagreement is at individual type. In the second it is at propositional type and revised Mathematical Possibilism also holds. The single entrance matters. In Dorr’s two-component model, lifting a chosen subrelation at one component can require an unavailable set at the other. Here there is just one component at which to prescribe a whole value. The arithmetic comparison is between that component and the entrance world itself. [Dorr’s two-component construction](../index.html#model/coalesced-two-extensional-arithmetics-root)

## Construction

Work in ZFC. Let \(T\) be Zermelo set theory with Choice together with one specified sign of CH. Either sign gives a consistent recursive theory. For the positive sign use the rank segment \(V_{\omega+\omega}\) of the constructible universe; for the negative sign use the corresponding segment of a forcing extension violating CH, or equivalently the relative consistency proof by forcing. Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem and completeness give a countable \(M\models T+\neg\mathrm{Con}(T)\). The argument uses the internal interpretations of individual sets and of each standard finite type in \(M\). It does not require \(M\) to be externally well-founded. [Arithmetic and CH components on the map](stone-terminals-infinite-individuals.html)

In the classical version let the old component \(s\) have only its identity arrow and use \(M\)'s full type hierarchy on \(\omega^M\). In the group version use one individual and \(G=(\mathbb Z^M,+)\) at \(s\). For a relational type \(\tau=\bar\sigma t\), the old domain is the internal powerset of \(G\times D^s_{\bar\sigma}\); take the trivial group in this formula in the classical version. The current row is at the identity. Translation shifts the addresses and is invertible at every sort. For a fixed formula the external evaluation agrees with its interpretation in \(M\), by induction on the formula. Distinct internal sets differ at an internal element. In particular, every internally available tuple row can be given at any one address.

Add a world \(r\) with no proper endomorphisms, an entrance \(h:r\to s\), and a distinct arrow \(gh\) for each \(g\in G\). The individual carriers agree, with identity projection. At each relation sort take
\[
 D^r_\tau=\mathcal P(D^r_{\bar\sigma})\times D^s_\tau.
\]
The first coordinate is the current row. The arrow \(h\) reads the second coordinate and \(gh\) translates it by \(g\). Write \(\pi\) for projection on tuples at \(h\). For abstraction take the actual satisfaction row at \(r\) and the abstract computed at \(s\) on the transported parameters. This also verifies term transport along every composite. A difference in the first coordinate is visible at the root; a difference in the second is visible at an old address. Thus the required intensional identity holds. [Typed action construction with whole component coordinates](free-words-over-infinite-arithmetic-shifts.html)

## Lifting a rigid subrelation

Every transport is onto, giving necessary Barcan including at individual type. Write a root relation as \(F=(E,U)\). It is weakly rigid exactly when its extension at every \(gh\) is \(g\pi[E]\). Weak persistence gives the inclusion from right to left. For the reverse, a tester can be full at the root and at every old address except for a single omitted tuple at \(g\). This is one internally available old value. An extra tuple there would violate weak inextensibility. Conversely, a tester which necessarily accepts each member of \(E\) accepts each of these images. The stated equalities give both clauses of weak rigidity. The same criterion at \(s\) uses its internal single-address, single-tuple omissions. By the group law, exact images at all addresses still have exact images after every translation. Thus weak rigidity and rigidity coincide throughout.

Now let \(Z\le ghF\) be a rigid subrelation at \(s\), for weakly rigid \(F\) at \(r\). Translate \(Z\) back by \(g^{-1}\), obtaining \(Z_0\) with current extension \(B\subseteq\pi[E]\). Put
\[
 E'=\{\bar x\in E:\pi(\bar x)\in B\}.
\]
Then \(\pi[E']=B\) and \((E',Z_0)\le F\) is rigid by exact images. Its image at \(gh\) is the **whole** \(Z\). The identity arrow needs no lift, and at \(s\) inverse translation lifts every eligible subrelation. Therefore each power predicate has exactly the direct images of its eligible current members at every arrow. This proves both necessary power principles, including for the empty tuple. A current relation at \(r\) also has an inextensible coextension obtained by setting its old value to bottom. At \(s\) the direct images of a given internal current row form an internally represented rigid coextension.

## Arithmetic, analysis and CH

Use \(\sigma=e\) in the classical version and \(\sigma=t\) in the group version. In each case \(D^s_\sigma\) is infinite inside \(M\). For each internal natural \(n\) let \(C_n\) have at every old address the row of properties whose current extension has internal size \(n\). It is fixed by translations. Counting the empty extension and deleting a member give \(\mathbf0=C_0\) and \(\operatorname{Suc}C_n=C_{n+1}\) as whole entities. The internal range is an admitted inductive property. Conversely, internal induction in \(M\) puts every \(C_n\) in every admitted inductive property. The \(C_n\) are distinct because each internal finite size is realized. The old guarded arithmetic, including its sum and product, is therefore the arithmetic of \(M\).

At the root \(D^r_\sigma\) is externally infinite and every subset is an admitted current row. The row selecting exactly the standard iterates of the Frege successor is available and inductive. Every such standard iterate belongs to every inductive current row. Finite current subsets of pairwise distinct sizes distinguish the iterates. Thus the root arithmetic is standard; the native definitions of sum and product compute their usual graphs by induction. Both points satisfy the Peano guard. They disagree on \(\mathrm{Con}(T)\), and both satisfy the Axiom of Infinity at \(\sigma\). The same guarded sentence witnesses failure of pure B at the root, since its root sign never holds at the component. In the classical version the scalar algebras have four and two elements, excluding Possible Infinity at \(t\); in the group version two individuals are never available.

For a common type carrying a real structure, take \(et\) in the classical model and \(tt\) in the group model. At the root these sorts contain copies of the powerset of a countably infinite set, and all current predicates and graphs are represented. In \(M\) the corresponding old sort contains disjoint internal copies of its naturals and their powerset, with every internally available row and graph represented. At each point any current real structure has precisely the naturals of its own universe by full current induction there, and each subset of the naturals has exactly one real representative. Hence its arithmetic and its structural CH have the signs of that universe. The arithmetic disagreement therefore refutes Necessity of Analysis too. Choosing for \(T\) the ambient sign of CH gives Non-contingency of CH at every type; choosing the opposite sign refutes it at the common type. ## Atoms, choice and the missing countable join

The root algebra at each relation sort is the product of its full current powerset and the old internal powerset. Its atoms are exactly the single tuples at single addresses. Each projects to bottom or translates to another such atom. Every nonzero entity contains one. A self-atom instantiated at the root is already an atom by definition. An uninstantiated nonzero self-atom has empty current row and is instantiated at some old address. Its old coordinate there is an atom, hence was itself an atom by inverse translation. This gives the boxed self-atom and atom strength principles, as well as necessary Strong Atomicity. At either object its identity-only scalar is a true strong atom. At the root that same scalar, impossible at the component, guards the ND-necessity of each present truth. In the classical component every truth is necessary. In the group component ordinary necessity is an admitted S5 operator (the constant answer to the test of truth throughout \(G\)). The proposition true just at the identity fails that test, so necessary 5-necessity of all truths fails at the root. For Modal Well-Ordering take an external actual well-order at \(r\) and an internal well-order of the old carrier at every address.

At the component let \(b_n\) be the \(n\)-th old individual (classical version), or the \(n\)-th nonnegative group address (group version). At the root form the countable family of old-only singleton relations on \(b_n\) for **standard** \(n\). In the group case these are scalars. All root current rows are available, including a graph injecting this family into the standard Frege numbers at \(\sigma\). A join would have to include each listed entry. Every other entry can be deleted from the top element by an admitted singleton omission. Thus its old coordinate would select exactly the standard cut, which is unavailable in \(M\) by induction. The group version already fails propositional Boolean Completeness. For failure of Weak Rigid Comprehension, choose a root row whose projection at \(h\) is the external standard cut in a copy of \(\omega^M\): in the group version take the old scalars which are singleton addresses \(b_n\), and in the classical version take the individuals \(b_n\). No internal old current row has that extension.

Use a single unary individual name in the classical version, empty at \(r\) and a singleton at \(s\). Its old extension is not invariant under all individual permutations, whereas the old value of each closed pure term is. In the group version use one propositional name, false at \(r\) with old support just the identity. Translation fixes every closed pure entity and moves this support. Thus both signatures satisfy Independence. In either model the name is never bottom, while the pure matrix \(X=\bot\) always has a witness. Possibly Witnessed Possibility fails.

## Revised Mathematical Possibilism

In the group version the single individual at all stages gives a finite bound on the inexact ordinal benchmark. Take the finite set of ordinal candidates given by one uniform Boolean table in the two current extension bits of an order and its field. Each table is read in the same way at every address. Zero and successor have this form. A rigid family drawn currently from this finite set is the union of its members’ tuple-haecceities by weak inextensibility and persistence, also when empty. Its limit tests well-orderhood and a fixed finite list of tables, giving another uniform table. The selecting current row is an admitted inductive property. Any externally infinite current carrier contains a countable subset strictly larger than this finite bound. Every smaller subset of that countable subset is finite, and all its subcollections can be coded on finitely many elements by an admitted current graph. The countable subset is therefore power-closed. Every revised-small algebra at the root must be finite. A Boolean algebra on \(k\) atoms is represented below the union of \(k\) distinct old singleton-address scalars, with its order isomorphism in a current graph. [Singleton-individual ordinal and smallness calculation](free-words-over-infinite-arithmetic-shifts.html)

In the classical version \(H\) has at least eight members: the first eight successive ordinal thresholds are distinguished by actual well-ordered finite sets of the corresponding sizes. Thus the eight-element Boolean algebra is small and cannot be represented in the four-element proposition algebra.

## A restriction on arithmetic disagreement under Functional Choice

Fix an input type $\sigma$, write $N$ for its Frege numbers and $I^*_\sigma$ for its Peano guard. Let $h:r\to w$ be an arrow between Peano points in an action model. Suppose $a$ is an actual propositional atom at $r$ and $ha$ is possible at $w$. Let $\mathcal A_r$ consist of the subsets of $N_r$ that occur as the actual trace of an admitted predicate of numbers. These hypotheses concern one arrow. Under Actuality and local propositional ND the same $a$ works at every accessible $w$, because a nonbottom source proposition remains nonbottom. Plenitude and Functional Choice both imply these two local conditions. Gallin Extensional Comprehension does also.

Every trace $A\in\mathcal A_r$ has an admitted target predicate that reads it correctly on the image of $N_r$. Choose a source predicate $X$ presenting $A$ and use

$$
P_{hX}(z)=\Diamond(ha\land (hX)z)
$$

at $w$. For each $n\in N_r$, the true atom entails $Xn$ if $n\in A$, and entails its negation otherwise. These entailments survive $h$, and $ha$ is possible. Thus $P_{hX}(hn)$ has precisely the desired bit. The assignment does not require choosing a representative for each coextension class inside the model.

If $b\in N_w$ exceeds every member of $h[N_r]$, then

$$
|\mathcal A_r|\leq |N_w|.
$$

In Peano arithmetic with the admitted parameter $P=P_{hX}$, Frege induction proves that for every $b$ there exists a unique number $d<2^b$ whose binary digits in positions $i<b$ are $P(i)$. At zero choose zero. At $b+1$ keep the old code if $P(b)$ fails and add $2^b$ otherwise. Uniqueness follows from the same two disjoint ranges and the uniqueness of the old code. This proof covers nonstandard $b$. Two different source traces cannot yield the same code, even if their presenting predicates and their target extensions away from the numerical image differ. Equivalently, the sets of possible codes for distinct traces are nonempty and disjoint; the least code may be picked externally using any well-order of $N_w$.

In particular the bound applies to **every arithmetic-disagreeing arrow** satisfying the atom hypothesis. Arithmetic transport between Peano points is $\Delta_0$-elementary by the primitive recursive characteristic-function calculation. Gaifman's cofinal-embedding theorem for PA implies that if the arrow changes a first-order arithmetic sentence, its numerical image is noncofinal. Both endpoints satisfy first-order Peano arithmetic. The numerical carriers may be externally nonstandard. [Kaye, On cofinal extensions of models of fragments of arithmetic](https://doi.org/10.1305/ndjfl/1093635836)

For a current-full root the lower bound on each disagreeing target is $2^{|N_r|}$. There is also a result without arithmetic disagreement: at any nonstandard Peano target, take $b$ above all standard numerals. The same proof shows that its ordinary standard system contains the restriction of every source predicate to the standard numerals. Full current source extensions therefore force the target's standard system to contain every real. No countable nonstandard target is then possible. If all accessible numerical carriers have size below $2^{\aleph_0}$, and the Peano guard holds necessarily, every accessible number at every stage must be a standard numeral. All the numerical maps are then onto; persistence, necessary distinctness and these exact images give rigidity of $N$ in the native Arithmetic Necessitism instance.

This restricts countermodels to the open implications from Actuality plus ND, Plenitude, and Functional Choice to Necessity of Arithmetic. A countable source with restricted Henkin rows may have $\mathcal A_r$ countable, and a current-full source can have targets of at least continuum size. The argument leaves the general question open.
