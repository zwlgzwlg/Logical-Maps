# Necessary well-orderings at each point without Intensional Choice

At every world in these two models every type has a well-order and every equivalence relation has a transversal. A necessarily inhabited property of propositions still has no necessarily single-valued subproperty. In the first model all worlds form one S5 cluster and have the same pure theory. The second also satisfies revised Mathematical Possibilism and has genuine strong atoms at every relational type.

## Worlds and domains

Take $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^{\mathbb N}$ and let $G_N$ consist of the group elements zero on the first $N$ coordinates. Write $s_n$ for the flip of coordinate $n$ alone and $H_n=\{1,s_n\}$. The worlds are the disjoint union of the regular $G$-orbit and one copy of $G/H_n$ for every $n$. The group acts by translation. There is one arrow between each ordered pair of worlds, one individual, and all transports are identities.

At a relational type with input tuple domain $D$, take all subsets of $W\times D$ invariant under the diagonal action of some $G_N$. Its extension at $w$ is the row there. The type domains and group actions are defined together by induction on type. Each tuple has a common supporting tail since it has finitely many entries. Group translation commutes with evaluation, including quantification over the entire domain and comparing tuple entries by identity. A lambda evaluated on supported parameters is supported by any common tail fixing those parameters; partial application adds the supports of the supplied arguments. A difference between whole relations appears at some world and tuple. These clauses give the S5 action semantics with Henkin domains, as in the [profinite Boolean models](profinite-s5-boolean-algebras.html). Original relational names may denote top.

**Full current rows at every world.** A world $w$ in the regular orbit has trivial stabilizer. A world of $G/H_n$ has stabilizer $H_n$, whose intersection with $G_N$ is trivial for $N>n$. Thus a suitable $G_N$ acts freely on its orbit through $w$. For any external set of tuples $E$, assign row $gE$ at $gw$ for $g\in G_N$ and empty rows outside that orbit. Freeness makes this well defined, and the whole profile is $G_N$-invariant. Its actual extension is $E$. In particular every external well-order, choice graph and transversal is realized in current extension at every world. Hence Well-Ordering, Relational Choice and Transversal Choice all hold necessarily. Identity transports give necessary Barcan and necessary ND at every type.

For any two worlds $w,w'$, choose one $G_N$ acting freely on both of its orbits through them. If the orbits differ, swap them by $gw\leftrightarrow gw'$ and fix all other worlds. If they coincide, use a translation taking $w$ to $w'$. The resulting permutation commutes with $G_N$. Recursively it acts on every relational sort by permuting the world and the tuple coordinates together. A relation supported by $G_m$ is sent to a relation supported by $G_{\max(m,N)}$, as the permutation commutes with that subgroup at every type. Its inverse enjoys the same property. Thus it induces an automorphism of the pure model taking $w$ to $w'$, proving No Pure Contingency.

## The unsatisfied demand

For $n\in\mathbb N$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$ let $p_{n,b}$ be the proposition consisting of the points of the regular orbit whose $n$th bit is $b$. It is supported by $G_{n+1}$. At every world of $G/H_n$ require one of the two propositions $p_{n,0},p_{n,1}$. On the regular orbit require top. This defines a property $F$ of propositions invariant under all of $G$, since translations preserve each orbit and preserve each demanded pair as a set. Every row of $F$ is nonempty.

Suppose a property below $F$ had exactly one member at each point. It would be supported by some $G_N$. Choose $n\ge N$ and evaluate at the identity coset of $H_n$. The element $s_n$ belongs to $G_N$ and fixes that world, so preserves the selected singleton. Yet $s_n$ exchanges the only two candidates. This contradiction refutes Intensional Choice. A necessarily well-ordering relation would give the least member of every row of $F$, so Modal Well-Ordering fails as well. The individual current well-orders need not be induced by a single relation at all worlds.

In the S5 model every atom is strong, since identity transports leave the whole Boolean order and its atoms unchanged. In fact every nonzero core relation can be split. Given an occupied $(w,\bar x)$, choose a tail fixing both the relation and $\bar x$. A single-bit flip in this tail, avoiding the one possible stabilizer bit of $w$, moves $w$ while preserving occupancy at $\bar x$. A proposition splitting its orbit by that bit is a supported subset, and cutting by it gives two nonzero parts. In particular Atomlessness holds. With unrestricted current rows the standard Frege numerals exhaust the current numbers; the proposition sort is infinite, so the Axiom of Infinity at $t$ holds necessarily.

For each relational type, unions of the countably many world orbits give continuum many globally fixed entities by assigning that truth set independently of the arguments. Constant-world relations on these pointwise fixed codes supply an ordinary real number structure and the families of all its finite-arity relations as represented rigid powers. Every subset or relation on these codes has a fixed constant-world representative. This proves Second-Order Analytic Necessitism exactly as in the [profinite model's fixed-code construction](profinite-s5-boolean-algebras.html). At $e$ there is no possible real number structure.

## Adding actual Boolean atoms and Mathematical Possibilism

For the second model add countably many separate ordinary full one-world terminals with one individual each. Every core world has one entrance to each terminal, and the entrance absorbs prior travel among core worlds. Each core relational entity now has, in addition to its supported core profile on the enlarged tuple domains, one independent whole coordinate of the same type at every terminal. The group acts trivially on these coordinates. Application and abstraction at an exit use its own domain and the projected parameters; the core closure argument continues to apply on the enlarged domains.

The same free-orbit extension supplies unrestricted current rows at every core world, and the terminals are full. Each entrance is onto at every type by the independent coordinates. These maps and the core identity transports give necessary Barcan and necessary Well-Ordering and Transversal Choice. In the choice obstruction above give $p_{n,b}$ bottom terminal values, and give $F$ the singleton row containing top at each terminal. Then $F$ is necessarily inhabited, and the same flip at the coset still prevents a necessary singleton selector.

The splitting argument applies to every relation with an occupied core row, now taking the splitting propositions with bottom terminal values on the core parts. Thus any atom must be supported only on the terminals. Since the terminal coordinates are independent, the atoms are exactly the single-tuple observations at a single terminal. They are strong: the core identity transport preserves them, and at an exit they become a singleton atom or bottom. Every terminal is itself a full finite classical model. Consequently Atoms are strong atoms holds necessarily at every relational type and the bridge has actual instances.

At the inexact ordinal type on individuals, the entities using a single extensional truth table on the order and field of the unique individual at all core and terminal observations form a finite set. Its current membership test is admitted. Ordinal zero and successor preserve it, since the order and field each have just one entry. A rigid family drawn currently from this finite set equals the finite union of its members' haecceities by persistence and weak inextensibility. Every transported member still has its uniform table, so its limit also has a uniform table. This bounds the ordinal benchmark by a finite number. Every infinite Boolean algebra contains a countably infinite set $Y$ larger than this benchmark; its strictly smaller subsets are finite and their powersets can be coded on finite subsets of $Y$, using the unrestricted current graphs. Thus every revised-small Boolean algebra is finite. The interval below any $k$ distinct propositional terminal atoms joined together represents the algebra with $k$ atoms, with the bottom interval for $k=0$. This proves Mathematical Possibilism at the core. [Inexact ordinals and revised smallness](stone-terminal-maximalist.html)

Both rigid powers and Tame Rigidity hold necessarily in this terminal extension. Let $F$ be weakly rigid at a core point, with current row $E$. Persistence puts $E$ in every core row and its image in every terminal row. For each $a\notin E$ use the tester deleting just $a$ at all core worlds and full at the terminals. It is admitted since the one tuple $a$ has a supporting tail, and every member of $E$ necessarily passes it. Weak inextensibility thus makes every core row exactly $E$. A tester deleting one tuple at just terminal $j$ likewise makes that row exactly the projection $h_j[E]$. The relation has exact images everywhere and is therefore fully rigid. Its constant core row implies $E$ is supported as a set by a single tail.

At a terminal any subrelation $Z\subseteq h_j[E]$ lifts as follows. Set $E'=\{a\in E:h_j(a)\in Z\}$. The terminal projection is equivariant with trivial target action, so every tail fixing $E$ fixes $E'$. Give the lift constant core row $E'$ and its exact image at each terminal. It is rigid, lies below $F$, and has terminal value $Z$ at $j$. Between core points the same exact-image criterion characterizes every weak or rigid subrelation below $F$, so their sets are identical at all core sites. At each terminal the lift supplies every such subrelation. Thus both powers have exact images and are fully rigid at each core site. At classical terminals these principles hold directly.
