# Pure Strong Possibility forces rigid relations to be Frege-finite

At a pure Strong Possibility point, the current extension of every rigid relation has a Frege finite cardinality. Consequently Rigid Power holds. If Tame Rigidity also holds there, then Weakly Rigid Power holds. The count can be nonstandard. The argument applies to every finite argument tuple, including the empty tuple.

Write $\mathrm{SA}$ for Strong Actuality. For a relation $F$ on a tuple of types $\bar\sigma$, write $\mathrm{Fin}(F)$ for the assertion that some current Frege number counts the property of being the tuple-haecceity of an $F$-tuple. This is one formula for each fixed arity. Tuple-haecceities are necessarily injective in their coordinates. [Internally finite bounds](../index.html#background/rigidity/internally-finite-rigid-power)

## A pure sentence consistent with ordinary Maximalism

Fix $\bar\sigma$ and consider the closed pure sentence
$$
 \Psi_{\bar\sigma} =
 \forall F^{\bar\sigma t}\bigl(\mathrm{Rigid}(F)\to
             \Box(\neg\mathrm{SA}\to\mathrm{Fin}(F))\bigr).
$$
The coalesced Maximalist point with finite descriptor support satisfies this sentence. Its only root loop is the identity. The proposition true just at that identity is a true strong atom, so $\mathrm{SA}$ holds there. For every weakly rigid $F$ at that root there are finitely many actual tuples $\bar a_1,\ldots,\bar a_m$ such that at every proper arrow $h$ the entire relation $hF$ is the union of the tuple-haecceities of $h\bar a_1,\ldots,h\bar a_m$. This includes old postcomposites. The current extension at such an arrow therefore has an ordinary finite number of tuples, possibly fewer than $m$ through coincidences. Its tuple codes have an actual Frege count. These two cases give the box in $\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$. The auxiliary symbols used to construct the point do not occur in $\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$. ## The return

Let $r$ satisfy pure Strong Possibility. The strong-atom obstruction gives $r\models\neg\mathrm{SA}$. Suppose $F$ is rigid at $r$ and has no current Frege finite count. Then $q=\neg\mathrm{SA}\land\neg\mathrm{Fin}(F)$ is a true proposition at $r$. The schema applies to the closed pure $\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$ and gives $\Diamond_{\ne}\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$. Testing with this particular $q$ gives arrows $r\to v\to z$ such that $\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$ holds at $v$ and the transported $q$ holds at $z$. Rigidity persists, so the image of $F$ at $v$ is rigid. Since $\mathrm{SA}$ fails at $z$, the box in $\Psi_{\bar\sigma}$ gives $\mathrm{Fin}(F)$ there for the very same transported relation. This contradicts $q$. [Strong-atom obstruction](../index.html#result/strong-possibility-and-strong-actuality-incompatible)

The internally finite bound theorem now gives $\Box\mathrm{Fin}(F)$ and the identity and rigidity of the canonical full and weak powers of $F$. This proves Rigid Power. Under local Tame Rigidity it also applies to every current weakly rigid $F$, proving Weakly Rigid Power. Thus at a pure Strong Possibility point Tame Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Power are equivalent, using the established converse in C. ## The ordinal benchmark at a Strong Possibility point

The Frege numbers at the individual type inject into the inexact ordinal benchmark $H$ used in revised Mathematical Possibilism. If every rigid family of ordinal candidates has internally finite current extension, the same map is a bijection onto $H$. This hypothesis holds under pure Strong Possibility and under Strong Possibility for any admitted signature. Consequently, at either kind of Strong Possibility point, the comparison $H<Y$ in the definition of smallness can be replaced by $\mathbb N_e<Y$. [Ordinal constructors](https://github.com/andrew-bacon/Logical-Maps/blob/82133f4969047baa6ce02700483c9741c5857699/logical-maps/topics/classicism/verification/finite-power-ch/Classicism/Results/MathematicalContingency/InductiveOrdinals.lean) · [Signature finite rigid bounds](strong-possibility-signature-and-finite-rigid-bounds.html)

Write $N=\mathbb N_e$ and let $A$ be a property of individuals. As in the map's at-least numbers, put $n^{\ge}(A):=\exists X\subseteq A\;n(X)$, using current inclusion. The required map is the single term

$$ O(n)(R,A):=\operatorname{WellOrderOn}(A,R)\land n^{\ge}(A). $$

All finiteness and inductions below use the internal Frege numbers. A property counted by a number has a represented current well-order: induct on the count, ordering the empty set at zero and appending a removed member at the successor step. The same induction shows that a nonempty finite subset of a linearly ordered set has a greatest and a least element. These arguments use only the indicated finite insertion or comparison, including at nonstandard counts. **Zero and successor.** $O(0)$ is the ordinal zero, which tests well-orderhood. For a number $n$, $O(\operatorname{Suc}n)$ is the ordinal successor of $O(n)$. On a well-ordered field $A$, a subset of size $\operatorname{Suc}n$ has a greatest member $a$. Removing $a$ leaves an $n$-element subset of the strict initial segment at $a$. Conversely, adjoin the segment endpoint to any such subset. The restricted order well-orders that segment. Numberhood persists, so this calculation applies at every future point with the transported $n$ and gives equality of the whole ordinal candidates. For any test property closed under the ordinal constructors, Frege induction now places every $O(n)$ in it. Thus $O(n)\in H$ for every $n\in N$.

**Injectivity.** Suppose $O(n)=O(m)$. Consider any future where $n$ counts $X$, and give $X$ a current well-order. Equality of the thresholds supplies an $m$-counted $Y\subseteq X$. Well-order $Y$ and use the same equality in the opposite direction to obtain an $n$-counted $Z\subseteq Y$. No proper subproperty of an $n$-counted property is $n$-counted. Thus $Z,Y,X$ have the same current extension, so $m$ counts $X$. The converse implication follows by exchanging $n,m$. They agree on every argument throughout the cone, giving $n=m$ by Intensionality. The graph of $O$ is therefore a represented injection in C, without a Peano assumption.

**Comparison.** Say $n\preceq m$ when it is necessary that every $m$-counted property has an $n$-counted subproperty. Then $0\preceq m$, and $n\preceq m$ implies $\operatorname{Suc}n\preceq\operatorname{Suc}m$. For the latter, remove a member of a successor-counted set, choose the indicated $n$-counted subproperty of its remainder, and put that member back. Every number is zero or a successor of a number, by Frege induction. Induct on $n$ with all $m$ quantified, distinguishing those two cases for $m$. This proves $n\preceq m$ or $m\preceq n$. If $n\preceq m$, then $O(m)\le O(n)$ in the intensional order on ordinal candidates. Hence the members of the definable image $O[N]$ are linearly ordered by intensional entailment.

**Rigid limits.** Assume all rigid families at the ordinal-candidate type have internally finite current extension. Let $Y$ be such a family with current members in $O[N]$. If it is empty, weak inextensibility gives $Y=\bot$, and its ordinal limit is $O(0)$. Otherwise its finite current extension has a least member $y_0$ in the intensional order. Every current $y\in Y$ necessarily satisfies $y_0\le y$, since this is an identity between entities. Weak inextensibility applied to that tester yields $\Box\forall y\,(Yy\to y_0\le y)$. Persistence keeps $y_0$ in $Y$ throughout the cone. The ordinal limit tests well-orderhood and requires every member of $Y$ to accept the given order and field. On the entire cone this is precisely $y_0$: it already entails well-orderhood, and it entails each other current threshold at every point. Thus $\operatorname{OrdLim}(Y)=y_0$. The image $O[N]$ is closed under every required rigid limit, as well as zero and successor, so the impredicative definition of $H$ gives $H\subseteq O[N]$ at the point. The inverse on $H$ is the converse of the represented graph of $O$.

The bijection compares the current carriers in precisely the sense used by revised Mathematical Possibilism. The argument supplies no completeness or representation assertion for an arbitrary infinite small Boolean algebra.

## Representation of internally finite Boolean algebras

Every complete Boolean algebra with a Frege-finite current carrier is represented by a full principal interval of propositions at any pure Strong Possibility point. The order isomorphism is an admitted relation, as required in revised Mathematical Possibilism. This includes internally finite algebras of nonstandard size.

Let $N=\mathbb N_e$ and let $b_n$ be the finite Fregean cell $\mathrm{Fr}\land(\operatorname{Suc}_e n)(\lambda x.\top)$. At a pure SP point the $b_n$ are pairwise disjoint nonzero scalar atoms, uniformly for all current $n\in N$, and $N$ is Peano. Thus the entire predicate of scalar atoms is not Frege-finite. Indeed $n\mapsto b_n$ is an admitted injection. On its image shift $b_n$ to $b_{\operatorname{Suc}n}$, fixing each other atom. The resulting admitted endomap of the atom carrier is injective and misses $b_0$. The finite pigeonhole theorem excludes such a map on a Frege-finite set. [Uniform finite Fregean cells](strong-possibility-r-inconsistent.html#finite-cells)

At any point with this many scalar atoms, fix a Boolean algebra with an internally finite carrier $B$ and order $E$. At the SP point the source number system at every type is Peano, as every type has possible arbitrary finite cardinality by ordinary Maximalism. Apply strong Frege-number induction to the cardinality of $B$ at its own carrier type, universally over the algebra. A one-element algebra is represented below bottom. Otherwise choose an atom $a$ of $B$. A nonempty finite set of nonzero elements of a partially ordered set has a minimal element, by finite induction. Joins, meets and complements here are the unique elements specified by the given order.

The interval of $B$ below the complement of $a$ is a proper subset of $B$. Its size is strictly smaller, so induction gives an admitted isomorphism $J$ from it to the full scalar interval below some $p$. That scalar interval is itself Frege-finite. Finiteness transports across a represented bijection even between different types: induct on the source count, remove one matched pair, and adjoin its image to the target remainder. A subpredicate of a finite carrier is finite by the same removal induction. If every scalar atom were below $p$, the atom predicate would therefore be finite. Choose an atom $c$ outside the interval. Then $c\land p=\bot$.

Given $x\in B$, use $J$ on $x\land\neg_B a$, and adjoin $c$ to the output just when $a\le_B x$. The graph is a lambda relation using the graph $J$ and uniquely specified Boolean operations. The decomposition of $x$ into its part below $\neg_Ba$ and its zero-or-$a$ part shows that it preserves and reflects order. Every scalar $y\le p\lor c$ splits into $y\land p$ and $y\land c$, the latter either zero or $c$. This proves bijectivity onto the whole principal interval. The induction and the graph involve only current witnesses. They do not require an operation choosing isomorphisms across different Boolean algebras.

The finite representation argument requires Peano arithmetic at the counting type and a scalar atom outside every internally finite set of scalar atoms. With these hypotheses at every counting type, any failure of revised Mathematical Possibilism must concern an internally infinite small Boolean algebra.
