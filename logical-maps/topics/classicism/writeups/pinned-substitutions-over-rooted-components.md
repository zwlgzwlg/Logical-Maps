# Rigid Power over rooted substitution components

Use [Dorr’s finitely pinned substitution construction](coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root.html), with the rooted action categories of Appendix D: every object of each component is reachable from its designated point. The original proofs of signature Possibility+ and Barcan are unchanged. Both Rigid Power and Weakly Rigid Power fail for every complete family satisfying this convention.

The argument works for any of the complete families of rooted components permitted in this construction. Let $K$ be a component whose root satisfies the Fregean Axiom and the Axiom of Infinity at $e$. Such a root is required by completeness: the ordinary full one-world model on the natural numbers satisfies this conjunction. A Fregean point makes each of its true propositions necessary. Every object of the rooted component is reachable from its root, so the whole component is Fregean and satisfies the Axiom of Infinity at $e$. In particular it has infinitely many individuals at every point: on an externally finite domain the corresponding standard numeral counts the universal property. Every relation at a Fregean point is rigid. Indeed current membership is necessary there, and any current universal inclusion is necessary as well; these properties hold at every successor. [Definition D.1 and the formalization's rootedness convention](https://github.com/zwlgzwlg/Logical-Maps/blob/4bb9287187ed7cb09567eb3a6302cb896c986ec4/logical-maps/topics/classicism/lean/Classicism/Semantics/Action.lean#L135)

For every unary property name $c$ in the expanded signature define a source property $x_c$ as follows. At every substitution its row is empty. At every interpretation into $K$ its transported value is $I(c)$, and in every other component its value is bottom. These prescriptions respect component arrows and the pin $\{c\}$ suffices. Different names give different entities because their values can be assigned independently in $K$. Substitution satisfies $f x_c=x_{f(c)}$.

Let $E=\{x_c:c\text{ has type }et\}$. Take the source relation $F$ on unary properties whose row is $E$ at every substitution, whose whole value in $K$ is top, and whose whole value in every other component is the haecceity of the bottom unary property. This is an empty-pinned relation and every substitution preserves it. Its current members persist throughout the model.

To check weak inextensibility, let a tester $W$ necessarily hold of every current member. Choose a name $c$ outside its finite pin. At an arbitrary substitution $f$, to test the member $x_d$ of the row $E$, change $f$ just by sending $c$ to $d$. The row of $W$ is unchanged, and it contains the image $x_d$ of $x_c$. At any interpretation into $K$, change $I(c)$ to the unary property being tested. Once again the row of $W$ is unchanged and it contains that property. The other components have only bottom to test, the image there of any $x_c$. These cases cover every arrow, so $F\le W$. At each substitution $F$ is still the same weakly rigid relation; at the other arrows it is either top in a Fregean component or a haecceity. This proves that $F$ is rigid.

Consider any source weakly rigid $Z\le F$. Its actual members form a subset $E'\subseteq E$. If $E'$ is finite, $Z$ equals the finite disjunction of the haecceities of those members: persistence gives one inclusion and weak inextensibility against that disjunction gives the other. If $E'$ is infinite, choose $x_c\in E'$ whose name lies outside a finite pin for $Z$. For every $d$ the substitution fixing that pin but sending $c$ to $d$ preserves the current row of $Z$. Persistence places $x_d$ in that row. Consequently $E'=E$, and $Z=F$ because two weakly rigid coextensive relations are identical.

At a point of $K$ take the property $U$ of unary properties that are individual haecceities, defined by $U(Y)\leftrightarrow\exists a\,(Y=\lambda z.z=a)$. This is a rigid subrelation of the transported $F$. It has infinitely many members and omits the bottom unary property. It therefore differs, at every interpretation into $K$, both from the image of any finite source subfamily and from the universal image of $F$.

Use a tester that is full at every substitution and in every other component, and in $K$ has the uniform definable value $\lambda Y.Y\ne U$. Its pin is empty. The finite-or-total alternative shows that every current weakly rigid or rigid subrelation of $F$ necessarily passes. At an interpretation into $K$, the rigid subrelation $U$ itself fails. Neither power predicate is weakly inextensible at the source.

This argument uses the paper’s rooted-component convention. The repository’s Lean premodel type also allows pointed categories with objects unreachable from the base. Its particular untruncated canonical family has a separate formal question: a Fregean base alone says nothing about unreachable objects in its component.
