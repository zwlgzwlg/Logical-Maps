# A sharp cardinal bound for rigid extensions at a complete point

For each of three cardinals $\lambda$, there is a point satisfying Functional Choice, Boolean Completeness at all relation types, and necessary Countable Boolean Completeness. A current tuple set has an inextensible coextension exactly when its internal size is at most $\lambda$. Every current set has a weakly rigid coextension. At the cutoff itself a rigid bound fails Rigid Power. Changing $\lambda$ separates three levels of rigidity for arithmetic, analysis and second-order analysis.

## The stages and the cardinal bound

Use the [principal entrances](principal-entrances-with-generic-returns.html) with countably many ordinary entrances and singleton individuals. In the ordinary site's return germ replace the finite ideal by the ideal $\mathcal I=[K]^{\le\lambda}$ for $K=\lambda^+$. In the ground set model take $\lambda=\aleph_0$, $2^{\aleph_0}$ or $2^{2^{\aleph_0}}$. Compute all parameters in each successor by elementary image. For an optional fourth setting take $\lambda=2^{\aleph_0}$ in a ground satisfying $\neg\mathrm{CH}$ and include the [countably closed collapse entrance](cardinal-ideal-retraction-with-continuum-collapse.html) making that ground cardinal have size $\aleph_1$. All four constructions start with a countable elementary substructure of a sufficiently large regular $H_\theta$. The optional forcing uses the relative consistency assumption of the linked collapse construction.

For a relation type, let $A_R,A_S$ be the corresponding products of lower carriers, including the singleton product for propositions. The two ordinary domains are
$$
 R=\mathcal P(A_R)\times\mathcal P(A_S)^\omega,\qquad
 S=\mathcal P(A_S)\times R^K/{=_{\mathcal I}}.
$$
At the $i$th entrance $h_i$, an entity $x$ goes to its $i$th row with constant return tag $x$. At a return by a generic ultrafilter the second coordinate gives the entire new root entity in the ultrapower. Hence every pair of steps starting at a root acts on its parameters by the elementary map $j$. With forcing, include the ground Boolean name for the forcing row and give its evaluation the literal ground return tag as in the linked construction. Iterate both kinds of excursion on finite histories. The verification of term transport and intensional extensionality for the principal entrances uses only finite unions of null sets, Łoś's theorem and a generic through each positive condition. These apply to $\mathcal I$ at every stage. All cardinality assertions below are internal to the set model at the indicated stage.

For each $|E|\le\lambda$, one has $jE=j[E]$. Indeed, a function from $K$ into $E$ partitions each positive condition into at most $\lambda$ fibers. They cannot all be of size at most $\lambda$, so the conditions deciding a fiber are dense. Apply this at every return. Write $K_E$ for the root predicate with current row $E$ and entrance rows $h_i[E]$, including the name for the direct image of the checked ground set at a forcing entrance. For every $E$, its members persist and every tester necessarily accepting each member contains these prescribed rows at every coordinate. Thus $K_E$ is weakly rigid. For $|E|\le\lambda$, its current extension at every continuation is exactly the direct image of $E$, by the partition argument on each completed ordinary excursion and the literal tag on forcing returns. This makes $K_E$ rigid everywhere in the cone.

Conversely, every inextensible root predicate $P$ with row $E$ lies below $K_E$ by weak inextensibility. If $|E|>\lambda$, choose an internal sequence of distinct $a_\xi\in E$ for $\xi\in K$. At an ordinary site after $h_i$, take the tester with full current row and low germ $\xi\mapsto(\lambda z.\,z\ne a_\xi)$. All current members of $h_iP$ are in $h_i[E]$. Each goes on return to the elementary image of a fixed tuple, which differs from $[a_\xi]$ because each individual fiber is null. The inequality persists at every further step out of this new root, since ordinary and forcing entrances are injective on root parameters and completed excursions preserve their distinctions. Yet the returned $jP$ contains $[a_\xi]\in jE$ there. This violates weak inextensibility of $h_iP$. Hence $P$ could not be inextensible. There are sets of size greater than any of the three cutoffs at a fixed finite type. The root scalar sort has at least continuum many distinct entrance profiles, and taking powersets of current rows at higher types exceeds the power of the continuum. This proves the asserted characterization and refutes Tame Rigidity and Inextensible Comprehension.

## Failure of both powers at the cutoff

Choose a current set $E$ of exactly $\lambda$ tuples in a sufficiently large type and an injection $D:K\to\mathcal P(E)$. The bound $K_E$ is rigid. At a fixed ordinary entrance, every current weakly rigid subrelation $Z$ of $h_i K_E$ has a row of the form $h_i[B]$ with $B\subseteq E$ in the same set model. It equals $h_i K_B$: the latter is rigid and coextensive there. Two coextensive weakly rigid relations entail each other, since each necessarily accepts every current member of the other.

At this site take the tester with full current row and the germ of $\xi\mapsto(\lambda z.\,z\ne K_{D(\xi)})$. The return of $h_i K_B$ is $jK_B$. It passes the test at every return, since $D(\xi)=B$ at most once. The distinction persists through the cone from the returned root. At a return generic, $B'=(jD)([\mathrm{id}])$ is a subset of $jE$ with $|B'|\le j\lambda$. Its canonical property $K_{B'}$ is a rigid subrelation of the returned $K_E$ and fails the test there. Thus even at the ordinary site, the predicates for rigid and for weakly rigid subrelations of the bound fail weak inextensibility. In particular the power of rigid subrelations at the original root is not rigid. This refutes Rigid Power. Weakly Rigid Power also fails, since it entails Tame Rigidity, already refuted at this root.

## Local algebras, countable joins and the signature

The proof for principal entrances gives all-type Boolean Completeness, Functional Choice and a necessary well-order chosen at the root. At a site a current row with bottom return is inextensible. Together with the root representatives $K_E$ this proves necessary Weakly Inextensible Comprehension. The one propositional name denotes an ordinary entrance address, moved by a permutation of the addresses but always nonbottom. This yields Independence and failure of Possibly Witnessed Possibility. With singleton individuals, the finite ordinal benchmark and arbitrarily many principal address atoms give revised Mathematical Possibilism by the finite-algebra argument for [principal entrances](principal-entrances-with-generic-returns.html).

At ordinary sites, countably many germ coordinates have a join taken pointwise on representatives. Every competing upper bound fails the pointwise inclusions on at most a countable union of null sets, still null. For all types, a family countable in the native sense has an internal enumeration: for each member choose its least assigned Frege number in its counting sort. These numbers form an initial segment of the internal naturals in finite sorts and the internal naturals in infinite sorts, by the full current rows. At root stages all joins exist. At a forcing site, the countably closed collapse adds no countable sequence of old elements. A countable family of its return coordinates has a countable enumeration in the ground, and its ground join and current union give the least upper bound. This proves necessary Countable Boolean Completeness in all four settings.

At a site a nonzero ordinary scalar germ splits on two positive subsets, giving failure of Atomicity at that site. A current-only root atom transports to such a splittable germ. A root scalar supported on just one ordinary address splits where instantiated. These arguments give respectively failure of Strong Actuality, failure of the scalar self-atom strength bridge and failure of local Self-Atomicity. As in the principal-entrance proof, any root self-atom must have empty entrance rows and a singleton current row. Local scalar Barcan fails by the tester omitting an ordinary-site scalar with a nonconstant low return. The current root bit guards two-hat necessity of every truth, while it is itself a truth failing ND-necessity. At an ordinary or forcing site a current-only true bit guards ND-necessity there. Without forcing the root has all-type Atomicity. With forcing the name coordinate has an atomless scalar interval.

## Arithmetic and higher analysis

All ordinary ultrapower maps are onto the target naturals by the partition argument. The initial structure has standard naturals; so does every structure in the history, also after the optional forcing. At every counting type satisfying the Peano guard, the full internal current rows identify the Frege numbers with these naturals. Their values transport as the corresponding numerals and exhaust the numbers everywhere under the necessary Peano guard. Hence Arithmetic Necessitism holds for all counting types.

At every relational type there are at least continuum many persistent distinct labels. At $t$ use scalars coding subsets of the countable entrance addresses. At other relational types use their all-or-nothing rows on nonempty argument domains. The injective entrance tags preserve distinctness, including at a forcing site. At $e$, singleton individuals preclude a real structure anywhere. At $t$ a real structure always exists at the root because all internal current relations exist. Any real structure has at least continuum many distinct real labels, as its number labels are an internal copy of the naturals with every internal subset available. For $\lambda=\aleph_0$, the size bound excludes a rigid real carrier there, so Analytic Necessitism fails.

For $\lambda$ at least the continuum, choose disjoint natural and real labels at each relational type and represent their carriers, successor and membership graphs by the corresponding $K_E$. Their sizes are at most $\lambda$. The exact-image calculation proves the rigidity of every such relation. At every ordinary return the ultrapower is onto its set of reals from the old reals; at the forcing site the collapse adds no real. Therefore every internal subset of the transported natural labels has its corresponding real label at each continuation. This proves Analytic Necessitism, including in the collapse setting. If $\lambda$ equals the continuum, a represented power of the real carrier would need at least $2^\lambda$ current members to cover the distinct current subsets. At $t$ its required rigidity contradicts the size bound. Thus Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fails at this cutoff.

For $\lambda=2^{2^{\aleph_0}}$ with no forcing, each set of finite-arity relations on the chosen natural and real labels has size at most $\lambda$. Take the family of the $K_B$ for every such subset $B$ of the appropriate finite product. This family itself has a rigid $K_E$ representative. At each stage every current subset of the transported product is the transport of an old one, by surjectivity on the powerset. Distinct chosen predicates have distinct current rows, and their extensions cover every current row, so the native represented-power clause holds throughout. This proves Second-Order Analytic Necessitism at this cutoff, although Rigid Power fails for larger carriers.

Without the collapse, full current relations interpret each fixed second-order analytical sentence in the underlying set model and elementary excursions preserve its sign. With the collapse, the ground and extension have real structures at type $t$ and agree on first-order analysis, while CH changes from false to true. This gives failure of Non-contingency of CH.
