**Argument** (Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), 3 October 2026; not yet checked by a
human). In a full action model, write $X_0(S)_k:=k\cdot S$. At any object,
$X_0(S)$ is in the domain, so a relation $Y$ is weakly rigid iff $Y=X_0(Y_1)$.
Every such $Y$ is rigid, since $(h\cdot Y)_k=k\cdot(h\cdot Y_1)$. So the rigid
relations are exactly the $X_0(S)$. Moreover $X_0(T)\le X_0(S)$ iff $T\subseteq S$.
For rigid $F=X_0(S)$ and $P:=\lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F$:
- at an arrow $k$, $P_k=\{X_0(T'):T'\subseteq k\cdot S\}$;
- transport gives $k\cdot P_1=\{X_0(k\cdot T):T\subseteq S\}$;
- these coincide, because the image map $T\mapsto k\cdot T$ from subsets of $S$ to
  subsets of $k\cdot S$ is onto.

So $P=X_0(P_1)$, and $P$ is rigid. The same holds at every object, so both forms of
Rigid Power hold in every full action model.

## Model verdicts on the analysis principles (23 October 2026)

**23 October 2026 (Claude Fable 5.1, prompted by Zachary Goodsell).** The Mathematical contingency
principles added on 23 October (Analytic Necessitism, its second-order form, the Necessity of
Analysis and of Second-Order Analysis, the Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis) were tagged
across the models by general arguments rather than record by record: `second-order-analysis-extensionally-full`
(every extensionally full model has the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis, all its real number
structures being the real numbers with full second-order semantics); `analysis-finite-everywhere`
(finitely many arrows and individuals at every reachable object: no real number structure is
possible, so the necessitist principles hold vacuously), under the new conditions
`finitely-many-arrows-everywhere` and `finitely-many-individuals-everywhere`; and four arguments
for the failure of Analytic Necessitism in full action models with an infinite type, from
`unretracted-arrow` or `noninvertible-retracted-arrow`, resting on one lemma: an instance of a rigid
carrier is determined by its transport along any arrow, and in a full model no entity of a
relational type is determined by its transport along an arrow without a retraction. The same lemma
runs in the finitely pinned models when some arrow identifies two points (new condition
`non-injective-arrow`, the argument `analytic-necessitism-collapsing-arrow-pinned`) and in the
symmetric ideally-full models (new condition `exact-collapses-of-every-size`, which also supplies
the real number structure of type $tt$ from the orbits of the propositions "the arrow is constant

**Arithmetic Necessitism, the same day.** Settled in every model. The map's inextensibility is Dorr's:
a property may not gain instances beyond the transports of its old ones. Since the numerals are
closed terms, which transport to numerals, $\mathbb N_\sigma$ is rigid wherever the numbers at every
reachable object are exactly the numerals, that is, wherever some property has the numerals as its
extension there; persistence and necessary distinctness are theorems of C given $\Box I^*_\sigma$.
Two general arguments give that: `arithmetic-necessitism-extensionally-full` and
`arithmetic-necessitism-finitely-pinned` (the constant intension of numerals is pinned down by the
empty set and symmetric). The complete coalesced sums have the principle vacuously, $\neg I^*_\sigma$
being a pure sentence consistent with C and so possible at the root (shared argument
`arithmetic-necessitism-complete-components`); the Boolean-valued model has it because
$\mathbb N_\sigma$ is crisp. The one failure is coalesced-two-extensional-arithmetics-root: in its
component $M_2\models\neg\operatorname{Con}(T)$ the least inductive property has a nonstandard
member $c$, and the root entity whose $M_2$-part is $x<c$ necessarily holds of every root number
without containing $c$. So Arithmetic Necessitism is not a theorem of C; whether C5 proves it stays
open, the countermodel not being S5.

A correction made on the way: coalesced-pinned-interpretations-root and -substitutions-root were
tagged `extensionally-full`, whose text and Lean definition ask for fullness at every object, on a
reason that covers the root alone; their components are any action models complete for C($\Sigma$).
The tag is removed, the one verdict it carried (Transversal Choice at the root) is kept by an own
argument, and the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis, which the general argument had given them
unjustifiably, is unknown for them again.

**The top-ranked questions afterwards.** They had become uniform: the Necessity of Second-Order
Analysis in the coalesced sums, and its pairing with Maximalism and Possibility+. Three cheap
additions settle them. The two-arithmetics root refutes the Necessity of Analysis itself, at the type
of Dedekind cuts over the numbers at $e$: its components disagree on the analytical sentence that some
number codes a proof of a contradiction from their theory. That makes the negation of an instance a
pure sentence consistent with C, so every complete coalesced sum refutes the principle
(`necessity-of-analysis-complete-components`) and Pure Maximalism is inconsistent with it

action model with countably many arrows (new condition `countably-many-arrows`), the small complete

## Self-Atomicity and the bridge principles (25 October 2026)

Cian Dorr proposed the notion of a self-atom, a possible entity that is necessarily an atom whenever it
is instantiated, the principle Self-Atomicity (every possible entity is entailed by a self-atom) as a
weakening of the Strong Leibniz Biconditionals, and four bridge principles: atoms are self-atoms,
self-atoms are atoms, self-atoms are strong worlds, atoms are strong worlds. Claude Fable 5.1
(Anthropic), prompted by him, surveyed the recorded Strong Leibniz results. Recorded, each at relational
types and at type t, boxed and unboxed, in the category More Boolean structure: the five principles;
the structural results (type-t instances, boxed to unboxed, No Pure Contingency); the Strong Leibniz
Biconditionals as Atomicity with Atoms are strong worlds, BF giving the latter (Bacon’s Exercise 8.12
refactored, the old records kept with cross-references); the implications among the bridges; □Actuality,
Two-hat implies ND-necessity, 5 ⇒ □B and the intensional-choice result with Self-Atomicity for the Strong
Leibniz Biconditionals; 5 and □5 giving self-atoms are atoms and are strong worlds. Models: the
three-object fork and the monoid with ac = bc (Self-Atomicity with Atomicity and Strong Actuality but
without the Strong Leibniz Biconditionals or BF), the Cantor fan (Self-Atomicity with Actuality, Strong
Actuality and BF (type t) but without Atomicity), general arguments for full models, group arguments for
the symmetry-constrained models and the coalesced sums. Open: whether Self-Atomicity implies Strong
Actuality (it does in every full and every plain ideally full model), and whether Self-Atomicity with
