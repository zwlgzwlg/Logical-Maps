Second-Order Analytic Necessitism implies the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis

Composed by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026, extending the write-up of analytic-necessitism-implies-necessity-of-analysis, after Theorem 7 of Neil Barton and Zachary Goodsell, Continuum Contingency (draft of 14 October 2026), with Proposition C.3 and Lemma C.3 of Andrew Bacon, Could the truths of mathematics have been different? (draft of 9 October 2025). Informal, not Lean-verified.

Setting. Work in C at a type \sigma. Fix a second-order analytical sentence A and let n be the largest arity of a relation variable in it. If no real number structure at \sigma is possible, the instance holds vacuously. Otherwise the instance of Second-Order Analytic Necessitism for n supplies a rigid real number structure \mathfrak R=(X,\le,+,\times,0,1,N) with necessarily distinct elements, \Box\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R), and for each k\le n a represented power \mathcal P_k: a rigid property of rigid k-ary relations holding only of tuples of instances of X, such that \Box\forall R\,\exists S(\mathcal P_kS\land\forall\bar x\in X(S\bar x\leftrightarrow R\bar x)).

Lemma 1′ (atomic facts). Lemma 1 of the first-order write-up holds also for S an instance of some \mathcal P_k: S\bar x\to\Box S\bar x and \neg S\bar x\to\Box\neg S\bar x for \bar x from X. The proof is the same, S being rigid and holding only of tuples of instances of X, which are necessarily distinct from \bar x when distinct from it.

Lemma 2′ (formulas with parameters). For every second-order analytical formula \varphi(\bar v,\bar V), every tuple \bar x of instances of X and every tuple \bar S of instances of the \mathcal P_k of the arities of \bar V: \varphi(\bar x,\bar S)\to \Box\varphi(\bar x,\bar S) and \neg\varphi(\bar x,\bar S)\to\Box\neg\varphi(\bar x,\bar S).

Proof. Induction on \varphi. The atomic, connective and first-order quantifier cases are those of the first-order write-up, with Lemma 1′ for the atomic formulas V\bar x. For a relation quantifier \forall R\,\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S) of arity k, first note that necessarily \forall R\,\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S)\leftrightarrow\forall R(\mathcal P_kR\to\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S)). The left-to-right direction is trivial. For the converse, at any world, a relation R agrees on tuples of instances of X with some instance S of \mathcal P_k, by the representation clause, and \psi(S,\bar x,\bar S)\to\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S) because an analytical formula applies its relation variables only to terms denoting instances of X (its first-order variables are restricted to X and 0,1 are instances of X), so it cannot distinguish relations agreeing on such tuples: this is Bacon’s Proposition C.3, Analytic Extensionality, a theorem schema of the non-modal fragment, whose necessitation holds. It therefore suffices to show that \forall R(\mathcal P_kR\to\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S)) is necessary if true and impossible if false, and this is the first-order quantifier case with \mathcal P_k as restrictor: if it holds, each instance S of \mathcal P_k has \psi(S,\bar x,\bar S), hence \Box\psi(S,\bar x,\bar S) by the induction hypothesis (the parameters S,\bar S being instances of the \mathcal P’s), and inextensibility of \mathcal P_k at \lambda R\, .\,\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S) gives \Box\forall R(\mathcal P_kR\to\psi(R,\bar x,\bar S)); if it fails, some instance S has \neg\psi(S,\bar x,\bar S), so \Box\neg\psi(S,\bar x,\bar S) by the induction hypothesis and \Box\mathcal P_kS by persistence, whence the negation is necessary by K. \square

Lemma 3′ (categoricity). \Box\forall\mathfrak R'(\operatorname{Real}_\sigma\mathfrak R'\to (A(\mathfrak R')\leftrightarrow A(\mathfrak R))): Huntington’s theorem, and the invariance of second-order analytical sentences under isomorphism, since an isomorphism between structures at the same type transports relations by λ-abstraction (Bacon’s Lemma C.3); both theorems of the non-modal fragment, necessitated.

Proof of the implication. As in the first-order case: Lemma 2′ at A gives \Box A(\mathfrak R) or \Box\neg A(\mathfrak R), and Lemma 3′ turns either into the corresponding disjunct of the instance. \square

Remarks. The represented powers enter only through the relation quantifiers, where the representation clause makes the restricted and unrestricted quantifiers necessarily equivalent and the rigidity of \mathcal P_k makes the restricted one non-contingent. Under Rigid Comprehension the rigid relations holding only of X-tuples represent every relation, and Barton and Goodsell’s Second-Order Continuum Necessitism, that the property of rigid properties of reals is rigid, is the arity-one represented power of the reals; relations of higher arity reduce to properties there by pairing. The continuum hypothesis is a second-order analytical sentence, so the Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis follows (necessity-of-second-order-analysis-implies-non-contingency-of-ch).
