1. Exact types and the current existence lemma

For an externally fixed source type \sigma, put

\nu_\sigma=(\sigma t)t,\qquad \rho_\sigma=\nu_\sigma t.

Let \mathrm{Tr}_\alpha denote the native Transversal instance whose properties have type \alpha t: a property of properties selects exactly one literal entity from each current coextension class on \alpha. The following is a C theorem for each \sigma:

I^*_\sigma\land\mathrm{Tr}_{\nu_\sigma} \ \longrightarrow\ \exists\mathfrak R\,\operatorname{Real}_{\rho_\sigma}(\mathfrak R). \tag{1}

The host on the right is explicit. There is no quantifier over types. Neither the guard nor Transversal is boxed in (1). Real has the native definition: literal equality, an antisymmetric linear order, functional relational graphs for field operations, completeness for every admitted bounded subproperty of its carrier, and its least inductive number property.

Write N=\mathbb N_\sigma. Its canonical leastness already gives induction over all admitted predicates. With I^*_\sigma, zero is not a successor and successor is injective. Thus it is a current Peano structure at the host \nu_\sigma. The usual least recursion graphs give current addition, multiplication and finite coding. This uses the ordinary canonical-arithmetic interpretation, not an external assumption that the numbers are standard.

For explicit rational codes, use the injective numerical pairing graph c=(a+b)^2+a. All displayed arithmetic expressions abbreviate finite compositions of the canonical Sum and Prod relations. If u=a+b, the code lies in [u^2,u^2+u]; these intervals are disjoint for different u, and the value within one interval determines a, then b. This is an internal Peano proof, with no operation object extracted.

A raw rational code is a nested pairing code of (a,b,d), representing (a-b)/(d+1). Two triples are equivalent exactly when

a(d'+1)+b'(d+1)=a'(d+1)+b(d'+1).

Take as Q\subseteq N the valid codes that are least in their classes. Each class is an admitted nonempty numerical predicate, so internal least-number induction gives a literally unique least code. Cross-multiplication defines order. Fraction addition, multiplication, negation and reciprocal are relational graphs followed by this least-code normalization. Their ordinary laws follow by internal cross-multiplication. A positive denominator has a predecessor by the Peano theorem; it is introduced through its successor graph. No choice of quotient objects or operation-valued representatives is required.

This internal rational field has the precise bound needed below: for rational q and \epsilon>0, some internal natural m satisfies m\epsilon>q. A positive fraction a/b, with b\ge1, is below a+1; apply this observation to q/\epsilon. This does not claim that the numbers are externally standard or the rational field externally Archimedean. Induction is available for admitted predicates with arbitrary typed parameters, including a cut parameter.

2. Literal cut representatives and field operations

Choose a witness F^{(\nu_\sigma t)t} to \mathrm{Tr}_{\nu_\sigma}. Define \operatorname{Cut}(A) to say that A^{\nu_\sigma t} is supported on Q, nonempty, proper, downward closed in the rational order, and has no greatest member. Put

X(A):=F(A)\land\operatorname{Cut}(A).

For each admitted rational-cut predicate D, Transversal gives one and only one A\in X agreeing with D on the entire host \nu_\sigma. Conversely any two X-members with equal cut extensions are identical, by uniqueness in F. This is the step that cannot be replaced by mere comprehension in an intensional model. It is current equality, not a claim that coextensive arbitrary properties are identical or necessarily coextensive.

Define A\leq_X B by cut inclusion, with both arguments in X. This order is antisymmetric because of literal representative uniqueness. The predicates of rationals below 0 and 1 have unique representatives; take these as the two field constants. Addition is the supported graph specifying that the result represents the lower cut

q\longmapsto Qq\land \exists r,s\in Q\,[Ar\land Bs\land q<r+s]. \tag{2}

Additive inverse represents q\mapsto Qq\land\exists r\in Q(\neg Ar\land q<-r). For nonnegative cuts the product represents

q\longmapsto Qq\land \bigl(q<0\ \lor\ \exists r,s\in Q\,[0<r\land0<s\land Ar\land Bs\land q<rs]\bigr). \tag{3}

Use additive inverse for the remaining sign cases. For a positive cut, its reciprocal represents q\mapsto Qq\land[q<0\lor\exists r\in Q(0<r\land\neg Ar\land q<1/r)]; negative reciprocals follow by sign. These are finite admitted formulas. Every specified output cut has exactly one representative. Consequently their graphs are total and functional on X. No operation-valued choice or Plenitude is used.

The internal small-gap argument is essential. Given an admitted cut A and rational \epsilon>0, choose a\in A, b\notin A, put \delta=\epsilon/2, and find an internal natural m with a+m\delta>b. The admitted predicate a+n\delta\notin A has a least positive member. Its predecessor gives l\in A and u\notin A with u-l=\delta<\epsilon. The arithmetic values are expressed by their unique relational graphs. Thus induction uses an admitted predicate with parameter A, rather than an external enumeration or a sequence of cut-valued outputs.

Write L_r for the rational principal cut below r. If l\in A,u\notin A,m\in B,v\notin B, the raw formulas give

L_{l+m}\subseteq\operatorname{AddRaw}(A,B)\subseteq L_{u+v}, \qquad L_{-u}\subseteq\operatorname{NegRaw}(A)\subseteq L_{-l}.

For positive lower endpoints, the product bounds are L_{lm} and L_{uv}, and the reciprocal bounds are L_{1/u} and L_{1/l}. These are weak principal-cut inclusions: for example -u need not belong to the negative cut. Add arbitrarily small rational slack before requiring strict endpoint membership. Zero inputs give the zero cut in the nonnegative product formula; strictly positive inputs have positive members and positive outside bounds, which make the reciprocal a nonempty proper rounded cut. Negation supplies the remaining sign cases.

For completeness about the field laws, the common-approximation invariant is as follows. Every fixed finite expression in the raw operations has rational brackets of arbitrarily small width, obtained from sufficiently small brackets for its finitely many inputs. On a rationally bounded region |x|,|y|\le M, sum error is at most 2\delta and product error at most (2M+\delta)\delta. For inversion first fix a positive rational lower bound d; reciprocal error is at most \delta/d^2. Signed cases follow from negation. These are finite rational inequalities, valid internally. Finite induction on an expression chooses sufficiently small input widths; it does not form an infinite approximation sequence.

For each field identity, evaluate both sides on the same rational approximants. Rational algebra makes the centers agree, and the preceding bounds put the two output cuts in a common arbitrarily narrow bracket. If they differed, choose q in one and not the other, then q'>q in the first by roundedness. A common bracket L_l\subseteq A,B\subseteq L_u with u-l<q'-q would force l\le q<q'<u, a contradiction. Thus the outputs are currently coextensive; selected-representative uniqueness makes them literally identical. This proves the ring laws, additive inverse and the nonzero reciprocal law. Inclusion is total on cuts; addition and positive multiplication preserve it directly, and cancellation gives strictness. The positive-member test gives positive products, so these are the ordered-field laws.

The explicit coding and weak-bracket estimates are from GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, Current Transversal supplies a literal real field, 30 October 2026, §§2–5 (unpaginated). They refine the details of this current construction without changing its hypotheses or types.

3. Completeness for every admitted subproperty

Let Y^{\rho_\sigma t} be any admitted nonempty subproperty of X, bounded above by B\in X. Comprehension forms

U(q):=Qq\land\exists A\,[XA\land YA\land Aq]. \tag{4}

This is a cut: it is nonempty because Y is; it is proper because its union is contained in the proper upper cut B; downward closure and absence of a greatest element follow from a witnessing member cut. Transversal supplies its unique representative C\in X. Each Y-member is below C, and every upper bound includes (4), so C is its least upper bound. The argument covered an arbitrary admitted Y, not just a definable or externally listed family.

Define the field’s number predicate by the least subproperty of X containing its zero and closed under its addition-by-one graph. The impredicative comprehension formula supplies exactly that property. It is the numberhood clause of native Real. With the preceding supported order and operation graphs, we have a full tuple satisfying \operatorname{Real}_{\rho_\sigma}, proving (1).

All representatives were requested one existential witness at a time. Transversal itself supplies their common extensional carrier, so neither Relational Choice nor a uniform choice operation is hidden in the proof. All conclusions in (1) are current; no rigidity or future fullness has yet been claimed.

4. The fixed Peano reference lemma, with proof

This section spells out the source’s transport argument. A carrier is fixed when it is rigid and its current members are necessarily distinct. Let (K,o,S) be a presentation at an admitted carrier type \kappa, with K fixed, S rigid, and \Box\operatorname{Peano}(K,o,S). Peano here includes support of S on K, a unique successor for each member, zero not a successor, injectivity of successor, and induction for every admitted predicate. Fix \sigma and assume \Box I^*_\sigma.

Define J^{\kappa\nu_\sigma t} to be the least relation containing (o,0_\sigma) and closed under simultaneous successors. Explicitly, J(a,b) says that every admitted R^{\kappa\nu_\sigma t} satisfying

R(o,0_\sigma)\ \land\ \forall x,x',y\,[R(x,y)\land S(x,x') \to R(x',\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma y)]

also satisfies R(a,b). Comprehension admits this relation and its boxed-instance predicate. Leastness, base membership and successor closure follow directly by universal instantiation. In particular, base membership is a universally closed theorem of the definition; its necessitation uses the same parameter o, without assuming a separate rigidity predicate for an individual.

The predicate \lambda a,b\,\Box J(a,b) contains the base pair. If \Box J(a,b) and S(a,a'), persistence of S gives \Box S(a,a'), and the necessary closure theorem for J gives \Box J(a',\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma b). Thus this predicate is closed under simultaneous successors. Leastness gives J(a,b)\to\Box J(a,b). This proves weak persistence from the persistence of S. Necessitate that closed conditional theorem and use the necessary persistence of S: J is persistent in the map’s full, boxed sense.

The ordinary second-order Peano matching argument gives the following facts. The product K\times N_\sigma is closed under the matching operator, so J is supported on it. Induction on K gives an image for each of its members, and induction on N_\sigma gives a preimage for each of its members. For uniqueness, the base pair cannot be confused with a successor pair; in the successor step, injectivity on both sides reduces a possible pair of matches to the preceding matches. Induction therefore gives uniqueness on both sides. This is the usual second-order proof that the least simultaneous-successor matching is a bijection. It uses the admitted matching and comparison predicates, so it applies to these Henkin Peano structures without an external standardness assumption. The proof is a closed theorem with the two Peano conditions as hypotheses. Since both hypotheses are necessary, necessitation and K make support, totality, functionality and bijectivity necessary as well.

Here is the needed functional-graph step, including inextensibility. Suppose D is rigid, L persistent and necessarily a total functional relation with domain D. Assume a test relation Q satisfies \forall a,b(L(a,b)\to\Box Q(a,b)). For a current a\in D, take its unique current L-image b. Persistence gives \Box L(a,b), the test hypothesis gives \Box Q(a,b), and necessary functionality yields

\Box\forall b'\,[L(a,b')\to b'=b], \qquad \Box\forall b'\,[L(a,b')\to Q(a,b')].

Apply inextensibility of D to T(a):=\forall b(L(a,b)\to Q(a,b)). With necessary support, this yields \Box\forall a,b(L(a,b)\to Q(a,b)). It proves weak inextensibility of L. Rigidity of D, persistence of L, and the boxed functional-domain condition imply their own necessity by modal 4. Necessitating the proved conditional therefore gives full inextensibility. Hence L is rigid. The argument chooses only an individual existential witness for a fixed a, not a choice function.

Apply this step to D=K and L=J. Its range B(b):=\exists a\,J(a,b) is rigid: persistence follows from a witness; for inextensibility pull any test Q(b) back to the relation \lambda a,b\,Q(b) and apply inextensibility of J. Necessary surjectivity gives \Box\forall b(B(b)\leftrightarrow N_\sigma b). In C this is B\le N_\sigma and N_\sigma\le B, so antisymmetry gives literal identity B=N_\sigma. Rigidity of N_\sigma now follows; it was not assumed in the identification.

For completeness, necessary distinctness of the canonical numbers follows already from \Box I^*_\sigma. Canonical numberhood is persistent: the predicate \lambda n\,\Box N_\sigma n contains zero and is successor-closed by the necessary least-inductive laws, so leastness gives its weak persistence and necessitation gives the full version. Induct on n\in N_\sigma to prove \forall m\in N_\sigma(n\ne m\to\Box(n\ne m)). Every number is zero or a successor. Necessary non-successorhood handles the zero case; in the successor case necessary injectivity and the induction hypothesis separate the predecessors. Necessary identity preserves each chosen predecessor equation, and persistence of numberhood keeps its parameters within the necessary Peano axioms. Thus the required necessary distinctness holds with no global ND axiom.

Finally, a rigid necessarily-real tuple supplies this reference. Its number predicate K is rigid by RigidStr and inherits ND from the fixed real carrier. Its successor is the supported addition-by-one graph on K; persistence follows from the rigid field operation and number predicate. The boxed Real condition makes this graph necessarily total and functional, so the functional-graph step makes it rigid. The ordinary real-field theorem gives Peano’s axioms for the least inductive number subproperty; necessitating that theorem and using boxed Real gives \Box\operatorname{Peano}(K,o,S). This proves the fixed-reference lemma from precisely the stated hypotheses, without a represented-power or Transversal assumption.

5. The map edge

By T, current I^*_\sigma holds; the Transversal instance in (1) gives a current real structure at \rho_\sigma.
