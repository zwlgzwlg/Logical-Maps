Definition

Let T be C with Extensionality, Well-Ordering at every type and I^* (zero is not a successor and successor is injective on the finite cardinalities of individuals). T holds in the full standard model on \mathbb N, by choice in the metatheory, so T+\operatorname{Con}(T) is consistent, and by Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem so is T+\neg\operatorname{Con}(T). Take one-world Henkin models M_1 of T+\operatorname{Con}(T) and M_2 of T+\neg\operatorname{Con}(T), as action models with one object and only its identity arrow, and coalesce them at a new root as in Definition E.2. The root’s individuals are the pairs of an individual of M_1 and one of M_2. Evaluation point: the root. Interpretation of Σ, fixed for this record: every constant of Σ is relational, and each denotes the top element of its type.

The root has one distinguished arrow to the root of each component, and every arrow from the root into a component factors through it.

Root domains are as full as compatibility allows: a root proposition is a choice, for each component, of a proposition of that component’s root domain, together with a truth value at the root’s identity arrow, and a root entity of a relational type is likewise a family of component entities with a root component that is an arbitrary function of the root arguments.

A member of the group Coalesced sums at a new root, which supplies this definition with the record’s settings, and the arguments marked as shared.

Arguments

Fails Necessity of Arithmetic.

At type e. The root sees three worlds: itself and the two component worlds, each of which sees only itself. I^* holds in both components, so I holds at both component worlds and at the root. The arithmetical sentence A:=\operatorname{Con}(T) holds at the world of M_1 and fails at that of M_2, so neither \Box(I\to A) nor \Box(I\to\neg A) holds at the root.

Holds □Strong Atomicity, □Strong Atomicity (type t), □BF.

At every relational type the root domain contains, for each world and each tuple there, the entity true of that tuple at that world and of nothing else: the root component is free, and each component contains the singleton of each of its tuples, definable from them. Such an entity lies below every entity or below its negation, and necessarily so, since its transport to a component world is that singleton or \bot; so it is a strong atom, and every nonzero entity has one below it. In the one-world components the same holds trivially. For BF, the root individuals are pairs, so each component individual is the image of a root individual, and likewise at every type, root entities being families of component entities; the components, with one world each, satisfy BF.

Fails B for pure sentences.

The closed pure sentence \exists p\, .\,p\land\Diamond\neg p is true at the root, where the proposition true only at the root is contingent, and false at each component world, which sees only itself; so it is not possible there, and B for it fails at the root.

Holds Infinity Schema (type e), Possible Infinity (type e). Fails Axiom of Infinity (type t), Infinity Schema (type t), Possible Infinity (type t).

Each component is one-world and extensional, so it has exactly two propositions, and the root, whose propositions are a truth value at the root with one of each component’s, has eight; so there are finitely many propositions at every world. The root individuals are the pairs from two infinite domains, with literal identity, so any number of them are distinct. T proves the Axiom of Infinity at type e: if a finite cardinality n held of the universal property, \operatorname{Suc}n and \operatorname{Suc}\operatorname{Suc}n would both be the empty cardinality, by Extensionality, while n\ne\operatorname{Suc}n, against I^*; so it holds at the component worlds and is possible at the root.

Holds □Truths are ND-necessary, Tame Rigidity, □Tame Rigidity, □Intensional Choice.

At a one-world component \Box is idle. Every truth is ND-necessary there, with \top as witness; every relation is weakly persistent and weakly inextensible, so Tame Rigidity holds there trivially; and Intensional Choice holds, the singleton of a chosen instance being definable. Fails Rigid Power, □Rigid Power, Weakly Rigid Power, □Weakly Rigid Power.

Throughout, M_1 and M_2 are the two one-world extensional Henkin components, with M_1\models\operatorname{Con}(T) and M_2\models\neg\operatorname{Con}(T), and D^\sigma_r, D^\sigma_1, D^\sigma_2 are the domains of type \sigma at the root and at the components. From the root three worlds are accessible, the root itself and the two components; from a component only itself. Since M_i is extensional and one-world, an entity of M_i of type \bar\sigma t is identified with its extension, a set of \bar\sigma-tuples of M_i, and a set S of \bar\sigma-tuples of M_i is called internal in M_i when S\in D^{\bar\sigma t}_i, that is, when it is the extension of an entity of M_i; every set definable in M_i with parameters is internal in M_i, by comprehension. The word always refers to one of the two components, named each time; at the root one speaks only of root entities and their root extensions. A root individual is a pair (a_1,a_2) with a_i\in D^e_i, transported to a_i along the distinguished arrow d_i. A root entity of a relational type \bar\sigma t is a triple Y=(Y_o,Y_1,Y_2): Y_o, its root extension, is an arbitrary set of root \bar\sigma-tuples, and Y_i\in D^{\bar\sigma t}_i, its M_i-component, is its transport along d_i; the root domains being fullest, every such triple is in D^{\bar\sigma t}_r. For a root tuple \bar x let \pi_i\bar x be the tuple of M_i-components of its entries, and for a set S of root tuples let \pi_i[S]:=\{\pi_i\bar x:\bar x\in S\}, a set of \bar\sigma-tuples of M_i that need not be internal in M_i. For any n\in D^\sigma_1 and m\in D^\sigma_2 some root entity x of type \sigma has \pi_1x=n and \pi_2x=m: the pair (n,m) when \sigma=e, the triple (\emptyset,n,m) otherwise. Six facts about the semantics are used. (a) At the root, \Box\varphi holds iff \varphi holds at the root, at M_1 and at M_2, root parameters replaced by their components. (b) Quantifiers at the root range over the root domains, at M_i over those of M_i. (c) At the root Y\bar x holds iff \bar x\in Y_o; at M_i, after transport, iff \pi_i\bar x\in Y_i. (d) So Y\le Z, that is \Box\forall\bar x\, .\,Y\bar x\to Z\bar x, holds at the root iff Y_o\subseteq Z_o, Y_1\subseteq Z_1 and Y_2\subseteq Z_2. (e) Transport commutes with denotation: the M_i-component of the root entity denoted by \lambda X\, .\,\varphi(X,\bar F) with root parameters \bar F is the entity of M_i denoted by \lambda X\, .\,\varphi(X,\bar F_i). (f) In the one-world model M_i the box is idle, so every entity Y of M_i is weakly persistent (Y\bar x\to\Box Y\bar x reduces to Y\bar x\to Y\bar x) and weakly inextensible (\forall X\, .\,(\forall\bar x\, .\,Y\bar x\to\Box X\bar x)\to Y\le X reduces to \forall X\, .\,Y\subseteq X\to Y\subseteq X); hence every entity of M_i is weakly rigid and, the box being idle, rigid. Rigidity at the root. \operatorname{Rigid}(Y) is \Box\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(Y); at the root, by (a), it says that Y is weakly rigid at the root and that Y_i is weakly rigid in M_i for each i, and the latter holds by (f). So Y is rigid at the root iff it is weakly persistent and weakly inextensible there. Weak persistence at the root, \forall\bar x\, .\,Y\bar x\to\Box Y\bar x, asks of each root tuple \bar x\in Y_o that Y\bar x hold at the root (trivial), at M_1 (\pi_1\bar x\in Y_1) and at M_2 (\pi_2\bar x\in Y_2); so it holds iff \pi_1[Y_o]\subseteq Y_1 and \pi_2[Y_o]\subseteq Y_2. Weak inextensibility at the root, \forall X\, .\,(\forall\bar x\, .\,Y\bar x\to\Box X\bar x)\to Y\le X with X=(X_o,X_1,X_2) ranging over root entities, has the hypothesis Y_o\subseteq X_o, \pi_1[Y_o]\subseteq X_1, \pi_2[Y_o]\subseteq X_2 and, by (d), the conclusion Y_o\subseteq X_o, Y_1\subseteq X_1, Y_2\subseteq X_2. The three parts of X being independent, this is a separate condition on each component: given X_1\in D^{\bar\sigma t}_1 containing \pi_1[Y_o], the root entity (Y_o,X_1,\top_2) satisfies the hypothesis, so Y_1\subseteq X_1 is required, and likewise for M_2; conversely the two component conditions give the conclusion for every X satisfying the hypothesis. So weak inextensibility at the root holds iff for i=1,2, Y_i\subseteq X_i for every X_i internal in M_i with X_i\supseteq\pi_i[Y_o]. Together: Y is rigid at the root iff for each i, Y_i is a set internal in M_i that contains \pi_i[Y_o] and is contained in every set internal in M_i that contains \pi_i[Y_o]; that is, Y_i is the least internal superset of \pi_i[Y_o] in M_i. Lemma. Let M be a one-world extensional model and S any set of \bar\sigma-tuples of M, internal or not; call an X internal in M a least internal superset of S if S\subseteq X and X\subseteq X' for every X' internal in M with S\subseteq X'. If S has a least internal superset X, then X=S; so S has a least internal superset iff S is itself internal in M, and then that superset is S. Proof: suppose X is a least internal superset of S but X\not\subseteq S, and pick \bar a\in X\setminus S. The haecceity \lambda\bar y\, .\,\bar y=\bar a is internal in M by comprehension with the parameter \bar a, hence so is X':=X\land\neg(\lambda\bar y\, .\,\bar y=\bar a), which as a set is X\setminus\{\bar a\}. As \bar a\notin S, still S\subseteq X'; by leastness X\subseteq X', so \bar a\in X', which is absurd. So X=S, and S is internal. Conversely an internal S is its own least internal superset. The point is that an internal set strictly above S is never the least one above S: it can be shrunk by a point while staying above S. Applying the lemma with S=\pi_i[Y_o] gives the criterion (Rig): a root entity Y=(Y_o,Y_1,Y_2) of type \bar\sigma t is rigid at the root iff for each i=1,2, (1) \pi_i[Y_o] is internal in M_i and (2) Y_i=\pi_i[Y_o]. A rigid root entity is thus determined by its root extension, and a set S of root tuples is the root extension of a rigid root entity iff \pi_1[S] is internal in M_1 and \pi_2[S] is internal in M_2, the rigid entity then being (S,\pi_1[S],\pi_2[S]). The power property. Rigid Power at type \bar\sigma t is \forall F\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(F)\to\operatorname{Rigid}(P_F) with P_F:=\lambda X^{\bar\sigma t}\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F, a root entity of type (\bar\sigma t)t; at the root, by (b), it says that P_F is rigid at the root for every root entity F that is rigid at the root. For such an F, the root extension (P_F)_o is the set of root entities X that are rigid at the root with X\le F at the root; by (Rig) such an X is (E,\pi_1[E],\pi_2[E]) for a set E of root tuples with both projections internal, and by (d) X\le F says E\subseteq F_o together with \pi_i[E]\subseteq F_i, which is automatic since F_i=\pi_i[F_o]; so (P_F)_o consists of the (E,\pi_1[E],\pi_2[E]) with E\subseteq F_o, \pi_1[E] internal in M_1 and \pi_2[E] internal in M_2. The M_i-component (P_F)_i is, by (e), the entity of M_i denoted by \lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F_i, and by (f) every entity of M_i is rigid, so (P_F)_i=\mathcal P_i(F_i):=\{X_i\in D^{\bar\sigma t}_i: X_i\subseteq F_i\}, the internal power set of F_i, itself internal in M_i as the extension of \lambda X\, .\,X\le F_i. Now apply (Rig) to P_F at type (\bar\sigma t)t. The projection \pi_i[(P_F)_o] is the set of the \pi_i[E] for E\subseteq F_o with both projections internal, the set of M_i-components of rigid subrelations of F; each member is an internal subset of \pi_i[F_o]=F_i, so \pi_i[(P_F)_o]\subseteq\mathcal P_i(F_i)=(P_F)_i always. Hence the criterion (RP): for a root entity F rigid at the root, P_F is rigid at the root iff for each i=1,2, (1) \pi_i[(P_F)_o] is internal in M_i and (2) (P_F)_i=\pi_i[(P_F)_o], which by the inclusion just noted says that every A\subseteq F_i internal in M_i is \pi_i[E] for some E\subseteq F_o whose other projection \pi_j[E] is internal in M_j. Clause (2) implies clause (1), (P_F)_i being internal, so (RP) comes down to clause (2): Rigid Power holds at the root iff for every rigid F and each component, every internal subrelation of F’s component there is the component of a rigid subrelation of F. To refute it one exhibits a rigid F, an i and an A\subseteq F_i internal in M_i that is \pi_i[E] for no E\subseteq F_o with \pi_j[E] internal in M_j. The witness, at type \nu t with \nu:=(et)t, the type of finite cardinalities of individuals. Let N_i\subseteq D^\nu_i be the set of finite cardinalities of M_i, the extension in M_i of \lambda n\, .\,\forall\mathcal C\, .\,\mathcal C0\land\forall m\,(\mathcal Cm\to\mathcal C(\operatorname{Suc}m))\to\mathcal Cn with \mathcal C ranging over D^{\nu t}_i; it is internal in M_i. Write \bar k^{M_i}\in D^\nu_i for the denotation of \operatorname{Suc}^k0 in M_i and \operatorname{Std}_i:=\{\bar k^{M_i}:k\in\mathbb N\} for the standard numbers of M_i; \operatorname{Std}_i\subseteq N_i, each \bar k^{M_i} lying in every inductive class, and the \bar k^{M_i} are pairwise distinct by I^*. Claim 1: \operatorname{Std}_2 is not internal in M_2. Since M_2\models\neg\operatorname{Con}(T) there is n_0\in N_2 that M_2 takes to code a T-proof of \bot; n_0 is not standard, since T decides every bounded arithmetical statement about standard numerals, so that if n_0 were \bar k^{M_2} then k would really code a T-proof of \bot, and there is none, T having a model. If \operatorname{Std}_2 were internal in M_2 it would be an inductive class in the sense of M_2, containing \bar 0^{M_2} and closed under \operatorname{Suc}, and so would contain n_0 by the definition of N_2; it does not. Define \psi:N_1\to\operatorname{Std}_2 by \psi(\bar k^{M_1}):=\bar k^{M_2} for k\in\mathbb N and \psi(n):=\bar 0^{M_2} for n\in N_1\setminus\operatorname{Std}_1 (the second clause matters only if M_1 has nonstandard numbers; M_1 might have been taken to be the full standard model on \mathbb N, which satisfies T+\operatorname{Con}(T), and then N_1=\operatorname{Std}_1); \psi is onto \operatorname{Std}_2 and uses no choice. Let F:=(F_o,\top_1,\top_2) be the root entity of type \nu t whose M_i-components are the universal properties of M_i, with extensions D^\nu_i, and whose root extension F_o is the set of x\in D^\nu_r such that either x_1\in N_1 and x_2=\psi(x_1), or x_1\notin N_1, where x_1 and x_2 are the M_1- and M_2-components of x: either the M_1-component is a finite cardinality of M_1 and the M_2-component is the standard number of M_2 that \psi assigns to it, or the M_1-component is not a finite cardinality and the M_2-component is anything. Claim 2: F is rigid at the root. By (Rig) it suffices that \pi_1[F_o]=D^\nu_1 and \pi_2[F_o]=D^\nu_2, these being internal as the extensions of \top_1 and \top_2. For the first, let n\in D^\nu_1: if n\in N_1, the root entity x:=(\emptyset,n,\psi(n)), with empty root extension, is in D^\nu_r (fullest domains) and in the first part of F_o, with x_1=n; if n\notin N_1, x:=(\emptyset,n,m) for any m\in D^\nu_2 is in the second part. For the second, let m\in D^\nu_2: the second part of F_o supplies x:=(\emptyset,n,m) provided M_1 has some n\notin N_1, an entity of type \nu that is not a finite cardinality. It does: \top_\nu, the property holding of every property of individuals. For 0 holds only of the empty property, while \operatorname{Suc}m holds only of nonempty properties, holding of A iff some x with Ax has m(\lambda y\, .\,Ay\land y\ne x); so the class of those n that do not hold both of the empty property and of some nonempty property contains 0, is closed under \operatorname{Suc} and is internal in M_1, hence contains every member of N_1; and \top_\nu holds of the empty property and of any singleton. Claim 3: P_F is not rigid at the root. Take i=1 and A:=N_1, internal in M_1 and a subset of F_1=D^\nu_1, so that A\in\mathcal P_1(F_1)=(P_F)_1. So N_1 lies in (P_F)_1 but not in \pi_1[(P_F)_o], clause (2) of (RP) fails for F at i=1, and P_F is not rigid at the root. Unwound, the failing instance of inextensibility is this: let H:=(\top,H_1,\top_2) be the root entity of type (\nu t)t with H_1:=\lambda X\, .\,\neg(X=N_1), internal in M_1. The obstacle in one line. The set S of root entities x of type \nu with x_1=\bar k^{M_1} and x_2=\bar k^{M_2} for some k\in\mathbb N, the entities that are the same standard number in both components, is the root extension of plenty of root entities of type \nu t, namely of (S,Y_1,Y_2) for any Y_1\in D^{\nu t}_1 and Y_2\in D^{\nu t}_2, the root domains being fullest; but by (Rig) none of them is rigid, since \pi_2[S]=\operatorname{Std}_2 is not internal in M_2. The witness F is this non-rigid relation padded until it is rigid, on the number side so that \pi_1 sees every finite cardinality of M_1 and on the non-number side so that \pi_2 sees all of D^\nu_2, and N_1 is the internal subset of F_1 that could only come from the unpadded part. For the one direction, fix a tuple \bar x_0 of M_i and a surjection \psi:D^{\bar\sigma}_i\setminus\{\bar x_0\}\to B, let F_o be the set of root tuples \bar x with either \pi_i\bar x\ne\bar x_0 and \pi_j\bar x=\psi(\pi_i\bar x), or \pi_i\bar x=\bar x_0, and let F:=(F_o,\top,\top); then \pi_i[F_o]=D^{\bar\sigma}_i and \pi_j[F_o]=D^{\bar\sigma}_j, the tuples with \pi_i\bar x=\bar x_0 pairing \bar x_0 with every tuple of M_j, so F is rigid by (Rig); and A:=D^{\bar\sigma}_i\setminus\{\bar x_0\}, internal in M_i as \top minus a haecceity, is \pi_i[E] only for E\subseteq F_o avoiding \bar x_0, whose \pi_j[E]=\psi[A]=B is not internal in M_j. For the other direction, which uses choice, suppose clause (2) of (RP) fails for some rigid F, i and A\subseteq F_i; choose for each a\in A one \bar x^a\in F_o with \pi_i\bar x^a=a, possible as A\subseteq\pi_i[F_o], and let E be the set of these; then \pi_i[E]=A, so B:=\pi_j[E] is not internal in M_j, and |B|\le|A|\le|D^{\bar\sigma}_i|. Consequences: with countable Henkin components, which is what the Henkin construction gives, D^{et}_2 is countable while \mathcal P(D^e_2) is not, so some set B of individuals of M_2 is not internal in M_2 and |B|\le\aleph_0=|D^e_1|; Rigid Power fails already at type e\to t, and in general at every type at which either component is not full. With M_1 the full standard model on \mathbb N, Rigid Power at e\to t fails iff M_2 has a countable set of individuals not internal in M_2, as it does if M_2 is countable. Rigid Power could hold at e\to t only if every non-internal set of individuals of either component outnumbered the individuals of the other. Both components cannot be full at type et: if M_2 were full at et and at eet, an internal bijection between A and B would be true equinumerosity and the absence of an internal injection of A into a proper subset of itself true finiteness, so the class of those n that hold of something, hold only of finite properties and are closed under equinumerosity would be internal and inductive, every finite cardinality of M_2 would be the property of having exactly k members for a true k, and M_2 would satisfy \operatorname{Con}(T). A filling in with Rigid Power at e\to t would thus need M_2 full at et but not at eet, with uncountably many individuals and every countable set of them internal; whether T has such a model has not been checked. Whatever the filling in, the failure at ((et)t)t remains, using only the countable non-internal set \operatorname{Std}_2 and the infinitude of D^\nu_1.

Fails Arithmetic Necessitism.

At type e: inextensibility of \mathbb N_e fails at the root. The antecedent \Box I^*_e holds, I^* being an axiom of T, true in both components, and true at the root, where the numbers are the standard numerals, the root parts of entities being free, and the individuals are infinitely many. In M_2, which satisfies \neg\operatorname{Con}(T), the numbers at e, the instances of the least inductive property in the Henkin domain, include a nonstandard number c, one coding a proof of a contradiction from T, since no standard numeral does; so no property of M_2 has exactly the standard numerals as its extension, such a property being inductive. The property \lambda x\,.\,x<c of M_2, definable from c by the order on numbers, holds of every standard numeral and not of c. Let X be the root entity whose root part is the set of root numerals, whose M_1-part is the numbers of M_1 and whose M_2-part is \lambda x\,.\,x<c. Every number at the root necessarily has X: at the root it is a root numeral, and along the distinguished arrows it becomes the corresponding numeral of M_1 or of M_2, numerals being closed terms. But \mathbb N_e\le X fails, since at M_2 the number c lacks X. So \mathbb N_e is not inextensible and Arithmetic Necessitism fails, although the persistence of \mathbb N_e and the necessary distinctness of the numbers hold, as in every model of C with \Box I^*_e. This is the failure the principle’s notes anticipate: at M_2 no property has exactly the numerals as its extension, so the least inductive property gains members there.

Fails Necessity of Analysis.

At the type \sigma:=((et)t)t of properties of the numbers at e. In each component, a model of T and so of C with I^*_e, there is a real number structure of type \sigma: the Dedekind cuts over the numbers at e, with the rationals coded as numbers, form a complete ordered field, completeness holding by comprehension since the union of a bounded family of cuts is a cut, and its least inductive subproperty is the image of the numbers, so its arithmetic is the component’s. Let A be the first-order analytical sentence \exists n\in N\,.\,\operatorname{Proof}_T(n,\bot), written with N, + and \times. In M_2, which satisfies \neg\operatorname{Con}(T), every real number structure makes A true, and in M_1, which satisfies \operatorname{Con}(T), every one makes it false, the real number structures at a world all being isomorphic there. The root reaches both components, so neither \Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to A(\mathfrak R) nor \Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to\neg A(\mathfrak R) holds at the root: the Necessity of Analysis fails, and with it the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis and Analytic Necessitism, which imply it.

Fails Self-atoms are atoms (type t), Self-atoms are strong atoms (type t).
