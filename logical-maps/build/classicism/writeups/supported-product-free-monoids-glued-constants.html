Supported-product action model: κ⁺ free-monoid coordinates, glued constants

Construction and verification by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 24 October 2026.

At its evaluation point this model satisfies Logical Necessity, □BF, □Relational Choice, □Countable Boolean Completeness and Atomlessness, and fails Actuality, Vicinity, Weakly Inextensible Comprehension (at tt) and Rigid Power (at (tt)t). So Logical Necessity, even with □BF and □Relational Choice, gives none of the comprehension or actuality principles, and Countable Boolean Completeness does not give Boolean Completeness. §9 adds the choice principles in their boxed and modalized forms, Truths are ND-necessary, □Tame Rigidity, Arithmetic and Second-Order Analytic Necessitism, and the failure of Forcing Possibilism.

The model

Fix an infinite cardinal \kappa\ge|\Sigma|. Let I be a set of size \kappa^+, and L the free monoid of finite words on an alphabet of size \kappa, with empty word \varepsilon. The arrows are the elements of

M:=L^I,\qquad (mh)_i:=m_ih_i\ \ (\text{concatenation}),\qquad 1:=(\varepsilon)_{i\in I},

forming a one-object category. For m,m'\in M and S\subseteq I write m\equiv_Sm' for m|_S=m'|_S.

Domains. D_e:=\{*\}. For a relational type \tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_nt, D_\tau is the set of profiles F:M\to\mathcal P(D_{\sigma_1}\times\cdots\times D_{\sigma_n}) that have a support: a set S\subseteq I with |S|\le\kappa such that

m\equiv_Sm'\ \Rightarrow\ F_m=F_{m'} .

For n=0 a profile is a subset of M, a proposition, true at m iff m\in p.

Transport. (h\cdot F)_k:=F_{kh}, and h\cdot *=*. Then (g\cdot(h\cdot F))_k=F_{kgh}, so g\cdot(h\cdot F)=(gh)\cdot F, and h\cdot F has the support of F.

Interpretation. The usual one-object action-model semantics (Classicism, §§3.4–3.5): a formula with parameters \bar a holds at m iff it holds at 1 with parameters m\cdot\bar a, quantifiers range over the domains, and

(\lambda\bar x\, .\,\varphi(\bar x;\bar a))_m:=\{\bar b:\ \varphi(\bar b;m\cdot\bar a)\text{ holds at }1\}.

Σ. Each individual constant denotes *; the relational constants are interpreted by gluing (§7).

Evaluation point: 1.

1. A model of C

Lemma 1. If F has support S and m\equiv_Sm', then m\cdot F=m'\cdot F.

Proof. (m\cdot F)_k=F_{km} and (km)_i=k_im_i=k_im'_i=(km')_i for i\in S. \square

So the profile of \lambda\bar x\, .\,\varphi(\bar x;\bar a) at m depends only on m\cdot\bar a, hence only on m restricted to the union of the parameters’ supports, a set of size \le\kappa: the domains are closed under the denotations of terms. Each h\cdot F is again supported, so transport maps domains to domains. An H-valid equivalence holds at every arrow under every assignment, so the corresponding abstractions have equal profiles: Logical Equivalence holds, and this is a model of C.

As there is one object, a closed pure sentence has the same truth value at every arrow; in particular every such sentence true at 1 is necessary there.

2. Transport is surjective; □BF

Lemma 2. For every h\in M and F\in D_\tau there is G\in D_\tau with h\cdot G=F.

Proof. Let S support F. For m\in M: if for every i\in S the word m_i ends with h_i, say m_i=w_ih_i, put r(m)_i:=w_i for i\in S and \varepsilon elsewhere, and G_m:=F_{r(m)}; otherwise G_m:=\varnothing. Then G has support S, and for every k, r(kh)\equiv_Sk, so

(h\cdot G)_k=G_{kh}=F_{r(kh)}=F_k .\qquad\square

BF. Suppose \forall a\, .\,\Box Ra at 1, that is, k\cdot a\in R_k for all a and k. At an arrow h, each b is h\cdot a for some a by Lemma 2, so b\in R_h. Hence \Box\forall b\, .\,Rb. This holds at every type, and BF is a closed pure schema, so □BF holds.

3. Atomlessness; Actuality and Vicinity fail

For i\in I let

q_i:=\{m:m_i=\varepsilon\},

a true proposition with support \{i\}. Since (km)_i=\varepsilon iff k_i=m_i=\varepsilon,

h\cdot q_i=\begin{cases}q_i&h_i=\varepsilon,\\ \bot&h_i\ne\varepsilon,\end{cases} \qquad\qquad \Diamond q_i=q_i . \tag{1}

The second holds as \Diamond q_i is true at m iff k_im_i=\varepsilon for some k, iff m_i=\varepsilon.

Atomlessness. Let p\ne\bot have support S, and take j\notin S. Changing the jth coordinate of a member of p to \varepsilon, or to a nonempty word, gives members of p in q_j and outside it. So \bot\ne p\land q_j<p.

Actuality and Vicinity fail. Let p be true, with support S; take j\notin S and h with a one-letter word at j and \varepsilon elsewhere. Then h\in p (as h\equiv_S1), while h\notin q_j=\Diamond q_j. So p\not\le\Diamond q_j, although q_j is true. No truth entails the possibility of every truth, and a fortiori none entails every truth.

4. Weakly Inextensible Comprehension fails at tt

Let X\in D_{tt} be the constant profile X_m:=\{q_i:i\in I\}, with support \varnothing. Suppose Y\in D_{tt} is coextensive with X, so Y_1=\{q_i:i\in I\}, and let S support Y. Take j\notin S and h with a one-letter word at j and \varepsilon elsewhere; then Y_h=Y_1\ni q_j. Let Z\in D_{tt}, with support \{j\}, be

Z_m:=\begin{cases}D_t&m_j=\varepsilon,\\ D_t\setminus\{q_j\}&m_j\ne\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Every instance q_i of Y satisfies \Box Zq_i: by (1), m\cdot q_i is q_i or \bot, and if m_j\ne\varepsilon neither is q_j (for i=j it is \bot). So

\forall p\, .\,Yp\to\Box Zp,

but q_j\in Y_h\setminus Z_h, so Y\not\le Z, and Y is not weakly inextensible. Boolean Completeness, which implies Weakly Inextensible Comprehension, therefore fails too.

5. □Relational Choice

Let U be serial at 1. By choice in the metatheory pick, for each x in the input domain, a y_x with \langle x,y_x\rangle\in U_1, and let S be the constant profile S_m:=\{\langle x,y_x\rangle\}, with support \varnothing. It is functional and included in U at 1. Relational Choice is a closed pure schema, so □Relational Choice holds.

6. □Countable Boolean Completeness

The order on profiles is pointwise inclusion at every arrow, and the pointwise union of at most \kappa supported profiles is supported (by the union of the supports), and is their least upper bound.

At a type \sigma, the actual instances of \operatorname{FiniteCardinality}_\sigma are exactly the \operatorname{Suc}^n_\sigma(\mathbf 0_\sigma), n\in\mathbb N: each is in every inductive property, and the constant profile with exactly these instances is an inductive property. So if \operatorname{Ctbl}_{\sigma,\tau}(A) holds at 1, the actual instances of A inject into \mathbb N, and their pointwise union is their least upper bound. This holds at every arrow, so □Countable Boolean Completeness holds.

7. Logical Necessity

Gluing lemma. Let (F_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa} (one for each letter) be entities of one relational type and j\in I outside all their supports. Let h_\alpha have the one-letter word \alpha at j and \varepsilon elsewhere, and put

G_m:=\begin{cases}(F_\alpha)_m&m_j\text{ ends in the letter }\alpha,\\ \varnothing&m_j=\varepsilon.\end{cases}

G has support \{j\}\cup\bigcup_\alpha\operatorname{supp}(F_\alpha), of size \le\kappa, and since (kh_\alpha)_j ends in \alpha and kh_\alpha\equiv_{I\setminus\{j\}}k,

(h_\alpha\cdot G)_k=G_{kh_\alpha}=(F_\alpha)_{kh_\alpha}=(F_\alpha)_k .

The interpretation of Σ. There are at most \kappa sentences of \mathcal L(\Sigma). For each sentence \varphi_\alpha true at 1 under some interpretation of Σ over these domains, choose one, J_\alpha. Pick j outside the supports of all the J_\alpha(c), at most \kappa\cdot\kappa\cdot\kappa coordinates, and interpret each relational constant c by the gluing G_c of (J_\alpha(c))_\alpha at j. Then h_\alpha\cdot G_c=J_\alpha(c) for every c simultaneously, so \varphi_\alpha holds at h_\alpha. Conversely, a sentence true at an arrow h is true at 1 under the interpretation c\mapsto h\cdot G_c. So

\Diamond\varphi\ \text{holds at }1\iff\varphi\text{ is true at }1\text{ under some interpretation of }\Sigma .

For P pure with free variables among \bar x, and distinct constants \bar c, P[\bar c/\bar x] is true under some interpretation iff \exists\bar x\, .\,P is true at 1. So \Diamond P[\bar c/\bar x]\leftrightarrow\exists\bar x\, .\,P, and, applied to \neg P,

\Box P[\bar c/\bar x]\leftrightarrow\forall\bar x\, .\,P ,

which is Logical Necessity at 1.

8. Rigid Power fails at (tt)t

For a set E of argument tuples write H_E for the profile m\mapsto m\cdot E, which need not be supported.

Lemma 3 (exact images). A supported Y with actual extension E=Y_1 is weakly rigid iff Y=H_E. In that case Y is rigid.

Proof. If Y=H_E: for \bar a\in E, k\cdot\bar a\in Y_k for every k, so Y is weakly persistent; and any Z with \Box Z\bar a for every \bar a\in E has m\cdot E\subseteq Z_m, so Y\le Z. Since (h\cdot Y)_k=k\cdot(h\cdot E), every transport of Y is again an exact image, so Y is rigid.

Conversely let Y be weakly rigid with support S. Weak persistence gives m\cdot E\subseteq Y_m. For T\subseteq I with |T|\le\kappa let

B_T(E)_k:=\bigcup\{n\cdot E:\ n\equiv_Tk\},

a profile with support T. Each \bar a\in E has \Box B_T(E)\bar a (take n=k), so weak inextensibility gives Y\le B_T(E). Now let \bar b\in Y_m, and let T contain S and the supports of the entries of \bar b. Then \bar b=n\cdot\bar a for some \bar a\in E and n\equiv_Tm. Define arrows r,u by

r_i=u_i:=\varepsilon\ (i\in T),\qquad r_i:=n_i,\ u_i:=m_i\ (i\notin T).

Then mr=un: on T both sides are m_i=n_i, off T both are m_in_i. As r\equiv_S1, Y_r=E, and weak persistence gives r\cdot\bar a\in E. As u is \varepsilon on the supports of the entries of \bar b, u\cdot\bar b=\bar b. So

\bar b=u\cdot(n\cdot\bar a)=(un)\cdot\bar a=(mr)\cdot\bar a=m\cdot(r\cdot\bar a)\in m\cdot E .\qquad\square

The counterexample. Fix o\in I and let J:=I\setminus\{o\}. For j\in J let y_j\in D_{tt} be the constant profile \{q_j\}; every arrow fixes it. Let \chi(n):=1 if n\ge0 is a perfect square and 0 otherwise, so that the sequences (\chi(n+z))_{n\ge0} for z\in\mathbb Z are pairwise distinct and each contains a 1. For z\in\mathbb Z and j\in J let e_{z,j}\in D_{tt} have support \{o,j\} and, with r:=|m_o| and n:=|m_j|,

(e_{z,j})_m:=\begin{cases} \{q_j\}\cup\{\bot:\chi(n+z)=1\}&r=0,\\ \{q_j\}\text{ if }n+z\ge0,\ \varnothing\text{ otherwise}&r=1,\\ \{q_j\}&r\ge2 . \end{cases}

These are pairwise distinct. Let a have a one-letter word at o, and b_j one at j, \varepsilon elsewhere. Directly from the definition:

b_j\cdot e_{z,j}=e_{z+1,j},\qquad b_j\cdot e_{z,k}=e_{z,k}\ (k\ne j),\qquad a\cdot e_{z,j}=y_j\iff z\ge0,\qquad a\cdot a\cdot e_{z,j}=y_j ,

and for z<0, a\cdot e_{z,j} has empty extension at 1, so is no y_k.

Let E:=\{e_{z,j}:z\in\mathbb Z,\ j\in J\} and B:=\{y_j:j\in J\}. An arrow m shifts z by |m_j| within the jth family, so m\cdot E depends only on |m_o|: it is E, a\cdot E or B according as |m_o| is 0, 1 or at least 2. So F:=H_E is supported (by \{o\}), and X:=H_B is the constant profile B. Both are rigid by Lemma 3, and X\le a\cdot F: at every m, (a\cdot F)_m=(ma)\cdot E is a\cdot E or B, and both contain B.

Suppose some rigid Y\le F had a\cdot Y=X. By Lemma 3, Y=H_A with A:=Y_1\subseteq E, and a\cdot A=B. Let S support Y and take j\in J\setminus S. As b_j\equiv_S1, b_j\cdot A=A, so A_j:=\{z:e_{z,j}\in A\} is invariant under z\mapsto z+1, hence empty or \mathbb Z. It is not empty, since y_j\in a\cdot A and the only e’s that a sends to y_j are the e_{z,j} with z\ge0. So e_{-1,j}\in A, and a\cdot e_{-1,j}\in B, a contradiction.

So \mathcal P:=\lambda Y\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\land Y\le F has X in its extension at a, while X is not a\cdot Y for any Y in its actual extension. By Lemma 3 applied to \mathcal P, it is not weakly rigid. Rigid Power fails for the rigid F, of type (tt)t.

9. Further verdicts

Added 25 October 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr. As there is one object, a closed pure principle holds at 1 iff it holds necessarily, so each verdict below holds in its boxed form too.

Constant profiles. For any set E of tuples, the constant profile m\mapsto E has support \varnothing and is fixed by every arrow.

Transversal Choice and Modal Well-Ordering. A transversal of an actual equivalence relation, chosen in the metatheory, is the extension of its constant profile. If W is a well-order of D_\sigma, chosen in the metatheory, its constant profile is W at every arrow, and every property’s extension at an arrow is a subset of D_\sigma, so

\Box\operatorname{WO}(W).

Intensional Choice. Let \Box\exists x\, .\,Fx, and let S support F. Then F_m\ne\varnothing for every m, and F_m depends only on m|_S; choose g(m)\in F_m depending only on m|_S. Then G_m:=\{g(m)\} has support S, and

G\le F,\qquad \Box\exists x\, .\,(Gx\land\forall y\, .\,Gy\to y=x).

Modalized Relational Choice. The same, choosing for each m|_S a functional graph inside R_m.

Truths are ND-necessary. Let p be true, with support S, and

q_S:=\{m:m_i=\varepsilon\text{ for all }i\in S\}.

q_S is true and \Diamond q_S=q_S, since (km)_i=\varepsilon iff k_i=m_i=\varepsilon. Each m\in q_S agrees with 1 on S, so m\in p. Hence q_S\land\Box(\Diamond q_S\to p), so \Box_{\ne}p.

Tame Rigidity. By Lemma 3, every weakly rigid relation is an exact image, and exact images are rigid.

Arithmetic Necessitism. \mathbb N_\sigma is a closed pure term, so its profile is constant, with the numerals as instances; they are closed pure terms, so fixed by every arrow. So \mathbb N_\sigma is the exact image of its extension, hence rigid, and distinct numerals are necessarily distinct.

Second-Order Analytic Necessitism. At a type \sigma:

  • If \sigma has a relational argument, there are continuum many distinct fixed entities of type \sigma (constant profiles). Put a real structure on them with constant relations, and take as represented power of arity k the constant profile of all constant k-ary relations on them. Every set of these entities, and of tuples of them, is the extension of a constant profile, so the structure is real, and the powers represent every relation, at every arrow.
  • If \sigma has only individual arguments (as t), use, for x\subseteq\mathbb Z, p_x:=\{m:\ \#_a(m_o)-\#_b(m_o)\in x\}, where a,b are two letters and o\in I is fixed. Then k\cdot p_x=p_{x-c} with c:=\#_a(k_o)-\#_b(k_o), so each arrow acts injectively on these propositions. Put a real structure on continuum many of them, and let \mathfrak R consist of the exact images of its carrier and relations, and the represented powers of the exact images of the sets of exact images H_D, D a set of k-tuples of the carrier. All are supported by \{o\}, hence rigid by Lemma 3; at each arrow k, k\cdot\mathfrak R is the image of the structure under the injection k\cdot{}, so a real structure, and any relation agrees on its carrier with some H_{k\cdot D}.

So Second-Order Analytic Necessitism, and with it Analytic Necessitism, holds.

Forcing Possibilism fails, as in every atomless model (Atomlessness is incompatible with it). Directly: four distinct constant profiles of type tt with the constant order of the four-element Boolean algebra form a complete, Dedekind-finite Boolean algebra. Forcing Possibilism would give a proposition with exactly four propositions below it; but a nonzero proposition has infinitely many below it (Atomlessness), and \bot one.

Boolean Completeness at t fails. Lower bounds of a property X of propositions are the supported subsets of \bigcap X (its actual instances), so a greatest lower bound exists iff the union U of all supported subsets of \bigcap X is itself supported, and is then U. Fix C\subseteq I with |C|=\kappa, a set P of \kappa^+ maps x:C\to L (there are \kappa^\kappa>\kappa), and distinct j_x\in I\setminus C, and put

p_x:=\{n:\ n|_C\ne x\}\cup\{n:\ n_{j_x}=\varepsilon\},

with support C\cup\{j_x\}. Let X be the constant profile \{p_x:x\in P\}. For m and T\supseteq C, the class [m]_T:=\{n:n|_T=m|_T\} lies in every p_x iff either m|_C\notin P, or m|_C=x\in P, j_x\in T and m_{j_x}=\varepsilon; and if m|_C=x\in P with m_{j_x}\ne\varepsilon no class of m lies in p_x. So

U=\{m:\ m|_C\notin P\}\cup\{m:\ m|_C=x\in P,\ m_{j_x}=\varepsilon\}.

Given S of size \le\kappa, take x\in P with j_x\notin S, m with m|_C=x and m_{j_x}=\varepsilon, and m' differing from m only at j_x: they agree on S, and m\in U, m'\notin U. So U has no support, and X has no greatest lower bound.

Rigid Rigidity. Lifting lemma: for every rigid Y and arrow k there is a rigid X with k\cdot X=Y. By Lemma 3, Y=H_D for D:=Y_1; let S support Y. For an entity q let

L(q)_n:=\begin{cases}q_{n^\flat}&\text{if }n_i\text{ ends with }k_i\text{ for every }i\in S,\\ \varnothing&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where n^\flat strips the suffix k_i from n_i for i\in S; L(q) has support S\cup\operatorname{supp}(q), and k\cdot L(q)=k''\cdot q, where k'' is k off S and \varepsilon on S. Write m=m_S\,m_{\bar S} for the factorization into the parts on and off S. Unwinding the definition, m\cdot L(q)=\Phi_{m|_S}(m_{\bar S}\cdot q) for an operation \Phi_{m|_S} depending only on m|_S. As Y has support S, m_{\bar S}\cdot D=D, so for C:=\{L(\bar q):\bar q\in D\} (applied to each entry)

m\cdot C=\Phi_{m|_S}[D],\qquad k\cdot C=k''\cdot D=D .

So H_C has support S, is admitted, rigid by Lemma 3, and k\cdot H_C=H_D=Y.

Now let R be \lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X), whose profile is constant, with the rigid relations as instances. It is weakly persistent, transports of rigid relations being rigid. If Z necessarily holds of every rigid X, then k\cdot X\in Z_k for all rigid X and arrows k; by the lifting lemma every rigid relation is such a k\cdot X, so R\le Z. So R is weakly rigid, and since the principle is closed and pure, □Rigid Rigidity holds. With □Tame Rigidity, Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity and its boxed form follow. The lemma does not conflict with §8: Rigid Power asks for a lift below a given rigid F.

Remarks

  1. Tame Rigidity. Lemma 3 also shows that every weakly rigid relation is rigid, at every arrow, so □Tame Rigidity holds (§9).
  2. Choice. §§5 and 7, and the choice principles of §9, use choice in the metatheory; the other arguments do not.
  3. Infinitely many individuals. Astra also gives a variant in which the arrows carry an extra coordinate, a surjection of a set of size \kappa^+ acting on the individuals. It adds the Axioms of Infinity at e and t and keeps these verdicts, except the principle Truths are ND-necessary and, at type e, Analytic Necessitism, which fail there. It is recorded separately, with surjections of the individuals in its name.
  4. Lean. Astra’s Lean development certifies, for one predicate constant, that Logical Necessity, □BF and □Relational Choice are consistent and imply neither Actuality nor Vicinity, using this construction. It is not yet connected to this record.
