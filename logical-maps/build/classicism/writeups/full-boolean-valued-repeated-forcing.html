Full Boolean-valued models after repeated forcing

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance. Arguments dated 29 October 2026; recorded here on 30 October 2026. The Boolean hierarchy, its forcing interpretation and the earlier real-field obstruction are credited in Section 7. No independent human check or Lean proof is recorded.

1. Two models and their scope

Relative to the consistency of ZFC, the following joint packages have models of C:

Forcing used twice Common positive package Additional verdicts
Cohen forcing on \omega C5, necessary BF, necessary Relational Choice, Necessity of Second-Order Analysis Analytic Necessitism fails
Countable partial binary functions on \omega_1 The same common package Analytic Necessitism holds; Second-Order Analytic Necessitism fails

All positive analytical schemata have their full native carrier scope. The failures occur at actual schema instances: AN at carrier et in the first model, and SOAN at carrier et and arity one in the second. Necessary BF is proved below but is a derived consequence of the C5 seed. The model records use only the existing unboxed AN and SOAN nodes.

In each case first force over a countable model N of ZFC, obtaining M=N[G]. Then construct a Boolean-valued hierarchy inside M using a second application of the same forcing Q. Its Boolean algebra is

A=\operatorname{RO}^{M}(Q^M),\qquad B=\mathbf2\times A. \tag{1}

The completion is formed anew in M. A completion computed in N need not remain complete after the first forcing.

For the second example take N\models\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{CH}. The usual relative consistency of CH and first-order completeness provide a countable such model from \operatorname{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}). The first example needs no CH assumption. Neither ground is required to be transitive or externally well founded.

For an arbitrary countable model, choose a filter meeting every dense set that the model regards as a dense subset of its forcing. Construct the extension from its internal names modulo generic forcing equality, using its internal forcing relation for membership. The forcing theorem for each fixed standard finite formula gives the truth lemma and ZFC in the extension. This uses no external recursion through ill-founded ordinals. Every forcing calculation below is a fixed internal ZFC theorem, interpreted in the relevant ground.

2. Why the second forcing preserves parameter-free theory

Suppose Q is parameter-free definable and N verifies these three conditions:

  1. Q is weakly homogeneous.
  2. Q forces that the recomputed Q has exactly its old set and order.
  3. Q\times Q is isomorphic to Q.

For every fixed standard parameter-free set-theoretic sentence \theta,

M\models\theta \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\models 1_Q\Vdash_Q\theta. \tag{2}

Here is the full argument. If conditions p,q forced opposite signs of \theta, weak homogeneity would give an automorphism taking p to a condition compatible with q. It fixes the sentence, producing a contradiction. Since deciding conditions are dense, the top condition decides one sign. Write \varepsilon\theta for that sign, either \theta or \neg\theta.

The isomorphism in condition 3 shows 1_{Q\times Q}\Vdash\varepsilon\theta. The product is forcing equivalent to the two-step iteration Q*\check Q. Condition 2 identifies its second factor with the forcing defined in the intermediate model. The iteration theorem therefore gives

N\models 1_Q\Vdash (1_Q\Vdash\varepsilon\theta).

The first generic extension satisfies both \varepsilon\theta and the assertion that its second forcing forces \varepsilon\theta. This proves (2), including the case where the decided sign is negative.

Equation (2) is an external schema over standard finite formulas. It does not provide a truth predicate inside N or M, and it does not assert that M is elementary in its next forcing extension with parameters from M. Those stronger statements are not used.

The two forcing checks

For Cohen forcing, Q=\operatorname{Fn}(\omega,2,{<}\omega). Given p,q, flip the finitely many bits on their overlap at which they disagree. This automorphism makes the image of p compatible with q. Forcing adds no finite sequences of ground ordinals, so the finite binary conditions and their order are unchanged after a first Cohen extension. Splitting \omega into two infinite sets identifies the product of two copies of Q with Q. Thus all three conditions hold.

For the other example,

Q=\operatorname{Fn}(\omega_1,2,{<}\omega_1),

where conditions are countable partial functions. The union of a descending countable sequence of conditions is a condition. To decide a name for an ordinal sequence of length \omega, recursively decide one coordinate at a time and then take such a lower bound. This gives dense conditions deciding the whole sequence, so the forcing adds no countable sequences of ordinals. It adds no reals and preserves \omega_1.

Every countable condition in the extension can be coded by a countable sequence of ordinals. That sequence is old; \omega_1 is unchanged. Consequently the recomputed Q is exactly the original forcing, including its order. This verifies condition 2, rather than merely asserting equivalence of two potentially different forcings.

Bit flips on the countable overlap of any two conditions make them compatible, proving weak homogeneity. A ground bijection \omega_1\times\{0,1\}\to\omega_1 merges the two countable domains and gives Q\times Q\cong Q. All three conditions again hold. These proofs, including the countable-sequence argument, are interpreted internally when the ground is nonstandard.

3. The Boolean hierarchy and its common positive package

Inside M, set D_e=\omega^M, D_t=B, and

D_{\sigma\to\tau}=(D_\tau^{D_\sigma})^M \qquad(\tau\ne e).

Use internal function application, Boolean connectives, and internal meets and joins for quantification. Externalize each standard finite type and evaluate a proposition by its first coordinate. First-coordinate projection preserves these internal meets and joins, so satisfaction has the ordinary classical quantifier clauses on the externalized domains. Fullness here always means internal fullness.

The Boolean soundness induction validates each fixed finite H proof. H-equivalent bodies have equal values at every assignment; internal function extensionality therefore identifies their abstractions. This validates C’s theoremhood-restricted Logical Equivalence. No optional root premise is used in that rule or necessitation.

Singleton characteristic predicates make defined identity crisp at every type. The defined necessity operator is the global top test

\delta(b)= \begin{cases}1_B&b=1_B,\\0_B&b\ne1_B.\end{cases}

Thus necessary distinctness is itself necessary, giving C5. Moreover \forall x\,\Box F x and \Box\forall x\,F x both have value top exactly when every F(x) has value top, and otherwise bottom. BF and its box hold at every admitted quantified type.

For Relational Choice, well-order the output domain internally and, for any admitted binary relation U, define

S(x,y)=U(x,y)\wedge \neg\bigvee_{z<y}U(x,z). \tag{3}

Internal Replacement and fullness admit this graph. Its row values are pairwise disjoint, lie below the original row values, and have the same join as those values. Crisp identity therefore makes the value of its functional-totality assertion equal to the value of the original row’s totality. Inclusion has value top. The existential choice conclusion has value at least the seriality antecedent, so every instance of Relational Choice has value top. Its universal closure and box do as well. The construction uses a relational graph even for individual outputs, with no forbidden function type ending in e.

Extensional forcing transfer

We use transfer only for the native analytical fragment: relations are applied to carrier elements, and identity compares carrier labels. It does not replace intensional identity between relation entities by equality of their forcing extensions.

For any internal set T, a name for a subset of \check T can be normalized to the coefficient function

t\longmapsto\|\check t\in\dot Y\|_A.

Conversely every internal map T\to A supplies such a name. For T=D_\sigma^k, the full relation domain supplies all these coefficient maps, with independently chosen first coordinates. Quantification over \check T reduces to meets and joins over the ground labels; Boolean substitution handles mixed element names. Induction on a fixed finite extensional formula now identifies its first coordinate with ground truth and its A coordinate with its forcing value. The same calculation works below any positive Boolean support on which its field hypotheses hold.

Necessity of Second-Order Analysis

Fix a native second-order analytical sentence \varphi. Translate it to the parameter-free set-theoretic sentence \theta_\varphi asserting its truth in the canonical real field with its natural-number predicate. An arity-k relation quantifier becomes a quantifier over subsets of \mathbb R^k. This is a finite first-order formula of set theory, with powersets as sets; it uses no truth predicate.

By (2), M and its second forcing give \theta_\varphi the same sign. Suppose the sign is positive. At any admitted carrier \sigma, complete ordered fields with their least inductive natural-number predicate are internally categorical. Thus every first-coordinate real structure satisfies \varphi. In the forcing coordinate the same categoricity and extensional transfer apply below the support of each candidate real structure. It follows that

\left\|\forall\mathfrak R\, (\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R) \to\varphi(\mathfrak R))\right\|_B=1_B. \tag{4}

Its box is top. If the sign is negative, the same proof gives the boxed assertion with \neg\varphi. This is exactly the native Necessity of Second-Order Analysis schema, for every carrier and every standard finite analytical sentence. Types without real structures are handled by the implication in (4); no capacity assumption is suppressed. This argument asserts no No Pure Contingency principle for the full intensional language.

4. Rigidity, complete fields and represented powers

Three calculations will distinguish the two models.

Endpoint rigidity. A relation Y:T\to B is rigid precisely when every value is 0_B or 1_B. Persistence requires Y(u)\le\delta(Y(u)), forcing those endpoint values. Conversely, write an endpoint-valued relation as \chi_E. For any admitted test Z, both

\forall u\,(Y(u)\to\Box Z(u)) \quad\text{and}\quad Y\le Z

have the same endpoint value: top exactly when Z(u)=1_B for all u\in E. The implication in weak inextensibility is therefore top for every test; its universal closure and leading box are top. Persistence is immediate. This verifies full rigidity, not merely fixed membership. Rigidity itself has endpoint value because its defining conjuncts are boxed.

Preserving a complete field. If the forcing adds no reals, every new bounded nonempty subset of the old real field has an old supremum. Its rational lower cut is coded by a subset of \omega, hence is old; the corresponding old real is its supremum. The least inductive natural-number predicate also remains the same, since forcing preserves \omega. The transfer just proved includes all the new subproperties quantified over in the completeness and induction clauses. Thus an endpoint copy of the old field is necessarily real.

Conversely, a genuinely new real supplies a new rational cut with no old supremum. Any internally complete ground field is ground-isomorphic to the ground reals, so its check fails completeness wherever that new cut appears. An AN witness cannot evade this by varying its operations: endpoint rigidity makes all five field relations ground relations. Further, each candidate’s \operatorname{RigidStr}\land\Box\operatorname{Real} has endpoint value. A join of bottom candidate values remains bottom.

Represented-power obstruction. Let X be a rigid carrier with ground support S. All rigid unary relations supported on X are the endpoint characteristic functions \chi_T, for ground subsets T\subseteq S. The full hierarchy admits the maximal endpoint family containing all of them. For an arbitrary subset-name coefficient map f:S\to A, the forcing coordinate of its existential agreement with that family is

\bigvee_{T\in\mathcal P(S)^M} \left(\bigwedge_{s\in T}f(s) \wedge\bigwedge_{s\in S\setminus T}\neg f(s)\right) =\bigvee_{T\in\mathcal P(S)^M} \|\dot Y=\check T\|_A. \tag{5}

Any candidate represented family that satisfies its rigidity and qualification clauses can select only a subcollection of these ground subsets. Its agreement value cannot exceed (5). If (5) is zero for one subset name, the boxed universal representation clause fails for every such family. Families failing rigidity or qualification already contribute bottom. The qualification clauses have endpoint values for rigid carriers and families, as do the rigidity and boxed representation clauses. Consequently Boolean mixing of different families cannot repair the failed existential.

The join in (5) is essential: a mixture of old subsets can have no single globally correct old representative while still being locally represented with total Boolean value top. We use names unequal to every old subset with equality value zero.

5. The second Cohen forcing: failure of AN

The second Cohen forcing adds a subset of \omega unequal to every old subset: for any old binary sequence and any condition, assign the opposite bit at a fresh coordinate. The corresponding disagreement sets are dense, so the new-real obstruction applies over M.

The et domain contains all endpoint sequences indexed by \omega^M and hence a ground copy of the real field. Give its field relations endpoint values. Its first-coordinate realhood is true, regardless of whether it stays complete after forcing. Thus \exists\mathfrak R\,\operatorname{Real}_{et}(\mathfrak R) has nonzero Boolean value, and its possibility has value top.

Any prospective rigid necessarily-real tuple must, by Section 4, be a ground complete ordered field with endpoint relations. The new rational cut prevents its check from being complete. Every tuple therefore contributes bottom to the AN consequent. The antecedent is top and the consequent bottom: the native AN instance at et fails. The map derives SOAN failure from this negative, so it is not a separate seed.

Together with (4), this gives full Necessity of Second-Order Analysis while AN fails. It does not claim that an arbitrary first Cohen forcing preserves all parameter-free truth over every ground; the first extension in the definition is what makes (2) available.

6. The second countably closed forcing: AN without SOAN

Both applications add no reals and preserve \omega_1, so CH holds in M and remains true after its second forcing. The no-new-reals field argument supplies necessary completeness, but full AN requires a separate check at every carrier.

For each ground real r\in2^\omega, the condition with domain \omega and values r belongs to Q. Distinct such conditions are incompatible, giving a ground-continuum antichain in A. Hence the ground reals inject into B. Constant functions recursively inject B into every admitted nonindividual type, including purely propositional types. Every such carrier therefore admits an endpoint copy of the old complete field, with all its operations and its natural-number predicate. Internal fullness admits these relations; Section 4 gives rigidity and necessary realhood.

At e the labels form \omega^M. In the ground and in the forcing universe, no subset of \omega carries a complete ordered field. Extensional transfer makes every possible \operatorname{Real}_e tuple have value zero, including on any proposed positive Boolean support. Thus the possible-real antecedent at e is bottom. This is an internal ZFC impossibility argument, not an inference from the external countability of the model. All carrier cases are now covered, so full current AN holds.

Let \dot H\subseteq\omega_1^M be the second generic subset. It is total because the conditions deciding each coordinate are dense. For every old T\subseteq\omega_1 and condition p, choose \alpha\notin\operatorname{dom}(p) and assign the bit opposite to T(\alpha). Hence

\|\dot H=\check T\|_A=0 \quad\text{for every old }T\subseteq\omega_1. \tag{6}

Now consider any proposed rigid necessarily-real tuple, at any carrier. If it fails either conjunct it contributes bottom to SOAN. Otherwise its endpoint field relations give an internally complete ground field on a support S. Ground categoricity and CH provide an internal bijection j:\omega_1\to S. Transfer \dot H along j, using its normalized coefficients and extending by zero outside S. If this subset name equalled any old subset of S on a positive condition, its inverse image would contradict (6). Thus every term in (5) is zero for this name.

Section 4 defeats every unary represented family for this particular field; the argument works for every prospective field. Consequently the full SOAN existential consequent has value bottom. At et the explicit rigid necessary field above makes the possible-real antecedent top. This refutes the native unary et instance of SOAN.

The resulting joint package preserves every parameter-free second-order analytical truth, preserves the real-field objects required by AN, and still fails the native demand for represented relation powers. No finite-type necessitism extension is asserted.
