Strong Atomicity, Weak Rigid Comprehension and Relational Choice imply Intensional Choice

The construction is Cian Dorr’s, proposed on 20 October 2026: choose, for each atom of type t, a strong atom of type \sigma t rather than an individual. The details and the pair lemma are by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), 20 October 2026. Lean-verified the same day: the proof follows this write-up step by step (Classicism.Proofs.strong_atomicity_weak_rigid_comprehension_and_relational_choice_imply_intensional_choice, with the pair lemma as box_pairs_of_weaklyInextensible), and is a theorem of C⁻.

Fix \sigma and let F^{\sigma t} satisfy \Box\exists y\, .\,Fy. Strong Atomicity is used at type \sigma t, and at type t through Atomicity (type t), which they imply. Relational Choice is used at types t,\sigma t, and Weak Rigid Comprehension at type (\sigma t)t. Only T and 4 are needed from the modal logic; BF and ND are not used. In this write-up an atom is an atom of type t.

The construction

Put U:=\lambda wX\, .\,\operatorname{Atom}(w)\to\bigl(\operatorname{SAtom}_{\sigma t}(X)\land X\le\lambda y\, .\,w\land Fy\bigr). It is serial. A non-atom is related to everything. For an atom w, \Diamond w and \Box\exists y\, .\,Fy give \Diamond\exists y\, .\,(w\land Fy), so \lambda y\, .\,w\land Fy is not bottom and Strong Atomicity gives a strong atom below it. Relational Choice gives a functional V with VwX\to UwX; write X_w for the value of an atom w. Weak Rigid Comprehension gives a weakly rigid R coextensive with \lambda X\, .\,\exists w\, .\,\operatorname{Atom}(w)\land VwX, so the actual instances of R are exactly the X_w. Put G:=\lambda y\, .\,\exists X\, .\,RX\land Xy .

Two facts about the actual instances

(a) Let A(X):=X\le F\land\forall Y^{\sigma t}\, .\,X\le Y\lor X\le\neg Y. Then \Box A(X_w) for every atom w: X_w\le F, and \le is boxed, so 4 gives the first conjunct necessarily; the second is the boxed clause of \operatorname{SAtom}_{\sigma t}(X_w).

(b) For all atoms w,w', \Box\bigl(X_w=X_{w'}\lor\neg(\exists y\, .\,X_wy\land\exists z\, .\,X_{w'}z)\bigr). If w=w', functionality gives X_w=X_{w'}, and identities are necessary. If w\ne w', then w\land w' lies below the atom w, so it is w or bottom, and likewise for w'; as w\ne w' it is bottom. Since X_w\le\lambda y\, .\,w and X_{w'}\le\lambda y\, .\,w', the two are necessarily not both instantiated.

Pair lemma

Lemma. Let R be weakly inextensible, and suppose \Box Z(a,b) for all a,b with Ra and Rb. Then \Box\forall ab\, .\,Ra\land Rb\to Z(a,b).

Proof. Fix b with Rb. Weak inextensibility with target \lambda a\, .\,Z(a,b) gives R\le\lambda a\, .\,Z(a,b), that is, \Box\forall a\, .\,Ra\to Z(a,b), and by 4 this holds necessarily. So every actual instance b satisfies \Box T(b), where T:=\lambda b\, .\,\Box\forall a\, .\,Ra\to Z(a,b). Weak inextensibility gives R\le T, and T applied inside the box gives the claim. ∎

Verification

G\le F. Weak inextensibility and (a) give \Box\forall X\, .\,RX\to A(X). So necessarily, if Gy, then RX and Xy for some X with X\le F, and T gives Fy.

\Box\exists y\, .\,Gy. Suppose otherwise. Atomicity (type t) gives an atom w_0\le\neg\exists y\, .\,Gy. Weak persistence gives \Box RX_{w_0}. As X_{w_0} is not bottom, \Diamond\exists y\, .\,X_{w_0}y, and X_{w_0}\le\lambda y\, .\,w_0, so \Diamond(w_0\land\exists y\, .\,Gy). This contradicts w_0\le\neg\exists y\, .\,Gy.

Necessarily at most one instance. Reason inside the box. Suppose Gy and Gz, with RX, Xy, RX' and X'z. The pair lemma with (b) gives X=X'. By (a), X\le\lambda u\, .\,u=y or X\le\lambda u\, .\,u\ne y. The second, by T, contradicts Xy. So the first holds, and T with Xz gives z=y.

So G\le F and \Box\exists y\, .\,(Gy\land\forall z\, .\,Gz\to z=y).

Remarks

Why strong atoms. An atom of type \sigma t settles only actual properties. The uniqueness step needs X to settle the haecceity \lambda u\, .\,u=y of an instance y at another world, and that y need not be actual. A strong atom settles every property necessarily, so the condition is persistent and passes through weak inextensibility as (a).

Comparison. The earlier proof, BF, Weak Rigid Comprehension, Atomicity and Relational Choice imply Intensional Choice, used BF to choose an individual x with w\le Fx, and again to pull instances below an atom back to actual ones. Choosing a property needs no BF, and the pair lemma replaces the pull-back.

Modalized Relational Choice. The argument does not extend. With inputs, values would be chosen for pairs of an atom w and an actual input x. Two distinct actual inputs x_1,x_2 may, without ND, be identified at a world where w is true, and the chosen values for (w,x_1) and (w,x_2) are then two values of the merged input; (b) fails for such pairs. Without BF there is a further gap: an input that is not actual has no chosen value.
