Symmetric surjection products with free-word gluing

Construction and proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, in the research note Symmetric surjection model with free-word gluing of 22 October 2026. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

These models extend Dorr’s symmetric all-surjections model (symmetric-all-surjections) in two ways, each needed to add Logical Necessity to that model’s package:

  1. pinning sets may have any size up to \kappa, inside a base of size \kappa^+;
  2. the arrows carry an extra monoid coordinate, which provides the sites for gluing the interpretations of Σ.

Finite pinning would not do, because the gluing has to absorb the pinning sets of infinitely many witnesses. Nor would the surjections alone, because a later collapse merges any gluing sites among the points. The arguments are the \kappa-pinned forms of the arguments recorded for the finite model, with the monoid coordinate held fixed. They are checked again here.

The verdicts for all four models:

  • Hold: Logical Necessity, □Boolean Completeness, □Tame Rigidity, □Weakly Rigid Power and Atomlessness.
  • Fail: Actuality, Vicinity and Relational Choice.
  • BF holds necessarily when the monoid factor is right cancellative, and fails at type t when it has an idempotent.
  • Individuals: with the members of U as individuals, the Axioms of Infinity at e and t hold too.

The models

Fix an infinite cardinal \kappa\ge|\Sigma|, a set U with |U|=\kappa^+, the monoid S of surjections U\to U, and a monoid K. The two choices of K are:

  • Free words. K=W, the free monoid of words on an alphabet of size \kappa, with empty word \varepsilon.
  • Free words with an idempotent. K=W\times\{1,d\}, where d^2=d\ne1.

There is one object, with arrows

(k,s)\in K\times S,\qquad (k,s)(k',s'):=(kk',\,s\circ s'),

evaluation point 1:=(1_K,\mathrm{id}), and symmetry group \{1_K\}\times\operatorname{Sym}(U). For a permutation \pi, write \pi also for the arrow (1_K,\pi).

Individuals. Either there is a single individual, fixed by every arrow, or the individuals are the members of U, with (k,s)\cdot a:=s(a), and a is pinned down by \{a\}.

Domains. At a relational type, the domain is the set of profiles F, assigning to each arrow a set of argument tuples, such that:

  • F is pinned down by some N\subseteq U of size at most \kappa: F_{(k,s)} depends only on k and s|_N;
  • F is symmetric: F_{(k,\pi s)}=\pi\cdot F_{(k,s)} for every permutation \pi.

Transport is (h\cdot F)_j:=F_{jh}. The semantics is that of one-object intensional action models: a formula holds at h iff it holds at 1 with its parameters transported along h.

1. A model of C

Transport along h=(k_h,s_h) takes a profile pinned down by N to one pinned down by s_h(N), and keeps symmetry: (h\cdot F)_{(k,\pi s)}=F_{(kk_h,\pi ss_h)}=\pi\cdot(h\cdot F)_{(k,s)}.

Every permutation \pi acts as an automorphism of the structure at 1. Symmetry gives F_\pi=\pi\cdot F_1, so \bar b\in F_1 iff \pi\cdot\bar b\in(\pi\cdot F)_1. So whatever a formula says of parameters \bar a and arguments \bar b, it says of \pi\cdot\bar a and \pi\cdot\bar b.

Now consider the profile of a \lambda-abstract with parameters \bar a. At (k,s) it depends only on (k,s)\cdot\bar a, so it is pinned down by the union of the parameters’ pinning sets. By the automorphism property it is symmetric.

So the domains are closed under the denotations of terms. This is the argument of Dorr’s Lemma 19 with sets of size at most \kappa in place of finite sets, the monoid coordinate passing through unchanged. Each entity a has its haecceity h\mapsto\{h\cdot a\} in the domain, pinned down by a pinning set of a. As there is one object, a closed pure sentence has the same truth value at every arrow.

Lifting along surjections. Let F be pinned down by N, and s\in S. Choose distinct s-preimages of the members of N, and extend n\mapsto (its preimage) to a permutation \pi. This is possible because the sets involved have size at most \kappa and their complements size \kappa^+. Then s\pi is the identity on N, and (1_K,s)\cdot(\pi\cdot F)=(1_K,s\pi)\cdot F=F. So transport along (1_K,s) is onto every relational domain. It is onto the individuals because s is a surjection.

2. Logical Necessity

There are at most \kappa sentences of \mathcal L(\Sigma). For each sentence \varphi_\alpha true at 1 under some interpretation of Σ over these domains, choose such an interpretation J_\alpha and a letter f_\alpha, distinct letters for distinct sentences. Then:

  1. Let the individual constants c denote distinct members a_c of U, or the only individual. Let s_\alpha be a surjection with s_\alpha(a_c)=J_\alpha(c) for each of them. There are at most \kappa prescriptions and \kappa^+ other points. With one individual, take s_\alpha:=\mathrm{id}.
  2. For each relational constant c, choose B_{\alpha,c} with (1_K,s_\alpha)\cdot B_{\alpha,c}=J_\alpha(c), by the lifting of §1.
  3. Interpret c by the profile G_c defined below.

With K=W, write an arrow as (w,s); with K=W\times\{1,d\}, as (w,j,s), the coordinate j being carried along unchanged. Put

(G_c)_{(w,s)}:=\begin{cases}(B_{\alpha,c})_{(u,s)}&w=uf_\alpha,\\ \varnothing&w=\varepsilon.\end{cases}

G_c is pinned down by the union of the pinning sets of the B_{\alpha,c}. That is at most \kappa sets of size at most \kappa. It is symmetric, since the permutations touch only the surjection coordinate.

At h_\alpha:=(f_\alpha,s_\alpha), for every arrow (u,r):

(h_\alpha\cdot G_c)_{(u,r)}=(G_c)_{(uf_\alpha,\,rs_\alpha)}=(B_{\alpha,c})_{(u,\,rs_\alpha)} =\bigl((1_K,s_\alpha)\cdot B_{\alpha,c}\bigr)_{(u,r)}=J_\alpha(c)_{(u,r)} .

Also h_\alpha\cdot a_c=s_\alpha(a_c)=J_\alpha(c). So at h_\alpha every constant takes its J_\alpha-value simultaneously, and \varphi_\alpha holds there. Conversely, a sentence true at an arrow h is true at 1 under the interpretation c\mapsto h\cdot c. Hence

\Diamond\varphi\ \text{at }1\iff\varphi\text{ is true at }1\text{ under some interpretation of }\Sigma .

Now take P pure and \bar c distinct constants. P[\bar c/\bar x] is true under some interpretation iff \exists\bar x\,.\,P holds at 1. So \Diamond P[\bar c/\bar x]\leftrightarrow \exists\bar x\,.\,P. Applied to \neg P, this is Logical Necessity. It is claimed at the evaluation point only.

3. BF and the monoid factor

When K is right cancellative, every arrow acts onto every domain. For (v,s)=(v,1)(1_K,s), compose the surjection lift of §1 with the lift along (v,1):

L_v(F)_{(k,s)}:=\begin{cases}F_{(u,s)}&k=uv,\\ \varnothing&k\notin Kv.\end{cases}

L_v(F) is well defined because u is unique, by right cancellation. It keeps F’s pinning set and symmetry, and (v,1)\cdot L_v(F)=F. So whenever \Box Ra holds for every a, any entity at any arrow h is h\cdot a for some a, and so lies in R there. That is BF at every type. Being closed and pure, it holds necessarily.

When K has an idempotent d_0\ne1_K, with 1_Kd_0=d_0=d_0d_0, BF fails at type t.

  • Let p be the proposition that the monoid coordinate is 1_K.
  • Let R, of type tt, hold of p alone at the arrows with monoid coordinate d_0, and of nothing elsewhere.

Both are pinned down by \varnothing and symmetric. Then \Diamond\exists q\,.\,Rq, witnessed at (d_0,\mathrm{id}).

Suppose \Diamond Rq for some q. Then k\cdot q=p for an arrow k with monoid coordinate d_0. But (k\cdot q)_{(1_K,s)}=q_{(d_0,ss_k)}=(k\cdot q)_{(d_0,s)}, since 1_Kd_0=d_0d_0, whereas p is true at (1_K,s) and false at (d_0,s). So BF fails at t.

4. Atomlessness; Actuality, Vicinity and Relational Choice fail

For distinct a,b\in U let q_{a,b}:=\{(k,s):s(a)\ne s(b)\}. It is pinned down by \{a,b\} and symmetric.

Atomlessness. Let p\ne\bot be pinned down by N, with (k,s)\in p, and let a,b\notin N. s|_N has at most \kappa values, so it extends to a surjection that separates a and b, and to one that identifies them. Both lie in p. So p\land q_{a,b} and p\land\neg q_{a,b} are nonzero. Hence there is no atom, and Actuality fails.

Vicinity. Let p be true, pinned down by N, and a,b\notin N. Let s fix N pointwise and identify a and b. Then p holds at (1_K,s). But q_{a,b}, which is true, is impossible there: every arrow j(1_K,s) has surjection s_js, which identifies a and b. So no truth entails the possibility of every truth.

Relational Choice, at input type tt and output type t.

  • Let U relate each property of propositions with exactly two actual instances to each of them, and every other property to every proposition. It is a closed term, and serial.
  • Let R\le U be functional, pinned down by N. For a permutation \pi fixing N, pinning gives R_\pi=R_1 and symmetry gives R_\pi=\pi\cdot R_1. So R_1 is invariant under \pi.
  • Take distinct a,b,c\notin N. The propositions q_{c,a} and q_{c,b} are distinct: a surjection identifying c with a but not with b separates them.
  • Let A be the union of their haecceities. It is in the domain, with exactly these two actual instances.
  • The transposition \pi of a and b fixes N and c. It exchanges q_{c,a} and q_{c,b} and fixes A.
  • R relates A to one of the two instances, so by invariance it relates A to the other as well, and R is not functional.

5. □Boolean Completeness

Let A, of type \tau t, be pinned down by N, with actual instances E:=A_1. For a permutation \pi fixing N pointwise, \pi\cdot E=E, by pinning and symmetry as above. Let

J_{(k,s)}:=\bigcup\{a_{(k,t)}:\ a\in E,\ t\in S,\ t|_N=s|_N\}.

J is pinned down by N. It is symmetric: replacing s by \pi s replaces each t by \pi t, and a_{(k,\pi t)}=\pi\cdot a_{(k,t)}. It is an upper bound of E, by taking t=s.

J is below every upper bound. Let Z, pinned down by T, be an upper bound of E, and let \bar b\in a_{(k,t)} with a\in E and t|_N=s|_N. Let L pin down a.

  • Choose a permutation \pi fixing N pointwise and moving L\setminus N off T. Then \pi\cdot a\in E.
  • Prescribe a surjection r to agree with s on T, and to send \pi(l) to t(l) for each l\in L.
  • The prescriptions are compatible. A point of T\cap\pi(L) is \pi(l) with l\in N, where \pi(l)=l and t(l)=s(l).
  • There are at most \kappa of them, so they extend to a surjection.
  • Then r\pi agrees with t on L, so

\bar b\in a_{(k,t)}=a_{(k,r\pi)}=(\pi\cdot a)_{(k,r)}\subseteq Z_{(k,r)}=Z_{(k,s)} .

So J\le Z, and J is the least upper bound of A’s actual instances, at every relational type. The schema is closed and pure, so it holds necessarily. This is the hull argument of Dorr’s Theorem 36, specialised to surjections with pinning sets of size at most \kappa.

6. Exact images and □Tame Rigidity

For a set E of argument tuples, let H_E be the profile h\mapsto h\cdot E.

Lemma. A weakly rigid Y, pinned down by N, with Y_1=E, is H_E.

Proof. Let B_N(E)_{(k,s)}:=\bigcup\{(k,t)\cdot E:\ t|_N=s|_N\}. It is pinned down by N, and symmetric by functoriality, and every member of E is necessarily in it. So weak inextensibility gives Y\le B_N(E), and weak persistence gives h\cdot E\subseteq Y_h.

Let \bar b\in Y_{(k,s)}, so \bar b=(k,t)\cdot\bar a with \bar a\in E and t|_N=s|_N, and let L pin down the entries of \bar a. Let u be a surjection fixing N and sending each l\in L\setminus N to an s-preimage of t(l). Then Y_{(1_K,u)}=E, so weak persistence gives u\cdot\bar a\in E. And (k,su) agrees with (k,t) on L, so \bar b=(k,s)\cdot(u\cdot\bar a)\in(k,s)\cdot E. \square

Conversely, every H_E in the domain is rigid, and so is each of its transports. So weak rigidity coincides with rigidity at every world, and □Tame Rigidity holds at every type.

7. □Rigid Power and □Weakly Rigid Power

Stability lemma. Let A be a set of tuples closed under every (1_K,u) with u fixing P pointwise, where |P|\le\kappa. Then H_A is pinned down by P, so it is in the domain and rigid.

Proof. Let s|_P=t|_P and \bar a\in A, pinned down by L. A surjection u fixing P with su=t on L gives (k,t)\cdot\bar a=(k,s)\cdot(u\cdot\bar a)\in(k,s)\cdot A. By symmetry in s and t, the two images are equal. \square

The power property is weakly inextensible.

  • Let F=H_E be rigid, pinned down by S_F, with power property \mathcal P_F. Let Z necessarily hold of every actual rigid Y\le F. Let N\supseteq S_F also pin down Z, let h=(k,s), and let X=H_D\le h\cdot F be rigid, pinned down by T.
  • Let j_0 agree with s on N and map U\setminus N bijectively onto U\setminus s(N), and put j:=(k,j_0). Then j\cdot F=h\cdot F, so D\subseteq j\cdot E.
  • Every nonsingleton fibre of j_0 lies inside N. So P:=N\cup j_0^{-1}(T) has size at most \kappa.
  • Let A:=\{\bar a\in E:j\cdot\bar a\in D\}. Then j\cdot A=D.
  • A is closed under the surjections fixing P.
    • Let u fix P. Then j_0u=vj_0 for the surjection v with v(j_0x):=j_0(ux).
    • v is well defined because u fixes the nonsingleton fibres, and v fixes T.
    • F and X are weakly persistent, u fixes S_F and v fixes T. So u\cdot E\subseteq E and v\cdot D\subseteq D.
    • Hence j\cdot(u\cdot\bar a)=v\cdot(j\cdot\bar a)\in D for \bar a\in A, and u\cdot\bar a\in A.
  • By the stability lemma, Y:=H_A is rigid, Y\le F, and j\cdot Y=X.
  • So X\in Z_j. And Z_j=Z_h, since j and h have the same monoid coordinate and agree on N.

So \mathcal P_F is weakly inextensible. It is persistent by C, and rigid by Tame Rigidity. Hence Rigid Power holds at every type. As weakly rigid and rigid subrelations coincide, the same power properties give Weakly Rigid Power. Both are closed and pure, so they hold necessarily.

8. The Axioms of Infinity

The numerals are closed pure terms, fixed by every arrow. So the constant profile whose instances are the numerals at a type is symmetric and pinned down by \varnothing.

  • The finite cardinalities are the standard ones. Every inductive property contains the numerals, and this constant profile is inductive.
  • The nth holds of exactly the properties with n actual instances, by finite induction.
  • With the members of U as individuals, the universal property of individuals has \kappa^+ instances. So no finite cardinality holds of it, and the Axiom of Infinity holds at e.
  • At t, in every case, the propositions q_{a,b} are infinitely many, so the Axiom of Infinity holds at t.

9. Further verdicts

Added 28 October 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr. Write q_{x,y}:=\{(k,s):s(x)\ne s(y)\} for any x,y\in U, so that \pi\cdot q_{x,y}=q_{\pi x,\pi y}.

Truths are ND-necessary holds. Let p be true, pinned down by N, and let q:=\{(1_K,s):s\text{ injective on }N\}. It is true, pinned down by N, and symmetric.

  • \Diamond q=q. In both monoid factors, jk=1_K only if k=1_K, and ts is injective on N only if s is.
  • q\le p. A member (1_K,s) of q agrees on N with a permutation \pi. And (1_K,\pi)\in p, since p is symmetric and true. So (1_K,s)\in p.

Hence \Box(\Diamond q\to p), and every truth is ND-necessary. The verdicts on the varieties of necessity follow.

Intensional Choice fails at type t. Fix c\in U, and let

F_{(k,s)}:=\{q_{s(c),y}:\ y\ne s(c)\},

which is pinned down by \{c\}, symmetric, and necessarily instantiated. Suppose G\le F is necessarily uniquely instantiated, and pinned down by N\ni c. Let h:=(1_K,s) with s sending N to a single point p. Then G_h=\{q_{p,y}\} for some y\ne p.

For a permutation \pi fixing p, the arrow \pi h agrees with h on N. So G_{\pi h}=G_h by pinning, while symmetry gives G_{\pi h}=\{q_{p,\pi y}\}. Hence every such \pi fixes y, which is false. This is intensional-choice-collapse at type t.

Arithmetic Necessitism holds. The profile whose value at every arrow is the set of numerals is pinned down by \varnothing and symmetric. So the numbers are the numerals, fixed by every arrow (as arithmetic-necessitism-finitely-pinned).

Analytic Necessitism holds, and so does its second-order form. Revised 29 October 2026: this section first recorded a failure. Its argument was withdrawn, because it inferred membership in a rigid carrier from inextensibility, which only bounds a carrier from above.

  • The entities. For finite N, the arrows whose surjection is constant on N form a symmetric proposition, pinned down by N. Its orbit under the permutations depends only on |N|. So the unions S_A of the orbits for the sizes in A\subseteq\mathbb N are continuum many invariant sets of propositions. The constant intensions Y_A with these values are in the domain and fixed by every arrow.
  • The structure. Every set of tuples of the Y_A is invariant, and so is every set of such constant relations, so each is the value of a constant intension in the domain. A real number structure on the Y_A by constant relations therefore has fixed parameters, and there is one object. So it is one at every world, rigid, with necessarily distinct instances. The constant intensions of the sets of its constant relations are represented powers.

10. Inextensible Comprehension

GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Cian Dorr, 29 October 2026; checked and recorded by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic). □Inextensible Comprehension holds in all four variants. The finite-model argument relabels an arrow fixing the pinning set into one that leaves the entity unchanged, and the monoid coordinate blocks that step. This argument instead switches the witness off for good once the monoid coordinate leaves 1_K.

The witness. Let X be pinned down by N, with |N|\le\kappa, and put

Y_{(k,s)}:=\begin{cases}X_{(k,s)}&k=1_K\text{ and }s|_N\text{ injective},\\ \varnothing&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Injectivity on N depends only on s|_N and is unchanged by composing with a permutation, so Y is pinned down by N and symmetric. And Y_1=X_1, so Y is coextensive with X at 1.

The defects are permanent. In both monoid factors, jk=1_K only if k=1_K: in W by length, and in W\times\{1,d\} because xy=1 forces x=y=1. And if s collapses two points of N, so does ts. So along an arrow h=(k,s) with k\ne1_K, or with s not injective on N, (h\cdot Y)_j=Y_{jh}=\varnothing for every j: the transport is \bot, which is weakly inextensible.

Along h=(1_K,s) with s|_N injective, h\cdot X is pinned down by s[N]. Since ts|_N is injective iff t|_{s[N]} is, h\cdot Y is the same construction applied to h\cdot X with pinning set s[N]. So it suffices to show that Y is weakly inextensible at 1, for every X and N.

Lifting. Put E:=X_1. A permutation \tau fixing N has X_{(1_K,\tau)}=X_1 by pinning and =\tau\cdot X_1 by symmetry, so \tau\cdot E=E. Let g be a surjection fixing N pointwise and \bar a\in E, its entries pinned down by T, |T|\le\kappa. For t\in T\setminus N choose a g-preimage p_t. These are distinct and lie outside N, since g fixes N. So the identity on N together with t\mapsto p_t is injective, with domain and range of size at most \kappa, whose complements have size \kappa^+, and it extends to a permutation \tau. Then g\tau is the identity on T, so (g\tau)\cdot\bar a=\bar a: for an entity F pinned down by T, F_{(k,t\circ g\tau)}=F_{(k,t)}, and an individual a is pinned down by \{a\}. Thus \tau\cdot\bar a\in E and g\cdot(\tau\cdot\bar a)=\bar a: every member of E is the g-image of a member of E.

Weak inextensibility at 1. Suppose Z necessarily holds of every member of E. Take \bar b\in Y_{(k,s)}, so that k=1_K and s|_N is injective. Extend s|_N to a permutation \pi and put g:=\pi^{-1}s, which fixes N. By pinning and symmetry, Y_{(1_K,s)}=X_{(1_K,\pi)}=\pi\cdot E; write \bar b=\pi\cdot\bar a with \bar a\in E, and lift \bar a to \bar a'\in E with g\cdot\bar a'=\bar a. Transport is a left action, so (1_K,s)\cdot\bar a'=(1_K,\pi)\cdot((1_K,g)\cdot\bar a')=\bar b. Since Z holds necessarily of \bar a', \bar b\in Z_{(1_K,s)}. At every other arrow Y is empty. So Y\le Z.

Conclusion. Every X is coextensive at 1 with an inextensible Y, so Inextensible Comprehension holds at 1. It is a closed pure sentence and the models have one object, so it holds at every arrow: □Inextensible Comprehension holds. Nothing depends on whether the individuals are U or a single point. The argument uses choice, for the preimages p_t and the extending permutations.

Remarks

  1. Choice. Logical Necessity uses choice in the metatheory, in choosing the witnesses J_\alpha, and so do the lifts in BF and Rigid Power.
  2. What is left open. Transversal holds in the finite model and is open here. The finite argument breaks in two places:
    • It takes least supports, which need not exist for pinning sets of size \kappa.
    • It relabels an arrow fixing the pinning set into one that leaves the entity unchanged. The monoid coordinate blocks that step. For Inextensible Comprehension, which had the same obstacle, §10 avoids it by making the witness empty once the monoid coordinate leaves 1_K.
  3. Source. Astra’s note also gives the general criterion: with any monoid factor K, BF holds iff K is right cancellative. The map records the two instances above.
