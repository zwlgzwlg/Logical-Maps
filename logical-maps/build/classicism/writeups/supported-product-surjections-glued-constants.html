Supported-product action model with surjections of the individuals

Construction by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026 (§11 of the note behind the base model). Checked, extended and written up for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

This is a variant of the base model, the supported-product action model with free-monoid coordinates and glued constants, whose write-up should be read first. The base model has one individual. Here each arrow also carries a surjection of a large set of individuals. That adds the Axioms of Infinity and keeps every verdict of the base model except two:

  • Truths are ND-necessary fails (§5);
  • Analytic Necessitism, in both forms, fails at type e (§6), though it holds at every other type.

The model

Fix an infinite cardinal \kappa\ge\max(|\Sigma|,2^{\aleph_0}). Let I and U be sets of size \kappa^+, and L the free monoid of finite words on an alphabet of size \kappa, with empty word \varepsilon. The arrows are the pairs

(m,s),\qquad m\in L^I,\quad s:U\to U\text{ onto},\qquad (m,s)(m',s'):=(mm',\,s\circ s'),

forming a one-object category with identity 1:=(\varepsilon,\mathrm{id}), the evaluation point. For S\subseteq I, write (m,s)\equiv_S(m',s') when m|_S=m'|_S and s=s'.

Domains. D_e:=U, with (m,s)\cdot a:=s(a). At a relational type \sigma_1\cdots\sigma_nt, D_\tau is the set of profiles F:\text{arrows}\to\mathcal P(D_{\sigma_1}\times\cdots\times D_{\sigma_n}) with a support: a set S\subseteq I of size at most \kappa such that F_h=F_{h'} whenever h\equiv_Sh'. So a profile depends on at most \kappa coordinates of m, and arbitrarily on s. Transport is (h\cdot F)_k:=F_{kh}, and the semantics is the base model’s.

The base model’s Lemma 1 holds as before: if F has support S and h\equiv_Sh' then h\cdot F=h'\cdot F, since kh\equiv_Skh'. An individual parameter a contributes nothing to the support, as h\cdot a depends only on the surjection coordinate. So the domains are closed under the denotations of terms, and this is a model of C. A closed pure sentence has the same truth value at every arrow.

Which arguments carry over unchanged. The base model’s propositions q_i=\{(m,s):m_i=\varepsilon\}, its profiles e_{z,j} and p_x, and every arrow its arguments use can be taken with surjection coordinate \mathrm{id}, and the profiles do not depend on the surjection. So the following arguments go through word for word:

  • Atomlessness;
  • the failures of Actuality and Vicinity;
  • the failure of Weakly Inextensible Comprehension at tt;
  • the failure of Boolean Completeness at t;
  • □Countable Boolean Completeness;
  • □Relational Choice, Transversal Choice and Modal Well-Ordering, by constant profiles, which every arrow fixes.

The rest of this write-up covers what needs adapting.

1. Transport is onto; □BF

Surjections are right cancellable: if t\circ s=t'\circ s with s onto, then t=t'.

Lemma 2’. For every arrow h=(h_L,s_h) and F\in D_\tau there is G\in D_\tau with h\cdot G=F.

Proof. Let S support F. Put G_{(n,r)}:=\varnothing unless n_i ends with (h_L)_i for every i\in S and r=t\circ s_h for some t, which is then unique. In that case put G_{(n,r)}:=F_{(n^\flat,t)}, where n^\flat strips the suffix (h_L)_i from n_i for i\in S and is \varepsilon elsewhere. G has support S, and

(h\cdot G)_{(k,t)}=G_{(kh_L,\,t\circ s_h)}=F_{((kh_L)^\flat,\,t)}=F_{(k,t)} ,

since (kh_L)^\flat\equiv_Sk. \square

At type e transport is onto because the surjections are. So BF holds as in the base model, at every type, and necessarily.

2. The Axioms of Infinity

As in the base model’s §6, the actual instances of \operatorname{FiniteCardinality}_\sigma are exactly the numerals \operatorname{Suc}^n_\sigma(\mathbf 0_\sigma), and the nth holds of exactly the properties with n actual instances, by finite induction. The universal property of individuals has \kappa^+ instances, so it has no finite cardinality, and the Axiom of Infinity holds at e. By Atomlessness it also holds at t. Both are closed and pure, so they hold necessarily.

3. Logical Necessity

Gluing. The base model’s gluing lemma holds with arrows h_\alpha:=(b_\alpha,\mathrm{id}), where b_\alpha has the one-letter word \alpha at the fresh coordinate j and \varepsilon elsewhere. This works because kh_\alpha\equiv_{I\setminus\{j\}}kh in the L-coordinate and leaves the surjection unchanged.

The interpretation of Σ. The individual constants c denote distinct individuals a_c, chosen so that \kappa^+ individuals are left over. For each \mathcal L(\Sigma)- sentence \varphi_\alpha that is true at 1 under some interpretation of Σ, choose such an interpretation J_\alpha. Then:

  1. Extend a_c\mapsto J_\alpha(c), for the individual constants c, to a surjection s_\alpha, sending the leftover individuals onto U.
  2. For each relational constant c, use Lemma 2’ to choose B_{\alpha,c} with (\varepsilon,s_\alpha)\cdot B_{\alpha,c}=J_\alpha(c). The lift does not enlarge supports.
  3. Choose j outside all these supports, and let G_c glue the B_{\alpha,c} at j.

The coordinates commute: (b_\alpha,s_\alpha)=(\varepsilon,s_\alpha)(b_\alpha,\mathrm{id}). So at the arrow (b_\alpha,s_\alpha) every constant takes its J_\alpha-value at once:

  • s_\alpha(a_c)=J_\alpha(c) for the individual constants;
  • (\varepsilon,s_\alpha)\cdot B_{\alpha,c}=J_\alpha(c) for the relational ones.

The rest is the base model’s §7: \Diamond\varphi holds at 1 iff \varphi is true under some interpretation of Σ, and this gives Logical Necessity.

4. Exact images, Tame Rigidity, Rigid Power, Rigid Rigidity

For a set E of argument tuples, let H_E be the profile h\mapsto h\cdot E.

Lemma 3’. A supported Y with E=Y_1 is weakly rigid iff Y=H_E, and then it is rigid.

Proof. The direction from Y=H_E, and rigidity, are as in the base model. Conversely, let Y be weakly rigid with support S. For T\subseteq I with |T|\le\kappa put

B_T(E)_{(k,t)}:=\bigcup\{(n,t)\cdot E:\ n|_T=k|_T\},

with the surjection held fixed. It has support T, and each member of E is necessarily in it, so Y\le B_T(E). Let \bar b\in Y_{(m,s)}, and let T contain S and the supports of the relational entries of \bar b. Then \bar b=(n,s)\cdot\bar a for some \bar a\in E and n|_T=m|_T. Take the base model’s r,u\in L^I, with mr=un, and put \hat r:=(r,\mathrm{id}) and \hat u:=(u,\mathrm{id}). Then

(m,s)\hat r=(mr,s)=(un,s)=\hat u(n,s).

\hat r\equiv_S1, so Y_{\hat r}=E, and weak persistence gives \hat r\cdot\bar a\in E. \hat u fixes \bar b: it fixes the individuals, its surjection being \mathrm{id}, and the relational entries, being \varepsilon on their supports. So

\bar b=\hat u(n,s)\cdot\bar a=(m,s)\cdot(\hat r\cdot\bar a)\in(m,s)\cdot E .\qquad\square

□Tame Rigidity follows as before.

Rigid Power fails at (tt)t. The base model’s argument goes through. The profiles e_{z,j} and y_j ignore the surjection, so h\cdot E depends only on |m_o|, and the arrows a and b_j can be taken with surjection \mathrm{id}. In the final step, b_j\equiv_S1 gives Y_{b_j}=Y_1 as before.

□Rigid Rigidity. We need the lifting lemma again: for every rigid Y and arrow k=(k_L,k_s), there is a rigid X with k\cdot X=Y. By Lemma 3’, Y=H_D; let S support it. Fix a section \iota of k_s, so that k_s(\iota(a))=a; this uses choice. Lift each entry of a tuple of D:

  • an individual a to \iota(a);
  • a relational entry q to \Lambda(q), where \Lambda(q)_{(n,r)}:=q_{(n^\flat,t)} when n_i ends with (k_L)_i on S and r=t\circ k_s, and \varnothing otherwise.

Let k'' be k_L off S and \varepsilon on S. As in Lemma 2’, k\cdot\Lambda(q)=(k'',\mathrm{id})\cdot q. Also k\cdot\iota(a)=a=(k'',\mathrm{id})\cdot a. So the set C of lifted tuples has k\cdot C=(k'',\mathrm{id})\cdot D. That is D, because (k'',\mathrm{id})\equiv_S1.

\Lambda commutes with arrows of the form (m_{\bar S},\mathrm{id}) with m_{\bar S} \varepsilon on S, and such arrows fix D and the individuals. So (m,s)\cdot C depends only on m|_S and s. Hence H_C is supported by S, and it is rigid by Lemma 3’, with k\cdot H_C=Y. The rest of the base model’s argument is unchanged.

5. Truths are ND-necessary fails

Let p:=\{(m,s):s=\mathrm{id}\}, a true proposition with empty support. Let q be any truth, and let \pi\ne\mathrm{id} be a permutation of U. Then \Diamond q holds at (\varepsilon,\pi), because (\varepsilon,\pi^{-1})(\varepsilon,\pi)=1\in q. But p is false there. So \Box(\Diamond q\to p) fails for every true q, and p is not ND-necessary.

In the base model q_S witnessed the ND-necessity of p, since \Diamond q_S=q_S. That relied on the free monoid having no invertible elements other than 1. The permutations here are invertible.

The varieties of necessity coincide. For S\subseteq I of size at most \kappa, put

V_S:=\{(m,s):\ m_i=\varepsilon\text{ for all }i\in S,\ s\text{ a bijection}\}.

Let q be a truth with support S. Then V_S\le\Diamond q: for (m,s)\in V_S, the arrow (\varepsilon,s^{-1})(m,s)=(m,\mathrm{id}) agrees with 1 on S, so lies in q. For the truth \{(m,s):m|_S=\varepsilon,\ s=\mathrm{id}\} there is equality. A left factor cannot empty a nonempty word, and t\circ s=\mathrm{id} forces s to be injective. So

\Box_{\ne}p\iff V_S\le p\text{ for some }S .

So \Diamond_{\ne}r holds iff r meets every V_S. Now evaluate \Diamond_{\ne}q at an arrow h=(h_L,s_h), with q a truth of support T. That means asking whether h\cdot q meets every V_S. It is enough to look at S\supseteq T. There, a member (m,\pi) of V_S lies in h\cdot q iff (mh_L,\pi\circ s_h)\in q, iff (h_L,\pi\circ s_h)\in q. So \Diamond_{\ne}q holds at h iff (h_L,\pi\circ s_h)\in q for some permutation \pi: it is q closed under permutations of the surjection coordinate. The closure of a truth with support T includes V_T, with equality for the truth above. So

\hat{\hat\Box}p\iff\text{some truth }q\text{ has }\Box(\Diamond_{\ne}q\to p)\iff V_S\le p\text{ for some }S\iff\Box_{\ne}p .

So \Box_{\ne} is identical to its hat, hence to \Box_5 (Background, Hats, two-hat necessity and 5-necessity). The principles saying that two of these operators agree therefore hold, necessarily, while those saying that every truth is necessary in one of these senses fail, with p as above.

6. Arithmetic and Analytic Necessitism

Arithmetic Necessitism holds as before: \mathbb N_\sigma and the numerals are closed pure terms, so every arrow fixes them.

Analytic Necessitism fails at e. A real number structure at e exists at 1: |U|\ge2^{\aleph_0}, and every set of tuples of individuals is the extension of a constant profile. So the antecedent \Diamond\exists\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_e(\mathfrak R) holds.

But no structure at e is rigid. Its carrier has two distinct actual instances a and b. Take a surjection s with s(a)=s(b). At (\varepsilon,s) we have a=b, since identity there is identity of the transports. So the instances of the carrier are not necessarily distinct. Both Analytic Necessitism and its second-order form therefore fail.

If |U|<2^{\aleph_0}, no real number structure on individuals would be possible at any arrow, and both principles would hold at e trivially. The choice \kappa\ge2^{\aleph_0} is made so that the verdict does not depend on \Sigma.

Analytic Necessitism holds at every other type.

  • Every relational type other than t. There are at least 2^{\kappa^+} constant profiles, all fixed by every arrow and pairwise distinct. With n\ge1 arguments, constant profiles of distinct sets of n-tuples of individuals are already enough. Put a real structure on continuum many of them with constant relations. Take as represented powers the constant profiles of all constant relations among them. Every parameter is then fixed by every arrow, so whatever holds of them at 1 holds at every arrow, and the base model’s argument applies.
  • At t, the base model’s argument applies unchanged. It uses the propositions p_x=\{(m,s):\#_a(m_o)-\#_b(m_o)\in x\}, x\subseteq\mathbb Z, which every arrow shifts injectively, and their exact images, which are supported by \{o\} and rigid by Lemma 3’.

Remarks

  1. Choice. As in the base model, Logical Necessity, the choice principles and the failure of Boolean Completeness at t use choice in the metatheory, and so does Rigid Rigidity, through the section \iota.
  2. Size of \kappa. Astra’s note takes \kappa\ge|\Sigma|. Only the verdict on Analytic Necessitism at e uses \kappa\ge2^{\aleph_0}. For countable \Sigma, the construction should also work with \kappa=\aleph_0, I of size \aleph_1 and U countable, leaving infinitely many individuals besides the a_c. In that version both forms of Analytic Necessitism hold.
