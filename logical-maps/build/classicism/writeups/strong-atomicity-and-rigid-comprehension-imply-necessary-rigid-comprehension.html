Strong Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension imply □Rigid Comprehension

Proof by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 24 October 2026.

Theorem. In C, Strong Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension imply □Rigid Comprehension, at every argument tuple including individual and mixed ones. Both premises are unboxed.

Overview. First, the premises give BF at every relational type (Lemma 4), using the cuts of the □Strong Atomicity write-up together with Rigid Power. Then, for a relation X with a relational argument type, each strong propositional world w yields a rigid P_w that is coextensive with X wherever w is true. Since some strong atom is true at every world, X has a rigid coextension at every world, and BF moves the quantifier over X inside the box (Lemma 5). A coding by haecceities extends this to individual and tuple arguments (Lemma 6).

We use Lemmas 1–3 and the notation of □Strong Atomicity write-up: \operatorname{Decides}, \operatorname{Union}, the cover A with (a) \Box A\bot, (b) A\le\operatorname{Decides}, (c) \operatorname{Union}(A)=\top, and the cuts \operatorname{Cut}_Y.

Lemma 4: BF at relational types

Let \sigma be relational, and assume Strong Atomicity at \sigma, and Rigid Comprehension and Rigid Power at \sigma t. Then BF holds at \sigma.

Proof. Let A^{\sigma t} be the cover of Lemma 1, and by Rigid Power let

\mathcal P:=\lambda R^{\sigma t}\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(R)\land R\le A,

which is rigid. By Lemmas 2 and 3,

\Box\forall Y^\sigma\, .\,\mathcal P(\operatorname{Cut}_Y)\land Y=\operatorname{Union}(\operatorname{Cut}_Y). \tag{1}

Let X^{\sigma t} satisfy \forall Y\, .\,\Box XY. Every R with \mathcal PR has \Box X(\operatorname{Union}(R)). By weak inextensibility of \mathcal P,

\Box\forall R\, .\,\mathcal PR\to X(\operatorname{Union}(R)),

and with (1), \Box\forall Y\, .\,XY. \square

Strong Atomicity implies Atomicity, and Atomicity with Rigid Comprehension implies Rigid Power (both recorded), so the premises of the theorem give BF at every relational type.

Lemma 5: one relational argument

Let \sigma be relational. Assume Strong Atomicity at t, Rigid Comprehension at \sigma t and at tt, and BF at \sigma and at \sigma t. Then

\Box\forall X^{\sigma t}\, .\,\exists Y^{\sigma t}\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\landX\equiv Y.

Proof. Let A^{tt} be the cover of Lemma 1 at t. Here \operatorname{Decides}(w) is \forall q^t\, .\,w\le q\lor w\le\neg q, and (c) says \Box\exists w\, .\,Aw\land w. For X^{\sigma t} put

\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X):=\exists Y^{\sigma t}\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\landX\equiv Y.

Fix X^{\sigma t}, and let w be an instance of A. By Rigid Comprehension at \sigma t there are rigid P and Q with

\forall z\, .\,(Pz\leftrightarrow w\le Xz),\qquad\forall z\, .\,(Qz\leftrightarrow w\le\neg Xz).

Each z with Pz has \Box(w\to Xz), and each z with Qz has \Box(w\to\neg Xz); by weak inextensibility of P and of Q,

\Box\forall z\, .\,Pz\to(w\to Xz),\qquad\Box\forall z\, .\,Qz\to(w\to\neg Xz). \tag{2}

By \operatorname{Decides}(w), every z has Pz\lor Qz, so \Box(Pz\lor Qz) by persistence, and by BF at \sigma

\Box\forall z\, .\,Pz\lor Qz . \tag{3}

From (2) and (3),

\Box\bigl(w\toX\equiv P\bigr),

and as \operatorname{Rigid}(P) is necessary, \Box(w\to\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X)). This holds for every instance w of A:

\forall w\, .\,Aw\to\Box\bigl(w\to\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X)\bigr).

By weak inextensibility of A, \Box\forall w\, .\,Aw\to(w\to\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X)), and with \Box\exists w\, .\,Aw\land w,

\Box\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X).

This holds for every X^{\sigma t}, and BF at \sigma t gives \Box\forall X\, .\,\operatorname{HasRigidCoext}(X). \square

Only the actual instances of BF are used, and P and Q may depend on w; no choice is made uniformly in w.

Lemma 6: coding tuples by haecceities

For a tuple \bar\sigma (possibly containing e, possibly empty) let \delta:=\bar\sigma t, and for \bar x^{\bar\sigma} let H_{\bar x}:=\lambda\bar z\, .\,\bar z=\bar x, of type \delta. Put \operatorname{Code}(H):=\exists\bar x\, .\,H=H_{\bar x}. C proves H_{\bar x}=H_{\bar y}\leftrightarrow\bar x=\bar y.

Claim. It is a theorem of C that if Rigid Comprehension holds for properties of type \delta t, it holds for relations of type \bar\sigma t.

Proof. Let X^{\bar\sigma t}. Take a rigid P^{\delta t} coextensive with \lambda H\, .\,\exists\bar x\, .\,H=H_{\bar x}\land X[\bar x], and put

Y:=\lambda\bar x\, .\,P(H_{\bar x}).

By injectivity of the codes, Y is coextensive with X.

P contains only codes. Each instance H of P is H_{\bar a} for some \bar a, so \Box\operatorname{Code}(H). By weak inextensibility of P,

P\le\operatorname{Code}. \tag{4}

Y is rigid. Persistence of Y is persistence of P at the arguments H_{\bar x}. For weak inextensibility, work at any world and suppose

\forall\bar x\, .\,Y[\bar x]\to\Box Z[\bar x].

Let PH. By (4), H=H_{\bar a} for some \bar a; then Y[\bar a], so \Box Z[\bar a], and \Box(H=H_{\bar a}). By injectivity of the codes,

\Box\forall\bar x\, .\,H=H_{\bar x}\to Z[\bar x].

So weak inextensibility of P, applied to \lambda H\, .\,\forall\bar x\, .\,H=H_{\bar x}\to Z[\bar x], gives \Box\forall H\, .\,PH\to\forall\bar x\, .\,(H=H_{\bar x}\to Z[\bar x]). Taking H:=H_{\bar x} gives Y\le Z. Since \operatorname{Rigid}(P) and (4) are necessary, this holds at every world, so Y is rigid. \square

Proof of the theorem

By Lemma 4 and the remark after it, BF holds at every relational type. For a tuple \bar\sigma, apply Lemma 5 with \sigma:=\delta=\bar\sigma t (a relational type): necessarily, Rigid Comprehension holds for properties of type \delta t. The necessitation of Lemma 6 then gives, necessarily, Rigid Comprehension for relations of type \bar\sigma t. \square

Remarks

  1. Lemma 4 strengthens the recorded Strong Atomicity, □Rigid Comprehension and Rigid Power imply BF at relational types by weakening □Rigid Comprehension to Rigid Comprehension. Including \bot in the cover is what makes every cut rigid at every world (Lemma 2), so the argument needs no comprehension at other worlds.
  2. With □Strong Atomicity and the recorded result that □Strong Atomicity and □Rigid Comprehension imply □BF at relational types, this gives □BF at relational types. That settles the conjecture from Bacon’s Theorem 8.2 (□Strong Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension imply □BF at relational types), whose book proof has a gap at non-actual worlds, from weaker premises.
  3. BF at e does not follow: the recorded model Full action model: two-object chain, one individual becoming two satisfies both premises and fails it.
  4. With the recorded C theorem that Rigid Comprehension implies Boolean Completeness, the theorem also gives □Boolean Completeness.
