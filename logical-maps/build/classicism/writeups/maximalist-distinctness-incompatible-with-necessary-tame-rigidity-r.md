## Premises

- **Maximalism (pure).** Every closed pure identity not provable in C is false. The non-theorem condition is metalinguistic.
- **□Tame Rigidity.** Every closed instance of Tame Rigidity is necessary. Box the entire object-variable closure; retain the same type range and other schema side conditions.

## Conclusion

- **⊥.** These premises cannot all hold together.

## Proof

Tame Rigidity fails in finite-support-two-object-all-maps, a model of C, so some closed pure instance of it is not a theorem of C and its negation is consistent with C. The possibility form of Maximalism (pure) makes that negation possible, and the boxed principle makes the instance necessary.
