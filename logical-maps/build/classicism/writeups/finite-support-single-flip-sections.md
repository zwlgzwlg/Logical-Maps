## Package

- **ND.** Distinct things of any type are necessarily distinct.
- **Atomicity (type t).** Every non-bottom proposition has an atom below it.
- **□Atomicity (type t).** Necessarily, every non-bottom proposition has an atom below it.
- **Weakly Rigid Power.** If a relation is weakly rigid, so is the property of being a weakly rigid relation that entails it.
- **Arithmetic Necessitism.** Given that necessarily zero is not a successor and successor is injective on numbers, the numbers at $\sigma$ form a rigid property with necessarily distinct instances. One sentence for each type $\sigma$.
- **Axiom of Infinity (type e).** There are not finitely many individuals. No finite cardinality holds of the universal property, so the domain of the type cannot be exhausted by finitely many steps from the empty property.
- **Infinity Schema (type e).** There are arbitrarily many pairwise distinct entities of this fixed type; one sentence for each positive integer n.
- **Axiom of Infinity (type t).** There are not finitely many propositions. No finite cardinality holds of the universal property, so the domain of the type cannot be exhausted by finitely many steps from the empty property.
- **Infinity Schema (type t).** There are arbitrarily many pairwise distinct entities of this fixed type; one sentence for each positive integer n.
- **□Intensional Choice.** Necessarily, if a property is necessarily instantiated, then some property entailing it is necessarily uniquely instantiated.
- **□Relational Choice.** Necessarily, every serial binary relation has a functional subrelation.
- **□Transversal Choice.** Necessarily, every equivalence relation, at any type including e, has a transversal, that is, a property with exactly one instance in each of its cells.
- **Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.** Every second-order analytical sentence is either true in every real number structure in every possible world or false in every real number structure in every possible world. One sentence for each type $\sigma$ and each second-order analytical sentence.
- **Strong Actuality.** There is a true strong atom: a true proposition that necessarily entails every proposition or its negation.
- **□Strong Actuality.** Necessarily, there is a true strong atom: a true proposition that necessarily entails every proposition or its negation.
- **Transversal Choice.** Every equivalence relation, at any type including e, has a transversal, that is, a property with exactly one instance in each of its cells.
- **¬ Boolean Completeness (type t).** Every property of propositions has a greatest lower bound.
- **¬ □ND (type t).** Necessarily, distinct propositions are necessarily distinct.

## Definition

An ideally full action model in the sense of Classicism, Appendix D, over a category with two objects, each carrying the ideal of finite sets: a model of C by Proposition D.4. The member's construction and evaluation point follow.

An ideally full finitely pinned action model over a two-object category,. $W_0$ has individuals $\mathbb N$ and $W_1$ has individuals $\mathbb N\times\{0,1\}$. Let $f_*(n):=(n,0)$, and let $f_k$ agree with $f_*$ except that $f_k(k)=(k,1)$. The arrows are the identity of $W_0$ (the only arrow $W_0\to W_0$); the sections $f_j$, $j\in\{*\}\cup\mathbb N$, from $W_0$ to $W_1$; the projection $\pi(n,i)=n$ from $W_1$ to $W_0$; and the identity of $W_1$ with the retractions $e_j:=f_j\circ\pi$ from $W_1$ to itself. They compose by $\pi\circ f_j=1$, $e_i\circ f_j=f_i$, $e_i\circ e_j=e_i$ and $\pi\circ e_j=\pi$. The evaluation point is the identity of $W_0$.

## Arguments

### Holds ND.

At every type. Every arrow out of $W_0$ has the left inverse $\pi$, as $\pi\circ f_j=1$, so transport along it is injective at every type: $f_j\cdot X=f_j\cdot Y$ gives $X=\pi\cdot f_j\cdot X=\pi\cdot f_j\cdot Y=Y$.

### Holds Atomicity (type t), □Atomicity (type t).

A proposition at $W_0$ is a truth value at the identity together with a set of sections, and it is pinned down by a finite $S$ just when it does not distinguish $f_*$ from the $f_k$ with $k\notin S$, these being the sections agreeing with $f_*$ on $S$ (their transports along $\pi$ and the $e_i$ are all equal). So its sections form a finite set of flips $f_k$, or $f_*$ with a cofinite set of them. The atoms are $\{1_{W_0}\}$ and the $\{f_k\}$, and every nonzero proposition contains one. At $W_1$, likewise, $e_*$ agrees with $e_k$ on a finite $S$ for all $k$ outside the finite set $\pi[S]$, so a proposition containing $e_*$ contains some $e_k$; and $\{1_{W_1}\}$, $\{\pi\}$ (the one arrow to $W_0$) and the $\{e_k\}$ are atoms. So Atomicity at type $t$ holds at both objects.

### Fails Boolean Completeness (type t).

Let $X$ of type $tt$ have value $\{\{f_{2n}\}:n\in\mathbb N\}$ at the identity and $\varnothing$ at the sections. It is pinned down by $\varnothing$: two sections transport it alike, along $\pi$ to the value at the identity, as $\pi\circ f_j=1$, and to $\varnothing$ otherwise. An upper bound of its instances contains infinitely many sections, so it contains $f_*$ and cofinitely many flips, among them some $f_{2m+1}$; removing $\{f_{2m+1}\}$ gives a smaller upper bound. So $X$ has no least upper bound, and $\lambda p\, .\,X\neg p$ has no greatest lower bound.

### Fails □ND (type t).

At $W_1$, $\{1_{W_1}\}$ is a proposition (identity-singletons) distinct from $\bot$, and its transport along $\pi$ is $\bot$, since $1_{W_1}$ is not of the form $k\circ\pi$. So ND at type $t$ fails at $W_1$, and it is not necessary at $W_0$.

### Holds Weakly Rigid Power.

At $W_0$, at every type. Write a relation $Y$ as its intension $k\mapsto Y_k$, put $X_0(E)_k:=k\cdot E$, and let $B_N(E)_k$ be the union of the $k^{\prime}\cdot E$ for $k^{\prime}$ agreeing with $k$ on $N$ and having its codomain; $B_N(E)$ is pinned down by $N$, so in the domain at either object. (1) An $X_0(E)$ in the domain is rigid: its transports are $X_0$ of the transported extensions, persistent and below whatever holds necessarily of their members. (2) A weakly rigid $Y$ with extension $E$ has $X_0(E)\le Y\le B_N(E)$ for every finite $N$, by weak persistence and weak inextensibility. (3) If $x$ is in $B_N(E)_u$ for every $N$, then $x=u\cdot a$ for some $a\in E$: for $N$ large, $x=u^{\prime}\cdot a_N$ with $u^{\prime}$ agreeing with $u$ on $N$ and $r_{u^{\prime}}$ agreeing with $r_u$ on a pin of $x$, so $a_N=r_{u^{\prime}}\cdot x=r_u\cdot x=:a$, and taking $N$ to contain a pin of $a$ also, $x=u^{\prime}\cdot a=u\cdot a$. So a weakly rigid relation at $W_0$ is $X_0$ of its extension, and rigid: Tame Rigidity. (4) Let $F=X_0(E)$, $h$ an arrow out of $W_0$, and $X$ weakly rigid at its target with $X\le h\cdot F$ and extension $D$. Applying (2) at the target and (3) to the arrows $k\circ h$, $X=X_0(D)$, and $D=h\cdot A$ for $A:=\{a\in E:h\cdot a\in D\}$; $X_0(A)$, the transport of $X$ along $r_h$, is in the domain, weakly rigid and below $F$, with $h\cdot X_0(A)=X$. So the property of being weakly rigid and below $F$ has at each world the transports of its extension at the identity: it is $X_0$ of that extension, which is rigid by (1). Weakly Rigid Power holds at $W_0$.

### Holds Arithmetic Necessitism.

The numbers at a type $\sigma$ are the instances of $\mathbb N_\sigma$, the least property containing $\mathbf 0_\sigma$ and closed under $\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma$ (Background); the numerals $\mathbf 0_\sigma$, $\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma\mathbf 0_\sigma,\ldots$ are closed terms, so each denotes at every object, and transport along an arrow carries its denotation at the source to its denotation at the target. ### Holds Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.

Let $v$ be any object reachable from the evaluation point and $\mathfrak R$ a real number structure at $v$ (Background, Analysis). Its completeness is tested against the subproperties of its carrier present at $v$, and by extensional fullness every subset of the carrier's extension there is the extension at the identity of one of them; so the carrier, with the extensions of $\le$, $+$ and $\times$, is a complete ordered field in the sense of the metatheory and, by Huntington's theorem, a theorem of ZF, is isomorphic to the real numbers. For the same reason the extension of $N$, the least subproperty containing $0$ and closed under adding $1$, is its set of natural numbers, that set being the extension of a subproperty. A second-order analytical sentence $A(\mathfrak R)$ quantifies over the relations among entities of the structure's type at $v$, applied to the structure's elements, and their extensions at the identity are all the relations on the carrier, again by extensional fullness. So $A(\mathfrak R)$ holds at $v$ exactly when $A$ holds in the real numbers under full second-order semantics, which depends neither on $v$ nor on $\mathfrak R$. Hence $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to A(\mathfrak R)$ holds when $A$ is true of the reals and $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to\neg A(\mathfrak R)$ when it is false: every instance of the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis holds, and with it the Necessity of Analysis and the Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis, as recorded. No choice is used.

### Holds Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.

Let $v$ be reachable from the evaluation point. If every type is finite at $v$ there is no real number structure there, an ordered field having the infinitely many distinct elements $0$, $1$, $1+1$, and so on. Otherwise let $D$ be as in the condition. Carry the real numbers onto $D$ by a bijection: the carrier, order, sum, product, $0$, $1$ and natural numbers are sets of tuples from $D$, so extensions at $v$ of entities there, which form a real number structure $\mathfrak R^*$ at $v$, its completeness and the leastness of its natural numbers being tested against subproperties whose extensions are all the subsets of $D$. Its second-order quantifiers, applied to tuples from $D$, range over all relations on $D$, so for each second-order analytical sentence $A$, $A(\mathfrak R^*)$ holds at $v$ iff $A$ is true of the real numbers under full second-order semantics. Any real number structure $\mathfrak R$ at $v$, of any type, is isomorphic to $\mathfrak R^*$ by a relation in the domain there (Huntington's theorem, a theorem of C: Background, Analysis), and $A(\mathfrak R)\leftrightarrow A(\mathfrak R^*)$ is a theorem of C given such an isomorphism, relations on one carrier being carried to the other by comprehension. So at every reachable object every real number structure satisfies exactly the second-order analytical sentences true of the reals, and each instance $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R) \to A(\mathfrak R)$ or $\Box\forall\mathfrak R\,.\,\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to\neg A(\mathfrak R)$ of the Necessity of Second-Order Analysis holds, with the Necessity of Analysis and the Non-contingency of the Continuum Hypothesis. ### Holds Strong Actuality, □Strong Actuality.

Let $V$ be the evaluation object or the target of an arrow out of it, and $w:=\{1_V\}$, in the domain at $V$. It is true at $1_V$. Under an arrow $f$ out of $V$ it becomes $\{k : k\circ f=1_V\}$, the set of retractions of $f$, which has at most one member and so lies below $q$ or below $\neg q$ for every proposition $q$ at $f$'s target. So Strong Actuality holds at $1_V$. A closed sentence holds at an arrow iff it holds at the identity of the arrow's target, so Strong Actuality holds at every world after the evaluation point, and □Strong Actuality holds there.

*General argument `arguments/strong-actuality-unique-retractions`. It requires that At the evaluation object, and at the target of every arrow out of it, the singleton of the identity arrow is in the domain. Here: $\{1_{W_0}\}$ is pinned down by $\emptyset$, the identity being the only arrow from $W_0$ to $W_0$, and $\{1_{W_1}\}$ by $\{(0,0),(0,1)\}$, on which no $e_j$ agrees with the identity.
