## Definition

Proposition D.5, part 3 (p. 77): the one-object category whose arrows are all the monotone maps of $\mathbb N$. In every part, $W_0^\ddagger$ is the set of finite subsets of $\mathbb N$ and the model is ideally full: a proposition is a set of arrows whose membership depends only on the arrows’ values on some finite set of numbers (it is pinned down by that set), and an entity of a higher type is an applicative behaviour profile pinned down by a finite set in the same sense. There is a single individual, which every arrow fixes; the arrows still act on $\mathbb N$, which supplies the pinning sets. Propositions and properties are thus about finitely many numbers and indifferent to how the arrows treat the rest. Evaluation point: the sole object, at the identity arrow.

Interpretation of Σ, fixed for this record: every constant of Σ is relational, and each denotes the top element of its type.

*A member of the group Finite-support action models on one object, which supplies this definition with the record's settings, and the arguments marked as shared.*

### Fails Truths are ND-necessary.

The shift $h$ that fixes $0,\ldots,m-1$ and adds one to everything from $m$ on is a monotone injection, so for any true $q$ pinned down by a finite $Y$ a monotone $k$ with $k(hy)=y$ on $Y$ exists and $k\circ h\in q$; thus $\Diamond q$ holds at $h$, while the true proposition that $m$ is fixed is false there.

### Fails Vicinity.

A true $w$ pinned down by a finite set $S$ contains every arrow agreeing with the identity on $S$, among them the monotone map equal to the identity up to $M$ and constant at $M+1$ beyond it, for $S\subseteq[0,M]$, one that collapses two numbers $a\ne b$ outside $S$. The proposition $\{k : ka\ne kb\}$ (with the natural numbers as individuals, that $a$ and $b$ are distinct) is true, and it is possible at no arrow that collapses them, since a collapse persists under composition, so $w$ does not entail its possibility.

### Holds Truths are two-hat necessary.

Write $\mathrm{fix}_N$ for the arrows agreeing with the identity on the finite set $N$, the least true proposition pinned by $N$, and $\delta_N$ for the arrows injective on $N$. An arrow $v$ is in $\Diamond\,\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}$ iff some monotone $x$ has $x\circ v$ fixing $[n]$, iff $v$ is injective on $[n]$; and $\Diamond\delta_{[n]}=\delta_{[n]}$. So $\Box_{\ne}p$ holds iff $p\supseteq\delta_{[n]}$ for some $n$, and the true proposition $\{h:h(0)=0\}$ is not ND-necessary, since the shift $m\mapsto m+1$ is injective. Two-hat necessity, however, is truth: $u\in\hat\Diamond\,\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}$ requires, for every $m$, a monotone $v$ injective on $[m]$ with $v\circ u$ fixing $[n]$; injectivity of $v$ on $[m]$ gives $v(j)\ge j$ for $j\le m$, so $i=v(u(i))\ge u(i)\ge i$ and $u$ fixes $[n]$. Thus $\hat\Diamond\,\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}=\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}$, every truth is two-hat necessary, and $\{h:h(0)=0\}$ is two-hat necessary but not ND-necessary. (The verdicts this argument also establishes but does not record are derived by the engine.)

### Holds Rigid Power.

Write $X_0(E)$ for the intension $k\mapsto k\cdot E$. For $E$ pinned down uniformly by a finite $S_0$, $X_0(E)$ is in the domain, pinned down by $S_0$, and so is $X_0(T)$ for every $T\subseteq E$ and $X_0(k\cdot E)$ for every arrow $k$. The extension at $k$ of the power property $\lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F$ is the set of rigid $X$ with $X\le k\cdot F$, the bound variable untransported and the parameter transported; by the condition, for rigid $F=X_0(S)$ it is $\{X_0(T'):T'\subseteq k\cdot S\}$, and transporting the extension at the identity along $k$ gives $\{X_0(k\cdot T):T\subseteq S\}$, the same set, since $T'\subseteq k\cdot S$ is $k\cdot T$ for $T:=\{s\in S:k\cdot s\in T'\}$. So the power property is $X_0$ of its own extension, which is pinned down uniformly, hence in the domain and rigid. In these models the rigid relations are thus (a) the haecceities, (b) the properties "being pinned down by $S$", which are $X_0(\operatorname{Pin}(S))$, and (c) the disjunctions, finite or infinite, of haecceities of entities pinned down by one finite set; the boxed form follows from No Pure Contingency.

### Holds Arithmetic Necessitism.

The numbers at a type $\sigma$ are the instances of $\mathbb N_\sigma$, the least property containing $\mathbf 0_\sigma$ and closed under $\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma$ (Background); the numerals $\mathbf 0_\sigma$, $\operatorname{Suc}_\sigma\mathbf 0_\sigma,\ldots$ are closed terms, so each denotes at every object, and transport along an arrow carries its denotation at the source to its denotation at the target. At any object $v$ reachable from the evaluation point, extensional fullness gives a property whose extension at $v$ is exactly the numerals there; it contains $\mathbf 0_\sigma$ and is closed under successor, so the numbers at $v$ are exactly the numerals, every numeral being an instance of every inductive property. Persistence of $\mathbb N_\sigma$ and, given $\Box I^*_\sigma$, the necessary distinctness of the numbers are theorems of C (the proof of necessary-rigid-comprehension-implies-arithmetic-necessitism). Inextensibility, $\Box\forall X\,.\,(\forall n\,.\,\mathbb N_\sigma n\to\Box Xn)\to\mathbb N_\sigma\le X$: at a reachable $v$ and for an arrow $j$ from $v$ to $u$, the numbers at $u$ are the numerals there, which are the transports along $j$ of the numbers at $v$, so any $X$ that necessarily holds of the numbers at $v$ holds at $u$ of the numbers there. So $\mathbb N_\sigma$ is rigid, and Arithmetic Necessitism holds at every type. No choice is used.

### Holds Arithmetic Necessitism.

### Holds □Intensional Choice.

### Holds □Self-atoms are atoms, □Self-atoms are strong atoms. Fails Self-Atomicity (type t).

So the projection is a true atom at $k$’s target, which the condition excludes. Hence no reachable object has a self-atom, and Self-atoms are atoms and Self-atoms are strong atoms hold vacuously at every type and every reachable object, while Self-Atomicity (type t) fails at the evaluation point, the top proposition having no self-atom below it.

### Holds Necessity of Second-Order Analysis.
