Wreath-supported cofinite entrance over old and new individuals

GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 30 October 2026.

This ZFC construction has Strong Atomicity and internal Boolean Completeness at every relational type, necessary Atomicity and necessary Rigid Power at every relational type, necessary Boolean Completeness at type t, and current propositional Barcan. Necessary Inextensible Comprehension, necessary all-type Boolean Completeness, relational Barcan, and necessary propositional Barcan fail. The exact witnesses and their modal scopes are given below.

The tail is the base-individual model [[symmetric-old-new-individuals]]. The entrance uses a global old-point action and independent new-point actions on its replicas. Completeness quantifies over admitted families, rather than arbitrary external subsets. All types are the map’s fixed relational types; write a relational type as bar-sigma t. A table at that type is a subset of the full product of its already constructed argument domains, with the empty tuple used at type t. Equality compares whole entities. A relational signature, if present, is interpreted by top at every constant.

1. The unchanged tail and the modification lemma

Let E have individuals N and O have N disjoint union N’, with the first summand called old. Let i:E→O be inclusion. The arrows E→E are all injections, E→O all injections with old range, O→E all injections, and O→O the disjoint classes G_O and K, where G_O consists of sort-preserving permutations and K of old-range injections. At E use all permutations as symmetries, and at O use G_O. The old relational domains consist of the symmetric profiles pinned by a finite set of individuals, as in the cited tail construction. Transport is precomposition of the outgoing-arrow rows, including typed argument transport. Old outgoing arrows, domains, application and quantification remain fixed.

Here are the tail facts used to define the entrance. An E-entity a is determined by A=a_{1_E},\qquad B=a_i, where, for some finite S⊆E, A is invariant under Fix_E(S) and B under Fix_O(iS). Conversely every such pair is admitted. For h:E→E, extend h restricted to S to an E permutation and take its image of A; for h:E→O extend the corresponding finite old partial map to an O symmetry and take its image of B. Invariance makes these definitions independent of the extensions. They prove the pair characterization and a\le a'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\subseteq A'\ \hbox{and}\ B\subseteq B'. Every finite tuple of old entities has a finite pin; individual arguments pin themselves.

Modification lemma. Given a and any O-table C invariant under Fix_O(Q) for some finite Q, define an O-profile b by b_g=g[C]\quad(g\in G_O),\qquad b_h=a_{hi}\quad(h:O\to E\hbox{ or }h\in K). These rows are symmetric. They are pinned by iS union Q union {v}, where v is any new individual. For O-target arrows the point v distinguishes G_O from K; within G_O use Q, and within an exiting class use iS. E-target arrows are all exiting. Thus b belongs to the actual old O-domain, not a larger raw-profile domain. Every further composite following an exit is still exiting, so as whole entities h_*b=(hi)_*a\quad(h\hbox{ exiting}). If C=B, covariance supplies the good rows too, hence b=i_*a. This default entity is independent of the auxiliary marker v and is fixed by every new-point permutation. Every singleton tuple table qualifies, using the union of the tuple entries’ finite pins.

2. Entrance category and root domains

Add R, with no nonidentity arrow ending there. Its individuals are E=N. Add distinct arrows y_u:R→E for every u:E→E, x_u:R→O for every u:E→O, and z_(n,g):R→O for every n∈N and g∈G_O. The y/x arrows postcompose as the corresponding arrows out of E. Set k z_{n,g}=z_{n,kg}\quad(k\in G_O),\qquad h z_{n,g}=y_{hgi}\ \hbox{or}\ x_{hgi}\quad(h\hbox{ exiting}), according to the target. Before the first exit, the G_O labels multiply; at the first exit insert i and forget n. Exits cannot return to G_O, so both bracketings of every composite give the same rule. This proves associativity. Individual maps are u on y/x and gi on z. They respect composition. Distinct x_i and z_(n,1_O) may have the same individual function; the category is not required to act faithfully on individuals.

A root entity X at a relational type has:

  • a current table E_R;
  • an admitted E-entity a, with pair (A,B);
  • O-tables C_n, each with some finite old/new pin, such that C_n=B outside a finite set.

The current table is required to have the support specified in §3. Apply the modification lemma to a and each C_n, obtaining b_n. Define whole-entity transports by (y_u)_*X=u_*a,\qquad (x_u)_*X=u_*a,\qquad (z_{n,g})_*X=g_*b_n. The modification lemma gives precisely the first-exit equation; all other composition laws follow from the tail’s transport. Outside the finite exceptional set, b_n=i_*a as whole entities. These rules define the root domain recursively on argument types.

3. Wreath action, finite supports and C closure

For s∈Sym(N), write shat for its action on old O-individuals, fixing new ones. Conjugate old arrows by s and shat. Act on root individuals by s, on a by s_a, on C_n by shat[C_n], and on E_R by the recursively defined image action. Relabel the entrance arrows by y_u\mapsto y_{sus^{-1}},\quad x_u\mapsto x_{\widehat s u s^{-1}},\quad z_{n,g}\mapsto z_{n,\widehat s g\widehat s^{-1}}. The equation shat i=is verifies composition and individual compatibility. Old symmetry covariance identifies conjugation of a whole typed profile with its typed transport; the modified O-entity is shat_b_n. Thus transport is equivariant at every type, including higher-type arguments. At the root identity the target action is the recursive root action, not the trivial action.

Independently let pi permute N, the replica indices, and choose an arbitrary sequence nu_n of permutations of N’, extended by identity on old points. These fix the old tail, root individuals and y/x arrows, and act by z_{n,g}\mapsto z_{\pi n,g\nu_n^{-1}},\quad C'_{\pi n}=\nu_n[C_n],\quad a'=a. On E_R use the recursive image action. Since nu_n i=i, every exiting composite and individual map is unchanged. The new auxiliary entity is nu_nb_n, so (g nu_n^{-1})(nu_nb_n)=gb_n. Postcomposition by a good symmetry is multiplication on the left and commutes with the inverse factor on the right. Composition of two local actions (pi,nu), then (sigma,mu), has index sigma pi and local factor mu_(pi n) nu_n. This also gives inverses. Global old actions commute with these local new actions. There is no finite restriction on the sequence of new permutations.

A finite support is (P,J,(Q_n)), where P⊆N, J⊆N and each Q_n⊆N’ for n∈J are finite. Its stabilizer fixes P pointwise under s, fixes each n∈J under pi, and fixes Q_n pointwise under nu_n. Outside J the new permutations are unrestricted. This is an actual subgroup; unions and conjugates of finite supports again have this form. Require E_R to be fixed by one such stabilizer, on the product of the already defined root argument domains.

Every whole root entity has finite support. Combine a pin of a, a support of E_R and the finitely many pins of exceptional C_n. Include their old parts in P, their indices in J and their new parts in Q_n. Default tables are fixed by every new permutation. The resulting support fixes all the data. Root individuals have singleton old supports, so every finite tuple has finite support. The actions preserve the recursively defined domains and act bijectively on them.

To check term closure, fix a finite environment of free root parameters. Its value at y_(1_E) is an admitted old E-entity a_t. Outside the finite union of those parameters’ exceptional replica sets, the whole O-environment is the i-image of the E-environment. Old term naturality therefore makes the whole term value i_a_t there. This assertion also applies to an abstraction before any bound argument is selected: its quantified arguments range over the full old target domain. At exceptional indices the ordinary old term value has a finite pin, and naturality at exits gives exactly the rows required by the modification lemma. Hence the resulting replica tables are admitted and eventually equal to the table of i_a_t.

At the root, the established interpretation automorphisms give term equivariance by induction. Application, identity and Boolean clauses respect the actions; quantifiers reindex the entire appropriate root or old domain, and modality reindexes arrows. A finite support for the free environment therefore supports the current term table. No common support for all quantified entities is imposed. This proves lambda and full-quantifier closure. The usual restricted-profile action semantics then validates the H laws, beta/eta and Logical Equivalence for H-theorem equivalences, establishing C. No optional model principle is used to satisfy Logical Equivalence’s theoremhood condition.

4. Exact order and internal Boolean Completeness

The free coordinates determining entailment at R are E_R, A and every C_n. Inclusion in all these coordinates is necessary. It is sufficient: choose n outside the two entities’ finite exceptional sets to get B⊆B’; the old pair criterion gives a≤a’, and all the modification and symmetry rows preserve the inclusions. Thus it checks every future row as well as the current one.

Let K be the current instance-set of an admitted family of entities of any fixed relational type. Let (P,J,Q) support that family. Form the coordinate unions E_R^*=\bigcup_{X\in K}E_R^X,\qquad A^*=\bigcup_{X\in K}A_X,\qquad D_n=\bigcup_{X\in K}C_n^X. A stabilizer element permutes K, so E_R^* retains its finite support. Global old permutations fixing P make A^* invariant under Fix_E(P). Replica transpositions outside J show D_n=D_m as whole tables whenever n,m are outside J; call this common table B^. An arbitrary new permutation acting only at such an n also fixes the support and permutes K. Consequently B^ is invariant under every new-point permutation. Together with global old Fix(P) invariance this is exactly Fix_O(iP) invariance. At an exceptional n∈J, D_n is invariant under Fix_O(iP union Q_n). Therefore (A*,B*) is an admitted old E-pair, and the D_n form an allowed eventually constant sequence. These data define a root entity.

Every member of K lies below it. Any admitted upper bound contains each coordinate union, so the exact order criterion makes it the least upper bound. Complements give greatest lower bounds, also for empty families. This is native internal Boolean Completeness at all relational types. It does not assert that transport preserves these joins, or that an arbitrary externally chosen family is admitted.

5. Strong Atomicity and necessary Atomicity

In the old tail, the proposition w_E=Hom(E,E) is strong: an E→E transport preserves it, and an E→O transport gives Hom(O,E), which returns to w_E along O→E and remains itself along either kind of O→O arrow. Each nonzero image is an atom of the full proposition algebra. Similarly w_O=G_O stays itself along G_O and becomes bottom after an exit. Both propositions are true at their own objects.

If w is a strong proposition and H_alpha the tuple haecceity, any nonzero w∧H_alpha is a strong relation. At a successor, take any admitted target relation Z. The strong proposition there decides the target proposition Z(transported alpha). Necessary equality substitution gives entailment of Z or its complement for the relation at every further coordinate. This places the universal quantifier over Z inside the box and uses the full target domain, without requiring a source preimage for Z or alpha.

The three coordinate-atom forms at R are:

  • one current root tuple, with all future data empty;
  • one E tuple in A, with B, all C_n and E_R empty;
  • one O tuple in a single C_m, with every other free coordinate empty.

They are admitted. In the first case use the root tuple’s finite full-group support. In the second use the old tuple’s finite pin. In the third include m, the tuple’s finite old pin and its locally tagged finite new pin. Exact coordinate order makes each an ordinary root atom, supplying the current decision clause. Its future images are, respectively, zero; images of w_E∧H_alpha; or images of w_O∧H_beta, killed by every exit. The preceding argument supplies every successor decision clause. They are therefore native strong atoms. Every nonzero root entity has a nonempty free coordinate and contains one of them. Strong Atomicity follows at every relational type.

For necessary Atomicity, it remains to check the unchanged old domains. Their finite-pin proof can be stated as follows. For a pin M, the symmetric profiles pinned by M form a complete atomic Boolean algebra. Its classes consist of pairs (g alpha,h’) with h’ agreeing with gh on M. Let P pin the tuple alpha. Enlarge M so h(M) contains the part of P reachable by arrows of h’s kind and, when the source is O, M includes a new point. Then this class is stable under every finite enlargement of M: the extra finite image matching extends to a sort-preserving target symmetry fixing P and h(M). Thus the class is an atom in the entire admitted domain. Given a nonzero entity, move an arrow on finitely many fresh source points to cover that reachable part of P without changing its existing pin; its class then supplies an atom below the entity. This works at every relational type at both E and O. Together with root Strong Atomicity, and since these are all target objects, it proves necessary all-type Atomicity at R.

6. Propositional completeness and current Barcan

An E-proposition has two bits (a,b), for the E-target and O-target arrow classes. An O-proposition has three bits (a,c,b), for Hom(O,E), G_O and K. These describe the whole four- and eight-element proposition domains. Hence BC_t holds at both old objects; root BC includes t, yielding necessary BC_t.

A root proposition has a current bit r, an E bit a, and replica bits c_n eventually equal to b. Every such eventually constant sequence is admitted. Its transports are y_u:(a,b),\qquad x_u:(a,b,b),\qquad z_{n,g}:(a,c_n,b). Thus every y_u and every fixed z_(n,g) is onto the entire target proposition domain. For z, prescribe its exceptional bit at n and b elsewhere. The x_u arrows are not onto.

Fix any admitted root F^(tt) and suppose every root proposition p necessarily has F. Reflexivity makes F universal currently. Surjectivity makes it universal over the full old proposition domains at every y and z arrow. In particular, every replica-current table C_n^F is all D_t(O). Choose n outside the finite exceptional set of this one F. Then its eventual table B_F is also all D_t(O). The old pair criterion says each x_u row is a symmetry image of B_F, so is universal too. All root successors therefore satisfy forall p F(p), proving current BF_t. The exceptional set belongs to F alone; this does not choose a common exception bound for the infinitely many possible bound propositions.

7. Failures inherited from the unchanged tail

Necessary Inextensible Comprehension fails at et. At E suppose an inextensible Y were currently coextensive with the universal individual property. If S pins Y, extend any E→E injection restricted to S to a permutation. Symmetry and pinning make Y universal at every E-target row. The admitted property D with rows all individuals at E and old individuals at O necessarily holds of each E individual. Inextensibility forces Y≤D, so Y_i has no new member v. At O each current Y_i-member is necessarily different from v because all individual arrows are injective. The predicate lambda x.x≠v is therefore a weak-inextensibility test, but after any m:O→E the universal row Y_(mi) contains m(v), which the test omits. This contradicts hereditary inextensibility. The accessible E world has no such coextension; necessary IC fails at R.

Necessary Boolean Completeness fails at et. At O, the admitted family of haecceities H_n of old individuals is defined using D. Suppose L with finite pin S were its least upper bound. Choose old n and new v outside S and an injection h:O→E. Choose h’ agreeing with h on S and with h’(n)=h(v). Since H_n≤L, the row L_(h’) contains h(v); pinning makes L_h contain it too. Yet lambda x.x≠v is an admitted upper bound for every old H_n and its h-row omits h(v). This contradicts leastness. O is accessible from R, so necessary all-type BC fails. The family used here is internal and admitted.

Necessary propositional Barcan fails. At E let q=G_O=(0,1,0), an O-proposition outside every E→O image (a,b,b). The E-pair consisting of all D_t(E) and D_t(O) minus {q} is admitted with empty pin, since target symmetries fix all proposition blocks. Every E-proposition necessarily has this property, but the full O quantifier includes q and falsifies its boxed universal. Thus BF_t fails at accessible E. The record need not add this redundant negative input: it is also derived from its other verdicts.

8. Relational Barcan fails already at et

For each new q∈O let H_q be its whole individual haecceity. The set NewHaec={H_q:q new} is invariant under every sort-preserving symmetry. Its complement is therefore an admitted empty-old-pin section table at type (et)t.

No root et-entity X can transport to H_q. At x_u its current extension is invariant under all permutations of new individuals and cannot equal {q}. At z_(n,g), the image is g_b_n. Choose another new r and any h:O→E, and put h’=h composed with the transposition of q,r. Since gi has old range, h’gi=hgi. The exit rule gives equal rows for g_b_n at h and h’. But H_q has rows {h(q)} and {h(r)}, which differ by injectivity. Thus even a good replica with current extension {q} cannot be the whole haecceity H_q.

Define F^( (et)t ) at R with universal current extension, old E-pair A=D_{et}(E),\qquad B=D_{et}(O)\setminus\mathrm{NewHaec}, and every replica table equal to B. The data have empty support under the actual full wreath action; B is fixed by all old/new symmetries. Its E-target rows are universal and its O-target rows exclude exactly NewHaec. Every root et-entity necessarily satisfies F by the no-image argument, while at x_i a new H_q belongs to the full target domain and fails F. This is BF_et failure, hence failure of the relational Barcan schema.

Separately, BF_e fails using the root individual property with universal current row, E-pair (all E, old O), and constant old-O replica tables. Every root individual transports to an old individual at O, whereas the full O quantifier includes new ones. No inference from relational BF to the individual instance is used.

9. Necessary Rigid Power

For a set D of tuples at an object V, write H_V(D) for the profile with row h[D] at each arrow h from V. This notation does not assert admission. If that profile is admitted, it is rigid: at any successor its later rows are the exact images of its current extension. Persistence follows, and any admitted predicate necessarily containing each current instance contains every later instance. This verifies weak inextensibility at every successor against the full target domains.

The goal is the native all-type statement that, for each rigid F, the admitted property P=\lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F is rigid. The quantifier over X includes every admitted relation of F’s type at each target, rather than only source images.

9.1. Exact tail facts

The rigid-power argument of [[symmetric-old-new-individuals]], parts (a)–(c), proves:

  • At E and O, a rigid relation with current extension D is H_V(D).
  • At E, a rigid relation’s current tuples share one finite pointwise pin S. Consequently H_E(D’) is admitted for every subset D’ of its current extension.
  • Every E-originating arrow acts injectively on every type; in particular ri:E→E is injective on tuples for every exit r:O→E.
  • Rigid Power holds at both E and O, for arbitrary admitted rigid relations there.

The pointwise pin assertion is specific to E. Its proof uses the E pair description and hereditary inextensibility. A test whose E table is universal and whose O table consists of tuples with an old-only finite pin first puts the rigid carrier’s O image inside that old-pinned part. If a current tuple’s least permutation support contains n outside the carrier’s pin S, choose a new point b and an exit r with rb=n and ri fixing S. At O test weak inextensibility by the profile whose h-row is the union of h’[F_i] over same-target h’ agreeing with h on a finite Q containing iS and b. This profile is Q-pinned and symmetric. It expresses the tuple as r’beta with beta old-pinned and r’b=n, so that tuple has a support omitting n, a contradiction. Finite partial-map extension then makes S a whole-tuple pin. Exact images of subsets are S-pinned and admitted; rigidity and current coextension identify the original relation with its exact-image profile.

At O the corresponding fact is weaker. A bijective exit j:O→E gives a common pin for the tuples in jF, so all exiting maps act on F’s current tuples through a fixed finite set, since every exit factors through j. Combine that finite exit-control set with F’s setwise symmetry support and a new marker separating good and exiting arrows. This admits H_O(F_current), which equals F by rigidity and current coextension. It need not make every original O tuple pointwise fixed by one common support. These are the precise tail facts used below, including for the empty argument tuple.

9.2. Every rigid root carrier is an exact-image profile

Write y=y_(1_E) and z_n=z_(n,1_O). Let F be rigid at R with current tuple set K and a finite wreath support (P,J,Q). Equivariance gives finite setwise supports for its images: yK is invariant under Fix_E(P), and z_nK under Fix_O(iP union Q_n), taking Q_n empty outside J. For the latter use global old permutations fixing P and an independent new permutation at n, with replica permutation identity. These are setwise assertions, not common pointwise pins for the tuples.

Fix n. The test with current row K, universal E component, and universal replica tables except the single table z_nK at n is admitted by the modification lemma. It necessarily contains every current K-member. Weak inextensibility gives F_(z_n)⊆z_nK; persistence gives the reverse inclusion. Covariance then gives equality at every z_(n,g).

For y use the test with current row K, E pair (yK, universal O table), and universal replica tables. The E pair is admitted with old pin P. This test necessarily contains K, including at arbitrary E-target injections u: for alpha∈yK, extend u on P together with a finite whole pin of alpha to one E permutation s. The test’s u-row is s[yK], and u(alpha)=s(alpha), so it contains u(alpha). This extends only a finite restriction of u. All O-target rows are universal; E-target exits from good replicas reduce to the same check. Inextensibility and persistence now give F_y=yK.

The whole old E component a=y_*F is rigid by heredity, so §9.1 makes it H_E(yK). Consequently every cone row is the corresponding image of K. Together with the good rows and the identity row already proved, this establishes F=H_R(K).

9.3. One cofinite bound works pointwise on the entire carrier

Admission of this one F supplies a finite set N_F outside which b_n=i_*a as whole entities. Since F is now an exact-image profile, their current rows give z_nK=i(yK)\qquad(n\notin N_F). Fix such n and q∈K. Choose q’∈K with z_nq=i(yq’). For any old exit r:O→E, the category law gives (ri)(yq)=r(z_nq)=(ri)(yq'). Injectivity of the E→E transport ri on tuples gives yq=yq’. Therefore z_nq=i(yq)\quad\hbox{for every }q\in K\hbox{ and every }n\notin N_F. The exception set was chosen before q. There is no intersection of infinitely many exception sets and no assumption that r is injective on arbitrary O higher-type entities.

9.4. Supported subsets have admitted exact-image profiles

Let K’⊆K be finitely supported under the full root wreath action. The common E pin of yK admits a’=H_E(yK’). Each z_nK’ has a finite O setwise support by §9.2 applied to K’. Use it as a current modification table, with E component a’, obtaining an admitted b’_n. For every exiting h the category identity yields h[z_nK']=(hi)[yK']. These are exactly the prescribed exit rows of b’_n. Its good rows are the symmetry images of z_nK’, so b’_n=H_O(z_nK’) as a whole entity. This proves admission without assuming arbitrary subsets of an O carrier are admitted.

For n outside N_F, §9.3 gives z_nK’=i(yK’) pointwise, so b’n=i*a’ as whole entities. Together with the supported current row K’, these are allowed root data. They admit H_R(K’), which is rigid and lies below F.

9.5. Every admitted target rigid subrelation lifts

Let h be any nonidentity root arrow and let Z be an arbitrary admitted target rigid subrelation of h_*F. By the tail characterization, Z=H_V(D), where D is its current extension and D⊆hK. Define K'=\{q\in K:hq\in D\}. Then hK’=D. We prove support of K’ without treating h as a finitely supported parameter.

For a cone arrow h=y_u or x_u, enlarge a support of K by the common pointwise E pin S of yK. A stabilizer action gamma has global old part s fixing S; its local part leaves y-images unchanged. Thus y(gamma q)=s(yq)=yq for every q∈K, so h(gamma q)=hq. This supports K’ for any such D and any allowed u, even an injection whose description or moved set is infinite.

For a good arrow h=z_(n,g), put D_0=g^{-1}[D]. Admission of Z and the old symmetry action give a finite old/new setwise pin Q for D_0. Enlarge a support of K by n, the old part of Q globally and its new part locally at n. A stabilizer fixes n and acts on z_nq by an O symmetry fixing Q. It therefore preserves membership in D_0 and supports K’. This support may depend on the particular target relation, as full target quantification permits; no common pointwise O support is claimed.

Section 9.4 now supplies X=H_R(K’), and naturality of exact images gives h_*X=H_V(hK')=H_V(D)=Z. These cases exhaust the nonidentity root arrows, so every admitted target rigid subrelation has a rigid root subrelation lift.

9.6. The power property and its necessary scope

The displayed P is an admitted C term. Let L be its current extension, the admitted rigid root subrelations of F. At each target its extension consists of all admitted rigid subrelations of h_F. Transport of a member of L remains rigid and supported below h_F, giving hL⊆P_h. Section 9.5 gives the reverse inclusion for every target member. Hence P_h=hL at every arrow, including the identity by definition. P is an already admitted exact-image profile and is therefore rigid by the opening criterion.

This proves root Rigid Power at every relational type, including t. The unchanged tail has Rigid Power at E and O for arbitrary relations there, not merely images of root relations. These are all accessible objects. Thus R satisfies necessary all-type Rigid Power; no necessitation of an optional current assumption has been used.

The support restrictions are essential. At R the current-only singleton predicates indexed by all root individuals form a rigid carrier, but its even-indexed external subfamily is not supported. At O, the good-block singleton predicates indexed by all new individuals form a rigid carrier with no common pointwise pin for its members. The proof uses supported inverse images of admitted target subrelations, and requires neither of those stronger subset or support claims.
