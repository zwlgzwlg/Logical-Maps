Supported Boolean model: finite tail subsets at a middle evaluation

GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 30 October 2026.

Necessary propositional Boolean Completeness, current all-type ND and Atomlessness can hold together while propositional Barcan and necessary ND fail. The same model also satisfies current all-type Rigid Power. The new completeness feature comes from restricting higher entities by finite support under permutations of a tail; the Rigid Power proof gives a stronger joint profile in that same construction.

All statements below concern the designated middle evaluation unless a box is displayed. In particular, current Rigid Power does not become a necessary schema merely because the auxiliary relations used in its proof are globally rigid.

1. Raw algebra and typed domains

Work in external ZFC. Let A be a complete homogeneous atomless Boolean algebra, for example the regular-open algebra of Cantor space, whose nonzero intervals are isomorphic to A. Put B=2\times A\times\mathcal P(\mathbb N). The Boolean operations are coordinatewise and the interior is

J(\epsilon,a,S)=\left(\mathbf1_{(\epsilon,a,S)=\top},\ \mathbf1_{a=1_A\text{ and }S\text{ cofinite}},\ \begin{cases}S,&a=1_A\text{ and }S\text{ cofinite},\\ \varnothing,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\right).

This is the modified interior of the earlier complete-Boolean-root construction. It is contractive, idempotent and preserves finite meets. Individuals form a singleton. All raw scalars in B are retained. The external group \operatorname{Aut}(A)\times\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb N) acts independently on the last two factors. A finite joint support consists of finitely many elements of A and finitely many subsets of \mathbb N. The tail stabilizer fixes each supported subset setwise; it need not fix its individual elements.

At every permitted function type admit all nonexpansive functions having a finite joint support under conjugation. The recursively defined open-valued equalities are

\delta_t(p,q)=J(p\leftrightarrow q),\qquad \delta_{\sigma\tau}(f,g)=J\bigwedge_x\delta_\tau(fx,gx),

with singleton equality at e. At a flattened relational type this is J of the meet of pointwise scalar equivalences. These descriptions agree: J applied to a meet of interiors equals J of the original meet, since any open lower bound of all arguments is below all their interiors. Nonexpansiveness means \delta_\sigma(x,y)\le\delta_\tau(fx,fy).

All displayed meets and joins in B are external ones. Quantifiers are interpreted by the meet or join of the whole admitted predicate range. Truth evaluations are the first coordinate, every principal tail coordinate n, and every middle ultrafilter U of A. The designated point is any such U; the quantifier argument below verifies ordinary truth there. All signature constants may be interpreted by their types’ top entities.

2. Closure and ordinary quantifier truth

The group acts by equality-preserving bijections at every type. Application and abstraction combine finitely many parameter supports. Boolean operations, J and equality are equivariant and nonexpansive. If an open o is below p\leftrightarrow q, then o\wedge p=o\wedge q and applying J gives o\wedge Jp=o\wedge Jq. This proves nonexpansiveness of the modal operation. An open bound below all instance equivalences remains below the equivalence of their infima or suprema. Thus both quantifiers are nonexpansive and have empty support, because group elements biject the full quantified domains and preserve arbitrary Boolean meets and joins. The standard induction proves parameterized lambda closure, including currying and all finite relational arities.

The haecceity x\mapsto\delta(x,a) is admitted. All admitted predicates respect \delta, while this haecceity supplies the reverse inequality in the definition of Leibniz identity. The stipulated equality is therefore native identity.

For material quantifier truth at a middle ultrafilter, a finite-attainment lemma is essential. If a set E\subseteq A is invariant under automorphisms fixing a finite set P, partition 1_A into the nonzero cells generated by P. Within a cell c, either all members are zero, some member contains the whole cell, or a member cuts it nontrivially. In the last case homogeneity supplies an automorphism fixing the cells and interchanging that part with its complement. The original and moved members cover c. Finitely many cells therefore show \bigvee E is the join of finitely many members. Dually \bigwedge E is a finite meet of members.

For any admitted predicate of any argument type, its middle range is invariant under the A-automorphisms fixing its finite A-support, using the identity tail permutation. The lemma applies regardless of tail support. Every middle ultrafilter consequently gives ordinary existential and universal truth. First and principal-tail evaluations preserve arbitrary meets and joins directly. Nonexpansiveness makes all these evaluations respect local equality, so the interpretations descend to their local domains. The evaluations separate B; H-theorems have value top at every assignment. Theoremhood-level Logical Equivalence and the lambda rules follow as in the supported nonexpansive Boolean construction. This verifies C, without treating arbitrary nonprincipal tail ultrafilters as ordinary truth evaluations.

3. Local scalar algebras and necessary scalar completeness

At a middle evaluation,

p=q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p_A=q_A\text{ and }p_N\triangle q_N \text{ is finite}.

The local Boolean algebra, with its native order, is therefore A\times\mathcal P(\mathbb N)/\mathrm{Fin}. At a principal n it is A\times2\times\mathcal P(\mathbb N\setminus\{n\})/\mathrm{Fin}; equality additionally demands agreement at n. At the first evaluation it is the entire complete raw algebra B. Every element of these quotients has an admitted raw scalar representative.

A finite tail support determines a finite partition into nonempty cells C_1,\ldots,C_m. For any family of classes in \mathcal P(\mathbb N)/\mathrm{Fin} invariant under the product of the full permutation groups of these cells, let C_* be the union of those infinite cells met infinitely by some family member. Then [C_*] is its least upper bound. Every member lies below it. If a bound [D] omits infinitely many points of an active cell, move infinitely many points of a witnessing member into that omission by a cell permutation. The moved member remains in the family and is not below [D], contradiction. Finitely many cells now give C_*\setminus D finite. This also handles the empty family.

For any admitted X:tt, its actual extension at a middle U is invariant under a finite tail stabilizer: those permutations leave the output’s A-coordinate unchanged. Its tail projection thus has the join just constructed, and its A-projection has a join by external completeness of A. Their pair is the least upper bound in the product quotient, represented by an admitted scalar. Material existential truth proves BC at t. At principal n, add the singleton subset \{n\} to the finite support and apply the same proof on the remaining tail, with the two complete factors. At the first evaluation external completeness suffices.

Thus every separating evaluation satisfies the closed BC_t instance. Its raw value is top, as is its interior: \Box\mathrm{BC}_t holds. This is internal completeness for represented families. The tail quotient is still externally incomplete; the old missing-join external family is excluded by the additional support condition.

4. ND, Atomlessness and Barcan

Every nonzero equality value is top or (0,1_A,S) for cofinite S. If two entities are distinct at a middle evaluation, their equality is raw bottom; its negation and the interior of its negation are top. Hence ND holds at every current type.

For each principal n, use p=\top and q=(1,1_A,\mathbb N\setminus\{n\}). Their equality is (0,1_A,\mathbb N\setminus\{n\}). At the first evaluation and at n it is false, while the interior of its complement is bottom. ND_t fails there. Since it holds at all middle evaluations, its closed raw value is exactly (0,1_A,\varnothing), whose interior is bottom. Necessary ND_t fails at the middle. The recorded theorem ND_t + BF_t implies necessary ND_t therefore refutes current BF_t.

The middle scalar algebra is atomless: split a nonzero A component in A, or an infinite pure-tail component into two infinite parts. Actuality fails. These claims concern the middle, not all evaluations.

5. Choice and Zorn fail at the middle

For a finite tail support, a tail class fixed by its full stabilizer is, modulo a finite set, a union of infinite partition cells. Otherwise an involution exchanging two infinite parts of a split cell moves the class. The fixed quotient algebra is consequently finite.

The admitted relation D(p,q)=(p=0\land q=0)\lor(p\ne0\land0<q<p) is serial by Atomlessness. For any putative functional subrelation choose an infinite cell C of its finite tail support and input (0,[C]). It is fixed by the stabilizer, so uniqueness forces the selected output class to be fixed too. The only fixed pure-tail classes below [C] are 0 and [C], contradicting the required proper nonzero part. This treats arbitrary Boolean-valued selectors and local quotient equality. Relational Choice fails at (t,t).

For Zorn, let z=(0,0_A,\mathbb N), \mathrm{Good}(p)=(p\ne0\land p\le z), and R(p,q)=\neg\mathrm{Good}(p)\lor(\mathrm{Good}(q)\land q\le p). Good classes are nonzero pure-tail classes ordered by reverse inclusion; all other classes lie below them. There is no maximal element, since every nonzero tail class has a proper nonzero part. Yet every admitted chain has an upper bound. Its good members are invariant under its finite tail stabilizer. An involution fixes each member of a chain, since otherwise it reverses a strict comparison with its image. The cell-swapping involutions therefore force every good member into the finite fixed-class algebra. A nonempty such chain has a least nonzero class in ordinary inclusion, which is an R-upper bound. If it has no good member, z bounds it. This verifies the native represented-chain hypothesis and refutes Zorn at t.

6. Two lemmas for Rigid Power

Write x\sim y for middle identity, equivalently nonzero \delta(x,y). This relation is independent of the middle ultrafilter. At a relational type it means equality of the entire A-output rows and equality of the entire n-output rows outside one finite set, over all raw arguments. Principal-n identity additionally requires equality of the entire n-row.

Uniform displacement. If a finite tail permutation g moves only K, then o_K=(0,1_A,\mathbb N\setminus K)\le\delta(x,gx) for every entity at every admitted type. The scalar case is coordinatewise. For a function, conjugation, input nonexpansiveness, the induction hypothesis at its output, and transitivity give the same bound for every argument. The full meet retains this open bound. Finite permutations consequently preserve all middle classes, and those fixing n preserve every principal-n class.

Finitary representative union. Suppose a set R of fixed-type tuples has a finite support under the full joint group, and every member is individually fixed by one common group of finite permutations preserving a finite tail partition. Then

H_R(x)=\bigvee_{r\in R}\delta(x,r)

is admitted and has raw rigidity value top. The set support and equality transitivity give finite support and nonexpansiveness. Its values are joins of opens, so persistence is top. Let Z be an admitted tester necessarily covering its current members. Add the finite tail support of Z to the common partition. Each Z(r) is fixed by every transposition within the refined cells. At a middle evaluation it has A-coordinate one and cofinite tail; it can omit only the union K of that partition’s finite cells. The same finite K works for every r. Nonexpansiveness transfers the bound through \delta(x,r), and its join proves H_R\le Z uniformly in all raw x. At principal n the premise additionally supplies the n-coordinate; at the first evaluation it supplies raw top. Weak inextensibility is true at every separating evaluation, hence raw top. This proves rigidity. The set’s full support and its members’ common finitary fixation are distinct requirements; both have been used.

7. Canonical representatives of a rigid carrier

Fix a relational F:\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_kt rigid at the middle, and a finite joint support (P_A,P_N). Let K_0 be the union of the finite cells generated by P_N. Persistence forces the A-coordinate of each F(x) to be zero or one. Its middle tuple extension E is independent of U and consists of middle equality classes.

For a class a=[p], the crisp test C_a(x), top iff x\sim p, is admitted. A support of p supports it; any nonzero input equality preserves the class, proving nonexpansiveness, including curried sections. For a\in E, let T_n(a) be the principal-n classes of tuples x\sim p with F(x)_n=1. Three observations determine these fibers:

  1. They are nonempty cofinitely. At a principal n where F is rigid, an empty fiber would make \neg C_a necessarily cover all its members. Weak inextensibility would give F\le\neg C_a, whose A-coordinate contradicts a\in E.
  2. They are singleton cofinitely. The admitted test \neg C_a\lor\delta(-,p) necessarily covers every middle member. One resulting open order bound forces every member of T_n(a) into [p]_n outside one finite set, uniformly over all raw tuples.
  3. A transposition within an infinite support cell fixes F and preserves a by uniform displacement. It bijects the corresponding fibers. Thus every fiber in that cell is a singleton, transported from one of the cofinally nonempty singleton fibers.

Write t_n(a) for this singleton class, for n\notin K_0. For fixed a it equals [p]_n cofinitely. There are no extra fibers from classes outside E in an infinite cell: persistence gives one finite exception outside which every F-member has A-membership one, and a transposition would move any proposed extra member beyond that exception without changing its middle class.

Define the representative r_a to keep p’s entire A-rows, set all first rows and rows on K_0 to zero, and realize the rows of t_n(a) at every n\notin K_0. Singleton individual components are left unchanged. This whole-row prescription is admitted by a finite construction. Choose finite D\subseteq\mathbb N\setminus K_0 outside which t_n(a)=[p]_n, and a tuple q_n in each exceptional fiber. Put c_n=(0,0_A,\{n\}), c_D=\bigvee_{n\in D}c_n, and m=(0,1_A,\mathbb N\setminus K_0). For each relational component use

r_i=m\wedge\left((\neg c_D\wedge p_i)\lor \bigvee_{n\in D}(c_n\wedge q_{n,i})\right),

with masks applied pointwise. This is a finite lambda term with admitted parameters. It realizes every prescribed entire row and has o_{D\cup K_0}\le\delta(p_i,r_i) uniformly in all raw inputs. Different choices give literally the same tuple because all rows are specified. Empty tuples and the empty extension cause no exception.

Uniqueness makes normalization equivariant under the full stabilizer of F: r_{ga}=gr_a. The set R_F=\{r_a:a\in E\} has F’s full finite support. A finite tail permutation fixing P_N fixes a, hence fixes r_a literally. The representative-union lemma makes H_{R_F} globally rigid. The fiber description gives equality with F on their whole A-rows and every whole n-row outside K_0, so

o_{K_0}\le\delta_\tau(F,H_{R_F}).

Unlike the finite sets used to realize individual representatives, this final exception depends only on the carrier F.

8. The whole canonical power is rigid

Let P_F(X)=\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F. For a middle member X let A_X\subseteq E be its middle tuple extension, and set Y_{A_X}=H_{\{r_a:a\in A_X\}}. The selected set has full finite support from both X and F; each representative still has the common individual finitary fixation supplied by F. Thus Y_{A_X} is admitted and globally rigid. It is currently coextensive with X, hence currently identical by the coextensive-weakly-rigid uniqueness lemma.

The set R_P of all these canonical Y_{A_X} has full finite support from F, since its stabilizer permutes all current rigid subrelations and their canonical constructions. Each member is individually fixed by the common finitary group. Therefore H_{R_P} is admitted and globally rigid. We must check whole equality with P_F, rather than only current coextension.

Fix n\notin K_0 and any raw X with P_F(X)_n=1. Its open defining conjuncts make it a middle member as well, rigid at n and below F=H_{R_F} there. Every principal-n member lies in a canonical fiber; persistence places its middle class in A_X. Conversely, if a\in A_X but X(r_a)_n=0, the unique-fiber property excludes every n-member of X from that middle class. The tester \neg C_a necessarily covers all its n-members, contradicting weak inextensibility through the A-coordinate. Hence X and Y_{A_X} are coextensive at n, and their rigidity gives identity there. Every fresh n-power member therefore belongs to H_{R_P}.

For the reverse inclusion each canonical Y_{A_X} is globally rigid and raw-below H_{R_F}, hence below F at n. Nonexpansiveness transfers its P_F-membership to every n-equal raw member. Consequently P_F and H_{R_P} agree on their whole A-rows and every whole n-row outside the same K_0, including all raw power arguments. Thus

o_{K_0}\le\delta_{\tau t}(P_F,H_{R_P}).

Middle identity with a rigid entity proves rigidity of P_F there. The argument applies to every relational bound type and every current rigid F, establishing current all-type Rigid Power. There is no common finite exception over all carriers, so this proof supplies no necessary RP verdict.
