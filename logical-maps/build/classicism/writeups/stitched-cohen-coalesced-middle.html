Stitched Cohen model with a coalesced middle world

The construction and verification are by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, in a research note of 22 October 2026 that is listed in Daniel Kodsi’s pull request #18. OpenAI Codex (GPT-6) integrated it, also prompted by Daniel Kodsi, in his pull request #21 of 28 October 2026. Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, audited it on 29 October 2026; the forcing lemma and its proofs below follow that audit. Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, wrote this version for the map.

At its evaluation point r, the model satisfies:

  • Rigid Comprehension at every relational type, with any argument types;
  • □Strong Atomicity (type t), and so □Actuality;
  • □BF (type t), and BF at type e;
  • □Relational Choice;
  • □Boolean Completeness (type t).

It fails:

  • □Rigid Comprehension, □Weak Rigid Comprehension and □Boolean Completeness, each at type et;
  • BF at type et, and so BF at relational types;
  • Atomicity at type (et)t;
  • ND at type t.

The model’s purpose is to bound [[strong-atomicity-t-rigid-comprehension-and-barcan-relational-imply-necessary-rigid-comprehension]]. That result’s premise, BF at relational types, cannot be weakened to □BF (type t), even with the additions listed above.

The construction

Metatheory. Let V be a model of ZFC, let \mathbb P be Cohen forcing, and let G be \mathbb P-generic over V, with Cohen real c\subseteq\mathbb N. The model is a structure in V[G], as in the stitched forcing models.

The category. There are four objects: r, s, A and B. Besides the identities, the arrows are h\colon r\to s, a\colon s\to A, b\colon s\to B, and the composites ah and bh. Every set of arrows out of an object is a proposition, so there are sixteen propositions at r, eight at s, and two at each of A and B.

The terminals.

  • A is the full extensional one-world hierarchy over \mathbb N built in V.
  • B is the same hierarchy built in V[G].

The middle world s. It is the coalesced sum of A and B, with the fullest domains.

  • Its individuals are \mathbb N\times\mathbb N, and a and b act on them as the two projections.
  • At a relational type \tau=\bar\sigma t, D_{s,\tau}=\mathcal P^{V[G]}(D_{s,\bar\sigma})\times D_{A,\tau}\times D_{B,\tau}.
  • An entity (F_s,F_A,F_B) applied to an argument \bar x gives the proposition (\bar x\in F_s,\ a\bar x\in F_A,\ b\bar x\in F_B).

The root r. It has the same individuals as s, and h is the identity on them. Call a \mathbb P-name u invariant if 1 forces u to be an element of D_{s,\tau} fixed by every finite bit-flip of the Cohen real. Let Q_\tau be the set in V of invariant names, taken up to equality forced by 1. Then D_{r,\tau}=\mathcal P^V(D_{r,\bar\sigma})\times Q_\tau, and every pair is admitted. An entity (E,[u]) has extension E at r and transport u^G along h.

This is Barton and Goodsell’s stitching (Continuum Contingency, §8, Definition 1, with trivial group) of a ground-model root onto the coalesced sum, with one change: elements definable in V[G] from ground parameters are replaced by invariantly named ones. The second class is strictly larger. For example, the set of finite modifications of c has an invariant name but is not ground-definable. Every argument below works for either class.

The forcing lemma

Cohen forcing is weakly homogeneous under finite bit-flips: for any two conditions, some bit-flip makes the first compatible with the second. An invariant sentence is a formula whose parameters are check-names and invariant names.

  1. Invariant sentences are decided. If p\Vdash\varphi and q\Vdash\neg\varphi, flip p to be compatible with q; the flipped condition still forces \varphi, which is a contradiction. So every invariant sentence is decided by 1.
  2. Ground values. An invariant name forced into a ground set X is forced equal to a unique element of X. An invariant name forced to be a subset of X names a set in V.
  3. Injectivity. For invariant names u and u', u^G=u'^G if and only if 1\Vdash u=u'.
  4. Closure. A name forced equal to a term in ground parameters and invariant names is invariant.

The structure at s, and the whole model, have invariant names, since they are forced equal to terms in ground parameters. So the truth at r of any formula with root parameters is decided by 1. In particular, the root extension of a term is a set in V, and its transport has an invariant name. A ground set of representatives for each Q_\tau exists by the usual mixing argument.

Image lemma. Let E be a ground set of root tuples, and let hE be the set of their images at s.

  • hE has an invariant name.
  • Each component of a tuple of E has, as its A-projection, an invariant name forced into a ground set. By (2) that projection is a value computed in V, so a(hE) is a ground set.
  • b(hE) is in B, which is full in V[G].

So R_E:=(hE,a(hE),b(hE)) is in D_{s,\bar\sigma t} with an invariant name, and Y_E:=(E,[R_E]) is a root entity.

The obstruction. By (2), the image of D_{r,et} at s is exactly \mathcal P^V(\mathbb N\times\mathbb N)\times\mathcal P^V(\mathbb N)\times\mathcal P^V(\mathbb N). So X:=(c\times\{0\},\varnothing,\varnothing), which lies in D_{s,et}, is not the image of any root entity.

It is a model of C

  • At A. The model sits in V[G], but A is a one-world structure built in V. The interpretation of a term in A is computed by recursion within A, so it is absolute and stays in A’s domains.
  • At s. The domains are the fullest ones, so an abstraction’s s-extension is just some subset in V[G]. Its terminal components are the interpretations in A and B, and application commutes with a and b.
  • At r. An abstraction with root parameters has a ground root extension and an invariant transport, by the forcing lemma. Equality of root entities is equality of profiles, by (3).

Verdicts at r

  • Rigid Comprehension. Given (E,[u]), the entity Y_E of the image lemma is coextensive with it.
    • It is weakly persistent at r, since every member of E has its image in hE, a(hE) and b(hE).
    • It is weakly inextensible at r, since every member of hE, a(hE) and b(hE) is the image of a member of E.
    • R_E is weakly rigid at s by the same exact-image argument, and everything is rigid at A and B.
    So Y_E is rigid. This holds with any argument types, including e.
  • Strong Atomicity (type t), necessarily.
    • At every object, every proposition is a set of arrows. Each singleton is possible, and it decides every proposition there.
    • Its transports are \bot or singletons, along h, or \bot or \top, at A and B.
    • So the singletons are strong atoms, and every nonzero proposition contains one.
  • BF (type t) necessarily, and BF at type e. Every transport map is onto, both on propositions and, by the projections, on individuals.
  • Relational Choice, necessarily. At r, a serial root relation has a serial ground extension, and choice in V gives a functional ground subrelation, with \bot as its transport. At s, choice in V[G] does the same. A and B are full, in V and in V[G] respectively.
  • Boolean Completeness (type t), necessarily. Every propositional algebra is a finite power set.
  • No Rigid Comprehension or Weak Rigid Comprehension at s, at type et.
    • Suppose Y=(c\times\{0\},C,C') is weakly rigid at s.
    • Weak persistence gives c\subseteq C.
    • For n\notin c, every (k,0) with k\in c necessarily falls under Z_n:=(\mathbb N\times\mathbb N,\mathbb N\setminus\{n\},\mathbb N). So weak inextensibility gives C\subseteq\mathbb N\setminus\{n\}.
    • So C=c\notin V, which is impossible, since C must be a ground set.
  • No Boolean Completeness at s, at type (et)t.
    • The family of the haecceities (\{(k,0)\},\{k\},\{0\}) of (k,0), for k\in c, would have a least upper bound J.
    • Then c\subseteq J_A, and J\le Z_n for each n\notin c, so J_A=c, which is not a ground set.
  • No BF at type et. The root entity Y_{D_{r,et}} holds necessarily of every root entity of type et. But at s it misses X.
  • No Atomicity at type (et)t.
    • The property F of being one of the s-entities that are not images of root entities, with empty terminal components, is a nonzero root entity.
    • A nonzero W\le F with a single instance at s would have that instance invariantly named, and so it would be an image. That is a contradiction.
    • So W has two instances, and a ground coordinate test at s splits it.
  • No ND at type t. \{1_r\} and \bot are distinct at r, but both become \bot at s.

Type clarification

OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 30 October 2026. In the Boolean Completeness failure argument above, the represented family has type (et)t, while its members and the hypothetical join J have type et. Thus the displayed witness refutes Boolean Completeness at et, as the model record states. The heading’s (et)t identifies the family type, not the type of the Boolean algebra. The original argument and its verdicts are unchanged.
