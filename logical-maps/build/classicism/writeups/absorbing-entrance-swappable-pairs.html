Absorbing entrance to an integer action, with finitely supported swappable pairs

Construction and proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, research note of 26 October 2026. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

At the entrance r this model satisfies Intensional Choice at every type, together with □Boolean Completeness, □Atomicity and □Actuality, while □Intensional Choice fails (at type e). The Barcan Formula and Distinctness-Necessity fail at t.

The idea is that Intensional Choice at an object is choice over the arrows out of it. Only two arrows leave r, so two picks suffice. The object s reached from r has infinitely many arrows out of it, and the finite-support restriction leaves it without a selector.

The model

The category. Two objects r and s, with:

  • one arrow a:r\to s;
  • the integers as the arrows s\to s, composed by addition;
  • no arrows s\to r, and only the identity r\to r;
  • k\circ a=a for every integer k.

So exactly two arrows leave r, namely 1_r and a. Associativity is immediate.

Individuals and swaps. The individuals at both objects are A_n^0 and A_n^1 for n\in\mathbb N, and every arrow acts on them as the identity. Separately, the group \Gamma=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N} acts on the individuals by \gamma\cdot A_n^i:=A_n^{i+\gamma(n)} (mod 2), and on everything else through them. It is an external symmetry, not part of the category. For finite N\subseteq\mathbb N, let \Gamma_N be the set of \gamma that vanish on N. An entity has support N if \Gamma_N fixes it.

Domains. At a relational type \tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_mt and an object v, start from the full action-model intensions: sets of tuples tagged by the arrows h out of v, the tuple at h coming from the domains at the codomain of h. The domain D_\tau(v) holds those that have finite support.

  • At t the swaps act trivially, so every set of arrows out of v is a proposition.
  • Transport is precomposition: (k_*Y)(h;\bar x)=Y(h\circ k;\bar x). It commutes with \Gamma and preserves supports.
  • The truth clauses are those of full action models, with quantifiers at h ranging over the whole domain at the codomain of h.

This is a model of C. Every logical operation is \Gamma-equivariant, since \Gamma permutes each domain. So the intension of a \lambda-abstract is supported by the union of its parameters’ supports, and the domains are closed under the terms of C. An H-provable equivalence holds at every arrow under every assignment, so the two abstractions are the same intension. Evaluation point: 1_r. The constants of Σ denote top elements, which every swap fixes.

Intensional Choice holds at r

Let F, of type \sigma t, be necessarily instantiated at r. Then its rows at 1_r and at a are nonempty. Pick x in the first and y in the second, and let G hold of x at 1_r and of y at a.

  • G is supported by the union of the supports of x and y, so it is admitted.
  • G\le F.
  • G has exactly one instance at each of the two arrows out of r.

So \Box\exists!z\,.\,Gz holds at r, and Intensional Choice holds there at every type, including e. This uses two picks, not choice.

Intensional Choice fails at s, at type e

Fix a bijection \nu:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Z, and let F, of type et at s, hold at the arrow \nu(n) of A_n^0 and A_n^1. Each pair is invariant under the swaps, so F has empty support and is admitted. Every row is nonempty, so \Box\exists x\,.\,Fx holds at s.

Suppose G\le F is admitted and necessarily uniquely instantiated, with support N. Take n\notin N. The row of G at \nu(n) is a singleton inside the nth pair. The swap of exactly the nth pair lies in \Gamma_N, so it fixes G. It also fixes the arrow \nu(n). So it fixes that singleton row, but it exchanges the pair’s two members, which is impossible.

Hence Intensional Choice at e fails at s. Since a leads from r to s, □Intensional Choice fails at r.

The other verdicts

□Boolean Completeness. At every object and relational type, the order is inclusion of tagged sets. The actual instances of a property with support N form a family invariant under \Gamma_N, so their union is supported by N. It is admitted, and it is their least upper bound.

□Atomicity. A singleton tagged tuple is supported by the union of its entries’ supports. So it is admitted, and it is an atom below any relation that contains it.

□Actuality. At each object the singleton of its identity arrow is a proposition. It is a true atom that entails every truth.

The Barcan Formula fails at t. Since k\circ a=a, a proposition at r transports along a to \varnothing or \mathbb Z. Let Q, of type tt at r, hold at 1_r of every proposition, and at a of \varnothing and \mathbb Z. It has empty support. Every proposition at r necessarily has Q, but at a the proposition \{0\} of s lacks it. In the same way, with constant-extension properties in place of the two propositions, the Barcan Formula fails at et.

Distinctness-Necessity fails at t. \{1_r\} and \bot are distinct at r, but both transport to \bot along a.

Also. The Barcan Formula and Distinctness-Necessity hold necessarily at e, since every arrow is the identity on the same individuals. Neither is a principle of the map at e alone.

Remark

Astra’s earlier note gives a full action model on the same category, in ZF with a sequence of nonempty sets of reals that has no choice function. It satisfies Intensional Choice at r but not □Intensional Choice at t, with one individual. The model above gets the same separation in ZFC, the missing selector coming from the finite-support restriction rather than from the metatheory. The choiceless model is not recorded.
