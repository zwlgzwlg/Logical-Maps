Coalesced sum: interpretation arrows into every component object, finitely pinned root

Cian Dorr gave this construction on 19 October 2026, to show that Maximalist Classicism is consistent with BF. In the same discussion Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) added a condition on the domains: transports must land in the component domains. This write-up gives the verification. No second reader has checked it, and no Lean verification is claimed.

Why the existing coalesced sums fail BF

In a coalesced sum (Classicism, Appendix E), every arrow from the root into a component factors through a single distinguished arrow, and a root entity may have any component parts. So any component that fails BF at its root makes BF fail at the coalesced root. The component’s counterexample becomes a component part of a root entity, as in the group’s argument barcan-t-component. Maximalism needs such components, since the negation of BF is consistent with C.

The construction below replaces the distinguished arrows. The root gets an arrow into every object of every component, one for each interpretation of a large signature. Its domains are pinned down by finite sets of constants, just as the finitely pinned models of Appendix D are pinned down by finite sets of individuals.

The construction

  • Components. Fix a set A of action models complete for C(\Sigma), with disjoint objects and arrows. Every \Sigma-sentence consistent with C(\Sigma) is true at the root of some model in A under some interpretation of \Sigma.
  • Signature. Fix \Sigma^+\supseteq\Sigma with countably infinitely many constants of every type.
  • Arrows. Add an object W_0. The arrows out of W_0 are its identity and the pairs \langle V,I\rangle, for V an object of a model in A and I an interpretation of \Sigma^+ at V, giving each constant of type \tau an entity of type \tau at V. No arrow enters W_0 except its identity.
  • Composition. For an arrow k:V\to V' of a component, k\circ\langle V,I\rangle:=\langle V',k\cdot I\rangle, transporting each constant’s interpretation. This is associative because transport is functorial.
  • Individuals. The individuals at W_0 are the individual constants of \Sigma^+, and \langle V,I\rangle sends c to I(c).
  • Domains at W_0. An entity at W_0 of a relational type is an intension F, assigning to each arrow out of W_0 a set of tuples of entities at its target, subject to two conditions:
    1. Finite pinning. For some finite S\subseteq\Sigma^+, two arrows \langle V,I\rangle and \langle V,I'\rangle into the same object with I{\restriction}S=I'{\restriction}S give F the same value. The identity is the only arrow into W_0, so its value is unconstrained.
    2. Landing. For every arrow h=\langle V,I\rangle, the transport h\cdot F, the intension k\mapsto F_{k\circ h} over the arrows out of V, is an element of V’s domain in its component.
    The types are well-founded, so this defines the domains by recursion on the type.
  • Evaluation point. 1_{W_0}.
  • Interpretation of \Sigma. Each constant c\in\Sigma denotes \hat c, defined below.

Without the landing condition the structure would not be an action model. For instance, \{\langle V',I'\rangle : I'(c)\in B_{V'}\}, for an arbitrary assignment B of sets of individuals to objects, is pinned down by \{c\}. But its transport along \langle V,I\rangle is the proposition that I(c) has the codomain property B, which need not be in the component’s domain, for instance when the component is symmetric.

The entities \hat c

For a constant c of type \sigma, let \hat c be the following.

  • If \sigma=e, \hat c is c itself.
  • If \sigma is relational, \hat c_{\langle V',I'\rangle} is the extension of I'(c) at 1_{V'}, and \hat c_{1_{W_0}}:=\emptyset.

Then \langle V,I\rangle\cdot\hat c=I(c):

(\langle V,I\rangle\cdot\hat c)_k=\hat c_{\langle V',k\cdot I\rangle}=(k\cdot I(c))_{1_{V'}}=I(c)_k.

So \hat c satisfies the landing condition, and it is pinned down by \{c\}. Hence \hat c\in D_\sigma(W_0).

It is a model of C

The argument follows Classicism, Proposition D.4, together with the treatment of the root in Appendix E. Closed formulas are evaluated at an arrow by evaluating at its target with transported parameters. So what has to be shown is that every term with parameters from the domains denotes an element of the domains. Let t be such a term, with parameters \bar P pinned down by finite sets whose union is S.

  1. Pinning. The value of t’s denotation at \langle V,I\rangle is computed in V’s component from the transported parameters \langle V,I\rangle\cdot\bar P. These depend on I only through I{\restriction}S. So the denotation is pinned down by S.
  2. Landing. Transport commutes with the operations of the language. So \langle V,I\rangle\cdot\llbracket t\rrbracket(\bar P) is the denotation in V’s component of t with the parameters \langle V,I\rangle\cdot\bar P. That lies in V’s domain, because the component is a model of C.
  3. At W_0 itself. The value at the identity is the set of tuples of W_0-entities satisfying the formula at W_0. Quantifiers there range over D(W_0), and no condition constrains this value.

Application is the special case h\cdot(Fa)=(h\cdot F)(h\cdot a). The axioms of C hold at W_0 because, as in every action model, denotations are computed extensionally on intensions. The worlds other than W_0 are worlds of the components, which are models of C.

BF holds at every type

Take the form \Diamond\exists x\,.\,Fx\to\exists x\,.\,\Diamond Fx. Let F be of type \sigma\to t, pinned down by a finite S, with \Diamond\exists x\,.\,Fx true at W_0.

  • The witness is at the identity. Then \exists x\,.\,Fx holds already, and Fx implies \Diamond Fx.
  • The witness is at some \langle V,I\rangle. Then y\in F_{\langle V,I\rangle} for some y of type \sigma at V.
    1. Choose a constant c of type \sigma outside S; infinitely many are available.
    2. Let I' agree with I except that I'(c)=y.
    3. Then \langle V,I'\rangle agrees with \langle V,I\rangle on S, so F_{\langle V,I'\rangle}=F_{\langle V,I\rangle}\ni y=\langle V,I'\rangle\cdot\hat c.
    4. So F\hat c holds at \langle V,I'\rangle, and \Diamond F\hat c holds at W_0.

This is the criterion that gives BF in the all-maps finitely pinned model. Every arrow agrees on a finite set with one under which a fresh constant is mapped wherever one likes.

□BF fails, as it must, since Maximalism is incompatible with it (recorded results). The components that refute BF are seen from W_0.

Maximalism holds, for \Sigma and in the pure form

A closed \Sigma-sentence holds at \langle V,I\rangle iff it holds at V in its component under I{\restriction}\Sigma, the constants of \Sigma denoting the \hat c. Let \phi be consistent with C(\Sigma). Then \phi holds at the root V of some model in A under some interpretation of \Sigma. Extend that interpretation arbitrarily to \Sigma^+, giving I; then \phi holds at \langle V,I\rangle, so \Diamond\phi holds at W_0. This is Maximalism for \Sigma in its possibility form, which is equivalent in C to the official form. The pure form follows.

Other verdicts

  • Actuality. \{1_{W_0}\} is pinned down by \emptyset, and its transports are empty. It is a true proposition that entails every truth.
  • Relational Choice. As at the other coalesced roots, a choice at the identity with empty component parts is in the domain.
  • Boxed principles. The group’s argument that every pure sentence consistent with C is possible at the root refutes □Vicinity, □Weakly Inextensible Comprehension and □Transversal here too.

The map derives much more. Inextensible Comprehension holds, from Actuality with BF, and so do the signature principles that follow from Maximalism for \Sigma. None of the group’s arguments that transfer a counterexample through a distinguished arrow applies, so this model’s other verdicts at the root need arguments of their own.
