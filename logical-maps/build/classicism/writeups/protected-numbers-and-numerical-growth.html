Predecessor operations with a growing numerical field

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance, 30 October 2026.

There is one C model in which, at every externally fixed counting type, numerical noncollapse holds necessarily and a total predecessor operation exists on the numerical successors. Nevertheless Arithmetic Necessitism fails at counting type t with its guard true. The distinction concerns stability of the current numerical objects and acquisition of further members by their whole numerical predicate.

This note records an unencoded theorem and model. It introduces no new principle node. All type indices below are external schema indices; there is no quantifier over types. No independent human check or Lean proof is recorded.

1. Exact formulas and a C theorem

Fix an admitted counting type \sigma, including e, and put \nu=(\sigma t)t. Write N, zero and S for its canonical numerical predicate, zero and successor. Let

\operatorname{Pred}_\sigma:= \exists P^{\nu\nu}\,\forall n(Nn\to P(Sn)=n).

This is a total operation with prescribed numerical-successor values. A total functional graph alone does not supply it. The operation type is admitted even at \sigma=e, since its output \nu is relational. The relation bottom \bot_\nu is distinct from numerical zero.

The following already follows in C:

\Box I^*_\sigma\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pred}_\sigma. \tag{1}

For a direct proof define the admitted pooling operation

D(y)=\lambda X^{\sigma t}\, \exists m^\nu(Nm\land Sm=y\land mX).

Fix a current number n. Numberhood persists in C. At any future evaluation, n is still numerical, and the boxed guard makes successor injective on all the numerical objects there. Therefore the witnesses in D(Sn)X are exactly n, for every full future argument X. Necessarily, D(Sn)X\leftrightarrow nX. Intensionality gives the whole identity D(Sn)=n. The same D works for all current n. No choice of preimages, standardness assumption or restriction to transported arguments was used.

The proposed current-guarded sentence I^*_\sigma\to\operatorname{Pred}_\sigma has a different strength. The established numerical-protection results identify predecessor with protection of current numbers from \bot_\nu, equivalently numerical noncollapse together with their necessary distinctness. Their all-type canonical transport, inverse and section formulations require both the numerical domain and numerical output restrictions. They do not provide decoding of arbitrary nonnumerical images. These are source equivalences, not separate new nodes or new incompatibility mechanisms.

2. A component with an additional numerical member

Use the countable finitary Henkin presentation of the fixed C language, retaining every standard finite parameterized lambda template and the actual H-only Logical Equivalence rule. Add, for every counting type \sigma, the sentence \Box I^*_\sigma. At \nu_t=(tt)t, add a constant c and a predicate Q with

\mathbb N_t(c),\quad c\ne\bar n_t\ (n\in\omega),\quad \Box Q(\bar n_t)\ (n\in\omega),\quad \neg Q(c). \tag{2}

Q is not required to be inductive. No compactness argument would justify an inductive predicate containing all standard numerals and omitting a number.

Every finite fragment is satisfiable in a full constant-domain S5 intensional hierarchy with countably infinitely many worlds and individuals. Every fixed type has an infinite domain; full current rows make its canonical numbers the standard numeral orbit, noncollapsed at every world. Choose c to be a standard numeral beyond all numeral indices mentioned in the fragment. Choose Q to be a constant finite initial segment containing all the mentioned numerals and omitting c. The full hierarchy admits Q and verifies the boxed requirements.

Finitary Henkin compactness therefore yields a pointed C component K satisfying (2) and every boxed noncollapse instance. Its original application, quantification and lambda operations are retained. K need not be an externally full or extensional action model. No optional property of the full S5 models used for finite satisfiability is transferred to K.

3. Add a full present while keeping every component quantifier

Use the existing pair construction with current individuals R_e=\omega, projected constantly to one K individual. At every flattened relational type take

R_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_k t} =\mathcal P(R_{\alpha_1}\times\cdots\times R_{\alpha_k}) \times K_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_k t}.

Application and abstraction calculate the arbitrary current row and the original K denotation separately. The K coordinate of a quantifier uses the whole K domain, including elements outside the projection image. H-valid lambda equivalences agree in both coordinates. This is the full-present C construction, not an attempt to reinterpret K as an ordinary terminal Kripke world. The same construction is described in the C5-component writeup, Section 2; the C5 assumption is used there for its later power arguments, not for the pair construction itself.

For each \sigma, the root admits an induction-test predicate whose current extension is exactly the closed numeral orbit. Every current inductive predicate contains that orbit. Thus

\operatorname{ext}_R(\mathbb N_\sigma) =\{\bar n^R_\sigma:n\in\omega\}.

Closed terms project correctly. The K images of these numerals are distinct, with zero not a successor, because K satisfies I^*_\sigma. An equality between root numeral entities would project to an equality between those images. Therefore the root also satisfies I^*_\sigma. Its K coordinate is the whole top because K satisfies \Box I^*_\sigma. The whole root value is consequently (\mathrm{true},\top_K), proving \Box I^*_\sigma at R. Equation (1) now gives every \operatorname{Pred}_\sigma in the same model.

4. The whole numerical predicate gains members

At counting type t, pair the root’s current numerical orbit with K’s Q to obtain an admitted predicate Z. Every current root number is a standard numeral, and (2) makes its Q-value the whole component top. Thus each such number has whole Z-value (\mathrm{true},\top_K), and

R\models\forall n(\mathbb N_t(n)\to\Box Z(n)).

But K’s c is numerical and lacks Q. The original K quantifier sees c, so the necessary inclusion \mathbb N_t\le Z fails at R. Hence \mathbb N_t is not weakly inextensible and is not rigid. The Arithmetic Necessitism instance at t fails with \Box I^*_t true. The model supplies the asserted all-type predecessors without asserting any analytic-necessitism verdict.
