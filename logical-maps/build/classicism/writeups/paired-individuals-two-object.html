Paired-individuals two-object models: Barcan, rigidity and comprehension

Proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Cian Dorr, research note of 29 October 2026. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 29 October 2026. No Lean verification is claimed.

The three records paired-individuals-two-object, paired-individuals-two-object-surjective and paired-individuals-two-object-surjective-at-v share their objects and differ in their arrows (all block-respecting maps, or the surjective ones) and in the evaluation object. Everything below is proved at both objects and in both categories. Since a closed sentence holds at an arrow iff it holds at the identity of the arrow’s codomain, and each object reaches the other, a closed sentence holding at both identities is necessary at either. So every verdict below holds necessarily in all three records:

  • BF at every type (needed only in the first record; the surjective ones have it by Classicism, Proposition D.6);
  • Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power, at every type;
  • Weakly Inextensible Comprehension fails at type (t)t, and with it Inextensible Comprehension and both boxed forms.

The rest (Rigid Rigidity, Weakly Rigid Power, Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity, Functionality, Tractarianism and the BF variants) follows by recorded results.

Setting

Write W for \mathbb N partitioned into the pairs \{2k,2k+1\} and V for W with the extra one-point block \{\star\}. An arrow sends each block into a block, a pair either bijectively or onto a point. Relations at an object A are given by their extensions: F_h is the extension of F at the arrow h\colon A\to B, a set of tuples from the domains at B, and transport is (h\cdot F)_k=F_{kh}. F is pinned down by a finite S\subseteq A when F_h=F_{h'} for arrows h,h' with the same codomain that agree on S. The domains hold exactly the intensions pinned down by finite sets, and every tuple of entities is pinned down by the union of its entries’ pins (an individual by its singleton). If a is pinned down by M and f,g agree on M, then f\cdot a=g\cdot a.

At the identity of A: F is weakly persistent iff k\cdot a\in F_k for every a\in F_1 and every arrow k out of A; F is weakly inextensible iff F_k\subseteq Z_k for every k whenever Z satisfies k\cdot a\in Z_k for every a\in F_1 and every k.

Finite prescriptions. Call a finite set saturated if it is a union of blocks and contains \star when its object is V. Every finite set lies in a saturated one, and outside a saturated set every block is a pair. A block-respecting prescription on finitely many source blocks extends to an arrow, and to a surjective one: infinitely many source pairs are left; match them bijectively with the target pairs still to be covered, and, if the target is V and \star is not yet hit, collapse one further source pair onto \star.

1. The hull criterion

For a set E of tuples at A and finite N\subseteq A let B_N(E)_h:=\{f\cdot a:a\in E,\ f\colon A\to\operatorname{cod}(h),\ f|_N=h|_N\}. It is pinned down by N, so it is in the domain, and k\cdot a\in B_N(E)_k for a\in E (take f=k).

Criterion. If F, pinned down by S with F_1=E, is weakly rigid, then F=B_N(E) for every finite N\supseteq S: weak persistence gives f\cdot a\in F_f=F_h when f agrees with h on N, and weak inextensibility applied to Z=B_N(E) gives F\le B_N(E). Conversely, if F_1=E and F=B_N(E) for every finite N containing a pin of F, then F is weakly rigid: weak persistence is f=k; and if Z, pinned down by T, satisfies k\cdot a\in Z_k for all a\in E, then B_N(E)\le Z for N\supseteq T (as f\cdot a\in Z_f=Z_h), so F\le Z.

2. Tame Rigidity

Factorization. Let h\colon A\to B, k\colon B\to C, S\subseteq A finite saturated, N\subseteq B finite, f\colon A\to C agreeing with kh on S, and M\subseteq A finite. Then there are arrows r\colon A\to B and j\colon B\to C with r|_S=h|_S, j|_N=k|_N and (jr)|_M=f|_M. Enlarge M to a saturated set containing S, so that M\setminus S is a union of pairs. Let K be the union of the blocks of B meeting N\cup h[S]. Put r=h on S, and send the pairs of M\setminus S bijectively onto distinct pairs of B outside K. Put j=k on K and, on the image r[P] of each pair P\subseteq M\setminus S, let j be f\circ(r|_P)^{-1}; this respects blocks because f does, and does not clash with j=k on K. Complete r and j to arrows (surjective ones in the surjective category). On S, jr=kh=f; on M\setminus S, jr=f by construction.

Proof of Tame Rigidity at A. Let F be weakly rigid with F_1=E and saturated pin S, and h\colon A\to B. F is persistent (C proves that weak rigidity implies persistence), so h\cdot F is weakly persistent. For weak inextensibility, let Z at B, pinned down by N, satisfy k\cdot b\in Z_k for every b\in F_h and every k. Let b\in F_{kh}. By the criterion b=f\cdot a with a\in E pinned down by M and f agreeing with kh on S. Take r,j from the factorization. Since r agrees with h on S, r\cdot a\in F_r=F_h, so j\cdot(r\cdot a)\in Z_j=Z_k; and j\cdot(r\cdot a)=(jr)\cdot a=f\cdot a=b. So F_{kh}\subseteq Z_k, h\cdot F is weakly inextensible at every h, and F is rigid. This holds at both objects, so □Tame Rigidity.

3. Weakly Inextensible Comprehension fails

Fix the evaluation object A. For even n let p_n be the set of arrows f\colon A\to A with f(n)=n, pinned down by \{n\}, and E:=\{p_n:n\text{ even}\}. The relation X of type (t)t with X_h=E for h into A and X_h=\varnothing otherwise is pinned down by \varnothing, so in the domain, with extension E.

Claim. For an arrow f\colon A\to A, f\cdot p_m=p_n iff m=n and f(n)=n. Here f\cdot p_m=\{g\colon A\to A: g(f(m))=m\}. If m\neq n, an endomorphism v sending both n and f(m) to n (collapsing their block if they share one) is in p_n but not in f\cdot p_m. If m=n and f(n)\ne n, an endomorphism v with v(n)=n and v(f(n))\ne n (the identity on the pair of n if f(n)=\pi n) separates them. The converse is immediate.

Now let Y be weakly inextensible with Y_1=E, pinned down by S. Take an even n whose pair misses S and a block permutation g of A fixing S (and \star) with g(n) in another pair. Then Y_g=Y_1=E\ni p_n. Every member of E necessarily satisfies Z:=B_{\{n\}}(E), so Y\le Z and p_n\in Z_g: p_n=f\cdot p_m for some f\colon A\to A with f(n)=g(n)\ne n, against the claim. So no weakly inextensible relation is coextensive with X. Inextensible Comprehension, and the boxed forms by T, fail too.

4. Special surjections, exact images, stable hulls

Special surjections. Given h\colon A\to B and finite saturated N\subseteq A there is a surjective arrow j agreeing with h on N such that (i) every pair of B is the bijective j-image of a pair of A, and (ii) every fibre of j is finite and only finitely many have more than one point. Keep h on N; for each pair of B meeting h[N] send a fresh pair of A bijectively onto it; if \star\in B is not yet hit, collapse a fresh pair onto it; match the remaining pairs of A and B bijectively (\star\in N if A=V).

Exact images. If F is weakly rigid at A with F_1=E, then F_j=j\cdot E for every special surjection j. Let S be a saturated pin of F. A member of F_j=B_S(E)_j is f\cdot a with f|_S=j|_S, a\in E pinned down by a saturated M\supseteq S. Define an endomorphism u of A: the identity on S; on a pair P of M\setminus S that f maps bijectively onto a pair Q, (j|_{P'})^{-1}\circ f|_P for a pair P' mapped bijectively onto Q by j; on a pair that f collapses to y, constantly a j-preimage of y; extended to an arrow (surjective if required). Then ju=f on M, and u\cdot a\in F_u=F_1=E by weak persistence, so f\cdot a=j\cdot(u\cdot a)\in j\cdot E. The other inclusion is weak persistence.

Stable hulls. If P\subseteq A is finite saturated and D is a set of tuples at A with u\cdot D\subseteq D for every endomorphism u fixing P pointwise, then Y:=B_P(D) has extension D and is rigid. Its extension is D by the closure (and u=1). For saturated Q\supseteq P and f\cdot a\in B_P(D)_h, with a pinned down by a saturated M\supseteq P, take a block permutation u fixing P and moving the pairs of M\setminus P outside Q, and an arrow r equal to h on Q and to f\circ u^{-1} on u[M\setminus P] (compatible, since f=h on P). Then u\cdot a\in D and f\cdot a=r\cdot(u\cdot a)\in B_Q(D)_h. So B_P(D)=B_Q(D), hence =B_N(D) for every finite N\supseteq P; Y is weakly rigid by the criterion, and rigid by Tame Rigidity.

5. Rigid Power

Let F be rigid at A with F_1=E, and let R_F:=\lambda X\,.\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F, persistent in C. Fix h\colon A\to B, a finite saturated N containing a pin of F, and a rigid X\le h\cdot F at B with extension D and pin T.

Take a special surjection j agreeing with h on N. Then j\cdot F=h\cdot F and D\subseteq F_h=F_j=j\cdot E. Let P\subseteq A be finite saturated, containing N, every fibre of j with more than one point, and j^{-1}[T], and put D_0:=\{a\in E:j\cdot a\in D\}. Let u be an endomorphism fixing P. Then ju is constant on each fibre of j, so ju=vj for a unique map v\colon B\to B. It respects blocks: a pair C of B is j[C'] for a pair C' of A, and v[C]=j[u[C']] lies in a block. It is surjective if u is, and fixes T because j^{-1}[T]\subseteq P. By weak persistence and pinning, u\cdot E\subseteq E and v\cdot D\subseteq D, so for a\in D_0, j\cdot(u\cdot a)=v\cdot(j\cdot a)\in D, and u\cdot a\in D_0. By the stable-hull lemma Y:=B_P(D_0) is rigid; Y\le F since D_0\subseteq E and P contains a pin of F; and by exact images (j\cdot Y)_1=Y_j=j\cdot D_0=D. Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical, so X=j\cdot Y.

So if Z, pinned down by N', necessarily holds of every actual instance of R_F, choose N above to contain N': each X\in(R_F)_h is j\cdot Y for an actual instance Y and a j agreeing with h on N, so X\in Z_j=Z_h. Thus R_F is weakly rigid, and rigid by Tame Rigidity. This holds at both objects, so □Rigid Power, and with □Tame Rigidity the recorded result gives □Weakly Rigid Power.

6. Local sections and BF

Given h\colon A\to B and finite N\subseteq A, T\subseteq B, there are arrows j\colon A\to B with j|_N=h|_N and \sigma\colon B\to A with j\sigma the identity on T. Enlarge N and T to saturated sets and keep h on N. For each pair C in T send a fresh pair of A bijectively onto C under j, with \sigma the inverse on C; if \star\in T, collapse a further fresh pair of A onto \star under j and let \sigma send \star to one of its points. Complete both (surjectively if required).

BF. Let R at A, pinned down by N, be necessarily true of each actual entity: k\cdot a\in R_k for all a and k. For h\colon A\to B and b at B pinned down by T, take j and \sigma as above. Then b=(j\sigma)\cdot b=j\cdot(\sigma\cdot b)\in R_j=R_h. So \Box\forall x\,Rx holds, at every type and at both objects; hence □BF. With □BF, □Rigid Power gives □Rigid Rigidity by a recorded result (directly: a rigid X at B pinned down by T is j\cdot(\sigma\cdot X) with \sigma\cdot X rigid), and the recorded results then give □Weakly Rigid Weak Rigidity.

7. The withdrawn argument barcan-t-fails

The argument recorded on 14 October for the failure of BF at type t in the all-maps record used the property Y from the argument for 5-necessity, at V the propositions pinned down by old individuals, and claimed that \Box(Yz) holds for every proposition z at W, because k\cdot z is pinned down by k[S] when z is pinned down by S. But k[S] need not consist of old individuals: an arrow i\colon W\to V may collapse the pair of a\in S onto \star. For z:=\{f\colon W\to V: f(a)=\star\}, pinned down by \{a\}, i\cdot z=\{k\colon V\to V:k(\star)=\star\}, which is pinned down by no finite set of old individuals (the identity and an endomorphism sending \star into \mathbb N off that set agree on it). So \Box(Yz) fails at i. Read literally instead (pinned down by individuals in the range of some arrow from W), Y holds of every proposition at V, and \forall z\,Yz is \top. Either way the counterexample fails, and §6 shows that BF holds. The argument for 5-necessity uses Y only along arrows injective on S\cup\pi S, which cannot collapse a pair of S onto \star, and is unaffected.
