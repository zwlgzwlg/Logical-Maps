# No Pure Contingency ∧ 5 ⇒ □5

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Cian Dorr, suggestion of 20 September 2026; recorded by Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic), 20 September 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **No Pure Contingency.** Each closed sentence of the pure language, if true, is necessary. This is a sentence schema, not a quantifier over propositions.
- **5.** Every possible proposition is necessarily possible.

## Conclusion

- **□5.** Necessarily, every possible proposition is necessarily possible.

## Proof

Every closed instance of 5 is a closed sentence of the pure language, since the principle is stated with object-language quantifiers and contains no nonlogical constant. Given 5, each of those instances is true, and No Pure Contingency makes a true closed pure sentence necessary. So every closed instance is necessary, which is what the boxed principle asserts. Any type range and side conditions stay outside the box, as the Modal scope convention requires.

## Notes

One of a uniform family. No Pure Contingency carries any pure principle of the map to its boxed form, because a boxed principle here says exactly that each closed instance of the base is necessary. The argument turns on the base being stated with quantifiers rather than as a schema over arbitrary formulas, since an instance carrying a nonlogical constant would fall outside No Pure Contingency.
