Supported model over the ultrafilters of a complete homogeneous algebra, with a free root

The construction is due to GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi. It appears as a Boolean-valued model in Astra’s note Observer separation with propositional completeness and divergent possibilities (25 October 2026, §§1–3), listed in Kodsi’s pull request #18. OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, integrated it as supported-boolean-complete-predecessor in his pull request #21 (28 October 2026). Cian Dorr reformulated it as an action model on 29 October 2026, and Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, wrote this version.

At the root r the model satisfies:

  • □Boolean Completeness (type t);
  • □BF;
  • Relational Choice;
  • Actuality.

It does not satisfy □Actuality, or Atomicity (type t). In the component, Actuality is necessarily false.

The algebra

Let B be the regular open algebra of Cantor space. It is complete and atomless, and homogeneous: every nonzero interval [0,b] is isomorphic to B. Let G=\operatorname{Aut}(B).

For a finite P\subseteq B, let H_P be the automorphisms fixing P pointwise. The finite subalgebra \langle P\rangle has finitely many atoms, the cells c_1,\dots,c_m, which partition 1.

Lemma 1. The elements of B fixed by H_P are exactly the joins of cells.

Proof. Every member of H_P fixes each cell, so it fixes their joins. Conversely, suppose 0<b\wedge c<c for a cell c. Then [0,b\wedge c] and [0,c-b] are both isomorphic to B. An isomorphism exchanging them, extended by the identity off c, lies in H_P and moves b. \square

Lemma 2 (finite attainment). If S\subseteq B is H_P-invariant, then \bigvee S is the join of at most two members of S per cell, and \bigwedge S dually.

Proof. Take a cell c that some b\in S meets.

  • If b\ge c, then b covers c.
  • Otherwise, the automorphism u of Lemma 1 gives u(b)\in S with u(b)\wedge c=c-b, so b and u(b) together cover c.

So finitely many members of S join to the union of the cells that S meets. Every member of S lies below that union, so it is \bigvee S. \square

The model

The worlds and the component. The worlds are the ultrafilters of B. Each b\in B determines the set of worlds \hat b:=\{U:b\in U\}, and b\mapsto\hat b is an isomorphism of B onto the clopen algebra of its Stone space.

  • The component has exactly one arrow between any two worlds, and a single individual. So an entity of relational type is a relation F between tuples and worlds, the same at every world.
  • G acts on worlds and, by conjugation, on entities.
  • An entity is admitted if it satisfies two conditions:
    • it is supported: g\cdot F=F for every g\in H_P, for some finite P\subseteq B;
    • its fibres are in B: for each tuple \bar x, the set of worlds at which F holds of \bar x is \hat b for some b\in B.
  • So the propositions of the component are the sets \hat b, a copy of B.

The root. A root r is added, with one arrow to each world and none back. A root entity is a pair (R,a) of an arbitrary current extension R and a component entity a. So the propositions at r are the pairs (\varepsilon,\hat b), a copy of 2\times B, and r sees itself and every world.

Closure. The domains contain the denotations of all terms, so the structure is a model of C.

  • The logical constants are invariant under G.
  • Application, abstraction and the Boolean operations preserve support and keep fibres in B.
  • Quantifiers in an action model are evaluated world by world. So the fibre of \forall x\,Fx is the intersection of the fibres \hat b_x of the instances, and that of \exists x\,Fx is their union.
  • If F is supported by P, the set of the b_x is H_P-invariant. By Lemma 2 the intersection is a finite intersection, so it is again \hat b for b=\bigwedge_xb_x, and likewise for the union.

Without finite support, the intersection of infinitely many clopen sets needn’t be clopen, and the domains wouldn’t be closed. At the root, current extensions are arbitrary and the component parts are component entities, so the same holds there, as for the coalesced roots.

Verdicts

  • Boolean Completeness (type t), necessarily.
    • At a world of the component. A family of propositions in the domain is supported, so by Lemma 2 its union is the finite union \hat b with b=\bigvee_ib_i. That is its least upper bound.
    • At r. A family of root propositions (\varepsilon_i,\hat b_i) is arbitrary, and its least upper bound is (\bigvee\varepsilon_i,\widehat{\bigvee b_i}), with the join taken in the complete algebra B. This is where completeness of B is needed: a union of clopen sets needn’t be clopen, but the least clopen set containing it exists.
  • BF, necessarily.
    • In the component, the same entities are available at every world, so BF holds trivially.
    • At r, every component entity is the transport of a root entity, the one with empty current extension.
  • Relational Choice. For a serial U at r, choose, in the metatheory, a functional subrelation of its current extension, and pair it with the empty component entity.
  • Actuality. At r, the proposition true at r alone is true and below every truth.
  • No Actuality in the component. At a world U, an actual-world proposition would be some \hat b with b\in U and \hat b\subseteq\hat c for every c\in U. Then b would be an atom of B, and B is atomless. So Actuality is false at every world of the component, and □Actuality fails at r.
  • No Atomicity (type t) at r. Below a nonzero (0,\hat b), every (0,\hat c) with c<b is again nonzero.

Remarks

The two presentations. Astra’s presentation is Boolean-valued: propositions are elements of B, relations are finitely supported functions into B, identity is crisp, and quantifiers are infima and suprema. There, Lemma 2 is what makes each ultrafilter a world respecting the quantifiers. In the action model the worlds come first, and Lemma 2 is what keeps the domains closed under the quantifiers. The two are the same model.

The fibre condition is needed. Without it, every supported relation would be admitted. The component’s propositions would then be all the H_P-invariant sets of ultrafilters, a larger algebra, since G isn’t transitive on the ultrafilters in a cell. That component is still a model of C, and it satisfies C5, BF, Boolean Completeness at t and Atomlessness. But its algebra isn’t externally complete, and the free root would then fail Boolean Completeness.

Cantor space. With the countable atomless algebra in place of B, Cantor space for the worlds and its homeomorphisms for G, the supported sets of worlds are exactly the clopen ones. So the fibre condition comes free. The component is then a clean model of C5 + Boolean Completeness at t + ¬Actuality, but the free root fails Boolean Completeness at t.
