Full present: Rosser-selected C5 component

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance. 30 October 2026.

There is a classical Henkin model of C satisfying necessary Weakly Rigid Power, and therefore necessary Rigid Power and necessary Tame Rigidity, while failing both Necessity of Arithmetic and Arithmetic Necessitism. The arithmetic failures occur at counting type e, with \Box I^*_e true. The power assertions hold at every finite argument tuple, including the empty tuple and tuples containing individuals.

The construction uses the arithmetically disagreeing component from the Rosser-selected Cohen model, §§1–2, but attaches a different present to it. The contribution is an exact description of rigid relations in a full present over a C5 component, followed by the power lift and the arithmetic separation in that same model.

1. The component and its arithmetic

We first specify the component whose existence the cited proof supplies. Its effective many-sorted first-order theory T_H has a sort for every admitted type, the original application and quantifier operations, a classical truth predicate, and all finite parameterized lambda-template operations. Its axioms include the Boolean and quantifier truth clauses, actual equality, the H axioms, the lambda conversion laws, C5, \Box I^*_e and the boxed absence of real structures at each type. Instances of Logical Equivalence are admitted only with certificates of H-theoremhood. Equivalence from the additional premises of T_H does not license them.

The standard-arithmetic component of the Cohen arithmetic-growth proof, §§2–5 is a model of T_H. Its canonical number field and its native Sum and Prod graphs are standard. Consequently the arithmetic theory

U=\{\varphi:T_H\vdash \operatorname{True}(\operatorname{val}(\varphi^e))\}

is a sound computably enumerable extension of PA. Here \varphi^e is the map’s individual-counting translation, with number variables of type \nu=(et)t and addition and multiplication expressed by their native graphs. Numerical induction and total unique recursion graphs give the effective PA interpretation; no operation is selected from a graph. Finite certificates for enumerated axioms make finite proofs effectively recognizable.

Rosser incompleteness gives a standard-true arithmetic sentence G unprovable in U. First-order completeness and countable Löwenheim–Skolem give a countable pointed classical Henkin model A of

T_H+\neg\operatorname{True}(\operatorname{val}(G^e)).

Retain this particular A and G. In particular,

(I^*_e)_A=\top_A,\qquad g:=(G^e)_A\ne\top_A. \tag{1}

The second assertion does not say g=\bot_A. Current falsity is sufficient. C5 makes equality crisp as a proposition: an identity has value \top_A and an inequality has value \bot_A. The component retains its original quantifiers, ranging over every admitted object of the corresponding sort. These facts, with its classical truth clauses and lambda operations, are the hypotheses used below. We do not transfer its absence of real structures to the model being constructed.

This is a construction in ZFC using the stated Henkin existence argument. It requires neither a countable transitive model of ZFC nor a generic over the entire universe.

2. A full present over the component

The following construction works first for any pointed classical Henkin C5 component K with crisp equality. Choose a nonempty set R_e and a map q_e:R_e\to K_e. For every flattened relational type \tau=\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_k t, define recursively

R_\tau= \mathcal P\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}R_{\alpha_i}\right)\times K_\tau, \qquad q_\tau(S,f)=f. \tag{2}

The product for the empty tuple is a singleton; in particular, R_t=2\times K_t. Every current row is admitted, independently of its component entity. For a tuple write \bar q for the componentwise projection.

Application uses the current row and the component application. Partial application takes the corresponding slice of the row and applies the component entity to the projected argument. A lambda abstraction has the extension of its body at the new present as its first coordinate and the original K abstraction with projected parameters as its second coordinate. A quantified formula has its ordinary classical truth value over the full R domain in the first coordinate and its original K quantifier value over the full K domain in the second coordinate. In particular, the second quantifier is not restricted to the image of q_e.

Induction on terms gives

q(T^R(\bar a))=T^K(\bar q(\bar a)).\tag{3}

Current singleton rows make equality at the new present literal identity of entities. The H axioms hold at that present and in the component. For an H-theorem equivalence of two bodies, their current lambda rows agree, and C in K identifies their component abstractions. Thus the lambda entities themselves agree. This validates precisely C’s Logical Equivalence schema, without admitting equivalences from optional premises. The construction therefore gives a model of C. It does not require an action representation of K.

For a proposition B with fixed root parameters, equality-defined necessity has the exact truth condition

R\models\Box B \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R\models B\ \text{ and }\ B^K=\top_K. \tag{4}

Since equality in K is crisp, \Box_K p is \top_K exactly when p=\top_K, and is \bot_K otherwise. For F=(S,f) and Z=(T,z), (3)–(4) give

R\models F\le Z \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S\subseteq T\ \text{ and }\ K\models f\le z. \tag{5}

The second condition is the old necessary universal inclusion. It is stronger than inclusion between two current component extensions. All later uses of an outer box use (4); they are not applications of necessitation to an argument from optional assumptions.

3. Exactly which pairs are rigid

Write

\operatorname{ext}_K(f)= \{\bar b:K\models f[\bar b]\}.

First observe that a persistent component relation has only top or bottom membership values. For each tuple \bar b, necessary universal elimination from persistence gives the Boolean inequality p\le\Box_Kp, where p=f[\bar b]. If p\ne\top_K, crisp equality makes \Box_Kp=\bot_K, hence p=\bot_K. In particular, membership in a rigid component relation is currently true exactly when its whole value is top. This observation does not assert that all component propositions are bivalent.

The key criterion is

R\models\operatorname{Rigid}(S,f) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K\models\operatorname{Rigid}(f) \ \text{ and }\ \operatorname{ext}_K(f)=\bar q[S]. \tag{6}

For necessity, write rigidity as necessary weak rigidity. By (4), rigidity of (S,f) makes the whole K value of weak rigidity of f top; hence f is rigid in K. Root weak persistence makes f[\bar q(\bar a)] top for every \bar a\in S, giving one image inclusion.

For the reverse image inclusion, fix any \bar b\notin\bar q[S]. The component predicate

z_{\bar b}:=\lambda\bar x\,.\,\neg(\bar x=\bar b)

is admitted. Its value is top on every tuple other than \bar b, by crisp equality. Pair it with the full current row to obtain a root test Z_{\bar b}. This test is necessarily true of every tuple in S. Root weak inextensibility gives (S,f)\le Z_{\bar b}, so (5) gives f\le z_{\bar b} in K. If \bar b belonged to \operatorname{ext}_K(f), T and current universal elimination would contradict z_{\bar b}[\bar b]=\bot_K. Each missing tuple is tested separately; no component predicate for the set of all missing tuples is assumed.

For sufficiency, suppose f is rigid and its current extension is \bar q[S]. The membership observation gives root weak persistence. Let Z=(T,z) be any root test necessarily true at every tuple in S. Then S\subseteq T and z has whole value top on \bar q[S]. Consequently the following current premise is true in K:

\forall\bar b\,.\,f[\bar b]\to\Box z[\bar b].

Apply f’s component weak inextensibility to obtain f\le z. Equation (5) yields (S,f)\le Z, establishing root weak inextensibility. Component rigidity makes the whole old weak-rigidity value top; (4), with the root weak rigidity just proved, gives full root rigidity.

This argument uses the component’s current quantifier truth clause and its weak-inextensibility clause. It does not infer that a universal has whole value top merely because all its separately evaluated instances do.

4. The canonical rigid power and its box

Fix a root rigid F=(S,f). If an admitted component relation h is rigid and h\le f, put

S_h=\{\bar a\in S:\bar q(\bar a)\in\operatorname{ext}_K(h)\}, \qquad X_h=(S_h,h).

Every component member of h belongs to \operatorname{ext}_K(f) and therefore has a preimage in S by (6). Thus \bar q[S_h]=\operatorname{ext}_K(h). Equations (5)–(6) show that X_h is rigid and below F. This lifts every admitted rigid component subrelation; it does not claim that every current subset of S has a rigid lift.

Now consider the literal native lambda entity

\mathcal P_R(F):= \lambda X\,.\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F.

Its component is exactly \mathcal P_K(f), by term projection. Its current row C_F consists of all root rigid subrelations of F. Projection sends each such member to a component rigid subrelation of f, and the preceding construction supplies a preimage for every such component subrelation. Hence

q_\tau[C_F]=\operatorname{ext}_K(\mathcal P_K(f)).\tag{7}

The recorded C5 power theorem makes \mathcal P_K(f) rigid. Its proof uses S5 stability of the boxed conjuncts defining the family and component Barcan to obtain persistence and inextensibility. Apply (6) at the next type to (7): the canonical \mathcal P_R(F) is rigid. No replacement by a merely coextensive family occurs.

This proves Rigid Power at the new present. C5 also gives the whole component value of the entire Rigid Power instance as top. Equation (4) therefore proves its outer box at the root.

Nothing requires q_e to be onto. Mixed tuples of individuals and relations are handled by the same complement tests. For the empty tuple, the argument product is a singleton and the missing-tuple complement is bottom; the same proof gives the nullary instance.

5. Tame Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Power

Suppose only that F=(S,f) is weakly rigid at the root. Both image inclusions in §3 used only weak persistence and weak inextensibility, so \operatorname{ext}_K(f)=\bar q[S] still follows. Root weak persistence makes f[\bar b] top for every current component member \bar b of f. Thus f is weakly persistent in K.

For any admitted component test z satisfying \forall\bar b(f[\bar b]\to\Box z[\bar b]) currently in K, pair z with the full root row. This root test is necessarily true on S. Root weak inextensibility and (5) give f\le z in K. Therefore f is weakly inextensible, hence weakly rigid, in K.

C5 implies Tame Rigidity, so f is fully rigid in K. Its whole weak-rigidity value is consequently top. Together with the given root weak rigidity, (4) proves full rigidity of F. This establishes root Tame Rigidity. The component’s necessary Tame Rigidity makes the entire old schema value top; (4) supplies the required outer box.

The recorded implication

\Box\text{Tame Rigidity}+\Box\text{Rigid Power} \ \Longrightarrow\ \Box\text{Weakly Rigid Power}

now gives the stated strongest power verdict. The proof of that implication uses necessary Tame Rigidity to identify the weak and full canonical power predicates at the required modal scope. The argument includes all the arities covered in §4.

6. Standard arithmetic at the new present

Specialize to the component A from §1, and take

R_e=\omega\times A_e,\qquad q_e(n,a)=a.

Use the map’s own \mathbf0_e, successor, \mathbb N_e, Sum and Prod. Let \bar n^R be the n-fold application of its closed successor term to its closed zero, and let O=\{\bar n^R:n\in\omega\}. The row O, paired with any component predicate, is an admitted induction test. It contains zero and is closed under the actual root successor. The least-inductive definition of \mathbb N_e therefore excludes every entity outside O. Conversely every admitted inductive predicate contains each standard iterate by finite induction. Thus

\operatorname{ext}_R(\mathbb N_e)=O.\tag{8}

Term projection sends \bar n^R to \bar n^A. The component’s I^*_e makes its standard numerals pairwise distinct; root numerals are therefore pairwise distinct as well. Equation (8) gives current I^*_e directly, and its component is top by (1). Hence

(I^*_e)_R=(1,\top_A)=\top_R,\qquad R\models\Box I^*_e.\tag{9}

Standard number objects alone do not settle the arithmetic graphs. For fixed standard m, the admitted current row

\{(\bar i^R,\overline{m+i}^R):i\in\omega\}

satisfies the native Sum initial and closure conditions and rejects every incorrect standard output. Conversely any current relation satisfying those conditions contains each correct pair by finite induction. The universal definition of Sum consequently gives

R\models\operatorname{Sum}(\bar m,\bar n,\bar o) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad o=m+n.

For Prod use \{(\bar i^R,\overline{mi}^R):i\in\omega\}. The just-established Sum calculation proves its closure condition. Finite induction puts each correct product in every test satisfying the recursion premises; the displayed graph excludes incorrect products. Thus Prod is standard too. These are relational tests admitted by full current rows, paired with arbitrary component entities. No arithmetic operation is selected from its graph.

Induction on the native first-order arithmetic grammar now gives standard arithmetic at the root. Its restricted current quantifiers range over O, while its component quantifiers retain the whole number field of A. For the same sentence G^e as in §1,

(G^e)_R=(1,g),\qquad g\ne\top_A.\tag{10}

7. Both arithmetic failures with the guard true

By (9), I_e=\Diamond I^*_e is also the whole top entity. In the exact Necessity of Arithmetic instance

\Box(I_e\to G^e)\ \lor\ \Box(I_e\to\neg G^e),

the first alternative fails by (4) and (10), because g\ne\top_A. The second fails because \neg G^e is currently false. This refutes the native guarded disjunction, with \Box I^*_e true.

For the entity-level failure, A has a current canonical number c which is not a standard numeral. Otherwise its PA structure, including the total unique Sum and Prod graphs, would be standard and could not falsify the standard-true G. The admitted component predicate

z:=\lambda n^\nu\,.\,n\ne c

has whole value top on each standard component numeral, by crisp equality. Pair z with the full current R_\nu row to obtain Z. Every current root number is a standard numeral by (8), so each necessarily satisfies Z. But the whole component quantifier sees the current counterexample c to \mathbb N_A\subseteq z. Hence its inclusion formula is not top, and (5) gives

R\not\models\mathbb N_e\le Z.

This is a direct weak-inextensibility countertest for the canonical \mathbb N_e. It is not rigid. Together with (9), it refutes the type-e instance of Arithmetic Necessitism with its antecedent true.

The two arithmetic failures and all the power verdicts concern this one model. No verdict about Analytic Necessitism, SOAN, Boolean Completeness or C5 at the root is asserted. In particular, the component’s absence of real structures does not pass to a root with this different admission rule. Neither arithmetic failure is claimed necessary or at every counting type.
