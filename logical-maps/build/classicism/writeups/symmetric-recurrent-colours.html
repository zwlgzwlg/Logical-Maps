Symmetric ideally-full model: recurrent finite colour approximations

Construction and proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, research note of 26 October 2026. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

At W_1 this model satisfies:

  • □BF, □Rigid Power, □Boolean Completeness;
  • Tame Rigidity and Atomlessness.

It fails:

  • Weakly Rigid Power and □Tame Rigidity, both at type et;
  • Weak Rigid Comprehension at et.

So BF, Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power, even with BF and RP boxed and with □BC, do not give Weakly Rigid Power.

The earlier separation of Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power from Weakly Rigid Power, symmetric-finite-compression-entrance, used a one-way collapse of labels. Here every object can return to a finite-colour object, and that is what makes BF hold everywhere.

The model

Objects and colours. There is an object W_m for each positive integer m, and an object W_\infty. Each has as base a copy of \mathbb N, in which a set E_V of coloured points is fixed: |E_{W_m}|=m, and E_{W_\infty} is infinite and coinfinite. Every uncoloured complement is infinite.

Arrows and symmetries. For every pair of objects, the arrows V\to W are the surjections f of the bases with f[E_V]\subseteq E_W. The symmetry group at V is the permutations preserving E_V setwise. Every object carries the ideal of finite sets.

Domains and evaluation. The domains are the symmetric ideally-full ones (Dorr, Definitions 15–18). The individuals are the base points, and an entity of relational type is a symmetric intension pinned down by a finite set. The constants of Σ denote top elements. Evaluation point: 1_{W_1}.

Finite prescriptions. A finite partial map V\to W that sends coloured points to coloured points extends to an arrow. Send the remaining coloured points to some point of E_W, and use the infinitely many uncoloured points to cover the rest of W. This is used throughout.

1. □BF

Every arrow is surjective. So the group’s argument barcan-surjective applies at each object: an entity pinned down by M at the target of h is the transport along h of the entity built from a finite section of h over M. Transport is therefore onto at every type and every object, and BF holds necessarily. In particular, every universal relation is rigid.

2. Exact images and Tame Rigidity at the finite-colour objects

For a set D of tuples, let H_D be the profile h\mapsto h[D].

Lemma. Fix A=W_m and a finite S\supseteq E_A. If D is closed under the endomorphisms of A that fix S pointwise, then H_D is pinned down by S and symmetric. So it is in the domain, and it is rigid.

Proof. Let h,h^{\prime}:A\to V agree on S, and let a\in D be pinned down by M. Choose u:A\to A fixing S with hu=h^{\prime} on M, using h-preimages on M\setminus S. This u is an arrow, because every coloured point is in S, which u fixes. Then u\cdot a\in D and h^{\prime}\cdot a=h\cdot(u\cdot a). So h^{\prime}[D]\subseteq h[D], and by symmetry the two are equal. Symmetry of H_D follows from (gh)[D]=g\cdot h[D]. An exact image is rigid. \square

Tame Rigidity at A. Let F be weakly rigid at A, and let S contain a pin of F and E_A. An endomorphism fixing S fixes F, so by weak persistence D:=F_{1_A} is closed under it. By the lemma, H_D is in the domain and rigid. Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical, so F=H_D. Hence Tame Rigidity holds at every W_m, at every type, and every rigid relation there is an exact image.

3. At W_\infty, a weakly rigid relation that is not rigid

At B:=W_\infty, let U have extension E_V at every arrow h:B\to V. It is pinned down by \varnothing and symmetric, and it is weakly persistent because arrows preserve colour.

U is weakly inextensible. Let a test Z, pinned down by a finite N, hold necessarily of every coloured point of B, and take h:B\to V and y\in E_V. Choose a coloured x\notin N, and extend h|_N together with x\mapsto y to an arrow j. Then y=j\cdot x\in Z_j=Z_h. So U\le Z.

U is not rigid. Take an arrow c:B\to W_1, so that c\cdot U has extension E_{W_1}=\{a\}. The haecceity of a is rigid and coextensive with c\cdot U. Along any arrow back to B, though, the haecceity has one instance and c\cdot U infinitely many. So c\cdot U is not weakly inextensible at W_1, and U is not rigid.

So Tame Rigidity fails at W_\infty, and □Tame Rigidity at et fails at W_1. Tame Rigidity holds at W_1 and is a closed pure principle, so No Pure Contingency fails as well.

4. Rigid Power

Lifting into a finite-colour target. Fix A=W_1 and a rigid F at A. Let N be finite, containing E_A and a pin of F. Let h:A\to V with V=W_m, and let Y\le h\cdot F be rigid at V, with a finite pin T\supseteq E_V.

  1. Extend h|_N to an arrow j that maps A\setminus N bijectively onto V\setminus h[N]. Then j\cdot F=h\cdot F, and the nonsingleton fibres of j lie inside N.
  2. Let P be finite, containing N and one j-preimage of each member of T.
  3. Let D':=\{a\in F_{1_A}:j\cdot a\in Y_{1_V}\}. Since F and Y are exact images, j[D']=Y_{1_V}.
  4. Let u fix P pointwise. Then ju=vj for a surjection v of V that fixes T, so v is an endomorphism. Weak persistence of F and Y makes D' closed under u.
  5. By the lemma of §2, X:=H_{D'} is rigid. Then X\le F and j\cdot X=Y, two coextensive rigid relations at V being identical.

Lifting into W_\infty, by compression. Now let the target be B=W_\infty, with Y\le h\cdot F rigid there and pinned down by T. Put L:=T\cup h[N].

  1. Choose a finite K\subseteq E_B with L\cap E_B\subseteq K, and let V:=W_{|K|}.
  2. Let k:V\to B be a bijective arrow mapping E_V onto K.
  3. Let c:B\to V be an arrow agreeing with k^{-1} on L. Then kc fixes T, so k\cdot(c\cdot Y)=Y.
  4. g:=k^{-1}h is an arrow A\to V, since the coloured points of A lie in N. Moreover c\cdot Y\le g\cdot F.
  5. The finite-colour case gives v agreeing with g on N and a rigid X\le F with v\cdot X=c\cdot Y.
  6. Then j:=kv agrees with h on N, and j\cdot X=Y.

Rigid Power at W_1. Let Z hold necessarily of every actual rigid X\le F, and let N also contain a pin of Z. At any arrow h, a rigid Y\le h\cdot F is j\cdot X for such an X and some j agreeing with h on N, by the two liftings. So Y\in Z_j=Z_h. Hence the property of being rigid and below F is weakly inextensible. It is persistent, so by Tame Rigidity it is rigid. This holds at every type.

□Rigid Power. With □BF the universal relation of each type is rigid. Rigid Power applied to it makes the property of being rigid itself rigid, which is Rigid Rigidity. Rigid Rigidity and Rigid Power give □Rigid Power (result rigid-rigidity-and-rigid-power-imply-necessary-rigid-power, which is Astra’s §11).

5. Weakly Rigid Power fails at W_1

Let F be the universal relation of type et, which is weakly rigid. Let \mathcal W be the property of being a weakly rigid relation that entails F. Its actual instances are rigid, by Tame Rigidity at W_1, so they necessarily satisfy the test of being rigid. But along an arrow h:W_1\to W_\infty, the relation U of §3 lies in \mathcal W and is not rigid. So \mathcal W is not weakly inextensible, and Weakly Rigid Power fails at F.

6. □Boolean Completeness and Atomlessness

□Boolean Completeness. Dorr’s hull theorem (Theorem 36) applies at every object. Take M_0 to be the colour set at a W_m, and \varnothing at W_\infty.

  • (B1). Prescriptions agreeing with one arrow on P and another on Q are consistent on P\cap Q\subseteq M, and they respect colour, so they extend to an arrow.
  • (B2). At a W_m, the points of P outside N\supseteq M_0 are uncoloured and can be moved off Q by a symmetry fixing N. At W_\infty both colour classes are infinite, so the same holds.

So Boolean Completeness holds at every object.

Atomlessness. Let p\ne\bot be pinned down by S and true at some arrow h. For distinct uncoloured a,b\notin S, there are arrows agreeing with h on S that collapse a and b, and others that keep them apart, and all of them lie in p. So the proposition that a and b are collapsed splits p. Actuality fails everywhere.

7. Weak Rigid Comprehension fails at et

Let a be the coloured point of W_1, and suppose G is weakly rigid and coextensive with \lambda x\,.\,x\ne a. Let G be pinned down by a finite S\ni a, and take an uncoloured x\notin S, so that G holds of x. An endomorphism fixing S and sending x to a fixes G, so weak persistence gives a in G’s extension, which is false.
