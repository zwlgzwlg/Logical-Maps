# Symmetric surjections: a two-individual finite terminal

GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 30 October 2026.

Atomlessness and necessary all-type Inextensible Comprehension do not imply
necessary Atomlessness. The root below also has current numerical noncollapse
at the proposition type, while a finite future prevents a predecessor operation.
This combines Dorr's all-surjections mask-and-lift construction with a finite
terminal. It supplies one joint separation between existing principles.

## 1. Category and the whole admitted hierarchy

Work in external ZFC. The objects have individual carriers $A=\mathbb N$
and $F=\{0,1\}$. Arrows are all surjections $A\to A$, all maps $A\to F$
with two infinite fibers, and all permutations $F\to F$. There is no arrow
$F\to A$. Composition is ordinary function composition. For example, the
inverse image of an infinite fiber under a surjection is infinite, since
its image is that fiber. Thus the specified hom-sets are closed under
composition. The symmetry groups are all permutations of the two carriers,
and all finite subsets are permitted pins.

At a relational type $\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_kt$, including $k=0$,
an entity $X$ at $V$ is a profile assigning to each $h:V\to W$ a subset
$X_h\subseteq\prod_iD_{\sigma_i}(W)$. Admit *all* profiles satisfying:

$$
X_{gh}=g[X_h]\quad(g\in\operatorname{Sym}(W)),\qquad
h|_S=k|_S\Longrightarrow X_h=X_k
$$

for some finite $S\subseteq V$, where the second condition compares arrows
with the same target. Lower domains and their symmetry actions are defined
first, so every row uses the entire recursively admitted target domain.
Transport is $(hX)_l=X_{lh}$ and has pin $h[S]$.

Same-target arrows agreeing on $S$ transport $X$ to the same *whole* entity:
any further composition still agrees on $S$. A finite union of free parameter
pins therefore controls a full transported environment. An abstraction's
row at an arrow is its body's truth set over all target tuples. Whole
environment agreement proves finite pinning, including for modal and
quantified bodies. Target symmetry bijections of the full domains prove
covariance by induction on formulas. Quantifiers are never restricted to
images of root domains, and their closure does not take a union of pins
of all quantified objects.

These are the symmetric ideally-full action-model clauses. The full
hierarchy is rebuilt with $F$ present; it is not the old $A$ hierarchy with
rows attached afterward. Boolean operations, application and abstraction
have their ordinary row interpretations. Transported haecceities distinguish
whole entities. H soundness and theoremhood-level Logical Equivalence follow
as in the symmetric construction, since H-equivalent abstractions agree at
every future tuple coordinate. No optional comprehension or arithmetic
assumption is used in that rule. Interpret relational signature constants
by top, and evaluate at the identity of $A$.

## 2. The finite hierarchy and root Atomlessness

At $F$ every arrow is a symmetry. A profile is determined by its identity
extension $E$ and has row $g[E]$ at $g$. Conversely every subset of a lower-type
product gives such a profile, pinned by the whole finite carrier. Induction
gives a full finite extensional hierarchy at every type. In particular,
$D_t(F)=\{\bot,\top\}$, so Atomlessness fails there.

At $A$, let $p\ne\bot$ have pin $S$ and choose an arrow $h\in p$.
Take distinct $c,d\notin S$. Extend $h|_S$ in two ways, with the same target,
one identifying $c,d$ and the other separating them. If the target is $A$,
the remaining infinitely many inputs can make both maps surjective. If it
is $F$, divide the remaining inputs into two infinite fibers. Both arrows
are admitted and belong to $p$ by pinning.

The equality cylinder $C_{cd}=\{k:k(c)=k(d)\}$ is covariant and finitely
pinned. Hence $p\land C_{cd}$ is a nonzero proper part of $p$. This splits
even propositions true only on $F$-landing arrows. Two terminal individuals
are essential for that case. Thus the root is atomless, while an accessible
finite terminal refutes necessary Atomlessness.

## 3. Inextensible Comprehension at every type and every object

Fix any root relation $X$ pinned by $S$. Enlarge $S$ to finite $T$ with
at least three elements. Let $\delta_T$ be the proposition that an arrow
is injective on $T$, and put $Y=\delta_T\land X$. It is admitted, pinned
by $T$, and currently coextensive with $X$.

If an arrow collapses $T$, every further composite does so, and its transport
of $Y$ is the whole bottom relation. Every $A\to F$ arrow is of this kind.
For a noncollapsing arrow $i:A\to A$, choose a permutation $\pi$ such that
$g=\pi i$ fixes $T$ pointwise. Whole pinning gives $gY=Y$.

Let $Z$ be an arbitrary admitted test necessarily true of each current
$Y$-tuple. To show $Y\le Z$, the collapsing case is trivial. In the other
case use covariance to transfer from $i$ to $g$. For any tuple
$r\in Y_g=Y_1$, choose a common finite pin $L$ for its entries. For each
$l\in L\setminus T$ select a distinct $g$-preimage $l'$ outside $T$.
Such choices exist because $g$ is onto and fixes $T$. Extend the finite
injection $l\mapsto l'$ to a permutation $\theta$ fixing $T$.
Then $g\theta$ fixes $L$, so $g(\theta r)=r$ as a whole tuple. Since
$\theta$ fixes $Y$, the tuple $\theta r$ is a current $Y$-instance, and
the hypothesis on $Z$ makes $Z_g(r)$ true. Covariance transfers back to
$i$. All target tuples, including newly admitted higher-type ones, have
been covered. This proves weak inextensibility of $Y$.

For its leading box, a collapsing transport is bottom. For a noncollapsing
$h$, the same choice of $\pi$ gives $\pi(hY)=Y$. Thus $hY$ is an
automorphic copy of $Y$ and is weakly inextensible. This establishes
inextensibility, including the new finite branches. At $F$, every transport
is a permutation, and each relation's future instances are precisely its
transported current instances. Every relation there is consequently rigid
and inextensible.

Inextensible Comprehension holds at both objects at every relational type.
A pure closed instance at any arrow is evaluated at its target, so the root
satisfies the *additional outer box* of necessary Inextensible Comprehension.
This is a direct all-target verification; it does not infer necessity from
an optional current premise.

## 4. Current numberhood and the finite collision

Let the counting type be $t$ and the number type $\nu=(tt)t$. Write
$\mathbf n_t=\operatorname{Suc}_t^n\mathbf0_t$. There are infinitely many
distinct root propositions: fix $a\in A$ and vary $b\ne a$ in $C_{ab}$;
finite collision prescriptions extend to surjections and distinguish them.
Every finite set of these propositions is the current extension of an
admitted finite union of haecceities. Finite Frege counting shows that its
cardinality is recognized by the corresponding numeral and by no differently
indexed numeral. All standard root numerals are distinct.

To exclude nonstandard current numbers, use an actual admitted induction
test. At an $A$-loop, let its extension be exactly the standard numeral
orbit at $A$, and let all $F$-landing rows be empty. This profile is
empty-pinned and target-covariant, since closed pure numerals are fixed by
symmetries. At $A$ it contains zero and is successor-closed. Impredicative
numberhood is therefore included in its current extension; external finite
induction gives the reverse inclusion. All current numbers are standard.
Thus successor is injective on them and zero is not a successor:
$I^*_t$, hence $I_t=\Diamond I^*_t$, holds. This notation is distinct from
the quantified Axiom of Infinity at $t$.

At $F$, the proposition carrier has exactly two elements and every property
of it is admitted. The universal property witnesses $\mathbf2_t\ne\bot_\nu$,
whereas no property has three or four instances. Thus

$$
\mathbf3_t=\mathbf4_t=\bot_\nu,\qquad
\mathbf2_t\ne\mathbf3_t.
$$

These are whole identities and inequalities in the full terminal hierarchy.
There is no later escape from this finite hierarchy, so $I_t$ fails at $F$.

## 5. Why a predecessor operation cannot be extracted

If a root operation $P:\nu\nu$ satisfied
$(P\mathbf3_t)=\mathbf2_t$ and $(P\mathbf4_t)=\mathbf3_t$,
transport to an $A\to F$ arrow would preserve both identities, closed
numerals and application. At $F$ the inputs coincide and the outputs do not,
a contradiction. A predecessor operation would require these two identities,
since $\mathbf2_t$ and $\mathbf3_t$ are current numbers. None exists.

The finite relational graph containing those two pairs is nevertheless a
rigid finite union of tuple haecceities. It becomes multivalued at the
finite terminal. A rigid graph at type $\nu\nu t$ is therefore insufficient
to produce the required operation at the different type $\nu\nu$.
More generally, finite interpolation of relational outputs requires
$\Box(x_i=x_j\to y_i=y_j)$ for every pair. Necessity follows by transporting
an operation's identities. For sufficiency, the finite lambda term
$f(x)[\bar z]=\bigvee_i(x=x_i\land y_i[\bar z])$ realizes the values under
that boxed compatibility condition. The finite terminal falsifies this
condition for the displayed numeral pairs.

This defeats predecessor extraction even with Atomlessness, necessary
Inextensible Comprehension and current $I_t$. It does not give a Maximalist
model. The pure sentence asserting exactly one individual is C-consistent
in a full one-world singleton model but impossible here, where the only
reachable carriers are infinite or two-element. Thus pure Maximalism fails.
No Boolean Completeness, Choice or Barcan verdict is inferred.

The pin-injectivity mask and arbitrary-tuple permutation lift come from
Dorr's all-surjections proof. The local change is the finite target and the
larger mask that makes every new finite branch whole bottom. The
codomain-indexed standard-orbit method and numerical collision obstruction
are inherited methods; the model's contribution is their verified joint
Atomlessness/comprehension package with a finite future.
