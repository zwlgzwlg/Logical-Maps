Qualitative-link model with a translation clock

GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi. Construction and proof dated 30 October 2026. The recursive old-domain injection and every-new-test slicing argument preserve the singleton qualitative-link Tame counterexample while the clock supplies all-carrier represented real powers. Actual individuals remain singleton; the auxiliary pinning base is separate.

1. Exact target and why full completion is the wrong extension

Native Tame Rigidity is forall F(WeaklyRigid(F)->Rigid(F)), at every relational type. A witness is persistent and weakly inextensible at the evaluation point but loses weak inextensibility later. Nullary propositions cannot witness this failure; the chosen witness has type (tt)t.

Unrestricted full action completion will not preserve this witness in general. In a fully admitted profile hierarchy, the exact direct-image relation of any actual extension is admitted and rigid. Every weakly rigid relation is coextensive with such a rigid relation and hence identical to it, so Tame Rigidity follows. This is the obstruction explained in Kodsi homogeneity-amalgamation-lane.md section 22. Merely applying the full-action carrier criterion to a completion would therefore destroy the negative side of the intended separation.

The extension below enlarges only an independent translation coordinate while keeping a uniform finite pin on the old coordinate. Its preservation proof addresses every new test, rather than presuming that an old weak- rigidity verdict survives additional predicates.

2. Old model and its concrete negative witness

Let B be the auxiliary qualitative-link base, M its monoid, and G its symmetry group, exactly as in native symmetric-qualitative-links.yaml. Use its singleton-individual variant. The actual individual sort is a singleton; it is separate from B. Relational domains D_tau contain exactly the external G-symmetric, finitely B-pinned row profiles at that type.

The native tame-rigidity argument records U:(tt)t as follows. Let a,b be the distinguished point pair, V the nonpoints, q_uv={h:hu=hv}, and C_z the rigid exact-image star of {q_zw:w in V}. The map z -> C_z is injective and transports by h to C_hz. Put

U_h = {C_z : z in R(ha,hb)}.

It is symmetric and pinned by {a,b}. It is weakly persistent because arrows preserve the qualitative relation R. Here is the weak-inextensibility argument relevant to preservation. For a test Z with finite pin S which necessarily holds of every initial C_z in the a,b link fibre, enlarge S to a finite closed set including a,b. At a symmetry h, every member of the future fibre is an image of an initial member. At a collapsing h, a fresh initial link outside the closed finite pin can be sent to either desired companion of ha=hb by an arrow h’ agreeing with h on S. The test holds there by its premise, and pinning makes its h and h’ rows equal. Thus the test contains every U_h. This uses the source’s finite coherent- prescription extension step (draft Proposition 50), exactly the step already used in the recorded negative verdict.

At a collapse h the U_h fibre consists of the two companion codes C_d0, C_d1. Their finite union of haecceities is rigid and necessarily holds of both. A further allowed collapse k merges d0,d1; the test then holds only of their single image code, while U_kh still contains both distinct companion codes of the new point. Thus h.U is not weakly inextensible.

The source dependence is precise: existence of those finite-prescription extensions, injectivity of the C_z codes, and the recorded model-of-C construction. No field-existence or AN verdict from that source is used.

3. Product category and its exact admitted domains

Form the disjoint auxiliary base B’=B disjoint-union omega. Let

M' = M x (omega,+).

The arrow (m,n) acts as m on B and as k -> k+n on the added omega. Let G’=G x {0}, acting trivially on that omega. Use the finite-set pin ideal and singleton actual individuals. Form the symmetric ideally-full model with these data; write its recursively generated domains D’_tau.

Composition is (l,r)(m,n)=(lm,r+n). Images of finite pins are finite, the symmetries are invertible and preserve the ideal, and every actual individual has the empty pin. These are ordinary data for the same symmetric ideally-full construction as the old model.

An exact useful description of the new domains is:

a profile is G'-symmetric and has ONE finite spatial pin S subset B
which pins its rows at every fixed clock coordinate n.

There is no restriction on its dependence on n. For sufficiency adjoin the singleton pin {0_clock}: agreement there forces the two clock shifts to be equal, and agreement on S then forces equal rows. For necessity take the B-part of any finite pin: at the same clock coordinate all clock pin values automatically agree. This equivalence is at each recursively defined type, with rows on the NEW argument domains. In particular it is not a construction that simply restricts the outermost row indices of an already full higher-type model.

The optional relational signature constants can be top, with any individual constants denoting the sole individual. No extra landing or Henkin conditions are imposed.

4. Embedding old profiles and slicing EVERY new test

Define injections i_tau:D_tau -> D’_tau by induction on types. At e use the singleton identity. For a relation F with tuple arguments of types sigma_1,…,sigma_k, define

(i F)_(m,n) = { (i x_1,...,i x_k) : (x_1,...,x_k) in F_m }.

For k=0 this is simply the old proposition independent of the clock. If F is pinned by S, iF is pinned by the same S and is symmetric by the inductive equivariance of the i_sigma. The injections are injective because an old distinguishing row and tuple remain distinguishing. They satisfy, as WHOLE entities,

(m,n).i_tau(x) = i_tau(m.x).

This follows by evaluating both sides at every further (l,r); both rows are the i-image of the old row at lm. In particular G’-equivariance holds. These maps need not preserve arbitrary logical operations or unrestricted quantifiers. They are equivariant injections of profile domains; the preservation argument below does not assume more.

Now let Z be ANY admitted new test of a relation’s tuple type. For each fixed external n define an old profile by

Z[n]_m = { x_tuple : i(x_tuple) belongs to Z_(m,n) }.

The exact domain description in section 3 gives it a finite old spatial pin. It is old G-symmetric: the i maps commute with G, and invertibility of g ensures membership in the image of i is preserved in both directions. Therefore Z[n] is admitted in the OLD domain. This is the crucial check which rules out newly available tests silently defeating old weak rigidity.

Suppose F is old weakly rigid. The image iF is weakly persistent, by equivariance and old weak persistence. If a new test Z necessarily holds of all members i(x) of its current extension, then for every x in F_1, every m, and every n,

i(m.x) belongs to Z_(m,n).

Thus each old Z[n] necessarily holds of every current F-member. Old weak inextensibility gives F_m subset Z[n]_m for every m. Consequently

(iF)_(m,n) subset Z_(m,n)

for every (m,n), proving new weak inextensibility. The same reasoning applies at all transported old profiles. Thus rigid old profiles also remain rigid.

If old h.F fails weak inextensibility with an old test Q, then at (h,0) the lifted test iQ has the same premise on the lifted current extension. All clock shifts are irrelevant to iQ. The old later omitted tuple lifts to a new omitted tuple, so the failure survives. Applying this to section 2 proves that iU is weakly rigid but not rigid. No elementary/conservative embedding claim for the entire language is needed or made.

5. A continuum whose members survive every clock shift

Partition omega into infinite unbounded sets

B_j = {2^j(2l+1)-1 : l in omega},  j in omega.

For A subset omega let s_A=union_{j in A} B_j. If A!=A’, their two sequences disagree at arbitrarily large coordinates. Therefore EVERY common shift n leaves them distinct: some k has k+n in their symmetric difference.

The proposition p_A in the new model is defined by

(m,n) belongs to p_A iff n belongs to s_A.

It is symmetric and pinned by {0_clock}. For each arrow (m,n), the map A -> (m,n).p_A is injective: its values at later clock shifts k detect the arbitrarily late disagreement. This establishes the same-type separation needed at t, not merely continuum size of the domain.

For EVERY relational carrier tau=sigma_1…sigma_k t, including k=0, define F_A^tau by putting the universal tuple row at (m,n) when n is in s_A, and the empty row otherwise. All argument domains are nonempty, so the two rows differ. These profiles are admitted with the same single clock pin and are fixed by every (g,0). Their whole transports depend only on the clock shift, and

A -> (m,n).F_A^tau

is injective for every arrow. This checks the carrier construction at each tau separately, including types whose arguments themselves involve e or arbitrarily high relational types.

6. Field and power admission, not just a full-model criterion

Fix an external injection r -> A_r from R into P(omega). At each chosen relational carrier tau put

j_n(r) = (1_M,n).F_(A_r)^tau.

Every j_n is injective and (m,k).j_n(r)=j_(n+k)(r). All j_n(r) are fixed by G’. Define the field carrier row at (m,n) as j_n[R], with order, addition and multiplication the j_n-images of the ordinary real-field relations, and natural row j_n[N]. Zero and one at the source are j_0(0) and j_0(1).

Each predicate profile depends only on n, so {0_clock} pins it. Its rows are G’-invariant since their members are G’-fixed. Thus the profiles are admitted. They have exact-image transport at every intermediate point. That gives persistence and the native leading-box inextensibility: every future member is the image of a current member, so any test necessarily true of all current members covers every future extension. The j_n injections give necessary distinctness. At each point the current field is externally R, complete against all admitted subsets and with its specified natural predicate least. Hence the tuple is rigid and necessarily real at THIS SAME tau.

For each external T subset R^k define Q_T with row j_n^k[T] at (m,n). It has the same pin and symmetry proof and the same exact-image rigidity. Define the representing family at (m,n) to be

{ (m,n).Q_T : T subset R^k }.

This is a set of whole relation entities. It depends only on n, its members are fixed by G’, and its further transport is exactly the family at the sum of the clock coordinates. Thus it too is admitted and rigid. At a target with clock n, restrict ANY target relation to j_n[R]^k and pull it back by j_n. The resulting external subset T has transported representative (m,n).Q_T in the family. This verifies the boxed arbitrary- target representation clause, not only representation of transported source relations. All finite arities use the same chosen field.

Therefore the construction supplies the SOAN consequent at every relational carrier type, without needing to inspect its antecedent. At e there is a singleton at every point, so no ordered real field is possible and the e instances are vacuous. This proves full SOAN and AN. It does not infer all-type AN from one real-bearing carrier.
