# Arithmetic can change between recurrent worlds

At one point of this model, B holds for pure sentences and for sentences of the fixed signature. Necessary Distinctness, Gallin Extensional Comprehension and local Well-Ordering all hold there, yet Necessity of Arithmetic fails. In particular, neither local Relational Choice nor local Transversal Choice prevents the disagreement.

*OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi. Independently reviewed ordinary mathematical proof in ZFC.*

The model has two worlds. Each can reach the other, so any closed pure sentence true at one world can be made true again after any move. The individuals carry Peano arithmetic at both worlds. One fixed arithmetic sentence is true at the first and false at the second. The work is to interpret relations of *all* the simple types while keeping induction for every numerical property they define.

An abstraction made now must say how its relation acts on an argument that first exists later. A fixed formula asks finitely many different questions about that future argument, each on arbitrarily many numerical inputs. Record all the answers as a set of numbers. In the construction, one future argument realizes the proposed answers exactly when every finite part can be realized. This is where countable saturation enters. At higher types we define application so that this same calculation still suffices when the argument is itself a relation.

## The arithmetic excursion

Use a recursively axiomatized set theory $S$ true in all sufficiently large regular $H_\lambda$. Concretely one can take ZFC without Power Set, with Collection and Separation, the existence of $V_{\omega+\omega+n}$ for each ordinary $n$, and the assertion that every consistent countable membership-language theory has a countably saturated set model. The latter holds in these $H_\lambda$ by the usual construction of a saturated elementary extension over countable parameter sets, of size at most the continuum for a countable starting model. Fix the arithmetical sentence

$$
                       \theta=\mathrm{Con}(S+\mathrm{True}_{\Pi^0_1}).
$$

Here the additional axioms are the true closed universal arithmetic sentences with recursive tests; fix the usual arithmetic coding of axioms and finite derivations. Refutation would consist of a finite proof with all its additional universal premises true, so consistency has a $\Pi^0_2$ formulation. The sentence is true. If $S$ with those premises proved it, a finite conjunction $\gamma$ of the premises would suffice. In $S+\gamma$ one verifies that these fixed premises meet the truth test. The asserted consistency would then imply consistency of $S+\gamma$, contrary to the second incompleteness theorem. Thus there is a negative set universe satisfying $S$, all the true universal arithmetic sentences, and $\neg\theta$.

Before identifying the positive return with the positive origin, the universes are $A,B,T$. The negative universe $B$ is set-presented in $A$, and $T$ is set-presented in $B$. Write $i:N_A\to N_B$ and $j:N_B\to N_T$ for the canonical numeral maps. Their images are initial segments, by induction in each presenting universe. An elementary map $e:A\to T$ agrees with $ji$ on numbers. The positive universe has a set giving the graph of $e$ on every member of its $V_{\omega+\omega}$. Moreover **$B$ regards its presented $T$ as countably saturated**. This assertion concerns types and sequences belonging to $B$.

For existence, start in ordinary mathematics with $H=H_\lambda$ large and well founded. Require the negative universe to present a countably saturated $T$ satisfying each ordinary sentence of $\mathrm{Th}(H)$. Any finite list from that theory is genuinely consistent. Its consistency is a true universal arithmetic assertion, so the negative universe can build the desired saturated model of that list. Compactness gives the pair. Take an externally $\mu^+$-saturated elementary extension of the pair for $\mu=|V_{\omega+\omega}|$. The extension can be taken of size at most $2^\mu$; choose $\lambda$ above this bound and the required finite rank segments before fixing $\mathrm{Th}(H)$. Use set presentation codes in $H$.

Choose $R_0\prec H$ of size $\mu$ containing the codes and **all** elements of $V_{\omega+\omega}$. Embed it elementarily into the positive member of the pair. Its graph on this low segment, using the representatives in the presentation, is a set in $H$. Include the graph in the next elementary substructure and extend the same embedding by saturation. Repeatedly include representatives of the images. The union gives a standard-arithmetic positive origin with the claimed set graph, and with the embedding landing in the structure it actually presents. No new member of the low segment was acquired. Standard numerals give the numerical composite equation on all origin numbers. All these properties survive elementary enlargement of the entire diagram.

Fold the positive return onto the origin by an isomorphism $l:T\cong A$ in a further elementary enlargement. Keep the graph and the countably many fixed finite future presentation codes among the paired marks. The codes are formed two steps at a time by attaching the $e$-image of the shorter tower. The fold exists by back-and-forth on the two elementarily equivalent marked positive reducts, taking elementary enlargements of the full diagram to realize each requested type. Thus $g=le$ is elementary and preserves the marked codes. Its restriction to the negative presentation gives an auxiliary elementary map $\delta$ of $B$. A positive tuple recurs at positive visits through $g$ and at negative visits through $\delta$, provided its transport out of positive is given by the marked sets. The true negative return first passes through positive. In particular truth of a fixed finite-horizon set test on a transported tuple repeats by parity at every **proper** visit. The initial negative test can differ. The two arithmetic signs survive every enlargement and the fold.

## Comparing claims at two worlds

Inside $B$ write $D=V_{\omega+\omega}^B$. All the finitely many ordinary type domains over its numbers that occur in a fixed formula, as well as their finite products and powersets, are sets in $D$. Use two sorts of set-language tests. The first runs in $D$; the second runs in the presented $T$, with any designated finite tower marks there. **The only expressions crossing from the first language to the second are numerals $j(b)$** for $b$ a negative number. Variables and quantifiers in the second language may range over arbitrary specified sets of $T$.

In any fixed mixed formula, eliminate a quantifier $\exists z$ over the second language as follows. After the inner quantifiers have been treated, list its finitely many second-language atoms $\psi_s(z,\bar t,j\bar b)$ involving $z$, including any fixed second-language formulas used as atomic tests. Choose a real of $B$ for the proposed answers to all those tests on all numerical tuples $\bar b$, marking the finite list by numerical tags. Substitute these answers in the first-language part, leaving every quantifier there in its original position. It remains to test whether one $z$ gives the proposed answers.

For each $d\in N_B$ code the prescribed bits for all tuples below $d$ by a number $u$ of $B$. A *single fixed formula* of $T$, with parameters $\bar t,j(d),j(u)$, says that some eligible $z$ realizes this block. Its bounded quantifiers see exactly the tuples below $d$ transported by $j$. Thus checking all blocks uses only numerical crossings, and the test in $T$ has lost the free variable $z$. If all blocks pass, the resulting type over the numerical images, with the fixed tuple $\bar t$, is a **countable type in $B$**. The code for that type is formed there from the chosen real, the standard test formulas and the presented maps. Countable saturation *in $B$* supplies a single realizing $z$. Conversely a given $z$ produces its trace real by separation in $B$. Applying this argument by structural induction leaves only finitely many second-language tests on the **free** positive arguments and on negative numerical indices. All other tests and quantifiers are in the first language. This is the step that allows an unrestricted predicate quantifier to choose a predicate here and use it later.

## What a relation can read

Describe a relation of type $\rho=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k t$ by its evaluations on a full tuple. The empty tuple gives propositions. For an individual at negative use a local number $b$ and return number $j(b)$. At a relation type use the following recursive rule, working with raw representatives before identifying equal relations. Write $\bar a$ for the tuple of first-language components of the arguments and $\bar t$ for their tuple of second-language components.

* At negative store three objects $(Y,V,z)$. The object $Y$ is an arbitrary local set of pairs $(\bar a,R)$, with $R$ a local real. The set $V$ in $T$ takes $\bar t$ and one number as arguments. The object $z$ is a positive representative of this same relation type in $T$. Local evaluation uses the one real

  $$
      R=\{b\in N_B: (\bar t,j(b))\in V\},\qquad
      (Y,V,z)[\mathrm{args}]\ \longleftrightarrow\ (\bar a,R)\in Y.
  $$

  The local component for any subsequent test is $Y$; the second-language component is $(V,z)$. Moving to positive keeps $z$.
* At positive store $(X,q,r,V_0)$. The set $X$ is the current row on lower positive representatives. The formula code $q$ describes a first-language test for $(\bar a,R)$ with one real parameter, and $r$ is a positive real. The last component $V_0$ is a positive set on the second-language argument shapes and a numerical index. On moving to negative take $V=e(V_0)$, $z=e(X,q,r,V_0)$, and use for $Y$ the set defined by $q$ in $D$ with real parameter $\{b:j(b)\in e(r)\}$. Ill-formed codes can give the empty set. Satisfaction in the *set* structure $D$ is available in $B$, including on its internal codes.

All raw coordinate shapes are finite-rank sets over the natural numbers. In defining them one can use unrestricted lower raw sets and impose admissibility separately. In particular the rank of $V_0$ depends only on the types of the **arguments**. A parameter of much higher type will be removed by taking a section at that parameter. The positive graph on the low segment therefore computes the entire positive-to-negative map inside $A$. It uses no graph on the universe containing its own presentation.

Require a current row to respect equivalent tuples of admissible lower-type arguments. At positive require this both here and at the immediate negative image. The marked return then gives it on all subsequent visits. At negative require it here and require admissibility of the positive tail. Two admissible positive representatives are equivalent when their current and immediate negative rows agree on all admissible arguments; at negative require current agreement and equivalence of the positive tails. These tests are well founded in the types: first use the two **lower** lists to compute current congruence and agreement at both sites, next the positive test at the present type, and finally the negative test with its positive tail. The negative current test is in the mixed language by the trace evaluation just displayed; the condition on the tail is one test in $T$. The positive tests are set definitions on finite marked presentations. Equivalence is precisely agreement of whole profiles on every tuple at every future, including tuples born there. The maps descend to the quotient.

## Why comprehension holds

Use first the relational language with atomic application on full tuples, identity, quantifiers, Booleans and modality. With the data above, its truth on any finite horizon is an ordinary local set definition. A possibility can be tested at the current, first and second sites, by parity on the transported tuple. Eliminating possibilities from the inside outward gives a finite-horizon test for any fixed ordinary formula. In particular its positive definition in a returned copy uses exactly the images of the corresponding finite positive marks.

At a negative site there is also a **mixed** definition. Application uses the trace rule, and equality and the guards on the quantifiers use the preceding type recursion. A move to positive carries a number to $j(b)$ and a relation to its positive tail. For a diamond, combine the current test for its matrix with the positive possibility test after that first move. The latter is a single set formula in $T$. No local set of $B$ is passed into a test in $T$. Now apply the quantifier elimination above.

Consider a positive abstraction with fixed parameters and arbitrary tuple of arguments. Its current numerical or higher row $X$ is given by the positive set definition. At its immediate negative image, the negative components of its *parameters* are already specified by their old programs. Each remaining test in $T$ has only three sorts of inputs: images of the fixed positive parameters and marks, the second-language components of the *arguments*, and numerical indices. By separation at positive these finitely many positive test formulas, with their parameters fixed, define **one** set $V_0$ on the argument shapes and the tagged numerical indices. Its image under $e$ gives exactly the required tests, including on new arguments of $T$. The real read from $e(V_0)$ at negative supplies every answer needed by the remaining first-language formula, including on indices chosen by its later quantifiers.

The first-language formula therefore gives $q$. To remove its fixed local parameter relations, bind each as the extension of its old positive program on its raw input shape, substituting the program codes and tagging their real banks together. These are operations on finite syntax *inside the positive universe*. At negative the two-sorted coding of numerals commutes with transport, and satisfaction in $D$ obeys substitution for those transported codes, even when they are nonstandard. Fixed numerical parameters can be included as singleton columns of the bank. The result has exactly the required form $(X,q,r,V_0)$. Its two verified rows persist through every positive return on all new arguments by marked elementarity.

For an abstraction originating at negative use the same eliminated formula. Put its finitely many second-language tests, now with the actual second-language parameters fixed, into one $T$-set $V$. The remaining local formula with the actual first-language parameters defines an allowed set $Y$. In the presented positive universe the positive construction just proved gives the tail $z$ for the abstraction on the transported parameters. These witnesses respect argument equivalence because their defining formula does, so they pass the admissibility tests. This proves comprehension at every type.

Full-tuple comprehension supplies the usual sections for curried application. Nested abstraction terms can be eliminated from relational formulas by binding their unique whole profiles, tested by necessary agreement on all remaining arguments. Equivalently, interpret terms successively by application and the comprehension already proved. Equality of profiles makes transport agree with these operations also on fresh arguments, and supplies the separating arrow and tuple for unequal relations. These are the action-model clauses for C. The definition of necessity as identity with the everywhere-true proposition gives exactly the reachability used here.

## Reading off the counterexample

At each site every admitted numerical row belongs to its local set universe. Induction holds there for every such row, even if it was obtained by quantifying over higher types. The ordinary arithmetic graphs on the individual numbers have admissible profiles: use their local arithmetic definitions at each phase and the corresponding positive tail. Each site thus has a Peano presentation on $e$. The [local Frege comparison](#comparing-the-peano-presentation-with-frege-arithmetic) identifies its arithmetic with that of the finite cardinalities of properties of $e$, and gives the guard $I_e$. Hence $I_e\land\theta$ at positive and $I_e\land\neg\theta$ at negative. From either site both signs are reachable after every move. Both sites satisfy B for closed pure sentences by returning to the original site. Thus the single instance at counting type $e$ and sentence $\theta$ fails.

At the positive site one obtains even $\forall p\,(p\to\Box\Diamond p)$. For any currently true proposition, the elementary positive return preserves the truth bit of its representative. From every accessible site one can reach such a positive return. This argument also covers propositions supplied as parameters.

## Comparing the Peano presentation with Frege arithmetic

Fix a pure type $\tau$. At one world, a *Peano presentation on $\tau$* consists of a domain $D\subseteq\tau$, a zero, a successor graph, and the ordinary arithmetic relations on $D$. Require the Peano axioms, including induction for every admitted predicate on $D$. The relations are entities of the given typed C model. The presentation is chosen at this world. Its values elsewhere play no role in the following argument.

**Local comparison.** Every such presentation is isomorphic, by an admitted graph at this world, to the Frege arithmetic counting at $\tau$. In particular that arithmetic satisfies its Peano guard.

Write $0_F,S_F,N_F$ for Frege zero, successor and numberhood. Let $J(a,X)$ be the least relation containing $(0_D,0_F)$ and closed under simultaneously taking the two successors. Form it by quantifying over the admitted relations of this mixed type. It is itself admitted by abstraction. Its projections lie in $D$ and $N_F$, and cover both by the respective induction principles. The relation unfolds: every pair in it is the initial pair or the successor of a pair in it. Indeed the pairs constructed in one of these two ways themselves form an inductive relation contained in $J$. Induction on $a$, using the disjointness of zero and the injectivity of successor in $D$, now gives exactly one $X$ for each $a$.

It remains to see that different $a$'s have different outputs. Write $D_{<a}$ for the initial segment strictly below $a$. Consider the local relation between $a$ and $X$ asserting that, for every admitted property $P$ of $\tau$,

$$
  X(P)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
  \text{there is an admitted bijection from }D_{<a}\text{ onto the current extension of }P.
$$

This relation contains the initial pair. It is also closed under paired successors. From a bijection on $D_{<S a}$ remove the last domain point $a$ and its image $y$. Conversely, append that pair to a bijection onto $P\setminus\{y\}$ when $P(y)$. Both new graphs are given by ordinary abstraction. This is exactly the defining test for $S_F X$ on $P$. So every pair in $J$ satisfies the displayed equivalence.

If $a<b$ had the same output, test it on $P=D_{<a}$. The identity bijection would give a bijection from $D_{<b}$ onto $D_{<a}$, contrary to the finite pigeonhole principle inside the presentation. For this use of pigeonhole, induct on the smaller bound: an injection into the successor of an interval either misses its last point or hits it once; in the latter case swap its preimage to the last point of the longer domain and delete the pair. These graphs are admitted, so this induction applies even to nonstandard bounds. Thus $J$ is a bijection. It preserves zero and successor by construction. The recursions for addition and multiplication agree across it by induction in the second argument. This establishes first-order arithmetic agreement. The argument uses whole-entity equality when unfolding $J$. It never infers identity of cardinality entities from agreement of their current rows.

## Gallin Extensional Comprehension at the positive world

Write $X$ for the current row of a positive relation. Keep that row and let the negative reading on *any* tuple be the value of the return row $e(X)$ on the tuple's projection into the positive successor. This is an allowed relation in the construction above. Put that test at bit zero of the return-side table and let the local program read bit zero. Equivalent admissible arguments have equivalent projections, so the test respects the quotient. On a positive-to-negative move the projected tuple is the elementary image of the original tuple; on a negative-to-positive move the test is precisely evaluation on the projected tuple. Both truth values therefore persist at every subsequent move, also for arguments first born at negative. This gives a coextensive Gallin witness at every relational type. At positive, distinct entities also stay distinct: equality at an intermediate visit would persist to a positive return, which reflects whole identity by elementarity. Quantified local B applies to every closed sentence in a coherent interpretation of the fixed signature.

## Local choice at the positive world

Fix a simple type. In the positive set universe, its admissible representatives form a set and agreement as whole entities is a definable equivalence on that set. Choice in that universe gives a well-order of the quotient. Pull the comparison back to the representatives and take it as the positive row of a binary relation. Use the always-false program for the outgoing negative row. The positive row respects equivalence by construction and the negative row respects it on every argument. The relation is therefore admitted. Every instantiated property has a nonempty internal subset of the quotient as its current extension, so it has a least member for this order. This verifies local Well-Ordering at each simple type. The order may be externally ill-founded.

Choosing the least available output in each current fibre gives local Relational Choice, and choosing the least member of each equivalence class gives local Transversal Choice. Both constructions give predicates with the required current rows. Plenitude would additionally require an operation whose value is the selected whole relation.

## Why Plenitude and Functional Choice remain open premises

Plenitude asks for an operation representing each locally total single-valued relation with relational outputs. At positive one can specify a local graph assigning to each numerical program code $n$ a predicate $d_n$ of type $(et)t$. Choose its positive row empty. At the next negative visit, $d_n(y)$ tests formula $n$ of the set structure $D=V_{\omega+\omega}^B$ on the current numerical extension of the predicate $y$. To recover that extension, the return-side table packs the bits $V_y(jb,jk)$ for pairs $(b,k)$; the local component $Y_y$ reads the section at $b$. Splice formula $n$ into that local test, giving malformed codes the false program. Thus each $d_n$ is admitted, including on fresh arguments. The assignment of positive descriptors is an internal set function. Its whole-equality graph on admissible representatives is an allowed positive current row with empty outgoing negative row.

Suppose one positive operation represented this graph. At negative give it the transported input $i(n)$ and a fresh predicate with arbitrary current real $r$, empty test table, and fixed empty positive tail. Its output would give the truth of formula $i(n)$ on $r$ in $D$. For the proposed operation these same answers are given by a single local program $H(i(n),r;u)$. Indeed the test table on these arguments depends only on the numerical argument and numerical bit index: the fresh predicate's two return-side components are fixed. All those bits and the operation's local real bank pack into one negative real $u$. Its local component on the fresh argument is specified simply by membership in $r$. The syntax of $H$, with the bank as a *variable*, is obtained by a fixed code transformation from the operation's positive program.

Inside the positive universe diagonalize that code to obtain a one-real formula $L(c)$ of code $\ell$ saying $\neg H(\ell,c;c)$. The numeral map respects the syntax operations; satisfaction for the *set* $D$ in the negative universe verifies substitution also for nonstandard transported codes. Correctness of the representing operation at $n=\ell$, $r=u$ would make $H(i(\ell),u;u)$ both agree and disagree with $L(u)$. Thus Plenitude fails at positive. At negative, a proposition true only at its initial visit and the constantly false proposition merge after leaving, violating ND. The map proves that Plenitude implies ND and that Functional Choice implies Plenitude. Neither world supplies either outstanding premise.
