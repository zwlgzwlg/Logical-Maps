Symmetric ideally-full model: finite-compression labels at an entrance

Construction and verification by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 23 October 2026; checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 25 October 2026.

At its evaluation point the model satisfies Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power, at every relational type, and fails Weakly Rigid Power at type et. So Tame Rigidity and Rigid Power do not imply Weakly Rigid Power. It also fails □Tame Rigidity and BF at type e.

The model

It is a symmetric ideally-full model in the sense of Dorr, Boolean Completeness does not imply Rigid Comprehension, Definitions 15–18, p. 8, and so a model of C by its Proposition 20.

Let N be countably infinite, partitioned into infinitely many infinite blocks B_i. Let Q be a set of labels, all distinct:

  • q_i for each block B_i, with fibre R(q_i)=B_i;
  • q_D for each finite D\subseteq N, the empty set included, with fibre R(q_D)=D;
  • q_\top, with fibre R(q_\top)=N.

There are two objects, the root A with base N and the tail B with base N\sqcup Q.

  • The arrows A\to A are the surjections N\to N.
  • The arrows A\to B are the surjections of N onto the summand N of B’s base.
  • There are no arrows B\to A.
  • An arrow B\to B is a pair (f,g) of a surjection f:N\to N and a map g:Q\to Q with g(q_\top)=q_\top such that f[R(q)]\subseteq R(g(q))\qquad\text{for every label }q. \tag{1}

These compose: f'f[R(q)]\subseteq f'[R(g(q))]\subseteq R(g'g(q)). The symmetry group at A is the group of all permutations of N. At B it is the group of permutations \pi of N that permute the blocks, acting on labels by q_i\mapsto q_{\pi i} (where \pi[B_i]=B_{\pi i}), q_D\mapsto q_{\pi[D]} and q_\top\mapsto q_\top. These preserve fibres exactly, so they are arrows. Both objects carry the ideal of finite sets. The individuals are the bases, and every constant of Σ denotes the top element of its type. Evaluation point: 1_A.

Write i:A\to B for the entrance that is the identity on N. Every arrow h:A\to B is i\circ f for a surjection f of N, and (f,g)\circ i=i\circ f. For an entity X and an arrow h out of its object, X_h is its extension at h, and (h\cdot X)_k=X_{kh}.

Two facts about rigidity are used throughout.

  • (R1) Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical: each is weakly inextensible with respect to the other, so each necessarily entails the other.
  • (R2) An admitted relation X with an exact-image profile, X_h=h[D] for every arrow h out of its object, where D=X_1, is rigid. Its extension at every later arrow is the image of its current one, which gives persistence. A test Z that necessarily holds of each member of h[D] holds at k of each member of k[h[D]], which gives weak inextensibility at h.

1. Tame Rigidity at the root

Stable-set lemma. Let D be a set of root tuples closed under every surjection of N fixing a finite S\subseteq N pointwise. Then E_h:=h[D] defines an admitted relation pinned by S, and it is rigid.

Proof. Let h,h':A\to V agree on S, and let a\in D have the finite pin M. Define u to fix S and, for x\in M\setminus S, to send x to a point u(x) with h(u(x))=h'(x), which exists because h is onto N. Extend u to a surjection of N using the infinitely many remaining points. Then hu agrees with h' on M, so h'\cdot a=(hu)\cdot a=h\cdot(u\cdot a)\in h[D], since u\cdot a\in D. By symmetry h[D]=h'[D]. The profile respects symmetries, since s[h[D]]=(sh)[D], so it is admitted, and it is rigid by (R2). \square

Now let F be a weakly rigid root relation, pinned by S, with D=F_{1_A}. A surjection u fixing S has u\cdot F=F, so weak persistence gives u[D]\subseteq F_u=D. The lemma’s exact image of D is rigid and coextensive with F, so by (R1) F_h=h[F_{1_A}]\qquad\text{for every arrow }h\text{ out of }A, \tag{2} and F is rigid. This holds at every relational type: Tame Rigidity holds at A.

In particular, let F be the exact image of N at type et. It is rigid, its extension at every arrow h:A\to B is N, and it is not the universal property: after an entrance, the labels are individuals outside it. So BF fails at type e: every individual at A necessarily satisfies F, but it is not necessary that every individual does.

2. A weakly rigid fibre relation that is not rigid

For a label q, let U_q be the property of individuals at B with (U_q)_{(f,g)}=R(g(q)). It is pinned by \{q\} and respects the symmetries at B, so it is admitted, and (f,g)\cdot U_q=U_{g(q)}. All its extensions lie in N.

U_{q_i} is weakly rigid. Weak persistence is (1). For weak inextensibility, let Z be a test with f(n)\in Z_{(f,g)} for every n\in B_i and every arrow (f,g), and let P be a finite pin of Z. Enlarge P to contain q_i and every point of each finite fibre R(q_D) with q_D\in P. Fix an arrow h=(f,g) and y\in R(g(q_i)); we show y\in Z_h. Choose n\in B_i\setminus P. Define j=(f',g'):

  • g'=g on the labels in P, and g'(q)=q_\top for every other label;
  • f'=f on P\cap N and on every block whose label is in P, except that f'(n)=y;
  • on an infinite block whose label is not in P, f' takes the values needed for surjectivity; elsewhere its values are arbitrary.

Condition (1) holds for the labels in P: for q_i because y\in R(g(q_i)), and for the other pinned labels because f'=f on their fibres (n lies in no pinned finite fibre). For the remaining labels it holds trivially, since their fibre R(q_\top)=N. Then j\cdot n=y\in Z_j, and Z_j=Z_h because j and h agree on P. As h and y were arbitrary, U_{q_i}\le Z.

U_{q_i} is not rigid. Fix distinct a,b\in N. An arrow k with g(q_i)=q_{\{a,b\}}, f[B_i]\subseteq\{a,b\}, f onto N by way of another block, and all other labels sent to q_\top, satisfies (1), and k\cdot U_{q_i}=U_{q_{\{a,b\}}},\qquad (U_{q_{\{a,b\}}})_{1_B}=\{a,b\}. The haecceity union H:=\delta_a\lor\delta_b necessarily holds of a and of b. Let m fix the label q_{\{a,b\}}, send a and b to a, be onto N, and send every other label to q_\top. It satisfies (1), since \{a\}\subseteq\{a,b\}. Then (U_{q_{\{a,b\}}})_m=\{a,b\}\not\subseteq\{a\}=H_m, so U_{q_{\{a,b\}}} is not weakly inextensible at B, and U_{q_i} is not rigid. In particular □Tame Rigidity fails, B being reachable from A.

3. Weakly Rigid Power fails at et

Let F be the rigid root property of §1, and W_F:=\lambda X^{et}\, .\,\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(X)\land X\le F . By Tame Rigidity at A, every current instance of W_F is rigid, and so necessarily satisfies \operatorname{Rigid}. At the entrance i, U_{q_i} is weakly rigid and entails i\cdot F, whose extension at every arrow out of B is N. So U_{q_i}\in(W_F)_i, but U_{q_i}\notin\operatorname{Rigid}_i. Hence W_F is not weakly inextensible, and Weakly Rigid Power fails for the rigid F.

4. Rigid Power

Finite-fibre lemma

Let T be weakly rigid at B with a finite pin P, every label in which is a q_D or q_\top, and suppose every current member of T has a finite pin inside N. Then T is the exact image h\mapsto h[T_{1_B}], which is pinned by a finite subset of N.

Proof. Let D=T_{1_B}, and let S\subseteq N contain P\cap N and every point of each fibre R(q_D) with q_D\in P. Let h=(f,g) and h'=(f',g') agree on S; their label maps may differ. For a\in D with a pin M\subseteq N, choose a surjection u_N fixing S with fu_N=f' on M, as in §1. Let u_Q fix the labels in P and q_\top and send every other label to q_\top. Then u=(u_N,u_Q) satisfies (1), since it fixes each pinned finite fibre pointwise. It fixes P, so u\cdot T=T, and weak persistence gives u\cdot a\in D. Then h'\cdot a=h\cdot(u\cdot a)\in h[D], so h[D]=h'[D]. The exact image is admitted and rigid by (R2), and by (R1) it is T. \square

Descent lemma

Let F be rigid at A, h:A\to B, and Y\le h\cdot F rigid at B. Then Y is an exact image, pinned by a finite subset of N.

Proof. By (2), every member of h\cdot F or of a transport of it is the transport of a root tuple, so it has a finite pin inside N; and so does every member of Y and of its transports. Let P be a finite pin of Y, and N_0\subseteq N a finite set containing P\cap N and every point of each fibre R(q_D) with q_D\in P. For each block label q_i\in P, choose z_i\in B_i\setminus N_0 and put D_i:=(B_i\cap N_0)\cup\{z_i\}. The D_i are distinct from one another and from every pinned finite fibre, which lies inside N_0.

  • The compression c=(f_c,g_c): f_c fixes N_0, sends B_i\setminus N_0 to z_i for each pinned block, and is onto N by way of an unpinned block. g_c sends each pinned q_i to q_{D_i}, fixes the pinned q_D and q_\top, and sends every other label to q_\top. Condition (1) holds, since f_c[B_i]=D_i.
  • The return r=(\mathrm{id}_N,g_r): g_r sends each q_{D_i} to q_i, fixes the pinned q_D and q_\top, and sends every other label to q_\top. Condition (1) holds, since D_i\subseteq B_i.

Then rc agrees with 1_B on P, so r\cdot(c\cdot Y)=Y. Now T:=c\cdot Y is rigid, with the pin c[P], whose labels are all q_D’s or q_\top, and its members have pins inside N. By the finite-fibre lemma T is an exact image pinned inside N. As r is the identity on N, r\cdot T=T, so Y=T. \square

The rigidity of Y is what makes c\cdot Y weakly rigid. The fibre relation U_{q_i} of §2 is weakly rigid but not rigid, and compression destroys its weak rigidity.

Approximate lifting

Let F be rigid at A, with extension E and pin S. Given an arrow h:A\to V, a finite N_1\supseteq S, and a rigid Y\le h\cdot F at V, there are an arrow j:A\to V agreeing with h on N_1 and a rigid root X\le F with j\cdot X=Y.

Proof. Let T\subseteq N be a finite pin of Y: any finite pin if V=A, and the pin of the descent lemma if V=B. Let j agree with h on N_1 and send N\setminus N_1 bijectively onto N\setminus h[N_1]. It is onto N, its fibres with more than one point all lie in N_1, and j\cdot F=h\cdot F. Let P\supseteq N_1 be finite and contain a j-preimage of each point of T, and put D:=\{a\in E: j\cdot a\in Y_{1_V}\}. By (2), (j\cdot F)_{1_V}=j[E]; since Y\le j\cdot F, j[D]=Y_{1_V}.

D is closed under the surjections u fixing P. Such a u fixes each fibre of j with more than one point pointwise, so ju is constant on the fibres of j, and ju=vj for a surjection v of N. (If V=B, extend v to an arrow of B by sending every label to q_\top.) v fixes T, since each t\in T is j(p) for some p\in P and v(t)=ju(p)=j(p). So v\cdot Y=Y, and persistence gives v[Y_{1_V}]\subseteq Y_{1_V}. Likewise u\cdot F=F gives u[E]\subseteq E. So for a\in D, j\cdot(u\cdot a)=v\cdot(j\cdot a)\in Y_{1_V}, and u\cdot a\in D.

By the stable-set lemma, the exact image X of D is a rigid root relation. X\le F, since h[D]\subseteq h[E] for every h. And j\cdot X is coextensive with Y, so by (R1) j\cdot X=Y. \square

Rigid Power holds

Let F be rigid at A and R_F:=\lambda X\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F, which is persistent in C. For weak inextensibility, let Z be a test necessarily holding of every current instance of R_F, and N_1 a finite set containing pins of F and Z. Fix an arrow h out of A and Y\in(R_F)_h, that is, Y rigid with Y\le h\cdot F. Lifting gives j agreeing with h on N_1 and a rigid root X\le F with j\cdot X=Y. Since X is a current instance of R_F, Y=j\cdot X\in Z_j, and Z_j=Z_h as j and h agree on the pin of Z. So R_F\le Z: R_F is weakly rigid, and by Tame Rigidity it is rigid. This holds at every relational type.

Remark

The source (§9) explains why higher Rigid Power survives the fibre witness of §2. That witness is a new relation entity at B, though all its members are old individuals. Every root rigid family of relations is an exact image, so its later members are transports of root relations. Such a family never acquires U_{q_i}, whose non-rigidity a compression detects.
