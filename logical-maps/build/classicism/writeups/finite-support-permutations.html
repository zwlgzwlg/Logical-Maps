Second-Order Analytic Necessitism in the finite-support permutation model

Construction and verification by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 23 October 2026, for the model finite-support-permutations: the one-object ideally full action model of Classicism, Proposition D.5, part 1, whose arrows are the permutations of \mathbb N, with the ideal of finite sets, and its generated variants (one individual; Σ true atom). It shows that Second-Order Analytic Necessitism, and so Analytic Necessitism, hold there, although Rigid Comprehension fails: a rigid real number structure need not be made of rigid properties of numbers.

Setting

Write G for the arrows, the permutations of \mathbb N. An entity of a relational type at the one object is an intension Y, assigning to each arrow j a set Y_j of tuples of entities of its argument types; Y holds of a tuple \bar a at the arrow k iff k\cdot\bar a\in Y_k, and transport along k is (k\cdot Y)_j:=Y_{j\circ k}. The intension Y is pinned down by a finite N\subseteq\mathbb N when Y_j=Y_{j'} whenever j and j' agree on N, and the domain of each relational type consists of the intensions pinned down by some finite set (finitely-pinned-domains). The individuals are \mathbb N, acted on by the arrows, or in the variant a single individual fixed by every arrow; in either case k\cdot is a bijection of every domain, with inverse k^{-1}\cdot, since k^{-1}\cdot(k\cdot Y)=Y.

Rigidity is as in the Background: \operatorname{Rigid}(X) is \Box\forall x\,.\,(Xx\to\Box Xx)\land(\neg Xx\to\Box\neg Xx), which at the evaluation point says that for all arrows k,j and all x, k\cdot x\in X_k iff (j\circ k)\cdot x\in X_{j\circ k}.

Lemma 1. An intension X is rigid iff X_j=j\cdot X_1 for every arrow j, where j\cdot X_1=\{j\cdot x:x\in X_1\}; a rigid X then has necessarily distinct instances, and at every arrow k it holds of exactly the members of X_1.

Proof. If X is rigid, the clause at k=1 gives x\in X_1 iff j\cdot x\in X_j, so X_j\supseteq j\cdot X_1, and for z\in X_j, z=j\cdot(j^{-1}\cdot z) with j^{-1}\cdot z\in X_1, so X_j\subseteq j\cdot X_1. Conversely if X_j=j\cdot X_1 for all j, then k\cdot x\in X_k iff x\in X_1 iff (j\circ k)\cdot x\in X_{j\circ k}, transport being injective. Necessary distinctness is the injectivity of transport, and at k, X holds of x iff k\cdot x\in k\cdot X_1 iff x\in X_1. \blacksquare

The same holds for relations, with tuples in place of x.

The structure

Fix a relational type \sigma=\bar\tau t and a tuple \bar u of entities of types \bar\tau. For A\subseteq\mathbb N let Y^A be the intension of type \sigma with Y^A_j:=\{\bar u\} if j0\in A and Y^A_j:=\emptyset otherwise. It is pinned down by \{0\}, so in the domain, the Y^A are pairwise distinct, and (k\cdot Y^A)_j=Y^A_{j\circ k} is \{\bar u\} iff j(k0)\in A, so k\cdot Y^A is pinned down by \{k0\}, its value at j depending only on j(k0). Let P:=\{Y^A:A\subseteq\mathbb N\} and fix a bijection \beta\colon P\to\mathbb R, through the bijection of the sets of natural numbers with the reals (a theorem of ZF).

Define the intensions X_j:=j\cdot P,\qquad {\le}_j:=\{(j\cdot x,j\cdot y):x,y\in P,\ \beta x\le\beta y\},\qquad {+}_j:=\{(j\cdot x,j\cdot y,j\cdot z):\beta x+\beta y=\beta z\}, \times_j likewise, N_j:=\{j\cdot x:\beta x\in\mathbb N\}, and the entities 0:=\beta^{-1}(0) and 1:=\beta^{-1}(1) of P.

Lemma 2. These intensions are in the domain, pinned down by \{0\}.

Proof. j\cdot Y^A depends on j only through j0, so each of X_j, \le_j, +_j, \times_j and N_j depends only on j0. \blacksquare

Lemma 3. \mathfrak R:=(X,\le,+,\times,0,1,N) is a rigid real number structure, necessarily a real number structure: \operatorname{RigidStr}(\mathfrak R)\land\Box\operatorname{Real}_\sigma (\mathfrak R) holds at the evaluation point.

Proof. By construction X_j=j\cdot X_1 and likewise for the relations, so by Lemma 1 all are rigid with necessarily distinct instances, and at every arrow k the instances of X are the members of P, while x\le y holds at k iff (k\cdot x,k\cdot y)\in{\le}_k iff \beta x\le \beta y, and similarly for +, \times and N. So at every arrow the structure is the \beta-preimage of the real numbers on P: an ordered field with + and \times functional, in which N is the set of natural numbers. Completeness at k: a subproperty Y of X bounded at k has as instances there a set \{x:k\cdot x\in Y_k\}\subseteq P bounded in the \beta-order, whose least upper bound in the reals pulls back to a least upper bound in P. And N is the least inductive subproperty at k: its instances contain 0 and are closed under adding 1, and any subproperty inductive at k contains them, the natural numbers being the least inductive subset of the reals. \blacksquare

Represented powers

For m\ge1 and E\subseteq P^m let \rho(E) be the relation of type \sigma^m t with \rho(E)_j:=j\cdot E=\{(j\cdot x_1,\ldots,j\cdot x_m):\bar x\in E\}. It is pinned down by \{0\} (Lemma 2), rigid by Lemma 1 with \rho(E)_j=j\cdot\rho(E)_1, holds at every arrow of exactly the tuples in E, which are tuples of instances of X, and k\cdot\rho(E)=\rho(k\cdot E), since (k\cdot\rho(E))_j=(j\circ k)\cdot E=j\cdot(k\cdot E).

Let \mathcal P^0:=\{\rho(E):E\subseteq P^m\} and define \mathcal P_m of type (\sigma^m t)t by (\mathcal P_m)_k:=k\cdot\mathcal P^0. Then (\mathcal P_m)_k=\{\rho(E'):E'\subseteq(k\cdot P)^m\} depends on k only through k0, so \mathcal P_m is in the domain; it is rigid by Lemma 1, and at every arrow it holds of exactly the members of \mathcal P^0, rigid relations holding only of tuples of instances of X. Representation: at an arrow k and for any relation R of type \sigma^m t, let E:=\{\bar x\in P^m:k\cdot\bar x\in R_k\}. Then \rho(E)\in\mathcal P^0, so \mathcal P_m holds of it at k, and for \bar x\in P^m, \rho(E) holds of \bar x at k iff \bar x\in E iff R holds of \bar x at k. So \operatorname{RepPower}_m(\mathcal P_m,X) holds for every m, and with Lemma 3 the consequent of Second-Order Analytic Necessitism holds at \sigma for every n.

Type e

At type e the antecedent fails: a real number structure of individuals would need continuum many individuals at some world, and the individuals are the natural numbers, or one, at the only object. So every instance of Second-Order Analytic Necessitism holds, and with it Analytic Necessitism. The interpretation of Σ plays no part.

Remark

The construction uses only that the arrows form a group acting on the pinning points and that every finitely pinned intension is present; it does not use the full strength of the permutation group. The other finite-support models were once recorded as failing Analytic Necessitism, because some arrow identifies two points and no entity of type tt is determined by its transport along it. That argument was withdrawn on 29 October 2026. A rigid carrier only needs to be transported injectively, and the constant intensions, fixed by every arrow, give one in each of them (the general argument analytic-necessitism-codomain-fixed). So Analytic Necessitism holds there as well.
