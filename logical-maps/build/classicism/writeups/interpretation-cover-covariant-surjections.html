Interpretation cover: covariant surjections

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance, 30 October 2026. The construction combines Cian Dorr’s interpretation roots and symmetric-surjection argument for Inextensible Comprehension with the typed-name interpretation covers developed by OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi. The prior arguments are identified in Section 7. No independent human check or Lean proof is recorded.

1. Result and assumptions

There is one relative model of C with the joint root properties

\text{Maximalism for }\Sigma, \qquad\text{Atomlessness}, \qquad\text{Inextensible Comprehension}, \qquad\text{BF}.

Inextensible Comprehension ranges over every relational type, including t; BF ranges over every admitted quantified type, including e. These are assertions at the root. In particular, the box in \operatorname{Inextensible}(Y)=\Box\operatorname{WeaklyInextensible}(Y) is proved, but no box is placed in front of the outer comprehension schema. Pure Maximalism follows from the signature result.

Work in ZFC and use the following existence input from the interpretation-root construction: a set-sized family of faithful pointed C action models contains a witness for every closed pure C-consistent sentence. Each component retains all of its original objects, arrows, domains, application and quantifier operations. This proof does not establish that representation/completeness input anew. Optional root properties are not added to the reference theory C or C(\Sigma) used in Maximalism.

Fix a prescribed set-sized signature \Sigma of free constants of admitted types, under the conventions in the map’s Background. It contains a relational constant. Choose infinite auxiliary sets C_\sigma, one at each admitted type, large enough to assign distinct names to the signature’s constants. Relational names are indices; they are not required to enumerate the relational entities constructed below.

2. Domains and transports

Add a root o to the disjoint union of the old components. Its individual domain is C_e. Its loops are all tuples

h=(h_\sigma)_\sigma, \qquad h_\sigma:C_\sigma\twoheadrightarrow C_\sigma.

The designated root symmetry group consists of all typewise permutations. For every old object V, include every typed interpretation I_\sigma:C_\sigma\to D_\sigma(V) as an arrow o\to V. Composition is Ih and kI, using the old transport for kI. All interpretations are included, so a non-surjective old transport still gives an allowed composite. There are no arrows back to the root.

Proceed recursively through the finite types. At a relational type \tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_n t, an entity X assigns to every arrow a:o\to V a row

X_a\subseteq D_{\sigma_1}(V)\times\cdots\times D_{\sigma_n}(V).

For n=0 a row is a truth value. Admit precisely the profiles satisfying:

  1. Finite pinning. A finite set S of typed names controls the rows: arrows to the same target that agree on S have identical rows.
  2. Permutation covariance. For each root permutation \gamma and root loop h, X_{\gamma h}=\rho_\gamma[X_h], where \rho_\gamma acts componentwise on the argument tuple.
  3. Old landing. At each interpretation I:o\to V, the entire section k\mapsto X_{kI} is an admitted old entity of type \tau at V.

The argument types have lower complexity than \tau, so their domains and transports have already been defined. The recursion uses full target argument domains, even when an entrance misses some target entities. Define transport by precomposition:

(\rho_hX)_a=X_{ah}.

If S pins X, then hS pins \rho_hX. Covariance follows from X_{\gamma ah}=\rho_\gamma[X_{ah}], and its old section at I is the already admitted section of X at Ih. Thus transport lands in the specified domains and is functorial. A root permutation acts bijectively.

We will repeatedly use the stronger consequence of pinning:

a|S=b|S\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho_aX=\rho_bX. \tag{1}

Indeed, every further pair of composites ka,kb still agrees on S, so the transported entities agree on every future row and tuple. Individual parameters have singleton-name pins.

Closure under C

Literal identity at the root has admitted separators. For an entity q, take the profile whose row at h is \{\rho_hq\} and whose old section at I is the old haecceity of \rho_Iq. Equation (1), covariance and old term closure prove admission. Its identity row distinguishes q from every different entity. A constant singleton row is not being assumed covariant.

For a term with finitely many parameters, combine their pins. Arrows agreeing on that union transport the complete parameter environment identically. Evaluating the body on every tuple in the target proves finite pinning for abstractions. Bound variables range over the whole target domain; no common pin for that domain is required. At an old interpretation the full section is its old term denotation, by induction on terms, and hence satisfies old landing.

Every root permutation is an automorphism of the typed application structure: covariance preserves application and its actions on all domains are bijective. Thus quantified truth is preserved by reindexing. The same holds for modal formulas, since the map a\mapsto a\gamma bijectively reindexes all continuations, including old interpretations. This proves covariance of all term denotations. Application, Boolean operations, quantifiers and abstraction therefore stay in the admitted domains. Beta and eta hold on profiles. Necessary coextension identifies every row at every continuation, and hence identifies the entities. H-theorem-equivalent bodies agree at all target assignments, validating C’s theoremhood-restricted Logical Equivalence. No optional premise enters that rule or necessitation.

For a relational name c, its named lift \widehat c has empty rows at root loops and whole old section I(c) at each old interpretation. It is pinned by \{c\} and admitted. For individuals use c itself. The identities

\rho_I\widehat c=I(c), \qquad\rho_h\widehat c=\widehat{h(c)} \tag{2}

concern the whole entities, not just their extensions at the targets.

3. Inextensible coextensions

Fix any X of relational type and a finite typed pin S for it. Let \delta_S be true at a root loop exactly when that loop is injective on S within each type, and false at every old interpretation. This proposition is pinned by S, covariant under postcomposition by permutations, and has whole Bottom sections in the old components. It is therefore admitted, including when S is empty. Put

Y=\lambda\bar x\,.\,\delta_S\land X[\bar x].

It coextends X at the root identity. The point of covariance is that preimages can be chosen inside this particular extension.

Tuple-preimage lemma. If a root loop f fixes S, and r is any root tuple with a finite combined pin T, there is a typed permutation \tau fixing S such that

\rho_f(\rho_\tau r)=r. \tag{3}

For each t\in T_\sigma\setminus S_\sigma, choose an f_\sigma-preimage outside S_\sigma. This is possible because f fixes S and is onto; different target names have different chosen preimages. Extend the finite assignments, together with identity on S, to permutations. Then f\tau fixes T, and (1) gives (3). If r\in X_1, pinning and covariance additionally give

X_\tau=X_1=\rho_\tau[X_1],

so \rho_\tau r\in X_1.

Now suppose an arbitrary admitted Z is necessary of every current Y-tuple. Consider any tuple z\in Y_h at a root loop h. Necessarily h is injective on S. Extend h|S to a typed permutation \pi and put f=\pi^{-1}h, which fixes S. Pinning and covariance give

X_h=X_\pi=\rho_\pi[X_1].

Write z=\rho_\pi r with r\in X_1. Lemma (3) supplies r'\in X_1=Y_1 with \rho_fr'=r, hence \rho_hr'=z. The assumed necessity of Z[r'] gives Z_h[z]. At old entrances and at loops collapsing S, the row of Y is empty. This proves Y\le Z for every admitted test Z, and therefore weak inextensibility of Y.

The leading box requires a further check. A collapsing loop sends Y to whole Bottom: every later root composite still collapses S, and every old row is false. Old entrances also send Y to whole Bottom. If h is injective on S, then

\rho_h\delta_S=\delta_{hS}, \qquad \rho_hY=\delta_{hS}\land\rho_hX,

and hS pins \rho_hX. The same full-domain argument proves weak inextensibility of this transported relation. Bottom is weakly inextensible in every component. Consequently Y is necessarily weakly inextensible, as required. The proof treats arbitrary finite argument tuples, including the empty tuple.

4. Atomlessness

Let p\ne\bot have finite pin S. Some row of p is true. If it is a root-loop row p_h, choose two fresh individual names u,v outside S. Extend finite prescriptions to surjections h_= and h_\ne that agree with h on S, with respectively equal and unequal images of u,v. The admitted scalar whose root-loop row at g says g_e(u)=g_e(v), and whose old sections are Bottom, splits p: both its intersection with p and its complement’s intersection with p have true rows.

If the true row is at an old interpretation I, choose a fresh propositional name c outside S. Change only I(c), once to old Top and once to old Bottom. Both interpretations are allowed, and pinning keeps p true. Equation (2) shows that \widehat c splits p. This second case includes propositions with no true root-loop row. Thus every nonbottom proposition has a proper nonbottom part, even if an old component has atoms.

5. Maximalism for the fixed signature

Every pure C-consistent sentence retains a witness in an unchanged old component and is therefore possible at the root. For \Sigma, interpret its individual constants by designated names and its relational constants by the corresponding named lifts.

Take any closed C(\Sigma)-consistent sentence. Replace its finitely many constants by variables and existentially close. This pure sentence is C-consistent: otherwise instantiating the refutation at the constants would refute the original sentence. Choose its old witness and assign the witnessed values to the finitely many names. Extend the assignment to an arbitrary typed interpretation. Equation (2) then recovers the entire original sentence at the old witness. This proves the signature possibility form of Maximalism, equivalent in C to the recorded Distinctness form. Constants may denote equal values after an entrance; no unique-name assumption is made.

6. BF

Use the dual form \Diamond\exists x^\sigma F x\to\exists x^\sigma\Diamond F x. If b is a witness at a root loop h, apply the tuple-preimage lemma with S empty to its finite pin. It supplies a with \rho_ha=b, so the same loop witnesses F a.

If the witness b is at an old interpretation I, choose a fresh \sigma-name c outside F’s pin. Change its interpretation to b, leaving the pinned names unchanged. The new entrance I' has exactly the same full predicate row as I. Its current named lift reaches b, by (2); for \sigma=e use the name itself. Hence that current entity possibly satisfies F. There is no assumption that an arbitrary fixed old entrance is onto, or that an old component satisfies BF.

7. Sources and scope

The interpretation-root and whole-landing methods come from Cian Dorr’s 19 October 2026 construction, with the landing correction supplied by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr; see the map’s interpretation-root write-up. The auxiliary typed names, named lifts and arbitrary-component version of the cover occur in Observer covers and Boolean completeness, Sections 2–3 and 8, by OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 October 2026, and the named-permutation cover.

The injectivity-mask and within-extension tuple-preimage proof is the all-type Inextensible Comprehension argument in symmetric-all-surjections, supplied by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted and checked by Cian Dorr, 27 September 2026. Here full typed permutation covariance and all root surjections preserve that argument while arbitrary old interpretations preserve the complete family of witnesses. The contribution is their simultaneous combination in this cover.

The construction supplies no boxed comprehension or BF assertion. It also supplies no Boolean Completeness, Forcing Possibilism, Strong Possibility or choice verdict. Its proofs should not be combined with those of a different cover to assign a stronger joint profile.
