# Strong Possibility (signature Σ) ∧ Distinctness-preserving collapse ⇒ ⊥

<p class='cert'>Result — Source: Misc.; produced by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), 23 September 2026, adapting the recorded pure-language proof; the observation prompting the pass is Cian Dorr’s; recorded by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), 23 September 2026.</p>

## Premises

- **Strong Possibility (signature Σ).** Every closed sentence of the fixed signature consistent with its Maximalist Classicism is possible under the distinctness-preserving modality.
- **Distinctness-preserving collapse.** Every truth is necessary under the distinctness-preserving modality.

## Conclusion

- **⊥.** These premises cannot all hold together.

## Proof

Let $Z$ be the closed pure sentence $\forall xy\, .\,x\ne y\to\Diamond(x\ne y\land E_2)$, where $E_2$ says that there are exactly two individuals: a closed instance of Possibility+ (pure). Both $Z$ and $\neg Z$ are consistent with Max(C(Σ)): the constant-indexed coalesced root satisfies Possibility Maximalism (signature Σ) and Possibility+ (pure), hence $Z$, and the maximalist coalesced root satisfies Possibility Maximalism (signature Σ) and refutes exactly this instance of Possibility+ (pure). Since $Z$ is pure it is a sentence of $\mathcal L(\Sigma)$. Let $P$ be whichever of $Z$, $\neg Z$ is false. Strong Possibility (signature Σ) gives $\Diamond_{\ne}P$. Distinctness-preserving collapse applied to the truth $\neg P$ gives $\Box_{\ne}\neg P$, which is $\neg\Diamond_{\ne}P$.

## Notes

The Σ counterpart of the recorded incompatibility of Strong Possibility (pure) with the collapse. It cannot be derived from that record while the arrow from Strong Possibility (signature Σ) to Strong Possibility (pure) is conjectured, so it is proved directly, with the two recorded Possibility Maximalism (signature Σ) roots as the witnesses that Max(C(Σ)) settles neither $Z$ nor $
eg Z$. The witnesses fix Σ as the constant-indexed root does, with infinitely many individual constants and a relational constant; the collapse holds at both roots. This is the one place where Strong Possibility bites beyond Possibility Maximalism: it makes $P$ possible under the distinctness-preserving modality, and the collapse denies exactly that of every falsehood.
