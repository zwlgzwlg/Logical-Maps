Coalesced sum: interpretation arrows into every component object, definable root

Cian Dorr gave this construction on 20 October 2026, for the sentence \Psi below; Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic) wrote the verification and the arguments. No second reader has checked it, and no Lean verification is claimed.

The construction

  • Components. Fix a set A of action models complete for C, with disjoint objects and arrows: every pure sentence consistent with C is true at the root of some model in A.
  • Signature. \Sigma_0=\{d\}, with d of type e.
  • Arrows. Add an object W_0. Its arrows out are its identity and, for each object V of a model in A and each individual a at V, an arrow \langle V,a\rangle into V. No arrow enters W_0 except its identity.
  • Composition. For an arrow k:V\to V' of a component, k\circ\langle V,a\rangle:=\langle V',k\cdot a\rangle.
  • Individuals. The only individual at W_0 is d, and \langle V,a\rangle sends it to a.
  • Domains at W_0. An entity of a relational type \bar\sigma t is an intension F over the arrows out of W_0 such that for some closed term t of \mathcal L(\Sigma_0), for every \langle V,a\rangle, the value of F there is the extension at 1_V of t as interpreted at V with d\mapsto a. The value of F at the identity is unconstrained. Write F=(E,t) for such an entity, E its value at the identity; t is a witness, not part of the entity.
  • Evaluation point. 1_{W_0}.
  • Interpretation of \Sigma. Every constant of \Sigma denotes the top element of its type, the entity (\text{everything},\lambda\bar x\,.\,\top).

Landing. The transport of (E,t) along \langle V,a\rangle is the intension k\mapsto (value at \langle V',k\cdot a\rangle) = extension at k of t interpreted at V with d\mapsto a: it is the entity t denotes at V under that interpretation, so it lies in V’s domain.

It is a model of C

Closed formulas are evaluated at an arrow by evaluating at its target with transported parameters. Let s be a term with parameters \bar F=(\bar E,\bar t) from the root domains. At \langle V,a\rangle its denotation is computed in V’s component from the transported parameters, which are the denotations of \bar t; so it is the denotation of the closed term s[\bar t/\bar F], uniformly in the arrow. At the identity the value is the set of tuples of root entities satisfying the formula at W_0, and nothing constrains it. So every term with parameters denotes a root entity, with s[\bar t/\bar F] as its witness. The axioms of C hold at W_0 because denotations are computed extensionally on intensions, and the other worlds are worlds of the components.

Two root entities with the same witness and different values at the identity are distinct at W_0 (a root property with the right value at the identity separates them) and identical at every component world. So ND fails at W_0.

Maximalism (pure) and Possibility+ (pure)

A closed pure sentence holds at \langle V,a\rangle iff it holds at V. Every pure sentence consistent with C holds at the root of some model in A, so it is possible at W_0. For Possibility+ with one free individual variable, a consistent P(x) holds of some a at some root V, and then P(d) holds at \langle V,a\rangle. With two or more variables the antecedent, that they are distinct, is false at W_0, which has one individual.

The sentence \Psi

Let C5 be \BoxND and

F(p):=(\Diamond p\land\Diamond\neg p)\land\mathrm{C5}\land \mathrm{Atomicity}\land\forall x\forall y\,.\,x=y,

a closed pure term of type tt: p is contingent while C5, Atomicity and “there is exactly one individual” hold. Let

\Psi:=\forall p\,.\,\neg\Diamond F(p).

\Psi holds at W_0. Take a root proposition p=(b,t) and a world seen from W_0.

  • At W_0 itself C5 fails, since ND fails there, so p is not F.
  • At a component world w where C5, Atomicity and \forall x\forall y\,.\,x=y hold, p is the proposition \llbracket t\rrbracket. C5 gives BF, and x=y\to\Box\,x=y is a theorem, so there is necessarily exactly one individual at w, and t and the pure sentence \forall x\,.\,t[x/d] have the same truth value at every world seen from w: they are the same proposition there. By the recorded result C5 + Atomicity (type t) ⇒ No Pure Contingency, that proposition is not contingent at w. So p is not F at w.
  • Everywhere else F fails by its side conditions.

So \Psi is consistent with Maximalist Classicism (pure). This is the premise of the result maximalism-signature-incompatible-with-strong-possibility-pure.

Why the extra conjunct. Without \forall x\forall y\,.\,x=y one would need every closed sentence of \mathcal L(\Sigma_0), d included, to be non-contingent where C5 and Atomicity hold. C5 + Atomicity does make the number of individuals non-contingent, but fixing it at one reduces every \Sigma_0-sentence to a pure one and costs nothing in the result.

Why this root. At the pinned roots the entity \hat c for a constant c\in\Sigma^+ of type t has, at such a world, whatever value the interpretation gives c, which may be contingent; so \Psi fails there. Here every root entity is definable by one closed term at every component world, which is what \Psi needs.

BF fails

At type e, \Diamond\exists x\,.\,x\ne d holds, but d is the only root individual. At type t, let X be the root property denoted by

\lambda p\,.\,\operatorname{Atom}(p)\land\exists q\,.\,(\operatorname{Atom}(q) \land q\ne p)\land\mathrm{C5}\land\mathrm{Atomicity}\land\forall x\forall y\,.\,x=y.

\Diamond\exists p\,.\,Xp holds at W_0: the full S5 model over the two-element group with one individual satisfies \exists p\,.\,Xp, so some component does. But no root proposition is possibly X: at a world where the side conditions hold, its sentence is, as above, a non-contingent proposition, hence (the world being S5, by C5) \top or \bot there, and neither is an atom beside another atom. So \exists p\,.\,\Diamond Xp fails.

The root propositions

Two root propositions false at the identity, (\bot,t) and (\bot,t'), are identical iff t\leftrightarrow t' holds at every object of every model in A under every interpretation of d, which by completeness is iff C(\Sigma_0) proves it; and (\bot,t)\le(\bot,t') iff C(\Sigma_0) proves t\to t'. So the root propositions false at the identity form the Lindenbaum algebra of C(\Sigma_0), and all root propositions form that algebra with a truth value at the identity adjoined. The Lindenbaum algebra is countable and atomless (a consistent sentence can always be consistently strengthened). Hence Atomicity fails at type t, and so at every relational type; Boolean Completeness at type t fails, a countable atomless Boolean algebra not being complete; and Countable Boolean Completeness fails, such an algebra not being countably complete either. Any family of root propositions is the value at the identity of some root property, the value at the identity being unconstrained. Atomlessness fails too, the base proposition (\top,\bot) being an atom; it also witnesses Actuality.

Correction of 28 October 2026. The Lindenbaum algebra is not atomless: for each n\ge1 the sentence “the Fregean Axiom holds and there are exactly n individuals” is complete over C(\Sigma_0), so it is an atom (found by GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi). The verdicts of this paragraph stand, by the argument of the next section.

Atoms, completeness and Forcing Possibilism

Added 28 October 2026, from GPT-6 Astra’s notes of 25 October 2026 in Daniel Kodsi’s pull request #18, checked and written up by Claude Opus 5.5, prompted by Cian Dorr.

For n\ge1 let A_n be the closed pure sentence “the Fregean Axiom holds and there are exactly n individuals”, and use the same letter for the root proposition it denotes.

Each A_n is an atom. At a component object V where A_n is true, the Fregean Axiom gives Extensionality at every type (recorded), so every entity at V is fixed by its extension there. By induction on types, the domains at V are finite, and every set of tuples is the extension of an entity, being definable from parameters by a finite disjunction of conjunctions of identities. So the domains at V, with application at its identity arrow, form the full extensional hierarchy over n individuals, and the truth at V of a formula depends only on this structure. Any two such structures are isomorphic, by an isomorphism that may be chosen to send any given individual to any other. So the truth of t(a) at V, for a closed term t(d) of \mathcal L(\Sigma_0), is the same for every such V and every individual a at V. Now take any root proposition p, with term t. At the identity, p\land A_n is false, as A_n is: there are infinitely many root propositions, so two distinct ones agree in truth value there and the Fregean Axiom fails. At an arrow \langle V,a\rangle with A_n false at V, p\land A_n is false; at those with A_n true at V, it is uniformly true or uniformly false. So p\land A_n is A_n or \bot. And A_n\ne\bot, since the full extensional hierarchy over n individuals is a model of C in which A_n is true, and the components are complete for C. The A_n are pairwise disjoint.

Completeness fails. There are only countably many root propositions, a truth value together with a closed term of \mathcal L(\Sigma_0). If every subfamily \{A_n:n\in N\} had a least upper bound s_N, then A_k\le s_N iff k\in N: for k\notin N, s_N\land\neg A_k is also an upper bound, so s_N\le\neg A_k. So N\mapsto s_N would be injective on the subsets of \mathbb N, which is impossible. Fix an N for which the join is missing; \{A_n:n\in N\} is the value at the identity of a root property of propositions, so Boolean Completeness at type t fails (for the meet form, take the complements \neg A_n). The family is moreover countable in the sense of Countable Boolean Completeness at \sigma=t: the numbers \operatorname{Suc}^n\mathbf 0 of type (tt)t are natural numbers, by theorems of C, and pairwise distinct at the root, since a root property of propositions can have exactly n instances there; and the relation with value \{(A_n,\operatorname{Suc}^n\mathbf 0):n\in N\} at the identity, and empty elsewhere, is a root relation injecting the family into them. So Countable Boolean Completeness fails. Atomicity fails as before, and also because Maximalism (pure) holds and excludes Atomicity (type t).

Forcing Possibilism holds, at every type \sigma. Every set of tuples of root entities is the value at the identity of a root relation, so the internal notions at W_0 agree with the external ones: a complete Boolean algebra (X,R) there is externally one, and injections and isomorphisms exist internally exactly when they exist externally. If X is Dedekind finite, it is finite. If X\prec\lambda p\,.\,\top, then X injects into the countable set of root propositions; were it infinite, it would be countably infinite, and there would be an injection back. So in both cases (X,R) is a finite Boolean algebra, with k atoms say. Put p:=A_1\lor\dots\lor A_k (p:=\bot if k=0). Then p\ne\top, as A_{k+1} is disjoint from it, and every q\le p is the join of the A_i below it, since q=\bigvee_i(q\land A_i) and each q\land A_i is A_i or \bot. So the propositions below p form the algebra of subsets of \{A_1,\dots,A_k\}, which is isomorphic to (X,R), and the root relation with that isomorphism as its value at the identity witnesses Forcing Possibilism. The argument uses choice in the metatheory, to pass from Dedekind finiteness to finiteness.

Other verdicts

  • Choice. Well-Ordering and Transversal Choice are evaluated at the identity alone; a well-ordering or a transversal chosen in the metatheory, with the term \bot elsewhere, is a root entity. Relational Choice holds by the group’s argument.
  • Strong Possibility (pure) fails. With a:=(\top,\bot), a truth possible only at the identity, \Box_{\ne}p holds of every truth p, so \Diamond_{\ne}P holds iff P does; \negActuality is consistent with Maximalist Classicism (coalesced-pinned-substitutions-root) and false here.
  • Tame Rigidity fails at type e\to t: the root property (\{d\},\lambda x\,.\,\top) is weakly rigid at W_0, the only test tuple being d and the only test properties the definable ones, which if they necessarily hold of d hold of every individual everywhere; but its transport, the universal property, is weakly inextensible at a component world only where BF at type e holds, and some component refutes that. Weakly Rigid Power fails with it.
  • Boxed principles. The group’s argument that every pure sentence consistent with C is possible at the root refutes □Vicinity, □Weakly Inextensible Comprehension and □Transversal.
  • The map derives the rest: the Maximalism incompatibilities, the consequences of Actuality, and the signature verdicts from the top interpretation of \Sigma.
