Relational Choice and Boolean Completeness imply Actuality

Proof by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 24 October 2026.

Theorem. In C, Relational Choice from t to t together with Boolean Completeness at t and at tt implies Actuality. All premises are unboxed.

Since Boolean Completeness at tt implies it at t (Remark 2), the instances used are \mathrm{RC}_{t,t} and \mathrm{BC}_{tt}. Nothing else is assumed: no ND, BF, Plenitude, or necessitated premise.

Overview. If Actuality fails, the false propositions have join \top (Lemma 3). Relational Choice refines them into a family of pairwise disjoint false propositions with the same join \top (Lemma 4). But Boolean Completeness at tt guarantees that a pairwise disjoint family whose join is true has a true member (Lemma 2).

Notation

Throughout, \le is \le_t, and p<q means p\le q\land p\ne q. For a property A^{tt} of propositions, \bigvee A is its least upper bound at t, which Boolean Completeness at t provides. For a proposition a, its haecceity is

H_a:=\lambda z^t\, .\,z=a\qquad(\text{type }tt),

and two propositions are same or disjoint when

\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y):=x=y\lor x\land y=\bot.

A property A^{tt} is pairwise disjoint when \forall a\,b\, .\,Aa\land Ab\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(a,b).

Recall that p\le q is equivalent to \Box(p\to q), and X\le_{tt}Y to \Box\forall z\, .\,Xz\to Yz (Background). In particular p\le q gives p\to q.

Lemma 1: evaluation preserves joins

For X^{tt} and propositions x,c:

X\le_{tt}\lambda z\, .\,(z=x\to c)\quad\text{iff}\quad Xx\le c.

Indeed the left side is \Box\forall z\, .\,Xz\to(z=x\to c), the right side is \Box(Xx\to c), and \forall z\, .\,(Xz\to(z=x\to c))\leftrightarrow(Xx\to c) is a theorem of C, so its necessitation holds.

Consequently, if J is the least upper bound at tt of a property \mathcal A^{(tt)t}, then for all x,c:

Jx\le c\quad\text{iff}\quad\forall Y\, .\,\mathcal AY\to Yx\le c.

For Jx\le c iff J\le_{tt}\lambda z\, .\,(z=x\to c) iff every Y in \mathcal A has Y\le_{tt}\lambda z\, .\,(z=x\to c) iff every Y in \mathcal A has Yx\le c.

Lemma 2: a pairwise disjoint family with a true join has a true member

Assume Boolean Completeness at t and tt. Let A^{tt} be pairwise disjoint, with every member false. Then \bigvee A is false.

Proof. Suppose \bigvee A is true. Let J be the least upper bound at tt of the haecceities of members of A,

\mathcal A:=\lambda Y^{tt}\, .\,\exists a\, .\,Aa\land Y=H_a .

Step 1. For all propositions x,y:

Jx\land Jy\le\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y). \tag{1}

Let a,b be members of A. Then \operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(a,b), and as a disjunction of identities it is necessary. Since x=a\land y=b\land\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(a,b)\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y) is a theorem of C, its necessitation gives the first line of

\begin{aligned} H_ax\land H_by&\le\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y),\\ H_by&\le H_ax\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y)&&\text{for all }b\text{ in }A,\\ Jy&\le H_ax\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y)&&\text{by Lemma 1},\\ H_ax&\le Jy\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y)&&\text{for all }a\text{ in }A,\\ Jx&\le Jy\to\operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(x,y)&&\text{by Lemma 1}, \end{aligned}

which is (1).

Step 2. For each member a of A, H_a\le_{tt}J and H_aa=(a=a)=\top, so Ja=\top.

Step 3. Put v:=\forall p\, .\,Jp\to\neg p. For each member a of A,

v\le(Ja\to\neg a)=\neg a,\qquad\text{so}\qquad a\le\neg v .

Hence \bigvee A\le\neg v. As \bigvee A is true, v is false, so there is a proposition q with

Jq\land q .

Step 4. Let a be a member of A. Then q\ne a, since q is true and a is false. By (1) and Step 2, \operatorname{SameOrDisjoint}(q,a), so q\land a=\bot and a\le\neg q. Hence \bigvee A\le\neg q, and \bigvee A is false since q is true: a contradiction. \square

Equivalently, for any pairwise disjoint A^{tt}, \bigvee A is true iff some member of A is true. Relational Choice is not used.

Lemma 3: without Actuality, the false propositions have join \top

Assume Boolean Completeness at t. Let k:=\bigvee\lambda p\, .\,\neg p.

  • If k is false, then \neg k witnesses Actuality: \neg k is true, and for each true q, \neg q is false, so \neg q\le k and \neg k\le q.
  • If k is true, then \neg k is false, so \neg k\le k, hence \neg k=\neg k\land k=\bot and k=\top.

So if Actuality fails, k=\top.

Lemma 4: disjoint refinement

Assume Relational Choice from t to t and Boolean Completeness at t. Let X^{tt} and j:=\bigvee X. There is a pairwise disjoint \Delta^{tt} such that

    1. each member of \Delta lies below some member of X, and
  • (ii) \bigvee\Delta=j.

Proof. Let U^{ttt} be

(Ub)x:=\bigl(b<j\to Xx\land x\not\le b\bigr)\land\bigl(\neg\,b<j\to x=\bot\bigr).

U is serial: if b<j, some member x of X has x\not\le b, since otherwise b would be an upper bound of X and j\le b. By Relational Choice there is a functional S with \forall b\,x\, .\,(Sb)x\to(Ub)x; write s(b) for the unique x with (Sb)x, and let

(Gb)c:=\exists x\, .\,(Sb)x\land c=b\lor x,\qquad g(b):=b\lor s(b).

Apply the Bourbaki–Witt lemma (Background, General lemmas) at type t with the property I:=\lambda b\, .\,b\le j, the order \le, and the function G:

  • \le partially orders I;
  • a chain Z\subseteq I has supremum \bigvee Z in I, because j bounds Z;
  • g maps I into I and b\le g(b): if b<j then s(b) is in X, so s(b)\le j; and g(j)=j\lor\bot=j.

Moreover g(b)\ne b when b<j, since s(b)\not\le b. Let M be the tower. By the lemma, M is a chain; if b<c in M then g(b)\le c; and the supremum m of M is in M with g(m)=m, so m=j and Mj.

For b in M with b<j, the increment at b is g(b)\land\neg b. Let

\Delta:=\lambda d\, .\,\exists b\, .\,Mb\land b<j\land d=g(b)\land\neg b .

Pairwise disjoint. Let b<c be in M, both below j. Then

(g(b)\land\neg b)\land(g(c)\land\neg c)\le g(b)\land\neg c\le c\land\neg c=\bot .

Since M is a chain, the increments at distinct members of M are disjoint.

(i). g(b)\land\neg b=(b\lor s(b))\land\neg b=s(b)\land\neg b\le s(b), and s(b) is in X.

(ii). Every increment is below g(b)\le j. Let k be any upper bound of \Delta. The property \lambda b\, .\,Mb\land b\le k is closed:

  • if b<j and b\le k then g(b)=b\lor(g(b)\land\neg b)\le k; and g(j)=j;
  • the supremum of a chain of propositions below k is below k.

So every member of M is below k, in particular j\le k. Thus j=\bigvee\Delta. \square

Proof of the theorem

Suppose Actuality fails. By Lemma 3, X:=\lambda p\, .\,\neg p has \bigvee X=\top. Let \Delta be as in Lemma 4. Each member of \Delta lies below a false proposition and so is false; \Delta is pairwise disjoint; and \bigvee\Delta=\top is true. This contradicts Lemma 2. \square

Remarks

  1. Transversal Choice. With the recorded results that Actuality implies Transversal and that Transversal and Relational Choice imply Transversal Choice, this settles the conjecture that Relational Choice and Boolean Completeness imply Transversal Choice. Astra also gave a direct proof of that conjecture, adapting Bourbaki–Witt to partial transversals, which needs Relational Choice from \sigma t to \sigma and suprema of represented chains at \sigma t.

  2. Boolean Completeness at tt gives it at t. For A^{tt}, let J be the least upper bound at tt of the constant properties \lambda z\, .\,p with Ap. By Lemma 1, J\top\le c iff p\le c for every member p of A, so J\top=\bigvee A.

  3. Where the higher type is used. Lemma 4 needs only Boolean Completeness at t. The type-tt instance is used only in Lemma 2, for the join of the haecceities. The argument does not show that Relational Choice and Boolean Completeness at t alone imply Actuality.

  4. The boxed form. The theorem is a theorem of C with its premises discharged, so necessitating it gives □Relational Choice and □Boolean Completeness imply □Actuality. The unboxed premises do not give □Actuality: the model Full Boolean-valued model: one atom and an atomless component separates them.
