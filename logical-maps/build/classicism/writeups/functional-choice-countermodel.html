Functional Choice without Boolean Completeness

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance.

Result and scope

Theorem (in ZFC). There is a pointed model of Classicism in its relational type system satisfying every instance of Functional Choice, but failing Boolean Completeness already at proposition type. Consequently it also fails Rigid Comprehension:

\mathcal M,r\models C+\mathrm{FC}+\neg\mathrm{BC}_t+\neg\mathrm{RC}.

Thus neither unrestricted entailment

C+\mathrm{FC}\vdash\mathrm{BC},\qquad C+\mathrm{FC}\vdash\mathrm{RC}

holds. Functional Choice here is unboxed and ranges over all admitted input types and all relational output types. The construction does not assert necessary Functional Choice.

The proof below constructs the entire finite-type hierarchy, validates C directly, represents every set function between the domains at the distinguished point, and supplies an admitted property of propositions with no supremum. In particular, incompleteness is not merely the absence of a supremum for an unrepresented external family.

1. Framework

The types are generated by e,t, with \sigma\tau admitted only when \tau\ne e. Every relational type has a unique flattened form

\tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_m t,

including m=0 for t. Application is written explicitly as F(a) or R(a,b) to avoid association conventions.

We use the map’s precise Functional Choice schema:

\forall U^{\sigma\tau t} \left[(\forall a^\sigma\exists b^\tau\,U(a,b)) \longrightarrow \exists F^{\sigma\tau}\forall a^\sigma\,U(a,F(a))\right], \qquad\tau\ne e. \tag{1}

Native necessity is \Box p:=(p=\top), and propositional entailment is p\le q:=\Box(p\to q). The map defines Boolean Completeness at t by a greatest-lower-bound requirement for every admitted X^{tt}. The least-upper-bound version is equivalent by complementation.

Classicism is H plus the Logical Equivalence schema: abstractions of formulas equivalent by a theorem of H are identical. The H-theoremhood restriction is retained throughout. We verify the H axioms and rules, the full beta and eta laws, and this schema; no identification of merely coextensive relations is assumed.

2. The topological space

Let

X=\{r\}\sqcup\mathbb N.

Its open sets are the empty set and

U_F=\{r\}\cup(\mathbb N\setminus F), \qquad F\subseteq\mathbb N\text{ finite}. \tag{2}

Every nonempty open set contains r. A neighborhood of a natural-number point n must also contain n. There is no least neighborhood of r.

A germ at x identifies two objects defined on neighborhoods of x when they agree on a smaller neighborhood of x. At r, agreement of families indexed by X therefore means agreement at r and at all but finitely many natural-number points.

The points are used to define the semantics. No extra primitive modality or Kripke accessibility relation is added to C.

3. The complete recursive type construction

We define sets D_\sigma(x) for every type \sigma and every x\in X, together with their local sections. The recursion is on type complexity.

For e, put

D_e(x)=\{*\}

at every point, with its unique constant section. The same construction works with any fixed nonempty individual set instead, using constant sections; no later step requires the singleton choice.

Suppose the argument-type domains have been defined and let \tau=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_m t. Over a nonempty open set U, a relation table of type \tau is an arbitrary family

R=(R_y)_{y\in U},\qquad R_y\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^m D_{\sigma_i}(y). \tag{3}

There is no continuity, symmetry, support or definability restriction on the rows. For m=0, a row is a truth value, so a proposition table is simply a subset of U.

Define

D_\tau(x)=\{[R]_x:R\text{ is a table over a neighborhood of }x\}, \tag{4}

where [R]_x=[S]_x precisely when some neighborhood V of x, contained in both table domains, satisfies R_y=S_y for every y\in V. The equality is of the entire rows, including all their fibre arguments.

Each table gives a section

s_R(y)=[R]_y\qquad(y\in U). \tag{5}

Local sections are maps locally of this form. Tables form a sheaf: compatible tables agree row by row and glue uniquely. A section is recovered from the table of its pointwise extensions. This also follows directly: near any point, the section is represented by a table, and its extension at each nearby point is that table’s row.

Write \operatorname{ext}_x([R]_x)=R_x. This is well-defined. At every point, every subset of a finite product of domains is a current extension of an admitted relation: prescribe that subset at the point and make all other rows empty.

Every relational object has a global representative section. A table defined on U extends to all of X by putting empty rows outside U. At a point of U this extension has the original germ. This fact is used for whole-output operations, not just for their graphs.

All domains are sets. Each relational-type construction uses only already constructed argument-type domains, powersets, products over a set of points, and quotients. The hierarchy over the set of finite types is therefore available in ZFC.

4. Application, abstraction and the logical constants

4.1 Application

For F=[R]_x\in D_{\sigma\tau}(x) and a\in D_\sigma(x), choose a section s through a on a common neighborhood. If \tau=\beta_1\cdots\beta_k t, define the sliced table

(R_s)_y(\bar b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_y(s(y),\bar b), \tag{6}

and put F(a)=[R_s]_x.

Shrinking neighborhoods proves independence of all representative choices. Equal input germs have equal representing sections on a smaller neighborhood, and equal operation germs have identical full rows there. Application therefore respects literal germ equality.

4.2 Truth and Boolean operations

For a proposition germ p=[A]_x, declare

\mathcal M,x\models p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in A. \tag{7}

Negation, conjunction and the other Boolean operations act on table rows in the usual way. Top and bottom are the full and empty proposition tables.

4.3 Identity

The identity constant at type \sigma is given by the global table

\operatorname{Eq}_{\sigma,y}(a,b) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=b\text{ in }D_\sigma(y). \tag{8}

At a point, identity thus has its required literal meaning. If identity is instead introduced through its Leibniz definition, the result is the same: arbitrary current unary extensions separate any two different elements of a fibre.

4.4 Quantifiers

The global table of \forall_\sigma is defined by

\operatorname{ext}_y(\forall_\sigma)(P) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{ext}_y(P)=D_\sigma(y). \tag{9}

Existential quantification is defined similarly. These are permitted tables at type (\sigma t)t. Consequently, truth of a quantified formula at y ranges over exactly D_\sigma(y), without a restriction to images of objects at r.

Quantification is performed at each point before taking a germ. It is not defined as an arbitrary meet in the eventual quotient algebra at r. This distinction is indispensable.

4.5 Locality, substitution, and abstraction

The following two facts make the whole-object substitution argument explicit.

Section recovery. If s is a section of a relational type \tau on U, the table T_y=\operatorname{ext}_y(s(y)) represents s on all of U. Indeed, near any x\in U choose a table R representing s. On that neighborhood T_y=R_y, so [T]_x=[R]_x=s(x). Consequently two sections with identical extension rows at every point of U are equal as sections, not just coextensive at one point. For the singleton individual type the corresponding assertion is immediate.

Term locality and substitution. Assign sections on an open set U to a term’s finitely many free variables. Interpretation produces a section on U, commutes with restriction, and its germ at x depends only on the assigned germs there. If B can be substituted for v without capture, then

\llbracket A[B/v]\rrbracket_U(s) = \llbracket A\rrbracket_U \bigl(s[v\mapsto\llbracket B\rrbracket_U(s)]\bigr) \tag{9a}

as sections of the output type.

Prove these claims together by structural induction on terms. A variable is its assigned section. Each logical constant is its specified global table restricted to U. Application is slicing: if the operation and argument are sections, Section 4.1 supplies their sliced table, whose germs are their pointwise applications. This construction commutes with restriction and respects equality of germs.

For the abstraction case \lambda v.A, write the output type of A as \beta_1\cdots\beta_k t. At each y\in U define the row

R_y(a,\bar b) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \bar b\in\operatorname{ext}_y \left(\llbracket A\rrbracket_y(s_y[v\mapsto a])\right). \tag{9b}

The induction hypothesis defines the body at every assignment of germs. Formula (9b) is therefore an admitted table. It uses all a\in D_\sigma(y) and all remaining arguments \bar b, without choosing representative sections for all of them simultaneously.

If two choices of the free-parameter sections have the same germs at x, shrink to a common neighborhood V on which those finitely many sections agree. At every y\in V the body’s parameter assignments are identical. Its values agree for every a and \bar b, by the induction hypothesis. Thus the entire rows in (9b) agree on this one neighborhood V. There is no intersection of argument-dependent neighborhoods. This proves locality for the abstraction.

For substitution inside an abstraction, first rename its bound variable to avoid capture. The induction hypothesis for its body identifies the values in (9b) at every point and every bound-argument tuple. Hence the resulting abstraction tables coincide row by row, proving (9a). The variable, constant, and application cases are the corresponding immediate substitution identities.

For beta, slice (9b) along the section denoted by B. Its rows are exactly the extension rows of the right-hand side of (9a). Section recovery makes the result the same whole output section. For eta, tabulating \lambda v.F(v) recovers the full row of a table representing F at every point and every argument, so it recovers the same germ. Term contexts preserve these identities: application respects germ equality, and an enclosing abstraction compares the same rows for each assignment of its bound variables. This validates beta and eta inside arbitrary formula contexts, including under further abstractions.

4.6 Soundness for C

At every point, propositional truth is classical, the quantifiers have the full fibrewise truth clauses, and identity is literal equality. This validates H’s propositional, quantifier and identity axioms. The preceding whole-object beta and eta equations validate substitution of beta/eta equivalents in arbitrary contexts. Modus ponens, generalization and existential instantiation preserve validity under all fibre assignments.

Now suppose H proves P\leftrightarrow Q. At every point and every assignment of the free and bound variables, their truth values agree. Fix representatives of the free parameters on U. The tables of the corresponding lambda abstractions therefore agree at every point of U, for every tuple of argument values. Their germs are identical. This proves the Logical Equivalence schema with its original H-theoremhood side condition.

The closed Boolean and Classicist identities can also be checked directly. The Boolean identities hold row by row. For the Identity Identity, equality of a,b\in D_\sigma(y) is equivalent to agreement on every unary predicate at y: when a\ne b, an admitted singleton extension separates them. Universal and existential absorption and distribution are the ordinary classical identities for quantification over D_\sigma(y). Each check is uniform in all row arguments, so the two sides are equal tables and hence equal germs. This provides a direct check of C’s identity axioms in addition to the H-theorem argument.

Thus

\mathcal M,x\models C\quad\text{for every }x\in X. \tag{10}

The native modality remains the identity-defined one. For a proposition section represented by A\subseteq U, the points at which its germ equals top are exactly the relative interior of A in U. Hence \Box is determined by (8), not separately stipulated.

5. Every function at r is represented

The fact that every nonempty neighborhood contains r provides a canonical map

\rho_{\sigma,x}:D_\sigma(x)\longrightarrow D_\sigma(r). \tag{11}

For a germ at x, take any representing section and evaluate it at r. This is well-defined: sections equal on a neighborhood of x are equal there at r too. For the individual type the map is the unique map. In particular,

\rho_{\sigma,r}=\operatorname{id},\qquad \rho_{\sigma,y}(s(y))=s(r) \tag{12}

for every section s on a nonempty open set and every y in its domain. Every \rho_{\sigma,x} is onto, since every object at r has a global representative section.

Fix any set function

f:D_\sigma(r)\longrightarrow D_\tau(r),\qquad\tau\ne e. \tag{13}

For every a\in D_\sigma(r) choose a global representative section t_a of f(a). This is an ordinary set-indexed choice in ZFC. Define a fibre operation at every point by

\widetilde f_x(b)=t_{\rho_{\sigma,x}(b)}(x). \tag{14}

For \tau=\beta_1\cdots\beta_k t, its representing table is explicitly

F_x(b,\bar c) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \bar c\in\operatorname{ext}_x \bigl(t_{\rho_{\sigma,x}(b)}(x)\bigr). \tag{15}

This is an allowed arbitrary table of type \sigma\tau.

Take a\in D_\sigma(r) and represent it by a section s on U. By (12), \rho_{\sigma,y}(s(y))=a for every y\in U. The sliced table (15) therefore agrees throughout U with the extension table of t_a. It follows that

[F]_r(a)=t_a(r)=f(a) \quad\text{as an equality of whole }\tau\text{-objects}. \tag{16}

This proves representation of every set function, not only the existence of an admitted functional graph. It covers each admitted input type and every relational output type.

If U^{\sigma\tau t} is serial at r, external Choice selects a function f from its actual extension. Equation (16) gives an admitted operation selecting the same witnesses. Therefore

\mathcal M,r\models\mathrm{FC}. \tag{17}

No necessary truth of FC, output-persistence property, or simultaneous internal choice over all serial relations has been inferred.

6. The proposition algebra and the failure of BC

At r, a proposition table is a subset of a cofinite neighborhood containing r. Extending it arbitrarily to X gives

D_t(r)\cong \mathbf2\times\bigl(\mathcal P(\mathbb N)/\mathrm{Fin}\bigr), \tag{18}

by

[A]_r\longmapsto \bigl(\mathbf1_{r\in A},[A\cap\mathbb N]_{\mathrm{Fin}}\bigr).

The isomorphism is of Boolean algebras. A germ equals top exactly when its first coordinate is 1 and its natural-number trace is cofinite. Consequently the map’s entailment order is exactly

(\epsilon,[A])\le(\delta,[B]) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \epsilon\le\delta\ \text{ and }\ A\setminus B\text{ is finite}. \tag{19}

Partition \mathbb N into disjoint infinite sets A_0,A_1,\ldots and put

p_n=(0,[A_n]),\qquad S=\{p_n:n\in\mathbb N\}. \tag{20}

The family S is an admitted property of propositions. Its type-tt table has root row exactly S and empty rows at all other points.

Suppose b=(\epsilon,[B]) is an upper bound of S. For every n, A_n\setminus B is finite, so B\cap A_n is infinite. Choose its least member b_n and set

B'=B\setminus\{b_n:n\in\mathbb N\}. \tag{21}

Each A_n\setminus B' is still finite. Hence (\epsilon,[B']) is another upper bound. But it is strictly smaller than b, since the removed set has one distinct member in each block and is therefore infinite. No upper bound can be least.

Thus S has no supremum. Equivalently, the admitted family \{\neg p_n:n\in\mathbb N\} has no greatest lower bound. This refutes precisely the native Boolean Completeness instance:

\mathcal M,r\models\neg\mathrm{BC}_t. \tag{22}

7. Rigid Comprehension also fails

The map’s established theorem \mathrm{RC}\to\mathrm{BC} already gives the conclusion. Here is the relevant argument directly for the family S.

Suppose an admitted G^{tt} is rigid and coextensive at r with the property of propositions in (20). Put

u=\exists p\,(G(p)\land p). \tag{23}

For every p_n\in S, persistence gives \Box G(p_n). The existential-introduction theorem then gives p_n\le u.

Let b be any upper bound of S. Every current member p of G satisfies \Box(p\to b). Apply the inextensibility part of rigidity to the admitted test Z(p):=(p\to b). It gives

\Box\forall p\,(G(p)\to(p\to b)).

Consequently u\le b. Thus u would be the least upper bound of S, contradicting Section 6.

There is therefore no rigid current coextension of this admitted tt-relation. In particular,

\mathcal M,r\models\neg\mathrm{RC}. \tag{24}

8. A diagnostic: why the quantifiers do not restore completeness

The root has every current extension and represents every current set function, but this does not make a relation identical to its current extension.

Choose distinct q_n\in D_t(r). Define a type-tt table K by

K_r=\varnothing,\qquad K_n=\{a\in D_t(n):\rho_{t,n}(a)=q_n\}. \tag{25}

Every K_n is nonempty, by surjectivity of \rho_{t,n}. Hence [K]_r is not the bottom relation: no cofinite neighborhood makes all its rows empty.

For a fixed root proposition p, however, every representing section has constant root image p. Thus K(p) has a true row at at most one natural-number point and is false at r. Therefore

K(p)=\bot\quad\text{for every }p\in D_t(r), \qquad K\ne\bot_{tt}. \tag{26}

Furthermore,

\exists p\,K(p)=(0,[\mathbb N])\ne\bot. \tag{27}

The existential quantifier sees a witness in every natural-number fibre even though each fixed root argument produces the bottom proposition. This is the required distinction between quantifying first and passing to germs first. In particular, the dual Barcan implication fails at r: possible existence does not supply a possible current witness. No Barcan principle was used to validate H or C.

This calculation also shows directly why a fixed-domain Boolean semantics with arbitrarily stipulated meets would not be a substitute for this construction.

9. Failure of necessary Functional Choice

For clarity, FC need not hold at a natural-number point. At such a point n, a proposition germ is determined by its germ at r together with its truth value at n:

D_t(n)\cong D_t(r)\times\mathbf2.

Choose two different elements of D_t(n) with the same image under \rho_{t,n}, and prescribe outputs with different images under \rho_{t,n}. Extend these prescriptions to a total set function on D_t(n). Its graph is an admitted current relation by fibrewise extension fullness.

Every admitted operation respects \rho: applying representatives and then taking their germ at r gives the same result as applying the germs at r. Hence no admitted operation at n can agree with that prescribed function on the two chosen inputs. The type-t to type-t FC instance fails there.

This occurs at every n, whereas the same instance holds at r. The constructed countermodel therefore concerns unboxed FC exactly, not its necessary form.
