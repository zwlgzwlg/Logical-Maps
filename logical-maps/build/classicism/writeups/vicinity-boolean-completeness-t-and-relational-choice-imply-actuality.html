Vicinity, Boolean Completeness at $t$ and Relational Choice imply Actuality

The theorem is due to GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, in research notes of 25–26 October 2026 in Daniel Kodsi’s pull request #18. Astra proves it for C5, and transfers it to C with Vicinity by interpreting C5 there. Cian Dorr suggested running the C5 argument directly, with 5-necessity in place of necessity. The proof below does that, and was written by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

Claim. C, together with Vicinity, Boolean Completeness at t and Relational Choice with inputs and outputs of type t, proves Actuality. Every premise is unboxed.

The idea. Vicinity gives a true proposition s, the strongest 5-necessary truth. Below s every identity statement is settled, and quantification over settled families of propositions is computed by meets and joins. So below s the situation is as in C5, with “settled” in place of C5’s “crisp”. Astra’s C5 argument then goes through.

  • If there is no true atom, follow the true parts of a system of partitions down a chain of truths. Its glb is false.
  • Code the chain by a single proposition, so that a settled chain follows it.
  • The glb of a settled family of truths is true, which is a contradiction.

The chain arguments are the Bourbaki–Witt lemma and its admissible variant (Background, Relations, orders and fixed points). They replace the chain lemmas of Astra’s proof.

0. What Vicinity provides

Let v witness Vicinity and put s:=v\land\forall r\, .\,r\to v\le\Diamond r, which is true. As in the proof that Vicinity and Relational Choice imply Transversal Choice, call p settled if s\le p or s\le\neg p, and a property settled if each of its instances is. A settled p is true iff s\le p. That proof gives three lemmas:

  • Lemma 1. Every identity statement is settled.
  • Lemma 2 (collection, Sun). If s\le Fx for every x, then s\le\forall x\, .\,Fx.
  • Lemma 3. A formula built by connectives and quantifiers from identities a=b, and from atoms Yb where each Y is a settled property or is bound by a quantifier restricted to settled properties, is settled.

One more is needed.

Lemma 4 (settled families). Let A be a settled property of propositions. Then:

  1. s\land\forall p\, .\,(Ap\to p)\le p for every p with Ap;
  2. s\land p\le\exists q\, .\,(Aq\land q) for every p with Ap;
  3. s\land\exists q\, .\,(Aq\land q)\le w whenever w is an upper bound of the p with Ap.

Proof. If Ap then s\le Ap, which gives (1) and (2). For (3), s\le(Aq\land q\to w) for every q: if Aq, because q\le w; if not, because s\le\neg Aq. Lemma 2 gives s\le\forall q\, .\,(Aq\land q\to w). \square

We work below s throughout. For p,q\le s, an identity s\land p=s\land q is an identity p=q.

1. Reduction to an atomless truth below s

“p is an atom” is built from identities, so it is settled by Lemma 3. Let z:=\exists p\, .\,(\operatorname{Atom}(p)\land p). If z is true, some atom is true, and a true atom witnesses Actuality.

Suppose z is false, and put h:=s\land\neg z, which is true. Then nothing below h is an atom. For let q\le h be an atom. Then s\le\operatorname{Atom}(q), so q=s\land q\le z by Lemma 4. Also q\le\neg z, so q=\bot, which is impossible for an atom.

So assume that h\le s is true and that every nonzero p\le h has some q with \bot<q<p. We derive a contradiction.

2. Partitions, by Relational Choice

Let

R(p,q):=\bigl[\bot<p\le h\land\bot<q<p\bigr]\lor\bigl[\neg(\bot<p\le h)\land q=\bot\bigr].

R is serial and settled. Relational Choice gives a functional K with K\subseteq R. Following Astra, put

c_p(q):=K(p,q)\land R(p,q)\land\forall z\, .\,(K(p,z)\land R(p,z))\to z=q,\qquad d_p:=\exists q\, .\,c_p(q),

a:=h\land\forall p\, .\,d_p,\qquad u_0(p,q):=p\land c_p(q)\land q,\qquad u_1(p,q):=p\land c_p(q)\land\neg q .

Each d_p is true, since the K-value of p makes some c_p(q) true. So a is true, and a\le h\le s.

Fix \bot<p\le a. The values u_i(p,q) have three properties.

  • They are pairwise disjoint. For q\ne q', c_p(q)\land c_p(q')\le(q=q'). That identity is false, so s\le\neg(q=q'), and since p\le s, u_i(p,q)\land u_j(p,q')=\bot. And u_0(p,q)\land u_1(p,q)=\bot.
  • The nonzero ones are proper parts of p. If u_i(p,q)\ne\bot then s\land R(p,q)\ne\bot, so R(p,q), which is settled, is true. Hence \bot<q<p, so u_0(p,q)\le q<p and u_1(p,q)\le p\land\neg q<p.
  • Their join is p. \exists q\, .\,(u_0(p,q)\lor u_1(p,q))=p\land d_p=p. And if w bounds every value, then s\le(u_0(p,q)\lor u_1(p,q)\to w) for every q. So, by Lemma 2, p=s\land p\le w.

Let

\operatorname{Child}(p,r):=\bot<p\le a\land r\ne\bot\land\exists q\, .\,(r=u_0(p,q)\lor r=u_1(p,q)),

which is settled by Lemma 3. For \bot<p\le a, the r with \operatorname{Child}(p,r), the children of p, are pairwise disjoint and strictly below p, and their join is p. In particular p has at least two children. By Lemma 4 and p\le s, p=s\land\exists r\, .\,(\operatorname{Child}(p,r)\land r). So if p is true, exactly one child of p is true: at least one by this identity, and at most one by disjointness.

3. The chain of true parts

On X:=\lambda p\, .\,p\le a, ordered by \ge (so that suprema are glbs), let

F(p,r):=\bigl[\neg p\land r=p\bigr]\lor\bigl[p\land\operatorname{Child}(p,r)\land r\bigr].

F is a functional relation on X with F(p)\le p, strictly when p is true. Boolean Completeness at t gives every chain in X a glb, which lies in X (the empty chain has supremum a).

The Bourbaki–Witt lemma, in its case for partial orders, gives a tower M, the least property closed under F and glbs of chains. Its glb m satisfies Mm and F(m)=m. Four facts about M follow.

  1. m is false, because F strictly decreases truths.
  2. Every other member of M is true. Let Mq with q false, so that F(q)=q. The property \lambda x\, .\,Mx\land q\le x is closed. For F, if q<x this is clause (b) of the lemma, and if x=q it holds because F(q)=q. For glbs it is immediate. So m\ge q, and q=m.
  3. m is the glb of M':=\lambda x\, .\,Mx\land x\ne m. That glb t satisfies Mt. If t\ne m, then t is true, so F(t)<t, and F(t) is true with M'F(t), which is impossible.
  4. q\le F(p) whenever q<p are members of M, by clause (b).

4. The discarded parts, coded by a second choice

Let

\Delta(d):=\exists p\,r\, .\,Mp\land p\land F(p,r)\land\operatorname{Child}(p,d)\land d\ne r,

so that the members of \Delta are the false children of true members of M.

  • They are pairwise disjoint. Two discarded at the same parent are disjoint children. If d is discarded at p and d' at q<p, then d'\le q\le F(p), while d is a child of p other than F(p), so d\land F(p)=\bot.
  • Every true member of M has one, since it has at least two children and only one is true.

Relational Choice, applied to the serial relation [\Delta(d)\land\operatorname{Child}(d,r)]\lor[\neg\Delta(d)\land r=\bot], gives a functional subrelation, whose value at d\in\Delta we write r_d. So r_d is a child of d. By Boolean Completeness, let b be the join of the r_d. For every d\in\Delta,

b\land d=r_d .

For r_d\le b\land d, and \neg d\lor r_d bounds every r_e, since r_e\le e\le\neg d for e\ne d. Let

\operatorname{Mixed}(x):=x\land b\ne\bot\land x\land\neg b\ne\bot,\qquad H(c):=\exists e\, .\,\operatorname{Child}(c,e)\land\operatorname{Mixed}(e),

both settled by Lemma 3.

  • H fails at every d\in\Delta. A child e of d is r_d, which is below b, or else disjoint from r_d, in which case e\land b=e\land d\land b=e\land r_d=\bot. Either way e is not mixed.
  • H holds at every true member c of M. A child d of c discarded there has d\land b=r_d\ne\bot and d\land\neg b=d\land\neg r_d\ne\bot.

So at a true member p of M, exactly one child satisfies H, namely F(p). It is a true member of M, while the other children are in \Delta.

5. The decoded step

Let

g:=\lambda p\, .\,p\land\exists c\, .\,(\operatorname{Child}(p,c)\land H(c)\land c),

a term of type tt. Let p be a true member of M. The property \lambda c\, .\,\operatorname{Child}(p,c)\land H(c) is settled, and F(p) is its only instance. So, by Lemma 4 and F(p)\le p\le s,

g(p)=F(p).

This is an identity, not merely an agreement in truth value.

6. The settled chain, and the contradiction

Apply the Bourbaki–Witt lemma for admissible chains to X, ordered by \ge, with the graph of g as the step and the settled properties as the admissible ones. Its hypotheses hold:

  • g(p)\le p;
  • settled chains have glbs in X, by Boolean Completeness;
  • the properties its proof forms (X, the tower, the pivots and each N_{c,e}) are settled.

The last holds by Lemma 3, as in the proof that Vicinity and Relational Choice imply Transversal Choice: every atom of their definitions is an identity, g being a term, or is applied to a variable bound to settled properties. So the tower N is closed under g and under glbs of settled chains, and it is a chain.

Claim A: M\subseteq N. It suffices that \lambda p\, .\,Mp\land Np is closed under F and under glbs of chains, since M is the least such property.

  • If p is in both and false, then F(p)=p.
  • If p is in both and true, then F(p)=g(p) is in N.
  • Let Z be a chain inside both, with glb t. Then Mt. Moreover L:=\lambda p\, .\,Np\land t\le p is a settled chain, by Lemma 3 with t as a parameter, so its glb is in N. That glb is t: Z\subseteq L makes it at most t, and t bounds L from below.

Claim B: every member p of N with m<p is true. N is a chain containing every true member of M. If p were false, it could not lie above a true member, so it would lie strictly below each of them. It would then be a lower bound of M', and p\le m by fact 3.

Claim C: m is true. Let I:=\lambda p\, .\,Np\land m<p, which is settled, and put w:=s\land\forall p\, .\,(Ip\to p). By Claim B, w is true. By Lemma 4 it lies below every member of I. These include every member of M', by Claim A, so w\le m by fact 3.

This contradicts fact 1 of §3. \blacksquare

Remarks

Where the premises are used.

  • Vicinity is used only through s and Lemmas 1–4.
  • Relational Choice is used twice, for the partitions and for the code b. Neither relation needs to be settled.
  • Boolean Completeness at t supplies the glbs of chains for M and for N, and the join b.

Comparison with Astra’s proof. Astra’s §§3–6, the partitions, the truth-guided chain, the code and the decoded operation, appear here in §§2–5, with “crisp” read as “settled below s”. Astra then builds a crisp chain for g that is wellordered against all subproperties, and compares it with M by initial segments. Claims A–C replace that comparison: closedness shows M\subseteq N, and the chain property of N shows its members above m are true. The fixed point of g is never needed.

The boxed form. The proof is a derivation in C, so its necessitation gives the boxed form.
