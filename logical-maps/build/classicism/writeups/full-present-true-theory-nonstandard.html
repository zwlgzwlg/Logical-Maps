Stable analytical truth with a nonstandard numerical component

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance, 29–30 October 2026.

There is a model of C with full Necessity of Second-Order Analysis in which Arithmetic Necessitism and Analytic Necessitism both fail. The numerical failure occurs with its boxed noncollapse guard true. The construction is in ambient ZFC and gives a consistency upper bound relative to Con(ZFC).

The proof combines the existing full-present construction with a component having the complete theory of a standard full hierarchy. Its canonical number field nevertheless contains a nonstandard member. No independent human check or Lean proof is recorded.

1. A countable component with the right complete theory

Let F be the full one-world extensional hierarchy with individual domain \omega, truth values \{0,1\}, and all functions at every admitted relational type. Necessity is the identity. There are countably many finite type expressions and standard finite parameterized lambda templates. Represent F in the corresponding countable many-sorted first-order Henkin language, with application and the operation for every such template.

Let T=\operatorname{Th}(F). This complete theory is a set; it need not be recursive. Put \nu=(et)t, add a constant c:\nu, and require

\mathbb N_e(c),\qquad c\ne\bar n_e\quad(n\in\omega).

Every finite part holds in F by choosing a standard numeral outside its finite list of exclusions. Compactness and downward Löwenheim–Skolem give a model H with every sort countable, elementarily equivalent to F in the original language, and with the displayed c. In particular H satisfies I^*_e, has infinitely many individuals, and preserves all parameterized Henkin operations. Its extensionality identifies each relation entity with its function on the appropriate H domains.

H is not asserted to be an elementary substructure of F. Its admitted predicates cannot include the external set of its standard numerals: that set would be inductive and omit its numerical c. Compactness was applied to a first-order presentation, without imposing full second-order semantics on H.

2. The full present

Take R_e=H_e, with identity projection. For each flattened relational type \tau=\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_k t, including k=0, set

R_\tau=\mathcal P(R_{\alpha_1}\times\cdots\times R_{\alpha_k})\times H_\tau.

A pair (S,a) has current extension S and projects to a. Application and lambda abstraction calculate the current row and the H denotation separately. A quantified formula uses the entire R domain currently and the entire original H domain in its H coordinate. In particular, old quantifiers are never restricted to images of current arguments.

These are the standard full-present operations, as also displayed in Full present over a C5 component, Section 2. Their C-validity uses the original component operations. For a theoremhood-qualified equivalence of the base logic H, the current lambda rows agree, and C-validity in the component identifies the second coordinates. Every finite parameterized lambda term remains admitted. No additional optional premise enters the Logical Equivalence rule. In the present case H is a one-world extensional Henkin model, so the construction is also the ordinary one-component coalesced root, with one nonidentity arrow h:R\to H.

Root identity is literal identity: arbitrary current singleton predicates separate distinct root entities. Terms commute with h. A formula at R is necessary exactly when it is true both currently and after transport to H, with the latter’s own full quantifier domains. Thus the root has four whole propositions, although its designated current truth is classical.

3. Every second-order analytical sentence has a fixed sign

Fix a standard finite sentence A in the native analytical grammar, and let \epsilon be its truth value in the ordinary full real field with its natural-number predicate. For each admitted carrier type \sigma, the sentence

U_{\sigma,A,\epsilon}:= \forall\mathfrak R\, (\operatorname{Real}_\sigma(\mathfrak R)\to A(\mathfrak R)^\epsilon)

holds in F by categoricity of full complete ordered fields and invariance under isomorphism. Here the superscript means A or its negation according to the selected sign. This is a single finite sentence of the Henkin language, even when no field is available at that carrier. It belongs to T and therefore holds in H. What transfers is the sentence; no claim that H’s internal real fields are externally complete is needed.

At R, every current real-field tuple is an external complete ordered field. Every subset of its carrier, and every finite-arity relation on it, is the current restriction of an admitted row: choose that row freely and any H component. The least inductive field predicate is consequently the ordinary natural-number part. The native analytical grammar uses relations only by applying them to field elements and uses identity only between those elements. It cannot distinguish two relation entities that have the same current row. Induction on this grammar therefore gives full external analytical semantics at R, with the same sign \epsilon.

Both R and H satisfy U_{\sigma,A,\epsilon}, so its box holds at R. This proves the whole Necessity of Second-Order Analysis schema, at every carrier and for every standard finite analytical sentence. It also covers fields that can occur at R but not at the corresponding type of F; the root argument uses their external categoricity directly.

4. The numerical field is not weakly inextensible

Let \bar n_R be the root’s closed e-numerals. Every inductive property contains their orbit. Conversely, R admits a property with precisely that orbit as its current row, and any H component. It is currently inductive. The definition of numberhood therefore gives

\operatorname{ext}_R(\mathbb N_e)=\{\bar n_R:n\in\omega\}.

The root individual domain is infinite and has all current unary rows. The current row of \bar n_R therefore counts exactly the n-element properties, distinguishing these numeral entities. Zero is not a successor, and successor is injective on their orbit. Thus I^*_e holds at R; it holds at H by T, so \Box I^*_e holds at R.

Parameterized Henkin comprehension in H admits X_H(x)=\mathbb N_e(x)\land x\ne c. Form the root pair X whose current row is the standard numeral orbit and whose H component is X_H. Each current root number is a standard numeral, and its image is the corresponding H numeral, different from c. Hence

R\models\forall n(\mathbb N_e(n)\to\Box X(n)).

But \mathbb N_e\le X fails, since H has the number c and lacks X_H(c). This is exactly a weak-inextensibility countertest for the whole numerical predicate. Its rigidity, and hence Arithmetic Necessitism at e, fails while the required boxed guard is true.

5. Analytic Necessitism fails nonvacuously

Since R_e is countably infinite and H_{et} is countable, R_{et}=\mathcal P(R_e)\times H_{et} has continuum many members. Current fullness supplies a complete real field at et. Any current complete real field there has continuum many elements, by categoricity.

If a rigid necessarily-real field witnessed Analytic Necessitism, its carrier’s necessary distinctness would make h injective on those current elements: two elements with equal H images would fail necessary distinctness. But H_{et} is countable. Thus no possible witness can have the required rigidity and necessary realhood. AN fails at et with a true possible-real antecedent; the recorded implication from SOAN to AN also gives SOAN failure.
