Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension imply Rigid Power

Composed by Claude Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), prompted by Zachary Goodsell, 19 October 2026, from Lemma C.6 of Andrew Bacon, Could the truths of mathematics have been different? (draft of 9 October 2025, Appendix C.2, pp. 15–16 of the separately paginated appendix), and the lemma, from Christopher Sun’s Possible Worlds Without Atomicity, that coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical (the proof of rigid-comprehension-implies-tame-rigidity). Lean-verified the same day (lean/Classicism/Results/Arity.lean, atom_power_pullback, atom_rc_rigid_power_core and atom_rc_rigid_power; certificate Classicism.Map.atomicity_and_rigid_comprehension_imply_rigid_power, with the necessitated form and the □BF consequence beside it).

Bacon’s Leibniz Biconditionals say that every possible relation is entailed by a world relation, a possible relation that entails every relation of its type or its negation; with the paper’s unboxed worlds this is the map’s Atomicity (Background, strong and atoms). Lemma C.6 is stated there with the relativized necessity \Box_N and used for the Necessity of Analysis; here it is run with \Box itself, and its conclusion is turned into Rigid Power.

Fix a type tuple \bar\sigma, let F^{\bar\sigma t} be rigid, and put P:=\lambda X^{\bar\sigma t}\,.\,\operatorname{Rigid}(X)\land X\le F, of type (\bar\sigma t)t. Recall that W\le\mathcal Y is necessary pointwise implication, that an atom W of a relational type is non-bottom and lies below every \mathcal Y of its type or below \neg\mathcal Y, and that a weakly rigid relation is persistent (Background).

Step 0 (P is persistent). Both conjuncts of PX are necessitated conditions, \operatorname{Rigid}(X) by its definition and X\le F as an identity, so PX\to\Box PX holds at every world, by 4 and NI.

Step 1 (Lemma C.6). Let W be an atom of type (\bar\sigma t)t with W\le P, that is, \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\land Y\le F. Then there is a rigid X\le F with \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y=X.

Proof. By Rigid Comprehension at \bar\sigma t let X be rigid and coextensive with Q:=\lambda\bar x\,.\,F\bar x\land\Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x.

  1. X\le F. Every actual instance of X is an instance of F, hence necessarily one, F being persistent; the weak inextensibility of X, applied to F, gives X\le F. So PX.

  2. \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to\forall\bar x\,.\,X\bar x\to Y\bar x. For an actual instance \bar x of X, Q\bar x gives \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x. The weak inextensibility of X, applied to \lambda\bar x\,.\,\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x, gives \Box\forall\bar x\,.\,X\bar x\to\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x, which is the claim.

  3. \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to\forall\bar x\,.\,Y\bar x\to X\bar x. Let \bar x be an actual instance of F. If X\bar x, then \Box X\bar x by persistence. If not, then \neg\Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x, that is W\not\le\lambda Y\,.\,Y\bar x, so, W being an atom, W\le\lambda Y\,.\,\neg Y\bar x: \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to\neg Y\bar x. Either way \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x\to X\bar x. The weak inextensibility of F, applied to \lambda\bar x\,.\,\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x\to X\bar x, gives \Box\forall\bar x\,.\,F\bar x\to\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y\bar x\to X\bar x. At any world, if WY then Y\le F, so by T every instance of Y there is an instance of F, hence of X.

  4. At any world where WY: Y is rigid, since W\le P; X is rigid, rigidity being a necessitated condition; and by (b) and (c) they are coextensive. Coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical, a theorem of C, so Y=X there. \square

Step 2 (P is weakly inextensible). Let \mathcal X^{((\bar\sigma t)t)t} satisfy \forall X\,.\,PX\to\Box\mathcal XX, and suppose P\not\le\mathcal X: the property Q':=\lambda X\,.\,PX\land\neg\mathcal XX is possibly instantiated, so it is not bottom. Atomicity at type (\bar\sigma t)t gives an atom W\le Q'; then W\le P, and Step 1 gives a rigid X\le F with \Box\forall Y\,.\,WY\to Y=X. Since PX, \Box\mathcal XX. But W is possible: at some world some Y has WY, so Y=X and, as W\le Q', \neg\mathcal XY, that is \neg\mathcal XX, contradicting \Box\mathcal XX.

Step 3 (P is rigid). By Steps 0 and 2, P is weakly rigid. Rigid Comprehension at (\bar\sigma t)t gives Tame Rigidity at that type (rigid-comprehension-implies-tame-rigidity): a rigid relation coextensive with P is identical to it. So P is rigid, which is Rigid Power at \bar\sigma t. \blacksquare

Remarks

  • Atomicity is used only at the type of the power property, and Rigid Comprehension at \bar\sigma t (Step 1) and at (\bar\sigma t)t (Step 3), all at the evaluation point; BF, B and 5 do not appear. This answers the question left open in CANDIDATES.md, whether unboxed BF, Atomicity and Rigid Comprehension give Rigid Power: they do, and BF is not needed.
  • Steps 0–2 use of F only its persistence and weak inextensibility. So Atomicity at (\bar\sigma t)t with Rigid Comprehension at \bar\sigma t already gives the variant (W,R,W) of CANDIDATES.md: the power property of every weakly rigid relation is weakly rigid.
  • The argument is a derivation in C from instances of the two premises, so its necessitation gives □Atomicity and □Rigid Comprehension imply □Rigid Power (necessary-atomicity-and-necessary-rigid-comprehension-imply-necessary-rigid-power), without the □BF of necessary-bf-atomicity-and-rigid-comprehension-imply-necessary-rigid-power.
  • Atomicity at type t does not suffice, even boxed and with □Rigid Comprehension: the stitched forcing model stitched-cohen-extension has □Atomicity (type t), □Rigid Comprehension and □Relational Choice, and Rigid Power fails in it.
