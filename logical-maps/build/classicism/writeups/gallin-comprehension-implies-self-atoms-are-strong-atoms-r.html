Gallin comprehension makes every current self-atom strong

GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 30 October 2026.

Over the fixed logic C,

\mathrm{Gallin\ Extensional\ Comprehension} \ \Longrightarrow\ \forall Q^\tau\bigl(\operatorname{SelfAtom}_\tau(Q) \to\operatorname{SAtom}_\tau(Q)\bigr) \qquad(\tau\ne e).

The conclusion concerns every current self-atom at every admitted relational type, including propositions. Its strongness must settle all predicates at every future point, including newly available predicates. The additional device is to apply a bivalent evaluator to the atom’s order-character, rather than to its current instances. That retains information about a self-atom even where it is not instantiated.

1. Evaluator and current atomicity

Apply Gallin’s type-tt instance to the truth predicate. It supplies an admitted operation E:t\to t coextensive with truth, with both E and its pointwise negation persistent. Hence

\forall p\,(Ep\leftrightarrow p), \qquad \Box\forall p\,(Ep=\top\lor Ep=\bot). \tag{1}

For the second assertion, at any accessible point and any proposition there, either Ep or \neg Ep holds. Persistence of the corresponding relation makes that truth or falsity necessary, so the value is literally the corresponding endpoint. This includes propositions first available at that point. Equation (1) does not assert future correctness of E.

Gallin also gives current all-type ND by [[gallin-comprehension-implies-nd]], and therefore current B. If Q is a current self-atom, ND makes it an ordinary atom. Indeed, suppose P\le Q, P\ne\bot and P\ne Q. ND preserves the two inequalities, and necessary inclusion preserves P\le Q. Nonbottom Q has a possible instance. There its self-atom clause makes it an atom, contradicting the preserved proper nonzero part. This also follows from [[modal-five-implies-self-atoms-are-atoms-r]].

2. Endpoint Boolean laws survive new future arguments

Let J:\tau\to t satisfy

\Box\forall X^\tau\,(JX=\top\lor JX=\bot). \tag{2}

Suppose that it currently preserves the Boolean constants, complement and binary meet. Under current B these laws hold necessarily, including for future arguments that are unavailable now.

For complement, let

\varphi:=\forall X\,[J(\neg X)=\neg JX].

It is true now. If it fails at a future point, choose a counterexample X there. Both sides are endpoints by (2); necessity of identity preserves their different endpoint assignments. The same witness therefore keeps \varphi false at every later point. This proves

\Box(\neg\varphi\to\Box\neg\varphi).

Current B, applied to the whole current proposition \varphi, gives \Box\Diamond\varphi. A future failure would contradict this, so \Box\varphi. For meet use \varphi:=\forall X,Y[J(X\land Y)=JX\land JY]; the same argument works with its two future witnesses. The constant equations persist by necessity of identity.

No box is exchanged with a quantifier. The range condition (2) already covers the full future domain, and the proof rules out a future counterexample to each universally quantified law.

3. Normalize the atom’s order-character

For the current self-atom Q, now known to be an ordinary atom, put

J_Q:=\lambda X^\tau\, .\,E(Q\le_\tau X). \tag{3}

Its type \tau t is admitted. Equation (1) gives its necessary endpoint range, even on new future X. Current correctness gives J_QX\leftrightarrow Q\le X.

The characteristic map of a nonzero Boolean atom preserves top, bottom, complement and binary meet:

\begin{gathered} Q\le\top,\qquad Q\not\le\bot,\\ Q\le\neg X\ \leftrightarrow\ \neg(Q\le X),\\ Q\le (X\land Y)\ \leftrightarrow\ (Q\le X\land Q\le Y). \end{gathered}

Since all outputs of J_Q are endpoints, these current truth conditions give literal equalities expressing its Boolean laws. Also J_QQ=\top. Section 2 makes the laws necessary; the latter identity persists by necessity of identity.

The order here is necessary inclusion. In particular X\le Y is equivalent in C to the literal Boolean identity X\land Y=X. Current coextension is never substituted for this identity. We have made the laws of a normalized operation necessary, not the current atomicity of Q.

4. The character decides every future predicate

Fix any accessible point v and any Z^\tau available there. Its value J_QZ is an endpoint. That endpoint identity persists along every continuation, as does J_QQ=\top.

Suppose first J_QZ=\top. At any later point u, let \bar a be a tuple satisfying Q\bar a. Self-atomhood makes Q an ordinary atom there, so

Q\le Z\quad\text{or}\quad Q\le\neg Z.

The second alternative would give the literal identity Q\land\neg Z=Q. Applying J_Q and its necessary Boolean laws yields

\top=J_QQ=J_Q(Q\land\neg Z) =J_QQ\land J_Q(\neg Z)=\top\land\neg J_QZ,

contradicting J_QZ=\top. Thus Z\bar a. Since u and its full-domain tuple were arbitrary, Q\le Z already at v.

If instead J_QZ=\bot, the same calculation using Q\land Z=Q rules out Q\le Z at every later instance of Q. The atom’s other decision gives \neg Z\bar a, and therefore Q\le\neg Z at v.

We have proved

\Box\forall Z^\tau\,(Q\le Z\lor Q\le\neg Z).

Together with Q\ne\bot, already part of self-atomhood, this is \operatorname{SAtom}_\tau(Q). The empty argument tuple gives \tau=t. Since the original Q was any current self-atom, the claimed schema follows.

The proof only uses atomicity of Q at later points where it is instantiated. It neither asserts atomicity at an unoccupied point nor requires a preimage of the future predicate Z.

5. Exact boundary and sources

The result leaves the quantifier over Q outside the box. It does not make every future self-atom strong from current Gallin alone, and it does not make arbitrary ordinary atoms strong. The equivalent premise ND + Strong Actuality follows through the existing Gallin characterization.
