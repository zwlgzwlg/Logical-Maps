ND (type t) and Inextensible Comprehension imply Weakly Rigid Power

Proof by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 22 October 2026; rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 24 October 2026.

Theorem. Let \tau=\bar\sigma t be a relational type. In C, ND at t and Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t imply Weakly Rigid Power at \tau: if F^\tau is weakly rigid, so is

P_F:=\lambda Y^\tau\, .\,\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(Y)\land Y\le F .

Both premises are unboxed. BF, Atomicity, Boolean Completeness and Rigid Comprehension are not used.

Overview. ND at t makes weakly rigid relations necessarily separated: instances that are distinct anywhere are necessarily distinct (Lemma 3). A separated persistent relation with an inextensible coextension is rigid (Lemma 2). Using this, F is rigid, and so is R_F:=\lambda Y\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\land Y\le F (Lemma 5). Finally, separation of F makes every weakly rigid subrelation of F, at any world, rigid there (Lemma 6), so P_F=R_F.

Notation

Recall (Background) that Y\le Z is \Box\forall\bar x\, .\,Y[\bar x]\to Z[\bar x], that \operatorname{Inextensible} and \operatorname{Rigid} are the necessitations of weak inextensibility and weak rigidity, and that a weakly rigid relation is persistent. Put

\operatorname{NecSeparated}(A):=\Box\forall\bar a\,\bar b\, .\, A[\bar a]\land A[\bar b]\land\bar a\ne\bar b\to\Box(\bar a\ne\bar b),

and for a tuple \bar a let \delta_{\bar a}:=\lambda\bar b\, .\,\bar b=\bar a be its haecceity. Haecceities are rigid in C.

Lemma 1: two consequences of ND at t

Assume ND at t.

  1. For entities a,b of any type, a\ne b\to\Box(a\ne b). For if a\ne b, then (a=b)\ne\top, so \Box((a=b)\ne\top); and a=b at some world would give \Box(a=b), that is (a=b)=\top, there.

  2. If \Box(q\to\Box q) and \neg q, then \Box\neg q. For q\ne\top, so \Box(q\ne\top), that is \Box\neg\Box q; with \Box(q\to\Box q) this gives \Box\neg q.

Lemma 2: separated persistent relations with an inextensible coextension are rigid

Assume ND at t. Let A be persistent with \operatorname{NecSeparated}(A), and let G be inextensible and coextensive with A. Then A=G, so A is rigid.

Proof. Each instance of G is an instance of A, hence necessarily one; weak inextensibility of G gives G\le A.

Let q:=\exists\bar a\, .\,A[\bar a]\land\neg G[\bar a]. We show \Box(q\to\Box q). At any world, let A[\bar a]\land\neg G[\bar a]. Each instance \bar b of G there is an instance of A with \bar b\ne\bar a, so \Box(\bar b\ne\bar a). Weak inextensibility of G there gives G\le\lambda\bar b\, .\,\bar b\ne\bar a, so \Box\neg G[\bar a]; and \Box A[\bar a] by persistence. So \Box q.

As A and G are coextensive, \neg q. By Lemma 1(b), \Box\neg q, which says A\le G. \square

Lemma 3: weakly rigid relations are necessarily separated

Assume ND at t. If A is weakly rigid, then \operatorname{NecSeparated}(A).

Proof. First, the product K:=\lambda\bar a\,\bar b\, .\,A[\bar a]\land A[\bar b] is weakly rigid. Weak persistence is clear. For weak inextensibility, suppose

\forall\bar a\,\bar b\, .\,A[\bar a]\land A[\bar b]\to\Box Z[\bar a,\bar b].

For each instance \bar a of A, weak inextensibility of A applied to \lambda\bar b\, .\,Z[\bar a,\bar b] gives \Box\forall\bar b\, .\,A[\bar b]\to Z[\bar a,\bar b]. Weak inextensibility of A applied to \lambda\bar a\, .\,\forall\bar b\, .\,A[\bar b]\to Z[\bar a,\bar b] then gives K\le Z.

Now let

\operatorname{Settled}(\bar a,\bar b):=\Box(\bar a=\bar b)\lor\Box(\bar a\ne\bar b).

Every pair of instances of A satisfies it, by necessity of identity and Lemma 1(a), and so necessarily satisfies it, by 4. Weak inextensibility of K gives K\le\operatorname{Settled}. At any world, distinct instances \bar a,\bar b of A therefore satisfy \Box(\bar a\ne\bar b), since \Box(\bar a=\bar b) would give \bar a=\bar b. \square

Lemma 4: Inextensible Comprehension descends

It is a theorem of C that Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t implies it at \tau.

Proof. Let X^\tau. By Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t, let G be inextensible and coextensive with \lambda Y\, .\,\exists\bar a\, .\,X[\bar a]\land Y=\delta_{\bar a}, and put

U:=\lambda\bar a\, .\,\exists Y\, .\,GY\land Y[\bar a].

U is coextensive with X. Each instance of G is a haecceity, so rigid, so necessarily rigid; weak inextensibility of G gives G\le\lambda Y\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y).

U is inextensible. At any world, suppose \forall\bar a\, .\,U[\bar a]\to\Box Z[\bar a]. For each Y with GY, Y is rigid there and each instance of Y is an instance of U, so weak inextensibility of Y gives Y\le Z. Weak inextensibility of G, applied to \lambda Y\, .\,\forall\bar a\, .\,Y[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a], gives \Box\forall Y\, .\,GY\to\forall\bar a\, .\,(Y[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a]), that is, U\le Z. \square

Corollary (Tame Rigidity at \tau). Under ND at t and Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t, every weakly rigid F^\tau is rigid: it is persistent, necessarily separated by Lemma 3, and has an inextensible coextension by Lemma 4, so Lemma 2 applies.

Lemma 5: the rigid subrelations of a rigid F form a rigid property

Assume ND at t and Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t, and let F^\tau be rigid. Then R_F:=\lambda Y\, .\,\operatorname{Rigid}(Y)\land Y\le F is rigid.

Proof. R_F is persistent, since both conjuncts are necessary when true. We show \operatorname{NecSeparated}(R_F); Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t and Lemma 2 then make R_F rigid.

At any world, let X\ne Y both satisfy R_F. Being weakly rigid and distinct, they are not coextensive (Sun’s lemma: coextensive weakly rigid relations are identical), say X[\bar a]\land\neg Y[\bar a]. Each instance \bar b of Y is an instance of F with \bar b\ne\bar a, and \bar a is an instance of F, so by \operatorname{NecSeparated}(F) (Lemma 3), \Box(\bar b\ne\bar a). Weak inextensibility of Y gives \Box\neg Y[\bar a], and persistence of X gives \Box X[\bar a]. So \Box(X\ne Y). \square

Lemma 6: weakly rigid subrelations of F are rigid, at every world

Let F^\tau be rigid with \operatorname{NecSeparated}(F). Then

\Box\forall X^\tau\, .\,\operatorname{WeaklyRigid}(X)\land X\le F\to\operatorname{Rigid}(X).

Proof. At any world v, let X be weakly rigid with X\le F. Put

\operatorname{Settled}_X(\bar a):=\Box X[\bar a]\lor\Box\neg X[\bar a].

Each instance \bar a of F at v satisfies \operatorname{Settled}_X: if X[\bar a], by weak persistence of X; if not, each instance \bar b of X is an instance of F with \bar b\ne\bar a, so \Box(\bar b\ne\bar a), and weak inextensibility of X gives \Box\neg X[\bar a]. By 4 this holds necessarily, so weak inextensibility of F gives

F\le\operatorname{Settled}_X .

Now let u be any world accessible from v, and suppose \forall\bar a\, .\,X[\bar a]\to\Box Z[\bar a] at u. Each instance \bar a of F at u satisfies \operatorname{Settled}_X: if \Box X[\bar a], then X[\bar a] and \Box Z[\bar a]; if \Box\neg X[\bar a], trivially; either way \Box(X[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a]). Weak inextensibility of F at u gives

F\le\lambda\bar a\, .\,X[\bar a]\to Z[\bar a],

and with X\le F, X\le Z. So X is weakly inextensible at every world accessible from v, and persistent, hence rigid at v. \square

Proof of the theorem

Let F^\tau be weakly rigid. By the Corollary it is rigid, and by Lemma 3 it is necessarily separated. By Lemma 6 and T, necessarily P_FY\leftrightarrow R_FY for every Y, so P_F=R_F, which is rigid by Lemma 5 and hence weakly rigid. \square

Remarks

  1. The premises used are ND at t and Inextensible Comprehension at \tau t. The Corollary is Tame Rigidity at \tau from the same premises, and Lemma 5 is Rigid Power at \tau.
  2. Over ND and Boolean Completeness, the map already derives Inextensible Comprehension from Weakly Rigid Power, Tame Rigidity and Rigid Comprehension, and these from one another, except for the step to Weakly Rigid Power. With this result the four are equivalent over ND and Boolean Completeness.
