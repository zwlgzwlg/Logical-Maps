Cofinite entrance to the full idempotent action

Construction and proofs by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, in the research note Strong worlds do not control modal domains of 26 October 2026, §§2 and 4–5. Checked and rewritten for the map by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, 28 October 2026.

At the entrance r these hold:

  • Strong Atomicity at every relational type;
  • the Barcan Formula at every type;
  • □Atomicity.

These fail:

  • □Strong Atomicity at t;
  • □BF at t.

So the Barcan Formula, even at every type, does not make Strong Atomicity necessary.

The model

The target s. Its arrows s\to s are 1 and an idempotent k\ne1. It carries the full action-model domains over one individual. An entity of relational type is a pair of extensions (U,V), at 1 and at k, and k\cdot(U,V)=(V,V).

The entrance r. It has only its identity, and arrows h_n:r\to s (n\in\mathbb N) and b:r\to s, with k\circ h_n=b and k\circ b=b. An entity of relational type at r is:

  • an arbitrary current extension, together with
  • target entities d_n (its transports along the h_n) and d_\infty (along b),

subject to

k\cdot d_n=d_\infty,\qquad k\cdot d_\infty=d_\infty,\qquad d_n=d_\infty\text{ for all but finitely many }n .

Write d_\infty=(V,V) and d_n=(U_n,V). Then U_n=V for all but finitely many n.

Evaluation point: 1_r. The constants of Σ denote top elements.

A model of C

Current extensions are unrestricted, and logical constants have constant target parts. So, as in the stationary entrance, it is enough to check the target parts of a term with parameters.

  • Cofiniteness. Let X be the union of the parameters’ finitely many exceptional indices. Outside X, the transported parameters at h_n and at b coincide, so the term’s denotations there coincide.
  • Fixed by k. At b the parameters are fixed by k, so by naturality the term’s denotation there is fixed by k.

So the domains are closed under the terms of C.

Strong Atomicity at r, and □Atomicity

At r, at every relational type, two kinds of singleton are admitted:

  • one current tuple, with all target parts 0;
  • one tuple \bar a at one arrow h_n, with d_n:=(\{\bar a\},\varnothing) and every other target part 0. This is allowed, since k\cdot(\{\bar a\},\varnothing)=0.

Every nonzero entity at r contains one of them. Consider where it is nonempty:

  • in its current extension, or at an exceptional h_n: immediate;
  • in V, its extension at b: then pick \bar a\in V and an n outside its exceptions, so that \bar a\in U_n.

There is no atom at b itself.

They are strong atoms. A current singleton transports to 0 everywhere. The singleton at (h_n,\bar a) transports to (\{\bar a\},\varnothing) along h_n and to 0 elsewhere. And (\{\bar a\},\varnothing) is an atom of the full target that k sends to 0, so it necessarily decides everything.

Hence Strong Atomicity holds at r. The full target is atomic at every type, so □Atomicity holds.

□Strong Atomicity fails at t

At s, the proposition \{k\} is an atom, but k\cdot\{k\}=\{1,k\}, which the proposition \{1\} splits at k. So \{k\} is not a strong atom. Being an atom, it has no other possible proposition below it. So Strong Atomicity fails at t at s, and □Strong Atomicity at t fails at r.

The Barcan Formula at r, and its failure at s

Transport along each h_n is onto. At e this is trivial. At a relational type, given a target entity d, the entity with d_n:=d, every other d_m:=k\cdot d and d_\infty:=k\cdot d is admitted, by idempotence, and transports to d along h_n.

BF holds at r, at every type. Let F at r be such that every entity necessarily has F.

  • At each h_n, every target entity is the transport of some entity at r, so the extension of F there is the whole domain.
  • At b, the extension of F is its extension at any h_n outside F’s finitely many exceptions, so it is everything too.
  • At r itself, F holds of everything by T.

So necessarily everything has F. The arrow b is not onto, but no admitted predicate at r can detect this.

BF fails at t at s. Let X hold at 1 of all four propositions, and at k of \bot and \top. Since k transports every proposition to \bot or \top, every proposition necessarily has X. But at k the proposition \{1\} lacks it. So □BF at t fails at r.

A target predicate can be lifted along any one h_n. But its lift need not satisfy the Barcan antecedent along the other arrows, so this failure does not contradict BF at r.
