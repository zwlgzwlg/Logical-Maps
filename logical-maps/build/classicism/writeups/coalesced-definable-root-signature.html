Coalesced sum: exits into pointed components for Σ, definable root

Cian Dorr gave the definable root on 20 October 2026 (coalesced-definable-root). GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), prompted by Cian Dorr, adapted it on 1 November 2026 to a signature interpreted in the components, with a freely chosen root extension for each constant. Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, wrote this description, the verification and the injectivity argument on 21–22 November 2026. Cian Dorr has checked the construction against the injectivity argument. No Lean verification is claimed.

The construction

Let \Sigma^{+} be \Sigma, with an individual constant d added if \Sigma has none. C(\Sigma^{+}) is conservative over C(\Sigma).

  • Components. A set A of pointed action models for \mathcal L(\Sigma^{+}), with disjoint objects and arrows, complete for C(\Sigma^{+}): every closed sentence consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}) is true at the distinguished object V_{\mathfrak A} of some \mathfrak A\in A.
  • Arrows. Add an object W_0. Its arrows out are its identity and, for each \mathfrak A and each arrow k of \mathfrak A out of V_{\mathfrak A}, an arrow k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}, where \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}:W_0\to V_{\mathfrak A} is the exit. No arrow enters W_0 except its identity.
  • Composition. k'\circ(k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}):=(k'k)\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A}.
  • Individuals. The individuals at W_0 are the individual constants of \Sigma^{+}; \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} sends each to its denotation at V_{\mathfrak A}. (Closed terms of type e are individual constants, no type ending in e being admitted.)
  • Domains at W_0. By recursion on types. An entity of type \bar\tau t is an intension F over the arrows out of W_0 such that, for some closed term u of \mathcal L(\Sigma^{+}) of that type, the value of F at k\circ\varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is the extension at k of the entity u denotes in \mathfrak A, for every \mathfrak A and k. Its value at the identity is any set E of tuples of root entities of the types \bar\tau. Write F=(E,u), and call E the root extension and u a descriptor.
  • Evaluation point. 1_{W_0}.
  • Interpretation of \Sigma. An individual constant denotes itself. A relational constant c denotes (E_c,c) for any chosen E_c. Its transport along \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is \mathfrak A’s own interpretation of c.

The differences from coalesced-definable-root: the components interpret the signature and are complete for C(\Sigma^{+}), not only for C; one exit into each pointed component replaces the arrows for every interpretation of d into every object; and the constants of \Sigma are not at the top but keep their own descriptors, with root extensions left to choice.

Descriptors. (E,u) and (E',u') are the same entity iff E=E' and C(\Sigma^{+}) proves u=u'. If u=u' is unprovable, u\ne u' is consistent, so it holds at some V_{\mathfrak A} and the component parts differ. So each root entity has a descriptor that is well defined up to provable identity.

Landing. The transport of (E,u) along \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} is the entity u denotes at V_{\mathfrak A}, so it lies in the component domain.

It is a model of C

As for coalesced-definable-root. Let s be a term with parameters (\bar E,\bar u) from the root domains. At an arrow into \mathfrak A its denotation is computed in \mathfrak A from the transported parameters, which are the denotations of \bar u; so it is the denotation of the closed term s[\bar u], uniformly in the arrow. At the identity its extension is the set of tuples of root entities satisfying it at W_0, and nothing constrains that set. So s denotes the root entity (E',s[\bar u]) for some E'. The axioms of C hold at W_0 because denotations are computed extensionally on intensions, and the other worlds are worlds of the components. Two consequences are used below.

  1. A closed term u denotes an entity with descriptor u, whatever the root extensions E_c are. Only its root extension depends on them.
  2. An application (E,u) of type \sigma t to a root entity x with descriptor v is the root proposition with descriptor uv, true at the identity iff x\in E.

Maximalism (signature Σ) and Possibility+ (signature Σ)

A closed sentence holds at \varepsilon_{\mathfrak A} iff it holds at V_{\mathfrak A}. Every closed sentence consistent with C(\Sigma) is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), so it holds at some V_{\mathfrak A} and is possible at W_0. A closed identity of \mathcal L(\Sigma) is true at W_0 iff C(\Sigma) proves it, by the descriptor criterion and conservativity. For Possibility+, distinct root individuals distinct from the constants in P are fresh distinct constants, and substituting them for the free variables of a consistent P gives a consistent sentence.

This holds for every choice of the root extensions E_c. The map (E,u)\mapsto[u] is a homomorphism from the root domains onto the closed terms modulo provable identity.

Constants are injective on closed terms

Proposition. Let c be a constant of \Sigma of type \bar\beta\,\sigma\,\bar\beta'\,t, and let A, B be closed terms of \mathcal L(\Sigma) of type \sigma, which may contain c. If

C(\Sigma)\vdash\forall\bar w\,\bar w'\,.\,c[\bar w,A,\bar w']\leftrightarrow c[\bar w,B,\bar w'],

then C(\Sigma)\vdash A=B. In particular, for a constant Q of type \sigma t: C(\Sigma)\vdash QA\leftrightarrow QB only if C(\Sigma)\vdash A=B.

Proof. Suppose C(\Sigma) does not prove A=B; then neither does C(\Sigma^{+}). Take the model above with

E_c:=\{(\bar w,x,\bar w'):\text{the descriptor of }x\text{ is provably identical to }A\}.

This is specified before any denotation is computed, and by consequence 1 the denotation of A has descriptor A and that of B has descriptor B whatever E_c is. So at W_0, c[\bar w,A,\bar w'] is true for all root \bar w,\bar w' and c[\bar w,B,\bar w'] for none. Every root domain is nonempty, so the displayed sentence is false at W_0, which is a model of C(\Sigma). \square

When \sigma=e the statement is trivial, closed terms of type e being constants.

Corollary. Maximalism (signature Σ) proves, for closed A, B and a constant Q of type \sigma\tau with \tau\ne e: QA=QB\to A=B. (If QA=QB is true it is provable, hence so is the displayed biconditional for the constant Q, hence A=B.)

The same choice of root extension shows more generally that a closed term built from c by filling its other arguments is injective on closed terms. This is the property of constants that the Russell–Myhill argument in strong-possibility-signature-r-inconsistent needs.

Other verdicts

  • Actuality. (\text{true},\bot) is true at the identity and nowhere else, and no arrow leads back to W_0; the group’s argument applies.
  • Relational Choice holds by the group’s argument, being evaluated at the identity, where root extensions are unconstrained.
  • Boxed principles. Every pure sentence consistent with C is possible at W_0, so the group’s arguments from that fact apply.
  • The map derives the rest from these and from Maximalism (signature Σ): the failure of ND (directly: (\text{false},\bot) and (\text{true},\bot) are distinct at W_0 and identical along every exit), of Atomicity, and of Strong Possibility (signature Σ); Well-Ordering and Transversal Choice; and the other signature principles.

The root algebra (23 November 2026)

Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, added this section and the argument root-algebra on 23 November 2026.

Let L be the Lindenbaum algebra of C(\Sigma^{+}). A root proposition is (e,[v]) with e its truth value at the identity and v a closed sentence. A closed sentence fails at some world seen from W_0 through an exit iff its negation is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), since by necessitation \vdash v iff \vdash\Box v. So at W_0

\Box(e,v)\iff e=1\text{ and }\vdash v,\qquad (e,v)\le(f,w)\iff e\le f\text{ and }\vdash v\to w .

The propositions at W_0 form 2\times L, and every set of them is the root extension of a root property. None of this depends on A or on the E_c.

Boolean Completeness (type t) fails. A greatest lower bound at W_0 is one in 2\times L, so it is enough that L is not complete.

  • \Sigma^{+} countable. Let \varphi_n say that there are exactly n individuals (n\ge1). Each is consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}), true in the full one-world model with n individuals under any interpretation of the constants, and they are pairwise inconsistent. If L were complete, S\mapsto\bigvee\{\varphi_n:n\in S\} would be injective on \mathcal P(\omega), because \varphi_m\wedge\bigvee_{n\in S}\varphi_n=\bigvee_{n\in S}(\varphi_m\wedge\varphi_n) is \varphi_m or \bot as m\in S or not. But L is countable.
  • \Sigma^{+} with infinitely many constants. Suppose first there are infinitely many relational constants c_0,c_1,\ldots, of types \bar\rho_i t. Fix closed terms \bar a_i of types \bar\rho_i not containing them (d at type e, \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot otherwise), and put P_i:=c_i(\bar a_i) and a_i:=P_i\land\bigwedge_{j<i}\neg P_j. These are consistent and pairwise inconsistent. Let w be an upper bound of the a_i with i even, and suppose w mentions only c_j with j<N. For even i>N, substituting \lambda\bar x\, .\,\top for c_i and \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot for c_j (N\le j<i) in a proof of a_i\to w gives \vdash \bigwedge_{j<N}\neg P_j\to w. So a_M\le w for every odd M>N, and w\land\neg a_M is a strictly smaller upper bound. With infinitely many individual constants instead, the same argument works with a_i:=(c_i=c_0)\land\bigwedge_{1\le j<i}c_j\ne c_0 (i\ge1): substitute c_0 for c_i and generalize on the fresh c_j to get \vdash\bigwedge_{1\le j<N}c_j\ne c_0\land\exists y\, .\,y\ne c_0\to w, which every a_M with odd M>N\ge2 entails.

Every infinite signature has infinitely many constants, so the two cases cover every \Sigma. Neither uses choice.

Self-atoms need not be atoms or strong atoms (type t). Let \mathrm F be the Fregean Axiom \forall p\, .\,p=\top\lor p=\bot, and s:=(0,\mathrm F).

  • s is possible at W_0, \mathrm F being consistent with C(\Sigma^{+}) (the full one-world model with one individual).
  • C proves \mathrm F\to\operatorname{Atom}(\mathrm F): under \mathrm F the true proposition \mathrm F is \top, and the only propositions are \top and \bot. So s\to\operatorname{Atom}(s) holds at every exit, and trivially at the identity, where s is false. So s is a self-atom.
  • (0,\mathrm F\land\forall x\forall y\, .\,x=y) lies strictly between \bot and s: \mathrm F holds in the full one-world models with one and with two individuals. So s is not an atom.
  • A strong atom (0,v) at W_0 must have, for each closed w, \vdash v\to w or \vdash v\to\neg w, so v must be an atom of L. \mathrm F is not, by the previous point. So s is not a strong atom.

The map derives the versions at all relational types.

Mathematical Possibilism fails for every signature

The Cantor regular-open obstruction for the pure definable root was recorded by OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Andrew Bacon, on 16 November 2026. The arbitrary-signature argument here is by OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026. Work in external ZFC.

No infinite principal interval is complete. Suppose [0,b] is an infinite complete principal interval in L. Choose countably many disjoint nonzero elements a_n\le b. Such a sequence exists in any infinite Boolean algebra in ZFC: choose countably many atoms if there are infinitely many, or repeatedly split a nonzero element in the atomless remainder. Choose closed representatives for b and for all the a_n. Together these use a countable sublanguage. For S\subseteq\mathbb N let s represent the join of the a_n for n\in S in the interval. Replace the finitely many constants of s outside the sublanguage by fresh variables of their respective types, and universally quantify those variables to obtain s_0. For every n\in S, C proves a_n\to s and, by generalization on the fresh constants, a_n\to s_0. As s_0\le s\le b, its being an upper bound implies [s_0]=[s]. Thus every such join has a representative in the same countable language. Distinct S have distinct joins: for m\notin S, b\land\neg a_m is an upper bound. There are only countably many representatives and continuum many S, a contradiction. An infinite principal interval of 2\times L has an infinite L-factor; completeness of the product interval would make this factor complete.

The small complete algebra. At every root relation type, an arbitrary set of current tuples is allowed as the identity row, independently of its old descriptor (take the everywhere-bottom term). Consequently every current subset and every graph between the fixed current carriers used below is admitted, including at the types of the order, subcollections and injections. The ordinal benchmark H=\operatorname{Ordinal}_e contains \alpha_n=\operatorname{OrdSuc}^n(\operatorname{OrdZero}) for every standard n. These are distinct root intensions. On a finite linear order, \alpha_n accepts precisely orders of length at least n, by the definition of successor on restricted initial segments. For m<n the consistent pure sentence asserting such an order of length m with \alpha_m true and \alpha_n false is realized at a component exit (for m=0 take the empty field). Equality at the root would persist at this exit. Thus |H|\ge\aleph_0.

Let \rho be the ordinal-carrier type. At \rho t the subsets of \{\alpha_n:n\in\mathbb N\}, with empty old descriptor, provide 2^{\aleph_0} distinct carrier elements. On elements of this type code the complete algebra of regular opens of Cantor space, with its order, by a root subset and a root graph. Its size is continuum: a countable clopen basis bounds the number of open sets above, and Boolean joins of subfamilies of a countably infinite disjoint family of nonzero clopens give continuum many distinct regular opens. Full current rows make this an internal complete Boolean algebra for the actual order and all actual subcollections.

Its carrier B is small in the revised sense. If H<Y\subseteq B, take a countably infinite A\subseteq Y. Then |A|<|Y| and the 2^{\aleph_0} external subsets of A are all admitted current subcollections. The distinct codes required by \operatorname{Pow}(C,A) would force |C|\ge 2^{\aleph_0}\ge |Y|, contradicting C<Y. Hence no such Y is power-closed. An actual relational order isomorphism of B to an entailment interval would make that interval infinite and complete, excluded above. This refutes the revised [[forcing-possibilism]] at a single specified carrier type, for every set-sized signature.

Left open

The model record lists only verdicts that hold for every \Sigma and every choice of the E_c, and many of these go different ways for different \Sigma. The facts below were found on 23 November 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), prompted by Cian Dorr, and are not recorded as verdicts.

Fresh constants. The axioms of C(\Sigma^{+}) are schemata, so a constant occurring in no premise can be generalized on: if \vdash\varphi(c) with c not in \varphi, then \vdash\forall x\, .\,\varphi(x). This is what makes the verdicts turn on how many constants \Sigma has.

Principle Fails when Holds when
BF (all types, type t, relational types), Functionality, Tractarianism \Sigma^{+} has finitely many individual constants (BF at type e); or finitely many relational constants, at every relational input type \Sigma^{+} has infinitely many constants of every type
Infinity Schema (type e), Axiom of Infinity (type e) finitely many individual constants infinitely many individual constants (at least the Schema)
Atoms are strong atoms (type t; relational types) \Sigma=\emptyset infinitely many relational constants
  • BF, Functionality, Tractarianism. These are equivalent on the map. For a root X=(E,u) of type \sigma t, \forall x\, .\,\Box Xx holds at W_0 iff every root entity is in E and \vdash uw for every closed w of type \sigma; \Box\forall x\, .\,Xx holds iff, in addition, \vdash\forall x\, .\,ux. With a constant of type \sigma not in u the second follows by generalization. At type e with only c_1,\ldots,c_n, \Diamond\exists x\, .\,x\ne c_1\land\cdots\land x\ne c_n holds and no root individual is possibly such. At type t with \Sigma=\emptyset, the argument barcan of coalesced-definable-root applies unchanged: a closed sentence of \mathcal L(\{d\}) is not contingent at a world with C5, Atomicity and one individual.
  • Infinity at type e. The root individuals are the individual constants of \Sigma^{+}, and distinct constants are distinct at W_0.
  • Atoms are strong atoms. With infinitely many relational constants, L is atomless: if v is consistent and c is a constant not in v, then v\land c(\bar a) and v\land\neg c(\bar a) are consistent (substitute \lambda\bar x\, .\,\top or \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot for c). Then the atoms at a relational type \bar\sigma t at W_0 are the entities with a single tuple as root extension and descriptor \lambda\bar x\, .\,\bot (the fresh-constant splitting works at every relational type). These are self-atoms and strong atoms, being \bot along every exit. With \Sigma=\emptyset, v:=\mathrm F\land\forall x\forall y\, .\,x=y is an atom of L, so (0,v) is an atom at W_0. But C does not prove \forall q\, .\,v\le q\lor v\le\neg q. It fails at the root of the coalesced sum of two copies of the one-point Fregean model, where v is the root proposition false at the root and true in both components, and q is true in just one component.

The number of relational constants and the two-world cell

Added 26 November 2026 by OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi. The two-world cell and its pointed completeness were proved in the October work used in [[strong-possibility-incompatible-with-barcan-t]].

Atoms are self-atoms at the root if and only if there are infinitely many relational constants. This holds for both the type-t bridge and the schema over relational types. With infinitely many such constants the preceding fresh-constant argument shows that every root atom is strong. Suppose instead that there are only finitely many relational constants. The number of individual constants can be arbitrary. Let B say that \Box\mathrm{ND}_t holds and there are exactly one individual and four propositions, as in the proof cited above. Form the closed sentence

\beta=B\land\bigwedge_{\text{relational }c\in\Sigma}(c=\bot_{\operatorname{type}(c)}).

The full two-world S5 model with one individual and these interpretations realizes \beta. C proves B\to\Box B by step (1) of the cited proof, so \beta\to\Box\beta by NI. The complete finite structure in step (2) of that proof decides every closed sentence of \mathcal L(\Sigma^+) under \beta. Indeed all relational names in such a sentence have their fixed bottom values throughout the cone. All individual names denote the sole individual there and can be replaced by a single individual variable. The pointed finite-structure argument applies to that variable as well. Thus [\beta] is an atom of L, and (0,[\beta]) is an atom in 2\times L. At an exit where \beta holds, that atom transports to \beta=\top. It is true there, and a four-element Boolean algebra has a proper nonzero element below \top. Hence this root atom is not a self-atom, refuting the scheme already at type t.

BF fails at each relational input type when there are finitely many relational constants. Consider an input type \tau=\bar\sigma t. Fix a closed tuple \bar d of argument terms in \mathcal L(\Sigma^+), with any individual name at e and bottom at other types. For arity zero take the empty tuple. Give a root property empty current extension and descriptor

\lambda Y^\tau\, .\, \beta\land Y[\bar d]\ne\top\land Y[\bar d]\ne\bot .

For any root Y its application to \bar d projects to a closed proposition. That proposition has a fixed truth value throughout all \beta-points by completeness. Since \beta persists, at any \beta-point it equals one of the two endpoints. Consequently no root Y can possibly satisfy the displayed property. At a \beta exit choose a proposition strictly between the endpoints; the constant relation with this value satisfies the property there. This refutes \Diamond\exists Y\, .\,HY\to\exists Y\, .\,\Diamond HY. It covers signatures with arbitrarily many individual names.

Conversely, infinitely many relational constants suffice for BF at type t, even if there are only finitely many of each type. Suppose X=(E,v) has \forall p\, .\,\Box Xp at the root. Then E is full and C proves v(s) for every closed proposition s of \mathcal L(\Sigma^+), by completeness at the exits. Choose a relational constant c absent from v, of type \bar\tau t. Use the closed proposition s=\forall\bar y\, .\,c[\bar y] (just c at arity zero). In the resulting theorem substitute \lambda\bar y\, .\,p for c, where p is fresh. Quantification of an input-independent proposition over nonempty sorts gives that proposition. Thus C proves v(p) uniformly and hence \forall p\, .\,v(p). This and the full identity row give \Box\forall p\, .\,Xp at the root. Together with the preceding proof, \mathrm{BF}_t holds here exactly when the signature has infinitely many relational constants. Freshness concerns relation symbols; the construction already supplies an auxiliary individual name if it needs one.

Every infinite signature refutes Tame Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Power in this model. When the relational constants are infinite, actual \mathrm{BF}_t makes the universal property of propositions weakly rigid: weak persistence is immediate and its weak inextensibility is exactly \mathrm{BF}_t. But some component fails \mathrm{BF}_t, because its failure is C-consistent, also in an expansion by freely interpreted constants. Thus this property is not rigid. This also follows from [[tame-rigidity-and-bf-t-imply-necessary-bf-t]] and pure Maximalism. If the relational constants are finite and the signature is infinite, its individual constants are infinite and the earlier individual-sort argument applies. In both cases apply [[weakly-rigid-power-implies-tame-rigidity]]. This leaves the finite signatures for the all-type weak power question in this family.

Strong Possibility in these signatures. The denotation of \beta itself at the root is (0,[\beta]). Its current bit is zero because the root has infinitely many propositions, as witnessed by the pairwise incompatible, consistent individual-cardinality sentences. Thus \operatorname{Atom}_t(\beta) is consistent with Maximalism in this very signature. It is a universal Boolean combination of identities, so the reflection lemma of [[strong-possibility-and-strong-actuality-incompatible]] applies in the fixed language. Strong Possibility (signature \Sigma) therefore gives \operatorname{Atom}_t(\beta). At every \beta-point \beta equals top in the four-element algebra, which proves in C(\Sigma) that \beta is not a self-atom. Hence Strong Possibility (signature \Sigma) is incompatible with Atoms are self-atoms (type t) under the finite-relational-constant hypothesis. The result for the pure language is recorded at [[strong-possibility-incompatible-with-atoms-are-self-atoms-t]].

Power at the singleton-individual root

OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026.

Suppose \Sigma^+ has exactly one individual name d, and let F be weakly rigid at the root. Separate its individual arguments \bar x from its relational arguments \bar z and write D(\bar x) for \bigwedge_i(x_i=d). Every actual member necessarily satisfies D. By weak inextensibility F\le D, so throughout the cone F equals D(\bar x)\land G(\bar z), where G(\bar z)=F(d,\ldots,d,\bar z). This applies also to infinite extensions and does not assume BF.

The slice s(X)(\bar z)=X(d,\ldots,d,\bar z) and the extension t(Y)(\bar x,\bar z)=D(\bar x)\land Y(\bar z) are inverse, order-preserving term operations on the subrelations of F and G at every future point. Weak persistence transfers by necessity of identity. For weak inextensibility of a slice, pull a test Q back to the tuple test (\bar x,\bar z)\mapsto Q(\bar z). For an extension, evaluate a tuple test at (d,\ldots,d,\bar z) and then substitute equal individuals. These arguments also transfer full rigidity by applying them throughout the cone. Thus each canonical power at one bound is the direct image of its counterpart at the other under the corresponding term operation. Direct images preserve rigidity ([[rigid-direct-image]]) and also weak rigidity: for the latter, lift an actual image member for persistence, and for inextensibility pull a necessary test back to the domain. Consequently the two power questions here reduce exactly to arities with only relational arguments, including the empty tuple. The case of a relation on individual arguments alone reduces to a proposition and satisfies both power principles. For any arity the separate [[internally-finite-rigid-power|Frege-finite bound lemma]] applies.

Finite subrelations detected by root tests

OpenAI Codex (GPT-6), prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026.

Suppose F is weakly rigid and a tester W holds necessarily of each current rigid X\le F. At any component point W holds of every weakly rigid Z\le F having externally finite current extension. The tuples of Z may be unnamed at that point. The general [[finite-subrelations-of-rigid-bounds|finite-subrelation lemma]] applies in any C model, including to transported nonstandard counts. The ground-term argument below also gives a finite-tuple realization property specific to this construction. The conclusion applies also to a tester of all current weakly rigid subrelations, which include the rigid ones.

Let u,w be the descriptors of F,W, and let S consist of the descriptor tuples of actual members of F. By persistence \vdash u(\bar s) for each \bar s\in S. The root tester with full actual row and arbitrary descriptor v shows by weak inextensibility and completeness of the old components that (\forall\bar s\in S\;\vdash v(\bar s)) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \vdash\forall\bar x\,(u(\bar x)\to v(\bar x)). Consequently any consistent formula \exists\bar x_1\cdots\bar x_m\, (\bigwedge_i u(\bar x_i)\land A(\bar x_1,\ldots,\bar x_m)) can be realized with \bar x_i equal to some \bar s_i\in S, possibly at a different point. For the first tuple take v(\bar x_1) to negate the existential condition on the remaining tuples. The displayed implication supplies an \bar s_1 for which that remaining condition is consistent. Substitute it before treating the next tuple. All provability and consistency tests here are over the fixed descriptor language; A can contain arbitrary typed quantifiers and modalities.

At a point with an externally finite weakly rigid Z, let \bar x_1,\ldots,\bar x_m enumerate its current members. As an entity Z equals the union H(\bar x_1,\ldots,\bar x_m) of their tuple-haecceities. Weak inextensibility tested on that union gives one inclusion and weak persistence the other. If W failed there, take A=\neg w(H(\bar x_1,\ldots,\bar x_m)) above. Choose original F-members with the resulting descriptors \bar s_i. Their finite haecceity union is rigid, entails F by persistence and necessarily passes W. On the old components this gives \vdash w(H(\bar s_1,\ldots,\bar s_m)), contradicting the retained consistency. For m=0 use the actual bottom subrelation directly.

Thus even on an infinite Z no test detectable by finitely many actual membership observations can witness a failure. In particular this covers testers of the form \forall\bar y\,\theta(Z,\bar y) where \theta is a Boolean combination of finitely many applications of Z to terms independent of Z and arbitrary formulas independent of Z. Fix witnesses \bar y to failure and take the haecceity union of the observed tuples belonging to Z. This rigid subrelation entails Z by weak persistence, has the same observed bits, and would itself fail the tester. The same argument applies to the boxed version at a further point provided Z is rigid throughout that part of the cone. External finiteness in the first conclusion allows each ordinary finite size; a single nonstandard finite extension need not admit such an external enumeration.

Fregean components in finite monadic signatures. Suppose the signature contains only finitely many individual, propositional and unary individual-predicate names, and every input of F is an individual or a first-order relation e^n\to t, including n=0. At any component satisfying the Fregean Axiom, the finite-subrelation argument covers all weakly rigid subrelations of the image of F, for any size of individual domain. To see this, color each individual by the truth values of the named unary predicates. Every name-fixing transposition of two individuals of the same color induces an automorphism of the local typed structure. At a Fregean point necessity is the identity on the two-element propositional algebra and actual coextension suffices for identity. Recursively the action on \bar\sigma t sends f to \lambda\bar y\, .\,\exists\bar x\,(\bigwedge_i T_i(x_i,y_i)\land f[\bar x]), where the T_i are the graphs of the actions already defined at the input sorts. At e use the definable graph of the two-point swap and at t use identity. Each resulting map is an involution and preserves application and quantification. This works with Henkin domains, because the transformed relation is given by a lambda term. All closed descriptors are fixed.

Use a tester with full actual row and descriptor saying that, if the Fregean Axiom holds, every relation argument is fixed by all such transpositions and each individual argument is one of the individual names of \Sigma^+. This necessarily holds on all root members, hence weak inextensibility puts F below the tester. In a Fregean component each permitted e^n\to t relation is a union of the finitely many tuple classes given by equality patterns, equalities to names and colors. Any two tuples of the same class are connected by a finite product of the allowed swaps. Every union of classes is given by a closed finite disjunction, and every allowed individual coordinate is named by the separate guard. Thus the image of F is supported on a finite list of named tuples there. This excludes these components as sites of failure for root power testers. Non-Fregean or higher-type cases remain.

Remaining questions for the family

  • Tame Rigidity and Weakly Rigid Power. Both fail for every infinite signature, as shown above. When there are infinitely many individual constants the original argument is as follows. The root property with every constant in its root extension and descriptor \lambda x\, .\,\top is weakly rigid at W_0, by generalization on a fresh constant. It is rigid only if C proves BF at type e. Weakly Rigid Power implies Tame Rigidity. With finitely many constants, a weakly rigid root relation with finite root extension has a descriptor provably equal to a disjunction of haecceities of closed terms, and so is rigid. For finite signatures, a bound with infinitely many current tuples is still a potential obstruction.

Weakly rigid bounds have finite numerical images

OpenAI GPT-6, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 November 2026. Independently reviewed ordinary proof.

Let F be weakly rigid at the signature-definable root, with descriptor u. Suppose d(\bar x) is a term such that \mathrm C(\Theta)\vdash u(\bar x)\to\mathbb N_e(d(\bar x)). There is a finite set K of standard natural numbers for which \mathrm C(\Theta)\vdash \forall\bar x\,\Big(u(\bar x)\to \bigvee_{k\in K}d(\bar x)=\bar k_e\Big). In particular every weakly rigid bound below the Frege numbers at e has finite descriptor support, even when its current row contains many entities in each descriptor fibre. The same holds whenever d is C-provably injective on u. Both canonical powers of these bounds are fully rigid.

Let S be the descriptor tuples of the actual F-members. Every \bar s\in S satisfies \vdash u(\bar s). Each d(\bar s) is C-provably equal to a standard numeral: the actual set of standard numeral denotations is an available identity row of an inductive predicate at the root, and equality of two closed terms there implies provable identity by completeness of the old components.

Suppose infinitely many standard indices occur on S. Let \Delta say that I_e^* holds and that d[u] is cofinal in \mathbb N_e. This is consistent: interpret the free constants in a full classical one-world hierarchy over e=\mathbb N. Its Frege numbers at e are standard, and each provable u(\bar s) and d(\bar s)=\bar k_e holds there. An infinite subset of these standard indices is cofinal.

In the ordinary first-order arithmetic language form the theory T=\{A:\mathrm C(\Theta)+\Delta\vdash A^e\}, where A^e is the Frege interpretation. It is consistent, recursively enumerable and extends PA under the Peano condition in \Delta. For effectiveness only the finite sublanguage of \Delta is needed, by conservativity of adjoining free constants. Fix the usual primitive recursive verification of proof certificates for T with enumeration certificates for its recursively enumerable axioms. For every fixed standard k, PA verifies the finite assertion that no code i\le k is a T-proof of contradiction. Thus the tester v(\bar x):=\Delta\to \forall i\in\mathbb N_e\, (i\le d(\bar x)\to\neg\mathrm{Prf}_T(i,\bot)) is C-provable on each \bar s\in S. Weak inextensibility, applied to the full current row with old descriptor v, gives \mathrm C(\Theta)\vdash\forall\bar x\,(u(\bar x)\to v(\bar x)). The cofinality conjunct of \Delta now implies T\vdash\mathrm{Con}(T), contrary to Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem. The arithmetic interpretation used here is also used in [[possibility-and-necessity-of-arithmetic-incompatible]].

The indices occurring on S therefore form a finite K. Their disjunction is C-provable on every member of S; the same weak inextensibility argument proves the displayed theorem. If the numerical labeling is injective on u, any two ground tuples with the same index are provably identical, leaving only finitely many descriptor classes. With one actual representative of each class, weak persistence and weak inextensibility identify every old transport of F with the finite union of their transported tuple-haecceities. At every old point each weakly rigid subrelation is consequently a finite subunion and lifts from a rigid finite subunion at the root. This gives full rigidity of both powers: root weak subrelations have the same finite-support property, and the old finite-bound instances follow from [[internally-finite-rigid-power]].
