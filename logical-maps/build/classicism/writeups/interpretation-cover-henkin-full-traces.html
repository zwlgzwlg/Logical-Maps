Interpretation cover: Henkin and full subset traces

Daniel Kodsi, with ChatGPT assistance, 30 October 2026, using the named interpretation cover and earlier component constructions credited in Section 6. No independent human check or Lean proof is recorded.

1. Result and construction

A specified enlargement of the named-permutation interpretation cover has the joint root properties

\text{Possibility+ for }\Sigma, \quad\text{BF}, \quad\text{Relational Choice}, \quad\text{Vicinity}, \quad\text{Atomlessness}, \quad\neg\text{Rigid Power}.

BF and Relational Choice range over all their admitted types. The positive schemata are unboxed. Possibility+ has exactly the native free-individual-variable and constant-exclusion conditions; its consequences include pure and signature Maximalism. Rigid Power fails at bound type (et)t, whose power property has type ((et)t)t.

Work in ZFC with the interpretation-root existence input: a set-sized family of faithful pointed C action models supplies every closed pure C-consistent sentence with a witness. Retain these components unchanged and add two disjoint isolated terminal components H and K, defined below. Their substructure relation is used in their construction only; it is not an arrow between them.

Choose infinite typed auxiliary name sets large enough to dominate all old domains and to accommodate the prescribed set-sized free signature. At the root take all typed name permutations as loops, and all typed interpretations as arrows to the old objects. There are no arrows back to the root. The designated root symmetry group is trivial. Relational entities are precisely profiles with a finite typed-name pin and an admitted whole section at every old interpretation. Rows use the full argument domains at their targets, and transport is precomposition. Constants denote designated names and named lifts.

This is a fresh instance of the cover construction. Its root is not asserted to be elementary over a previously chosen root, so the positive verdicts are checked again in Section 5.

2. Two terminal components

Let K be the full finite-type hierarchy on \mathbb N, with two truth values and extensional equality. Present its semantics in a countable many-sorted first-order language containing application, the logical and quantifier operations, a denotation constructor for every finite-parameter typed lambda template, and a name for every individual n\in\mathbb N.

Countable closure under these functions and existential witnesses gives an elementary substructure H containing every individual. Its whole collection of sorts is countable and H_e=\mathbb N. Elementarity of the quantifier clauses makes its inherited quantifiers range over the full displayed H domains. All parameterized lambda denotations and singleton predicates remain admitted.

Relations of H can be regarded as relations on its own recursively restricted argument domains. This loses no identities: if two H relations differ, elementarity of extensionality supplies a distinguishing tuple from H. Thus both components are terminal extensional Henkin models of C. Their modal operator is identity; every admitted relation is rigid, and each component individually satisfies Rigid Power.

Write s_n for the property of the individual n, and \operatorname{Sing} for the closed relation whose instances are exactly these singleton properties. Both components admit them. There are only countably many sets

\{n\in\mathbb N:Ys_n\},\qquad Y\in D_{(et)t}(H). \tag{1}

Choose A\subseteq\mathbb N outside this collection of traces. Full K admits the relation Y_A with extension \{s_n:n\in A\}. No H relation has even the same trace on the singletons. This formulation does not identify an unreified K representative with its restriction to H.

Admission and C closure at the enlarged root

An empty pin means that a row may depend on its target object but not on the interpretation into that fixed object. This allows the component-specific profiles used below. A profile at relational type \sigma_1\cdots\sigma_n t has rows on the already defined lower argument domains, so domain formation is a recursion on finite types.

If two arrows to the same target agree on the finite pin of an entity, then all their continuations still agree there; its complete transported sections coincide. Combining the pins of finitely many parameters therefore fixes their whole transported environment. Evaluating a term over all target tuples proves pinning, and its entire old section is exactly the old admitted term denotation. Quantification keeps the full old domain, even at a non-surjective entrance. This establishes closure under abstraction, application, quantification and the modal terms.

Literal root identity is separated by an admitted predicate with the same singleton row at every root loop and Bottom at all old targets. Beta and eta are whole profile identities. H-theorem equivalence holds at every target assignment, validating C’s Logical Equivalence without using optional root premises. The named lifts satisfy \rho_I\widehat c=I(c) as whole old entities, and individual names transport by I_e.

3. A rigid bound with incompatible subsets

For each n, define a root entity a_n:et with empty rows at every root loop, section s_n at each H and K entrance, and whole Bottom sections at every other old component. It is admitted with an empty pin. Every root loop fixes it as a whole entity, and the a_n are pairwise distinct because their H and K sections differ.

Put E=\{a_n:n\in\mathbb N\}. Define F:(et)t by the following sections: row E at every root loop, the admitted \operatorname{Sing} at H and K, and the haecceity of \bot_{et} throughout every other component. The latter sections transport naturally as the old equality terms. Thus F is admitted with an empty pin.

At every arrow h out of the root,

F_h=\rho_h[E]. \tag{2}

The same identity persists after further arrows. It proves full rigidity of F: membership persists, and any future member is the transport of a member at the starting target. Every test necessary on the latter members therefore covers the former, proving weak inextensibility at every target.

Now let R:(et)t be any current rigid relation with R\le F, and set

B=\{n:R a_n\}.

Persistence places s_n in its image at every H and K entrance whenever n\in B. To obtain the reverse inclusion, take n\notin B and choose V separately as H or K. Admit the test Z_n^V with row D_{et}(o)\setminus\{a_n\} at every root loop, section D_{et}(V)\setminus\{s_n\} at V, and whole Top at all other old components. It has an empty pin and lands as an admitted singleton complement at the terminal object V. Isolation of the two added components makes these assignments compatible with every continuation.

The test is necessary on every current R-member a_m: root loops fix a_m, its V image is s_m\ne s_n, and elsewhere the test is Top. Weak inextensibility of R gives R\le Z_n^V. Applying the separately admitted test for each omitted n shows that every H image and every K image of R has exactly the singleton trace B. No external intersection of all these tests is required.

Consequently no such R can have image Y_A at any K entrance. If it did, its trace would be A, and any H entrance would produce an admitted H relation with the forbidden trace (1).

4. Failure of the actual canonical power

Take the native lambda entity

P_F=\lambda R^{(et)t}\,.\, \operatorname{Rigid}(R)\land R\le F.

Term transport sends it at each K entrance to the canonical power of \operatorname{Sing}_K. That power contains Y_A: every relation in terminal K is rigid and Y_A\le\operatorname{Sing}_K.

Admit a higher test Z which is Top at every root loop and every old component except K, where it excludes exactly Y_A. Its K section is the complement of Y_A’s haecceity, and it is pinned by the empty set. Every current member of P_F necessarily satisfies Z, because Section 3 excludes Y_A as its image at every K entrance. Nevertheless, at a K entrance the new member Y_A belongs to the transported P_F and fails Z. Hence

\forall R\,(P_FR\to\Box ZR) \quad\text{holds, but}\quad P_F\nleq Z.

The canonical power P_F is not weakly inextensible and therefore not rigid, although F is rigid. This refutes Rigid Power at the stated bound type. The proof concerns the actual lambda entity, not a replacement with the same extension.

Within this particular isolated-terminal architecture, the same argument shows that weak inextensibility of the canonical power forces agreement of the two admitted singleton-trace families: intersect a representative with \operatorname{Sing} and repeat the missing-trace test. This is a necessary condition only, not a sufficient condition or a general characterization of Rigid Power.

5. The positive package on the enlarged domains

BF. Root-loop witnesses lift by inverse permutations. For a witness at an old interpretation I, let S pin its predicate. Choose a typed interpretation having infinite fibres and mapping onto every target domain, and modify it on S to agree with I. The name sets were chosen large enough to permit this; finite modification leaves every fibre nonempty. Named lifts make the resulting entrance onto on every actual relational domain, as well as on individuals. The full predicate row is unchanged by pinning, so the target witness has a current preimage at that entrance. This proves current BF at all admitted types.

Relational Choice. Every external current relation table is admitted: use that table at all root loops and whole Bottom in all old components. External ZFC choice supplies a selecting relational graph for every current serial relation. This includes graphs with individual outputs and does not assert Functional Choice.

Vicinity. The proposition true at all root loops and false at all old entrances is admitted with an empty pin. Each loop has an inverse returning to identity, so this proposition entails the possibility of every current truth. It is a true Vicinity witness.

Atomlessness. If a nonbottom scalar has a true root-loop row, vary a fresh individual name by a permutation while preserving its finite pin. An admitted root-only scalar testing that name’s image splits it. If its truth occurs at an old interpretation, vary a fresh propositional name between old Top and Bottom and use the corresponding named lift. Both cases apply to all newly admitted scalar profiles as well.

Possibility+ for the prescribed signature. Replace the finitely many constants in a C(\Sigma)-consistent formula by variables and existentially close these and its displayed individual variables. A retained pure witness supplies the required values. The native antecedent says the root individuals are pairwise distinct and distinct from the individual constants occurring in the formula. It therefore makes their name prescriptions compatible with those for the constants. Assign the witnessed values and extend to a typed interpretation. Named lifts recover the whole denotations, making the original formula possible. Target values may coincide. No free relational-parameter strengthening is used.

6. Attribution and limits

The underlying named cover and its positive properties are prior work: Cian Dorr’s interpretation-root machinery and Observer covers and Boolean completeness, Sections 8.2–8.3, by OpenAI Codex, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 26 October 2026, followed by the explicit named expansion and additional verdicts by GPT-6 Astra, prompted by Daniel Kodsi, 29–30 October 2026. Coalesced-sum arguments already use component-specific profiles and haecceities of Bottom. Elementary Henkin closure and singleton-complement tests are also prior methods.

The added argument uses two individually Rigid-Power-positive terminal components whose admitted subset traces differ. One root subrelation must land in both, and this prevents a lift of the missing trace. The negative belongs to this specified augmented model; it is not assigned to every possible witness family in the original named-cover record. The relative existence input remains explicit, and no boxed positive, Boolean Completeness or Strong Possibility verdict is inferred.
